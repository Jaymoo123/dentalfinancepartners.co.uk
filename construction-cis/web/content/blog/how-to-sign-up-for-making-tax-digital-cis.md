faqs:
- question: How do I actually sign up for Making Tax Digital for Income Tax?
  answer: Sign in to the HMRC sign-up service using the same Government Gateway user ID and password you used to register for Self Assessment. HMRC will verify your identity, ask which tax year you are starting from, and ask for details of each self-employment and property income source you have (business name, address and nature of the business, plus the start date if the business began in the last 2 tax years). You then choose MTD-compatible software and authorise it to exchange data with HMRC. You must be registered for Self Assessment and have submitted a tax return in the last 2 years before you can sign up.
- question: Do I have to sign up if HMRC has already told me I am in scope?
  answer: Yes. Being within the qualifying income threshold puts you in scope, but the sign-up is a separate action you or your agent must complete. HMRC writes to people it identifies from their Self Assessment returns, and that letter is a prompt to sign up, not a sign-up in itself. Nothing happens automatically and no quarterly update can be submitted until the sign-up is complete and software is linked.
- question: Can my accountant sign me up for MTD instead of me doing it?
  answer: Yes. An agent needs an agent services account, which is separate from the older HMRC online services for agents account. If they already hold a Self Assessment authorisation for you, that authorisation is recognised for MTD for Income Tax and they can proceed. If they hold no authorisation, they send you a digital handshake request from their agent services account which you approve, and they then sign you up and submit on your behalf.
- question: What information do I need to hand before starting the sign-up?
  answer: Your Government Gateway user ID and password from Self Assessment registration, your National Insurance number, the name and trading address of your business and a description of what it does, the date the business started if that was within the last 2 tax years, the tax year you are starting MTD from, and details of any other self-employment or property income you have. If you cannot pass the identity check on the credentials alone, HMRC may ask security questions about records it already holds for you.
- question: Which update periods should a CIS subcontractor choose?
  answer: Standard periods run to 5 July, 5 October, 5 January and 5 April, with deadlines of 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May. Calendar periods run to 30 June, 30 September, 31 December and 31 March with the same four deadlines. Most CIS subcontractors work to the tax year and should take the standard periods, because the income figures then line up with the tax year totals used in the final declaration. If your accounts run to 31 March, calendar periods are the cleaner fit. You choose before your first update and cannot change part way through a tax year once you have started submitting.
- question: Will quarterly updates get my CIS refund back to me faster?
  answer: No. Quarterly updates carry income and expense totals only. No tax is calculated and no repayment is triggered at the quarterly stage. Your CIS deductions suffered are entered at the final declaration after the tax year ends, which is when the overpayment is identified and repaid. Signing up for MTD changes how often you report, not when a sole trader refund arrives.
- question: I am a subcontractor with £46,000 of CIS income and £9,000 of rent. Am I in scope?
  answer: Yes, from 6 April 2026 if those figures were on your 2024/25 return. Qualifying income combines self-employment and property income and is measured before expenses, so the test is on £55,000 rather than on either source separately. This scenario catches a lot of tradespeople who bought a rental property, because neither income stream looks big enough on its own. The rental business and the CIS trade are then reported as two separate businesses within your software, each with its own quarterly update.
- question: What if I sign up and my income later drops below the threshold?
  answer: You stay in MTD for Income Tax once you have started, rather than dropping out automatically the first year turnover falls. A run of quiet quarters on site does not remove the obligation. If your self-employment and property income sources have ceased entirely you tell HMRC, and HMRC updates your records and writes to confirm you no longer need to use MTD. Otherwise the practical answer is to keep filing the quarterly updates.
- question: Can I get an exemption from MTD for Income Tax?
  answer: There are grounds for exemption, the main one being that you are digitally excluded, for example because of age, disability, location with no reliable internet access, or religious belief. Applications are made to HMRC and can be submitted by you, by your tax agent, or by a friend or family member with your authorisation. A temporary exemption is also available until at least April 2027 in defined circumstances. Apply before your start date rather than waiting, because until an exemption is granted the obligation stands.
- question: Do limited company directors in construction need to sign up?
  answer: Not for the company. MTD for Income Tax applies to individuals and partnerships filing Self Assessment, not to companies, so a CIS limited company continues to reclaim deductions through the Employer Payment Summary and file Corporation Tax returns as before. A director personally can still be caught if they have qualifying self-employment or property income of their own outside the company, in which case they sign up as an individual for that income only.

<h2>Which return HMRC tests, and when your MTD sign-up is due</h2>

<p>HMRC decides whether you are in Making Tax Digital for Income Tax by reading the Self Assessment return you have already filed. Qualifying income over £50,000 on the 2024/25 return brought sole traders into MTD from 6 April 2026, over £30,000 on the 2025/26 return brings them in from 6 April 2027, and over £20,000 on the 2026/27 return from 6 April 2028. This page covers the mechanics of the sign-up itself for a CIS subcontractor: what HMRC asks for at each stage, when your accountant can do it instead, which update periods to pick, and what quarterly reporting does to the timing of a CIS refund.</p>

<p>Qualifying income is measured before expenses and combines self-employment and property income. For a CIS subcontractor it is the <strong>gross</strong> value of the work invoiced, not the net figure that lands in the bank after 20% deductions. That distinction, and the way it catches subcontractors who assume their bank receipts are the test, is covered in full in our guide to <a href="/blog/vat-and-mtd/mtd-income-tax-cis">Making Tax Digital and CIS</a>.</p>

<p>Being in scope and being signed up are two different things. HMRC writes to the people it identifies from their returns, but that letter is a prompt, not a registration. Nothing is switched on automatically, and no quarterly update can be filed until the sign-up is done and software is linked to your HMRC record.</p>

<h2>What a subcontractor needs before starting the sign-up</h2>

<p>Two conditions have to be met before HMRC will let you through the service at all. You must be registered for Self Assessment, and you must have submitted a tax return in the last 2 years. A subcontractor who registered for CIS but has never filed a return cannot sign up until that first return is in.</p>

<p>Have the following to hand before you start:</p>

<ul>
<li>The Government Gateway user ID and password you got when you registered for Self Assessment. The sign-up will not accept a different account.</li>
<li>Your National Insurance number and Unique Taxpayer Reference.</li>
<li>The trading name and address of your business, and a short description of the work (for example, groundworks, electrical installation, plastering).</li>
<li>The date the business started, if that was within the last 2 tax years.</li>
<li>The tax year you are starting MTD from.</li>
<li>Details of every other self-employment or property income source you have, because each is reported as a separate business.</li>
</ul>

<p>HMRC will run an identity check. If the Gateway credentials alone are not enough it asks security questions based on records it already holds, so having a recent payslip, P60 or previous return nearby saves a restart.</p>

<h2>Signing yourself up for MTD: the walkthrough</h2>

<p>The sequence on the HMRC service is short, but the order matters. Choosing software last is the most common cause of a sign-up that stalls half finished.</p>

<ol>
<li><strong>Check you are not exempt.</strong> If you have grounds for exemption, apply first. Until an exemption is granted the obligation stands.</li>
<li><strong>Pick MTD-compatible software before you sign up.</strong> It must be on HMRC's list of recognised software for MTD for Income Tax. Handling CIS or Self Assessment is not the same qualification. Our review of the <a href="/blog/software-and-tools/best-cis-accounting-software">best CIS accounting software</a> compares the packages that handle contractor statements and quarterly submissions together.</li>
<li><strong>Sign in to the HMRC sign-up service</strong> with your Self Assessment Government Gateway user ID and password, and clear the identity check.</li>
<li><strong>Confirm the tax year</strong> you are starting from. Choosing the wrong year is the error that causes the most trouble later, because it determines which quarterly updates HMRC expects and which are treated as missing.</li>
<li><strong>Enter your business details</strong> for each income source: name, address, nature of the trade, and start date where it falls in the last 2 tax years. A CIS trade and a rental property are entered separately.</li>
<li><strong>Choose your update periods</strong>, standard or calendar. See the section below.</li>
<li><strong>Authorise your software</strong> to interact with HMRC. This is a separate approval inside the software itself, granting it permission against your HMRC record. Sign-up without this step leaves you unable to submit.</li>
<li><strong>Enter your CIS records from 6 April of the start year onwards</strong> so the first quarterly update reflects a full period rather than the weeks since you set the software up.</li>
</ol>

<p>You can also sign up voluntarily before you are required to. For a subcontractor heading towards the April 2027 or April 2028 thresholds, a voluntary year is a genuinely useful rehearsal, because the first update deadline after a rushed sign-up is not the moment to discover that contractor statements are not reconciling.</p>

<h2>Letting an accountant sign you up: agent authorisation for MTD</h2>

<p>An agent can complete the whole sign-up for you, and for most subcontractors that is the sensible route, because the same person then owns the quarterly submissions and the final declaration.</p>

<p>The agent needs an <strong>agent services account</strong>, which is a different account from the older HMRC online services for agents. Authorisation then falls into one of three positions:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Already authorised for Self Assessment.</strong> That existing authorisation is recognised for MTD for Income Tax. The agent checks it appears in their agent services account and adds it if it is missing. No new paperwork from you.</li>
<li><strong>Already authorised through a digital handshake.</strong> The agent can sign you up without adding anything.</li>
<li><strong>Not yet authorised.</strong> The agent sends an authorisation request from their agent services account, you approve it, and they proceed.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Choosing update periods as a CIS subcontractor</h2>

<p>At sign-up you choose between standard and calendar update periods. Both carry the same four deadlines. The difference is where each period ends.</p>

<p>Most CIS subcontractors should take the standard periods, because they end on the same dates as the tax year the final declaration is built on, so nothing needs re-cutting at year end. Calendar periods suit a business whose accounts already run to 31 March. You choose before your first submission and cannot switch part way through a tax year once you have begun.</p>

<p>One point specific to construction: the CIS payment and deduction statement from each contractor is your source document for the income figure in each update, and the gross amount on it is the income entry. Getting statements in promptly matters more under quarterly reporting than it did under an annual return, because a missing March statement now shows up in an update filed in May rather than in a return filed the following January. Our guide to <a href="/blog/cis-compliance/cis-record-keeping-guide">CIS record keeping</a> sets out what to retain and for how long.</p>

<h2>What quarterly updates do to CIS refund timing</h2>

<p>This is the question subcontractors ask most often at sign-up, and the honest answer is that quarterly reporting does not bring the money forward.</p>

<p>A quarterly update carries income and expense totals only. No tax is calculated at that stage, no payment is due, and no repayment is triggered. Your CIS deductions suffered across the year are not part of the quarterly figures at all: they are entered at the final declaration after 5 April, which is the point at which the deductions taken at 20% on your labour are set against your actual liability and any overpayment is identified.</p>

<p>So the refund route for a sole-trader subcontractor is unchanged in timing. You can file from 6 April after the tax year ends, the final declaration deadline is 31 January following, and the repayment follows the filing. What MTD changes is that the final declaration cannot be submitted until the quarterly updates for that year are in, which means a subcontractor who ignores the quarterly obligation for a year has blocked their own refund rather than merely delayed some paperwork. The mechanics of the claim itself are covered in <a href="/blog/cis-refunds/cis-tax-refund-how-to-claim">how to claim your CIS tax refund</a>.</p>

<p>Limited companies are outside MTD for Income Tax entirely, and the real-time recovery of deductions through the Employer Payment Summary is unaffected by any of this. If the speed of recovery is what is driving your thinking, the structural comparison in <a href="/blog/cis-basics/cis-sole-trader-vs-limited-company">CIS sole trader or limited company</a> is the more useful read.</p>

<h2>Penalties and exemptions once you are signed up</h2>

<p>For subcontractors mandated from April 2026, HMRC will not issue penalty points for late quarterly updates during 2026/27. That easement covers quarterly-update points and nothing else. Late final declarations and late payments are penalised in full from day one.</p>

<p>From 2027/28 onwards the points system runs normally: one point per missed quarterly update or tax return deadline, a £200 penalty at 4 points, and a further £200 for each miss after that. On the payment side for 2026/27, nothing is charged up to 15 days late, 3% of the tax outstanding at day 15 applies from day 16, a further 3% of the amount outstanding at day 30 applies from day 31, and an annualised 10% then runs on the balance until it is paid. The first two percentages rise to 4% for 2027/28.</p>

<p>If you cannot reasonably use digital tools, exemption is available on digitally excluded grounds, which include age, disability, location without reliable internet access and religious belief. A temporary exemption is also available until at least April 2027 in defined circumstances. You, your tax agent, or a friend or family member with your authorisation can apply, and a single application should cover every ground you are relying on. Apply before your start date, because the obligation continues until the exemption is granted.</p>

<p>The sign-up itself takes minutes. The work that matters sits either side of it: picking software that handles contractor statements properly, choosing the right start year and update periods, and getting your CIS records in from the first day of the tax year rather than from the day the software was installed. Get those three right and the quarterly cycle becomes routine, and the final declaration produces the refund it always did.</p>
