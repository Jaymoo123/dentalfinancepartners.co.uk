faqs:
- question: How many days should I allow to rewire a three-bedroom house?
  answer: UK trade cost guides published in 2026 typically allow 5 to 7 working days for a full 3-bed rewire, 3 to 4 days for a 2-bed, 7 to 10 days for a 4-bed and 10 to 14 days for a 5-bed. Those figures usually assume more than one operative on site for the first-fix phase and an occupied property. An empty property with lifted floors and no furniture to work around runs faster, and trade guides commonly report empty-property rewires costing 15% to 25% less than the same job occupied. Price the days you will actually be on site, not the days the guide quotes.
- question: Is a full house rewire notifiable under Part P?
  answer: Yes. Installing new circuits and replacing the consumer unit are both notifiable electrical work in dwellings in England and Wales, and a full rewire does both. You either self-certify through a government-approved competent person scheme (NICEIC, NAPIT, ELECSA and BRE Certification are the main electrical schemes) and notify through them, or the work is notified to local authority building control, or an unregistered installer has the work certified by a registered third party. All work in a special location, which includes any room containing a bath or shower, is notifiable in its own right.
- question: What does the CIS deduction apply to on a rewire invoice?
  answer: The labour element only. If you invoice a contractor £4,350 for a rewire split as £2,900 labour and £1,450 materials, the 20% deduction is £580, not £870. Materials you buy and supply are excluded from the CIS deduction base entirely. If your invoice shows a single total with no split, a contractor will usually apply the deduction to the whole figure, and on a materials-heavy rewire that difference is real money out of your account until you file.
- question: Do I have to operate CIS if I take a mate on to help with a rewire?
  answer: If you pay someone else who is self-employed to carry out construction work for you, you are acting as a contractor under the Construction Industry Scheme, whatever the size of your own business. You must verify them with HMRC, deduct at the verified rate, issue payment and deduction statements, and file a monthly CIS300 return by the 19th of the following tax month. From 6 April 2026 a nil return is required for every tax month in which you make no payments, unless you have pre-notified HMRC of inactivity. If instead you put them on the payroll as an employee, employer NIC applies at 15% on earnings above the £5,000 secondary threshold.
- question: What if the rewire uncovers problems that were not in my quote?
  answer: Price a survey and an inspection before you quote a fixed price, and write your variation mechanism into the quote itself. The common surprises on older stock are perished rubber or lead-sheathed cable that cannot be reused as a draw route, undersized or non-existent main earthing and bonding, brittle plaster that comes away in sheets when chased, asbestos-containing materials in artex or board, and a supply that needs the distribution network operator involved. Each of these can add days. A quote that states an hourly or daily rate for work outside the specified scope, with the customer signing off before you proceed, is the difference between a variation and an argument.
- question: Should I quote a house rewire as a fixed price or a day rate?
  answer: Fixed price is what most domestic customers expect, and it is what your competitors are quoting, so a day rate on domestic work tends to lose you the job. The safe position is a fixed price for a clearly specified scope (number of circuits, accessory positions, consumer unit specification, what is and is not included in making good) with a stated day rate for anything outside it. On subcontract work for a builder or developer the position often reverses, because a day rate transfers the risk of surprises to the main contractor.
- question: Is making good included in a rewire price?
  answer: Usually not, and this is the single most common source of dispute on domestic rewires. Chasing walls for cables and lifting floors causes real damage, and plastering and decorating afterwards is a separate trade. Trade cost guides report that making good adds roughly 15% to 25% to a rewire cost. State clearly in the quote whether you are leaving the property chased and made safe, filling and finishing to a plasterable standard, or arranging a plasterer yourself. If you are arranging another trade and paying them for construction work, that payment sits inside CIS.
- question: Does the domestic reverse charge apply to a rewire?
  answer: Only where you are supplying another VAT-registered and CIS-registered business that is not the end user. A rewire for a private householder is not covered, because the householder is an end user and is not registered for CIS. A rewire carried out as a subcontractor for a builder or developer who will sell the work on is normally within the domestic reverse charge, meaning you invoice with no VAT and the customer accounts for it. New-build housing is zero-rated and outside the reverse charge.
- question: Can I charge 5% VAT on a rewire in an empty house?
  answer: Only if you are supplying the householder or property owner directly. The reduced rate for renovating or altering a dwelling that has not been lived in for the two years immediately before work starts is set out in section 8 of VAT Notice 708, but section 2.1.3 makes subcontractor supplies on the same job standard-rated. So an electrician quoting the owner of a long-empty house directly can charge 5%, while the same electrician working under a main contractor on that house charges 20%. That is a 15-point difference in the price the customer sees, and it is worth establishing who you are actually contracting with before you quote.
- question: Will a big rewire year push me into Making Tax Digital?
  answer: Possibly. Making Tax Digital for Income Tax applies from 6 April 2026 where qualifying income exceeded £50,000 in 2024/25, from 6 April 2027 where it exceeded £30,000 in 2025/26, and from 6 April 2028 where it exceeded £20,000 in 2026/27. Qualifying income is gross turnover before expenses and before CIS deductions, so a year with three or four large supply-and-fix rewires in it can cross a threshold even though the cash that reached your account was much lower. Separately, VAT registration is compulsory once taxable turnover in the last 12 months exceeds £90,000.
- question: How do rewire materials affect my CIS refund?
  answer: Materials are excluded from the CIS deduction base, so they do not increase the amount deducted, but they are still a deductible business expense that reduces your taxable profit on your Self Assessment return. An electrician who buys £14,000 of cable, accessories and consumer units across a year of rewires and fails to record them properly is declaring £14,000 more profit than they actually made. Keep the wholesaler invoices and match each one to the job it was bought for.

<h2>What a rewire actually costs a CIS subcontractor to deliver</h2>

<p>Trade cost guides published in 2026 put a full rewire of a three-bedroom house at roughly <strong>£5,000 to £7,500</strong>, spread over five to seven working days. Checkatrade's guidance puts the national average for a full house rewire at around <strong>£6,500</strong>, and other cost guides quote a wider £3,500 to £12,000 across all property sizes. Those are retail ranges quoted to homeowners, and they are the numbers your customer has already read before they ring you.</p>

<p>They are not, however, your cost of delivery, and they are certainly not what a main contractor will pay you for the same work on a refurbishment. Pricing a rewire profitably means building the number from the bottom up, from operative days, materials, certification and travel, then checking it against the market range rather than starting from the market range and hoping. This guide covers the cost stack, the compliance costs that belong in the quote, the margin traps specific to rewires, payment staging, and the tax consequences that only show up after the job is priced.</p>

<h2>Building the price: the cost stack for a rewire subcontractor</h2>

<p>A rewire price has six components, and underpricing almost always comes from omitting one of the last three rather than getting the first three wrong.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Operative days.</strong> First fix (lifting floors, chasing, cable runs, back boxes), second fix (accessories, consumer unit, terminations), then inspection and testing. Count operative days, not calendar days. A three-bed done in six calendar days with a mate on site for four of them is ten operative days.</li>
<li><strong>Materials.</strong> Cable, back boxes, accessories, the consumer unit and protective devices, fixings, fire-stopping and consumables. On a supply-and-fix rewire this is commonly a third or more of the invoice value.</li>
<li><strong>Certification and notification.</strong> Testing time, the Electrical Installation Certificate with full schedules, and either your competent person scheme notification or a building control fee.</li>
<li><strong>Travel and vehicle.</strong> Every day on site is a return journey. Claimed at the <strong>Approved Mileage Allowance Payments rate of 55p per mile for the first 10,000 business miles from 6 April 2026</strong> (25p thereafter), a 30-mile round trip across a six-day job is £99 of cost that most quotes silently absorb.</li>
<li><strong>Overhead recovery.</strong> Scheme registration, public liability and tools insurance, calibration of test equipment, phone, accountancy, and the unbilled hours spent surveying and quoting. Spread across your realistic billable days in the year, this is rarely less than £40 to £60 a day.</li>
<li><strong>Risk and margin.</strong> The contingency for what the floorboards are hiding, plus actual profit.</li>
</ul>

<p>Test equipment deserves a note of its own. Multifunction testers, insulation resistance testers and their annual calibration are a substantial ongoing cost for an electrician and are dealt with through capital allowances rather than as a straight expense. Our guide to <a href="/blog/expenses/vans-tools-capital-allowances-trades-2026-27">vans, tools and capital allowances for trades in 2026/27</a> covers how to claim them, and the wider <a href="/blog/expenses/allowable-expenses-cis-subcontractor">expenses CIS subcontractors can claim</a> sets out the mileage, PPE and insurance side.</p>

<h2>Part P notification and certification costs the subcontractor carries</h2>

<p>A full rewire installs new circuits and replaces the consumer unit, and both are notifiable electrical work in dwellings in England and Wales under Part P of the Building Regulations. Any work in a special location, which includes every room containing a bath or shower, is notifiable in its own right.</p>

<p>There are three routes. You self-certify as a member of a government-approved competent person scheme (NICEIC, NAPIT, ELECSA and BRE Certification are the principal electrical schemes) and notify through the scheme, which issues the Building Regulations Compliance Certificate. You notify local authority building control before starting, where trade sources report typical fees of <strong>£250 to £400</strong> for electrical work. Or, if unregistered, you have the work certified by a registered third party. The competent person route carries no per-job fee beyond your annual scheme registration, which is why the annual fee is worth checking directly with your scheme and then recovering across your billable days rather than treating it as a sunk cost.</p>

<p>A full rewire is certified with an Electrical Installation Certificate and full schedules of inspection and test results. A Minor Works Certificate does not cover it. Testing and certification time is chargeable time and should appear in your build-up.</p>

<p>One live point for 2026 quotes: <strong>BS 7671:2018+A4:2026</strong> was published in April 2026, and the previous Amendment 3 version remains a valid standard only until <strong>15 October 2026</strong>. Installations completed after that date are designed and certified to Amendment 4, which among other changes retains the requirement for arc fault detection devices on single-phase socket-outlet final circuits up to 32A in houses in multiple occupation, purpose-built student accommodation, care homes and high-rise residential buildings, while continuing to recommend them elsewhere. If you are quoting an HMO rewire, the AFDD requirement is a specification and cost question, not an optional extra.</p>

<h2>Five margin traps that turn a profitable rewire into a bad CIS job</h2>

<p>Rewires go wrong in predictable ways. Each of these is a variation clause waiting to be written.</p>

<p><strong>Old wiring that will not draw out.</strong> Perished rubber, lead-sheathed or early PVC cable frequently cannot be used as a draw route for the new run, which turns a straightforward replacement into fresh chasing and floor lifting. Survey before you quote.</p>

<p><strong>Earthing and bonding that is not there.</strong> Older properties routinely need main protective bonding to gas and water, and sometimes a new main earthing arrangement or supplier involvement. That is days, not hours, and it is not part of a rewire quote unless you say it is.</p>

<p><strong>Plaster that fails.</strong> Lime plaster and old lath come away in sheets when chased. The scope boundary between "chased and made safe" and "made good ready to decorate" needs to be written down. Trade guides put making good at 15% to 25% on top of the rewire cost, so this is not a rounding error.</p>

<p><strong>Scope creep by conversation.</strong> Additional sockets, an outside light, a garage supply, a shower circuit. Each is a fair variation and each is unpaid if it is agreed verbally at the top of a ladder. Price additions in writing before you fit them.</p>

<p><strong>Paying a mate without operating CIS.</strong> This one is a compliance trap rather than a pricing one. The moment you pay another self-employed worker for construction work, you are a contractor under the Construction Industry Scheme. You must verify them, deduct at 20% or 30%, issue payment and deduction statements and file a monthly CIS300 by the 19th. From <strong>6 April 2026 a nil return is mandatory</strong> for every tax month with no payments unless you pre-notify inactivity, with penalties starting at £100 for one day late. See our guide to the <a href="/blog/cis-compliance/cis-nil-return-explained">CIS nil return</a> for how that obligation now works.</p>

<h2>Payment staging and cash flow for a rewire subcontractor</h2>

<p>A rewire is materials-heavy at the front end and cash-negative for the first week if you fund the wholesaler bill yourself. Stage the payments so the job pays for itself as it progresses.</p>

<h2>Worked example: a 3-bed supply-and-fix rewire as a CIS subcontractor</h2>

<p>An electrician takes a full rewire of an occupied three-bed semi as a subcontractor to a small developer. Six calendar days on site, with a second operative for four of them.</p>

<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Line</th><th>Amount</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Labour: 10 operative days charged</td><td>£2,900</td></tr>
<tr><td>Materials: cable, accessories, consumer unit, fixings</td><td>£1,450</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Invoice total</strong></td><td><strong>£4,350</strong></td></tr>
<tr><td>CIS deducted at 20% on labour only (£2,900 x 20%)</td><td>£580</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Net payment received</strong></td><td><strong>£3,770</strong></td></tr>
<tr><td>Deduction if the invoice had shown one total (£4,350 x 20%)</td><td>£870</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Cash-flow cost of failing to split the invoice</strong></td><td><strong>£290</strong></td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<p>Against that £4,350, the electrician's own costs on the job might run to £1,450 of materials, roughly £900 paid to the second operative (itself a CIS payment requiring verification and a monthly return), £99 of mileage at 55p, and around £360 of overhead recovery at £60 a day across six days. That leaves approximately £1,541 of profit before tax, or about 35% of the invoice value. Lose two days to perished cable and unrecorded bonding work, and that margin halves.</p>

<p>The £290 in the table is not lost permanently, because over-deducted CIS is recovered through <a href="/blog/cis-refunds/cis-tax-refund-how-to-claim">your Self Assessment refund claim</a> or, for a company, through the EPS route. It is a cash-flow loss for up to a year on a single job, which is why the labour and materials split on a rewire invoice matters so much. The mechanics are set out in full in our guide to <a href="/blog/cis-basics/cis-invoice-splitting-labour-materials">splitting labour and materials on a CIS invoice</a>.</p>

<h2>Whether CIS applies at all, and the VAT position on a rewire</h2>

<p>A rewire for a private householder is not within the Construction Industry Scheme, because a homeowner having work done on their own home is not a contractor. You invoice in full, no deduction is made, and the income goes on your Self Assessment return in the ordinary way. The same rewire carried out for a developer, a housebuilder, a landlord operating as a business, or a main contractor is CIS work and a deduction applies. Our guide to <a href="/blog/cis-basics/what-construction-work-is-not-cis">what construction work is not covered by CIS</a> sets out the boundary in detail.</p>

<p>VAT follows a different logic again, and on rewires there are three positions to keep straight:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Direct to a householder, standard property:</strong> 20% VAT if you are VAT-registered.</li>
<li><strong>Direct to the owner of a dwelling empty for two years or more:</strong> the reduced <strong>5%</strong> rate under section 8 of VAT Notice 708. Section 2.1.3 of the same notice makes subcontractor supplies on that job standard-rated, so this relief only reaches the business contracting with the owner.</li>
<li><strong>As a subcontractor to a VAT-registered and CIS-registered contractor who is not the end user:</strong> the domestic reverse charge applies, you invoice with no VAT, and the customer accounts for it. New-build housing is zero-rated and outside the reverse charge.</li>
</ul>

<p>Getting this wrong on a large rewire is expensive in both directions. Our guide to the <a href="/blog/vat-and-mtd/vat-reverse-charge-for-cis-subcontractors">VAT reverse charge for CIS subcontractors</a> covers the end-user rules and the de minimis position.</p>

<h2>The thresholds a big rewire year pushes you across, and what MTD means</h2>

<p>Rewires are large-ticket jobs, and a run of them changes your tax position faster than a year of small works. Three thresholds are worth watching as you quote.</p>

<thead>
<tr><th>Threshold</th><th>Figure</th><th>Consequence</th></tr>

<tr><td>MTD for Income Tax, from 6 April 2028</td><td>Qualifying income over £20,000 in 2026/27</td><td>Digital records and quarterly updates</td></tr>
</tbody>

<p>Qualifying income for Making Tax Digital is <strong>gross turnover before expenses and before CIS deductions</strong>. An electrician invoicing £34,000 of supply-and-fix rewires and receiving considerably less after materials costs and 20% deductions is still measured on the £34,000. The £50,000 threshold has applied since 6 April 2026. Our guide to <a href="/blog/vat-and-mtd/mtd-income-tax-cis">MTD for Income Tax and CIS</a> explains how quarterly updates work alongside CIS payment statements.</p>

<p>Volume of this kind is also the point at which the structure question becomes real. Sustained rewire turnover, a second operative on the books and a growing materials spend all change the sole trader against limited company calculation, and gross payment status becomes worth pursuing once the £30,000 net CIS turnover test is comfortably met. Our comparison of <a href="/blog/cis-basics/cis-sole-trader-vs-limited-company">CIS as a sole trader or limited company</a> is the place to start.</p>

<p>A rewire priced from the bottom up (operative days, materials, certification, travel, overhead, risk) will sit inside the market range far more often than one priced by copying a competitor's number. The compliance costs are real costs and belong in the quote, and the labour and materials split belongs on the invoice, because that is what determines how much of your own money HMRC holds until you file. Get both right and a rewire is one of the most profitable jobs on a domestic electrician's books.</p>
