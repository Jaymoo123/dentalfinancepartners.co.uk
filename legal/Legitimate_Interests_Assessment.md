## How the distribution model works, stated plainly

This assessment is only meaningful if it describes the real mechanism, so it is set out here in full before it is assessed.

1. An enquirer submits an enquiry through one of our sites. We attempt to verify the telephone number and email address by contacting the enquirer.
2. A verified enquiry is graded by case type under our published rubric and offered to the firms in the network as a redacted alert: tier, case type, rough area, a one-line intent summary, the price, and the enquiry in the enquirer's own words with the identifying parts taken out. Names, telephone numbers, email addresses, postcodes, web addresses and company names are removed and replaced with markers, so the enquirer cannot be identified from the alert. It carries no contact details and no unredacted enquiry. Every active firm on the relevant lane receives it.
3. A firm claims the enquiry to receive it in full. Claims run in two independent lanes, each capped:
   - Accounting lane: up to 3 firms. Each claiming firm receives the same full details. A firm may instead claim exclusively, which locks the enquiry to that firm alone and means no other accounting firm receives it.
   - Adjacent lane: up to 3 firms. Additive, and independent of the accounting lane: an adjacent claim never consumes an accounting slot, and an exclusive accounting claim does not close the adjacent lane.
   - The maximum number of firms that can receive one enquirer's full details is therefore 6, and it is 6 only where three accounting firms and three adjacent firms each separately choose to claim. Where an accounting firm claims exclusively, the maximum is 4.
4. If no accounting firm claims within 48 hours, the enquiry cascades: it is withdrawn from the accounting lane and offered to the adjacent lane instead. The price does not change while an enquiry is unclaimed, and nothing is re-sent at a discount. Cascade stops the moment any firm claims.
5. Enquiries we were never able to verify are not offered individually at all. After a 7-day follow-up window they become eligible for bulk supply: supplied as a batch, as a whole, to a single firm, with no alert and no per-enquiry claim.

Both caps are set in the distribution system's configuration and enforced in code, and an automated check fails the build if either lane is left uncapped. This assessment is calibrated to those numbers.

## Part 1 - Purpose test (is there a legitimate interest?)

### 1.1 What is the purpose of the processing?

To connect a person who has asked, through one of our websites, for specialist professional help with one or more specialist firms that can answer their enquiry, and to operate a lead distribution network with the firms in it.

### 1.2 What benefit do we expect, and for whom?
- For the enquirer: a prompt response and specialist advice on the matter they asked about, which is the outcome they were seeking. Where more than one firm takes the enquiry, the enquirer receives more than one offer of help and chooses between them, which is a better outcome than a single firm that may be slow, may have no capacity, or may not suit them.
- For us and for the receiving firms: a legitimate commercial benefit from operating a lead distribution arrangement (we are paid for enquiries firms choose to claim).
- More widely: efficient matching of people who need specialist professional help with firms that provide it.

### 1.3 Are these interests legitimate?

Yes. Both a commercial interest in a lead distribution arrangement and the interest in responding to a person's request for help are recognised legitimate interests. They are real and specific, not speculative.

### 1.4 Is the processing lawful, and what must a receiving firm be?

The data is ordinary contact and enquiry data, not special category data. In each case the receiving firm is a reputable and appropriately regulated or professionally qualified specialist firm (an accountancy or tax firm, a mortgage or finance broker, an independent financial adviser, a solicitor or a specialist consultant), engaged under a written controller-to-controller data sharing agreement signed before any enquiry or alert is sent to it. At the point of collection we tell the enquirer, in plain language, that their details may be shared with more than one regulated firm from our specialist partner network, that those firms may include non-accounting professions, and that each may contact them directly; each receiving firm then identifies itself to the enquirer in its own Article 14 notice when it first makes contact. A receiving firm's first contact is a response to the enquiry, not direct marketing; neither party carries out other electronic marketing without a lawful basis under PECR.

## Part 2 - Necessity test (is the processing necessary?)

### 2.1 Does sharing the data actually achieve the purpose?

Yes. A firm cannot respond to or advise the enquirer without the enquirer's contact details and a description of their enquiry. Sharing those details is what enables the enquiry to be answered.

### 2.2 Is sharing with more than one firm necessary?

This is the question the previous assessment did not have to answer, and it is answered separately for each limb.

- The redacted alert is necessary because a firm cannot decide whether it can help without knowing what the enquiry is about. Showing the enquiry in the enquirer's own words, rather than a summary of it, is what lets a firm judge the work accurately and decline the ones it cannot help with, which keeps the number of firms that ever receive identifying details down. It is the least intrusive step that achieves this: every direct identifier is stripped out first, so a firm that does not claim never learns who the enquirer is.
- Delivery to more than one firm is necessary to the purpose as we operate it, and we do not claim it is necessary to answer the enquiry once. It is necessary because a single-firm model fails the enquirer in the ordinary case: the firm may not respond, may be at capacity, may have a conflict, or may simply not suit. Offering the enquiry to a small number of firms is what makes a response reliable and gives the enquirer a choice of adviser. We have bounded this at what the purpose requires rather than at what the market would bear: 3 firms per lane, not the 5 to 8 common in this sector.
- The two lanes are necessary because an accountant and, for example, a mortgage broker answer different parts of the same enquiry and do not compete for the work. An enquirer restructuring a property portfolio may need both. The lanes are capped separately so that this does not become a route around the overall bound.
- Cascade is necessary because an enquiry no accounting firm has claimed would otherwise go unanswered. Offering it to a different profession that may be able to help is better for the enquirer than dropping it. Note that cascade follows silence rather than an express decline: we treat 48 hours with no claim from any firm on the lane as evidence that the lane cannot or will not help, which we consider a fair inference given every active firm on the lane received the alert.
- Bulk supply is assessed separately at 3.3c, because it is the narrowest limb.

### 2.3 Is it a reasonable and proportionate way to achieve the purpose?

Yes, subject to the caps. We share only the fields specified in the data sharing agreement's data specification, in one direction only, and only to a firm that has actively claimed the enquiry. Firms that do not claim see only the redacted alert. The number of firms that can ever receive one enquirer's details is fixed in configuration, enforced in code and tested, and cannot be increased without redoing this assessment.

### 2.4 Is there a less intrusive way to achieve the same result?

Not one that achieves the same purpose. Sharing less than the contact details would prevent any firm from responding. Sharing with one firm only is less intrusive and we offer it: an exclusive claim produces exactly that, and an enquirer who objects is not shared at all. But a one-firm-only model as the default would remove the reliability and choice described at 2.2, which are part of the purpose. The alert-then-claim design is itself the less intrusive alternative we adopted: the alternative of sending full details to every firm and letting them decide afterwards would disclose far more, and we do not do it.

## Part 3 - Balancing test (do the individual's interests override?)

### 3.1 Nature of the data.

Ordinary identification and contact data, plus a free-text enquiry that may include the enquirer's financial, property or business circumstances, together with verification status and the case grading. It is not special category data and not criminal-offence data. The service is aimed at adults seeking professional advice, not children. We design the enquiry form to avoid special category data and to minimise free-text sensitive detail.

### 3.2 Reasonable expectations.

The enquirer actively completes and submits an enquiry seeking specialist professional help and expects to be contacted as a result. At the point of submission we tell them, in plain language, that their details may be shared with more than one regulated firm from our specialist partner network, that those firms are independent controllers who will each contact them, and that the firms may include non-accounting professions. Our privacy policy says the same and states the maximum number of firms. Each firm then identifies itself, and provides a link to its own privacy policy, in the Article 14 notice it gives when it first makes contact.

We record candidly that being contacted by several firms is a different experience from being contacted by one, and that an enquirer who was told only about "a firm" would not reasonably expect it. That is why the disclosure at the point of collection states plainly that it may be more than one and gives the number. On that footing, and only on that footing, the sharing is within the enquirer's reasonable expectations, because it is exactly what they were told would happen when they submitted the enquiry. The transparency wording is therefore load-bearing for this assessment, not a formality, and a site that does not carry it must not have its enquiries entered into the pool.

### 3.3 Likely impact on the individual.

Low, and in the ordinary case positive. They receive responses from specialist firms about the matter they raised, which is what they asked for, and can choose between them. The data is not used for unrelated purposes; a receiving firm's first contact is a response, not marketing; each firm is contractually barred from disclosing the data onward to another controller, selling it or list-broking it; and the transfer is made by a secure method, encrypted in transit.

### 3.3a The additional impact of multiple concurrent recipients.

This is the change that required the reassessment, so it is weighed on its own.

Against the enquirer: their details are seen by more firms than in a single-firm model, each disclosure is a further point at which the data could be mishandled, and they may receive up to six approaches rather than one. Some enquirers will find that intrusive even though they asked to be contacted.

In mitigation, on the facts of how this network operates:
- (a) The possibility of multiple firms, the professions involved and the maximum number are all disclosed at the point of collection and in the privacy notice, so it is not an unexpected use.
- (b) The number is bounded and small. Three firms per lane, six in total, four where a firm claims exclusively. The bound is enforced in code, not by policy, and tested.
- (c) No additional data is shared with the second or third firm, and the purpose is unchanged, so no disclosure is wider in content than the one the enquirer already expected.
- (d) Firms self-select from a redacted alert, so the enquirer's identity is disclosed only to firms that have positively decided they can help. Firms that pass never learn who they are.
- (e) Every recipient is under the same written agreement with the same safeguards, is barred from further referring, selling or list-broking the data, and must delete or anonymise it once it is no longer needed.
- (f) We do not share where the enquirer has objected, withdrawn, or asked us to stop, and an objection stops any further claim on an enquiry already alerted.
- (g) The enquirer keeps a real benefit from the plurality: more than one specialist offering to help with the matter they raised, and a choice between them.

Weighing the additional disclosure against the alternative, we are satisfied it is proportionate provided the caps hold and the transparency wording is live on the site the enquiry came from. Those two conditions are the basis of this conclusion; if either fails, the balance is not made out.

### 3.3b The impact of cascade to a different profession.

A cascade discloses the enquiry to a profession the enquirer did not name, after the profession they probably had in mind did not take it. Against the enquirer: an approach from, say, a broker where they expected an accountant may be unwanted. In mitigation: the cascade is disclosed at collection and in the privacy notice as a category, the receiving profession is regulated and under the same agreement, the alert-then-claim step still applies so identity is disclosed only to a firm that has decided it can help, and the alternative is that the enquiry is answered by nobody. The adjacent lane is capped at 3 on its own account so cascade cannot become a broadcast. We are satisfied this is proportionate.

### 3.3c Bulk supply of unverified enquiries.

This is the narrowest limb and we assess it as such rather than treating it as covered by the analysis above.

What it is: enquiries where the person submitted the form but never responded to our follow-up, so we could not confirm their contact details. After a 7-day window they are supplied as a batch to a single firm.

Against the enquirer: there is no per-enquiry decision by the receiving firm, so the mitigation at 3.3a(d), that identity is disclosed only to a firm that has chosen the enquiry, does not apply here. The enquirer's non-response is also capable of being read as disengagement, and a batch is a bulk disposal rather than a match.

In mitigation:
- The enquirer did submit an enquiry asking to be put in touch with professional help, and was told at that point that their details may be shared with firms in our network. Not answering a verification message is not a withdrawal of that request, and many non-responses are simply missed messages.
- Only one firm receives the batch. Bulk supply reaches fewer recipients than a claimed enquiry, not more.
- The purpose is unchanged: the firm's permitted use is to respond to the enquiry the person made.
- The receiving firm is under the same written agreement, with the same bar on onward disclosure, sale and list-broking, and the same deletion obligation.
- Enquirers who have objected or opted out are excluded from the batch.
- The window means a person who does re-engage is never in a batch.

We are satisfied bulk supply is proportionate on these terms, and we record that it is the limb most exposed to challenge. It is conditional on: one recipient per batch, the objection exclusion, and the disclosure of bulk supply in the privacy notice as a distinct thing rather than as part of the general sharing wording. If any of those is not in place, bulk supply should stop rather than continue on a weaker footing.

### 3.3d The redacted alert itself.

The alert is personal data in our hands (it relates to an identifiable person, whom we can identify), even though the receiving firm cannot identify the enquirer from it. Broadcasting it to the pool is a disclosure and is assessed as such. It carries the enquiry in the enquirer's own words, which is more than a bare summary, so the assessment turns on the quality of the redaction rather than on the length of the text: names, telephone numbers, email addresses, postcodes, web addresses and company names are removed, the area is given at county or region level rather than as an address, and the templates used to render an alert have no field for a contact detail or the raw enquiry, so a redaction failure cannot become a disclosure of identity by accident. An automated check tests the redaction against each identifier shape on every run.

We accept the residual risk that a determined reader of an unusually specific enquiry might guess who wrote it, and weigh it against what showing the real words achieves: a firm can tell whether it can genuinely help, so fewer firms claim enquiries they cannot serve, and fewer enquirers have their identity disclosed to a firm that was never going to act. On balance we are satisfied the redacted alert is proportionate, and that it remains the mechanism that keeps the impact of everything downstream low.

### 3.4 Would the individual object, and can they?

Most enquirers are unlikely to object, having asked to be contacted, though some will object to the number of firms rather than to the sharing itself. We tell them of, and provide a route to exercise, the right to object under Article 21, and we will not share (or will stop sharing) where a person objects, withdraws any consent relied on, or where the required basis is absent. We and each receiving firm maintain a suppression list and tell the other promptly of any objection or opt-out. An objection received after an alert has gone out stops any further claim on that enquiry.

### 3.5 Safeguards in place to reduce any impact.
- Transparency: a clear notice at the point of collection stating that details may be shared with more than one regulated firm from our specialist partner network, that the firms may include non-accounting professions, and how many firms at most; plus a privacy notice explaining the share, the lanes, the cascade, bulk supply and the retention period. Each receiving firm then identifies itself, and links to its own privacy policy, in its Article 14 notice at first contact.
- Bounded recipients: 3 per lane, 6 in total, enforced in configuration and code and covered by a test that fails if a lane is uncapped.
- Redaction before selection: the full details of an enquiry are disclosed only to a firm that has claimed it; every other firm sees only the redacted alert.
- Data minimisation: only the fields in the data sharing agreement's data specification are shared; no special category data; no extra data on a later claim, a cascade or a batch.
- Firm quality gate: every recipient is a reputable, appropriately regulated or professionally qualified specialist firm under a signed written controller-to-controller agreement, in place before it receives any enquiry or alert.
- No onward disclosure: every recipient is barred from disclosing the data onward to another controller, selling it or list-broking it, and must delete or anonymise it once it is no longer needed.
- Objection and withdrawal, honoured by both parties, with suppression on both sides.
- Secure transfer (encrypted in transit) and access controls.
- Retention: we delete or anonymise our copy no later than the period published in the privacy notice of the site the enquiry came from, and in any event no later than 24 months from the enquiry. Each receiving firm keeps the data only as long as necessary for the enquiry or its engagement.

### 3.6 Processors.

Distinct from the firms assessed above, we use processors under Article 28 to operate the service: hosting and database, transactional email, SMS, an AI service used to produce the case grading and the one-line intent summary, and a public-register lookup. They act on our instructions and are not recipients in the sense assessed here. Processing is not exclusively within the United Kingdom, and the processor locations and transfer mechanisms are recorded in the data sharing agreement and in each site's privacy notice.

## Outcome

Having weighed our (and the receiving firms') legitimate interests against the interests, rights and freedoms of the enquirer, and taking account of the safeguards above, we are satisfied that:
- there is a genuine legitimate interest;
- the sharing is necessary for that interest; and
- that interest is not overridden by the enquirer's interests, rights or freedoms.

Legitimate interests under Article 6(1)(f) UK GDPR is an appropriate lawful basis for the redacted alert, for delivery in full on claim to up to 3 firms per lane, for cascade to the adjacent lane, and for bulk supply of unverified enquiries to a single firm, in each case to firms meeting the criteria in section 1.4 and with the safeguards above. Article 6(1)(b) is not our basis for the share; it is available to a receiving firm for its own processing where its first contact is a step taken at the enquirer's request.

This conclusion is expressly conditional on three things, and fails without them:
1. the claim caps remaining at 3 per lane, so no enquirer's details reach more than 6 firms;
2. the point-of-collection notice and privacy notice on the site an enquiry came from disclosing multiple recipients, both professions and the maximum number, before that site's enquiries enter the pool; and
3. for bulk supply only, a single recipient per batch and the exclusion of anyone who has objected.

We will keep this assessment under review and repeat it at least annually, and sooner if we begin sharing with a materially different type of recipient, if either claim cap changes, or if the data, the lawful basis or the law materially changes.
