## Part 1 - Purpose test (is there a legitimate interest?)

**1.1 What is the purpose of the processing?** To connect a person who has asked, through one of our websites, for specialist professional help with a specialist firm (the Partner Firm) that can answer their enquiry, and to operate a lead-referral network with the firms in it, including passing an enquiry to another firm in the network where the first firm is unable to help.

**1.2 What benefit do we expect, and for whom?**
- For the enquirer: a prompt response and specialist advice on the matter they asked about, which is the outcome they were seeking, and, where the first firm cannot help, a second route to that response rather than a dead end.
- For us and for the Partner Firm: a legitimate commercial benefit from operating a lead-referral arrangement (we are remunerated for qualifying enquiries we refer).
- More widely: efficient matching of people who need specialist professional help with a firm that provides it.

**1.3 Are these interests legitimate?** Yes. Both a commercial interest in a lead-referral arrangement and the interest in responding to a person's request for help are recognised legitimate interests. They are real and specific, not speculative.

**1.4 Is the processing lawful, and what must the Partner Firm be?** The data is ordinary contact and enquiry data, not special category data. In each case the Partner Firm is a reputable and appropriately regulated or professionally qualified specialist firm (whether an accountancy or tax firm, a mortgage or finance broker, an independent financial adviser, a solicitor or a specialist consultant), engaged under a written controller-to-controller data sharing agreement signed before any enquiry is delivered to it. At the point of collection we tell the enquirer, in plain language, that their details may be shared with a firm from our specialist partner network, and that if that firm is unable to help their details may be passed to another firm in the network for the same purpose; the receiving Partner Firm then identifies itself to the enquirer in its own Article 14 notice when it first makes contact. The Partner Firm's first contact is a response to the enquiry, not direct marketing; neither party carries out other electronic marketing without a lawful basis under PECR.

## Part 2 - Necessity test (is the processing necessary?)

**2.1 Does sharing the data actually achieve the purpose?** Yes. The Partner Firm cannot respond to or advise the enquirer without the enquirer's contact details and a description of their enquiry. Sharing those details is what enables the enquiry to be answered. Where the first firm is unable to help (for example it has no capacity, has a conflict, or the matter falls outside its specialism), passing the same details to another firm in the network is what enables the enquiry to be answered at all, rather than not at all.

**2.2 Is it a reasonable and proportionate way to achieve the purpose?** Yes. We share only the fields specified in the data sharing agreement's data specification (the enquirer's contact details, verification of those details, and their enquiry and its context), with one Partner Firm at a time, in one direction only, only once that firm has accepted the enquiry, and with a further firm only where the first has said it is unable to help. Firms considering an enquiry before acceptance see only a summary from which direct identifiers have been removed. A re-referral shares no additional data and does not change the purpose, so the number of firms that see the enquiry stays limited to what it takes to get it answered.

**2.3 Is there a less intrusive way to achieve the same result?** No realistic one. The enquirer came to our website specifically to be put in touch with specialist help; the only way to deliver that is to pass their contact details to the specialist firm so it can make contact. Sharing less would prevent the Partner Firm from responding. There is also no less intrusive alternative to re-referral: making the enquirer start again with a second firm themselves would defeat or delay the response they asked us for, and the only other option (dropping the enquiry when the first firm declines) leaves them worse off. They remain free to object, or to tell us to stop, at any point before or after a re-referral.

## Part 3 - Balancing test (do the individual's interests override?)

**3.1 Nature of the data.** Ordinary identification and contact data, plus a free-text enquiry that may include the enquirer's financial, property or business circumstances, together with verification of the contact details and context about how the enquiry was made. It is not special category data and not criminal-offence data. The service is aimed at adults seeking professional advice, not children. We design the enquiry form to avoid special category data and to minimise free-text sensitive detail.

**3.2 Reasonable expectations.** The enquirer actively completes and submits an enquiry seeking specialist professional help and expects to be contacted as a result. At the point of submission we tell them, in plain language, that their details may be shared with a firm from our specialist partner network, an independent controller that will contact them, and that if that firm is unable to help their details may be passed to another firm in the network for the same purpose. Our privacy policy says the same. That firm then identifies itself, and provides a link to its own privacy policy, in the Article 14 notice it gives when it first makes contact. Sharing their details with a specialist firm so it can answer the enquiry they submitted, and with a second firm if the first cannot, is therefore squarely within their reasonable expectations, because it is exactly what they were told would happen when they submitted the enquiry.

**3.3 Likely impact on the individual.** Low, and positive. They receive a response from a specialist firm about the matter they raised, which is what they asked for. The data is not used for unrelated purposes; the Partner Firm's first contact is a response, not marketing; we and the Partner Firm each retain the data only as long as needed (we delete or anonymise our copy within 3 months of referring it); and the transfer is made by a secure method (encrypted in transit).

**3.3a The additional impact of a re-referral.** A re-referral is a second disclosure of the same data to a firm the enquirer did not individually choose, so it needs weighing on its own rather than being treated as covered by the first. Against the enquirer: their details are seen by more than one firm, and each disclosure is a further point at which the data could be mishandled. In mitigation, and on the facts of how this network operates: (a) the possibility of a re-referral, and its purpose, are disclosed at the point of collection and in our privacy policy, so it is not an unexpected use; (b) no additional data is shared and the purpose is unchanged, so the disclosure is no wider in content than the one the enquirer already expected; (c) the re-referral is made by us, not by the declining firm, and every firm we refer to is under the same written agreement with the same safeguards and is expressly barred from further referring, selling or list-broking the data; (d) the declining firm's need for the data ends when it declines, and under that agreement it must then delete or anonymise what it holds; (e) a re-referral only happens when a firm has said it cannot help, so it is bounded by need and is not a broadcast of the enquiry across the network; and (f) we do not re-refer where the enquirer has objected, withdrawn, or asked us to stop. Weighing the extra disclosure against the alternative (the enquiry going unanswered after the enquirer asked us to find them help), we are satisfied the additional transfer is proportionate and does not tip the balance against us. Our network now includes more firms, across accounting and adjacent regulated professions, so the pool of possible recipients is broader than when this assessment was first made; that breadth does not change what any one enquirer experiences, because the full details of an enquiry still go to one firm at a time, and firms considering an enquiry before acceptance see only a summary from which direct identifiers have been removed. If the network model ever changed so that an enquiry's full details could be sent to several firms at once, or to firms outside the criteria in section 1.4, this assessment would have to be redone.

**3.4 Would the individual object, and can they?** Most enquirers are unlikely to object, having asked to be contacted. Even so, we tell them of, and provide a route to exercise, the right to object under Article 21, and we will not share (or will stop sharing) where a person objects, withdraws any consent relied on, or where the required basis is absent. We and the Partner Firm each maintain a suppression list and tell the other promptly of any objection or opt-out.

**3.5 Safeguards in place to reduce any impact.**
- Transparency: a clear notice at the point of collection telling the enquirer their details may be shared with a firm from our specialist partner network, and that if that firm is unable to help their details may be passed to another firm in the network for the same purpose, plus a privacy notice explaining the share; the receiving Partner Firm then identifies itself, and links to its own privacy policy, in the Article 14 notice it gives at first contact.
- Data minimisation: only the fields in the data sharing agreement's data specification are shared; no special category data, and no extra data on a re-referral.
- One firm at a time, one direction; the full details of an enquiry are delivered only to a firm that has accepted it, and firms considering an enquiry before acceptance see only a summary from which direct identifiers have been removed; a further firm only where the first has said it cannot help, and no re-referral once an enquirer has objected or asked us to stop.
- The right to object and to withdraw, honoured by both parties.
- Secure transfer (encrypted in transit) and access controls.
- Short retention (we delete or anonymise within 3 months; the Partner Firm keeps only as long as necessary for the enquiry or its engagement).
- A written controller-to-controller data sharing agreement, signed before any enquiry is delivered, allocating each party's responsibilities, with the Partner Firm a reputable, appropriately regulated or professionally qualified specialist firm.
- Every Partner Firm we refer to, including on a re-referral, signs the same written agreement and is subject to the same safeguards, and is barred from disclosing the data onward to another controller, selling it or list-broking it. The declining firm must delete or anonymise the data once it is no longer needed.

## Outcome

Having weighed our (and the Partner Firm's) legitimate interests against the interests, rights and freedoms of the enquirer, and taking account of the safeguards above, we are satisfied that:
- there is a genuine legitimate interest;
- the sharing is necessary for that interest; and
- that interest is not overridden by the enquirer's interests, rights or freedoms.

Legitimate interests under Article 6(1)(f) UK GDPR is an appropriate lawful basis for our referral of enquiries from any of our websites to a Partner Firm meeting the criteria in section 1.4, and for a re-referral to another such firm where the first is unable to help, with the safeguards above. Article 6(1)(b) is not our basis for the share; it is available to the Partner Firm for its own processing where its first contact is a step taken at the enquirer's request. We will keep this assessment under review and repeat it at least annually, and sooner if we begin sharing with a materially different type of recipient, or if the data, the lawful basis or the law materially changes.
