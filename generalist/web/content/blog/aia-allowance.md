- question: Is the AIA still £1 million?
  answer: Yes. The AIA limit is £1,000,000 per 12-month accounting period and it is permanent. The £1m level started as a temporary increase in January 2019 and was made permanent from April 2023; the £200,000 reversion was cancelled and no reduction is scheduled.
- question: Does the AIA reduce my tax bill directly?
  answer: It reduces taxable profit, which reduces the tax calculated on it. A £10,000 AIA claim cuts taxable profit by £10,000; the cash saving is that amount at your marginal rate. For a company that is 19%, 25%, or an effective 26.5% in the marginal-relief band; for a higher-rate sole trader it can exceed 40% once Class 4 NIC is included.
- question: Can I carry unused AIA forward to next year?
  answer: No. The AIA is use-it-or-lose-it. If you spend £100,000 in a year, the other £900,000 of headroom does not roll into the next period; each 12-month period brings a fresh £1m limit. Unclaimed expenditure (as opposed to unused headroom) is different, it simply falls into a pool for writing down allowances.

  answer: No. The AIA applies from the first pound of qualifying expenditure. A £300 office chair and a £300,000 machine both get 100% relief in the year of purchase, provided the asset qualifies as plant and machinery and the £1m cap is not exhausted.

<p>The <strong>annual investment allowance (AIA)</strong> lets a UK business deduct the full cost of qualifying plant and machinery from taxable profit in the year of purchase, up to £1,000,000 per 12-month accounting period.<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup> The £200,000 reversion was cancelled; the £1m level was made permanent from April 2023 and no reduction is scheduled.</p>

<h2>The AIA in One Screen</h2>

<div style="overflow-x:auto;">
<table>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td><strong>Relief rate</strong></td><td>100% of qualifying cost, in the year of purchase</td></tr>
    <tr><td><strong>Annual limit</strong></td><td>£1,000,000 per 12-month period (permanent); scaled pro rata for shorter periods, e.g. £500,000 for 6 months</td></tr>
    <tr><td><strong>Who can claim</strong></td><td>Limited companies, sole traders, partnerships (not partnerships with a corporate partner)</td></tr>
    <tr><td><strong>New or used</strong></td><td>Both, provided the seller is not a connected person</td></tr>
    <tr><td><strong>Covers</strong></td><td>Plant and machinery: equipment, tools, vans and lorries, computers, furniture, fixtures and integral features</td></tr>
    <tr><td><strong>Never covers</strong></td><td>Cars (including double cab pickups for expenditure from April 2025, per HMRC CA23511), buildings and land, leased assets, gifts</td></tr>
    <tr><td><strong>Carry forward</strong></td><td>None; unused headroom is lost each period</td></tr>
    <tr><td><strong>Above the cap</strong></td><td>Excess spend goes to a pool for writing down allowances: 14% main rate for periods beginning from April 2026 (18% before), 6% special rate</td></tr>
    <tr><td><strong>How you claim</strong></td><td>In the capital allowances computation on the CT600, SA103 or SA800; no separate form</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

<h2>What the AIA Is Worth: One Worked Example</h2>

<p>An Aberdeen landscaping company, year ending 31 December 2025, buys a second-hand excavator for £60,000. Profit before capital allowances is £110,000.</p>

<p>Without the claim, corporation tax is 25% x £110,000 = £27,500, less marginal relief of 3/200 x (£250,000 - £110,000) = £2,100, so <strong>£25,400</strong>. With a full AIA claim, taxable profit falls to £50,000, taxed at the 19% small profits rate: <strong>£9,500</strong>. The AIA saves <strong>£15,900</strong> in the year of purchase. Because the excavator is second-hand, full expensing would not have been available; the AIA is the only 100% route here.</p>
