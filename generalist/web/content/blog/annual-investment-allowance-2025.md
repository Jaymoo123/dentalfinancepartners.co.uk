<p>For accounting periods falling in 2025/26, the <strong>annual investment allowance</strong> is £1,000,000 per 12 months, giving 100% relief on qualifying plant and machinery in the year of purchase.<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup> That is the same permanent level in force since it stopped being a temporary measure in April 2023, so there is no expiry date and no £200,000 reversion to plan around.</p>

<p>The allowance itself is the quiet part of the year. The rules around it are not: 2025 reclassified double cab pickups out of the AIA, 1 January 2026 brought a new 40% first-year allowance, and April 2026 cut the writing down allowance that catches anything the AIA does not cover. This page sets out the year-specific position with the dates and figures labelled.</p>

<h2>The 2025/26 Position in Numbers</h2>

<p>For the 2025/26 tax year (6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026) and for company accounting periods falling across it:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>AIA limit:</strong> £1,000,000 per 12-month period, permanent. A 9-month period gets £750,000; an 18-month period must be split into two chargeable periods, each with its own scaled cap.</li>
<li><strong>Relief rate:</strong> 100% of qualifying plant and machinery, new or second-hand.</li>
<li><strong>Main-rate WDA on pooled spend:</strong> 18% for periods beginning before 1 April 2026 (CT) / 6 April 2026 (IT); 14% for periods beginning on or after those dates (FA 2026 s.28).<sup><a href="#ref-2">[2]</a></sup></li>
<li><strong>Special rate pool:</strong> 6%, unchanged.</li>
<li><strong>For history:</strong> the cap was £200,000 up to 31 December 2018 and £1m from 1 January 2019 onward, which is why searches for the 2018 or 2019 allowance return the lower figure. Nothing before 2019 is relevant to a current claim.</li>
</ul>

<p>What qualifies, and the claiming mechanics generally, do not change year to year; they are covered in our <a href="/blog/corporation-tax/aia-capital-allowances">AIA capital allowances claiming guide</a>. For the quick one-screen version see the <a href="/blog/corporation-tax/aia-allowance">AIA at a glance</a>.</p>

<h2>What Changed for the AIA in 2025, and What Changes Next</h2>

<p>Three dated changes bracket the 2025/26 year. None alters the £1m limit; all three alter what a claim looks like in practice.</p>

<p><strong>From April 2025: double cab pickups are cars.</strong> For expenditure incurred from 1 April 2025 (corporation tax) or 6 April 2025 (income tax), a double cab pickup is treated as a car for capital allowances whatever its payload (HMRC CA23511). Cars never qualify for the AIA, so a double cab bought in 2025/26 gets writing down allowances by CO2 band instead. The transitional carve-out: a contract entered into before April 2025, with the expenditure incurred by 1 October 2025, keeps van treatment and full AIA. Ordinary vans, lorries and single cab pickups are unaffected and still qualify in full.</p>

<p><strong>From 1 January 2026: the 40% first-year allowance.</strong> FA 2026 s.29 introduced a 40% FYA on new and unused main-rate plant (not cars, not second-hand), open to companies and unincorporated businesses.<sup><a href="#ref-3">[3]</a></sup> Within the £1m cap the AIA at 100% always beats it, so its practical role is taking the next slice once the cap is exhausted. Which relief to lead with is a decision question, covered in <a href="/blog/corporation-tax/annual-investment-allowance-explained">annual investment allowance explained</a>.</p>

<p><strong>From April 2026: the main pool slows down.</strong> The main-rate WDA falls from 18% to 14% for periods beginning 1 April 2026 (CT) or 6 April 2026 (IT).<sup><a href="#ref-2">[2]</a></sup> The AIA is untouched, but the fallback got worse: spend above the cap, spend on assets you chose not to claim on, and pre-existing pool balances all relieve more slowly. An AIA claim taken in 2025/26 is therefore worth relatively more than the same claim deferred.</p>

<h2>Worked Example: A Year Ended 31 March 2026</h2>

<p>An Exeter print business, accounting period 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026, buys a new digital press for £48,000 and a second-hand guillotine for £9,000. Both qualify; total AIA claim £57,000. Profit before capital allowances is £180,000.</p>

<p>With the claim, taxable profit is £123,000. Corporation tax is 25% x £123,000 = £30,750, less marginal relief of 3/200 x (£250,000 - £123,000) = £1,905, giving <strong>£28,845</strong>. Without the claim, tax on £180,000 is 25% x £180,000 = £45,000 less 3/200 x £70,000 = £1,050, giving <strong>£43,950</strong>. The AIA saves <strong>£15,105</strong>, which is exactly 26.5% of the £57,000, because the whole deduction lands in the marginal-relief band.</p>

<p>Note the split: the new press could alternatively have used full expensing (companies only) with the same 100% result, but the second-hand guillotine could not; the AIA covers both without caring which is new.</p>

<h2>Periods That Straddle April 2026</h2>

<p>Two different rules, and only one of them needs arithmetic.</p>

<p><strong>The AIA cap does not straddle.</strong> Because the £1m limit is the same on both sides of April 2026, a period running across the date has a plain £1m cap (scaled only for its length). The old-era exercise of blending two different caps across a change date died when the £1m became permanent.</p>

<p><strong>The WDA rate does.</strong> A chargeable period already running at April 2026 uses a hybrid main-pool rate, time-apportioned between 18% and 14%. A company with a year ending 31 December 2026 has 3 months at 18% and 9 months at 14%: (3 x 18% + 9 x 14%) / 12 = <strong>15%</strong> on the main pool for that year. So if the same company spent £1.2m on qualifying plant in that year, the first £1,000,000 gets 100% AIA and the £200,000 excess enters the main pool, attracting a WDA of £200,000 x 15% = £30,000 in year one, then 14% reducing balance thereafter. The full rate mechanics live in our <a href="/blog/corporation-tax/writing-down-allowance-rates">writing down allowance rates guide</a>.</p>

<h2>Timing a Purchase Either Side of the Change Dates</h2>

<p>For a purchase that the £1m AIA will cover, timing is tax-neutral on the relief rate: 100% is 100% in any period. What timing does move:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Which period's cap it consumes.</strong> Expenditure counts when the obligation to pay becomes unconditional, normally delivery. A Dundee haulier taking delivery of a £150,000 tractor unit on 30 March rather than 2 April moves the claim, and the cap it uses, a whole period.</li>
<li><strong>The rate on any excess.</strong> Spend above the cap pooled in a period beginning before April 2026 starts writing down at 18%; the same excess in a later period starts at 14%. For very large programmes, earlier is better.</li>
<li><strong>Double cab pickups.</strong> The April 2025 boundary has already passed, so for any new commitment in 2025/26 the vehicle is a car and timing cannot rescue the AIA; only the pre-April-2025 contract transitional route (expenditure by 1 October 2025) preserved it.</li>
<li><strong>New plant above the cap from January 2026.</strong> Expenditure from 1 January 2026 on new main-rate plant can take the 40% FYA on the slice the AIA misses; the same spend committed in December 2025 could not.</li>
</ul>
