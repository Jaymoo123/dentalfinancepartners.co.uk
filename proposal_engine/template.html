SECTION __Contactability and automated follow-up

On $nurture_golive an automated follow-up sequence went live on $nurture_scope, built to make sure every lead that reaches you can actually be contacted. The flow, in order: the moment an enquiry is submitted it receives an instant acknowledgement, then a short SMS and email sequence that confirms the enquirer's contact details are live, captures anything missing from the original submission, and offers a link to book a call directly. Only leads that respond to verification are passed on: the sequence acts as a quality gate in front of delivery, so a lead that never engages is never handed over, and never charged for.

The standard terms below apply verbatim to every partner firm; the same wording appears on the price sheet, in every delivery email and on every invoice.

    $terms_items

Dead-lead credit, evidenced pursuit

A lead whose contact details work but who never responds can be credited, provided the lead was genuinely pursued. A no-response credit is valid only where you evidence 7 to 9 proven contact attempts over 14 days from delivery, spread across telephone call, voicemail, SMS and email, supported by a contact log (call records, message copies or CRM export). Flags raised with the touchpoint log within the 14-day window are reviewed in good faith and, where accepted, credited against the next invoice.

Leads are billable on claim rather than on conversion. The credit routes above ensure dead contact details, materially misdescribed enquiries and properly pursued non-responders are not charged.

SECTION __Setup and how delivery works

  1. Nominate an inbox. You give us a single delivery email address. Redacted alerts arrive there as leads qualify; each lead you claim is released to the same inbox in full, in real time.
  2. Consent and data protection are handled at source. Every lead form carries an acknowledgement that details will be shared with the receiving firm. Data is shared controller-to-controller under UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018 as amended by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025. Once a lead lands in your inbox, you hold it as an independent controller.
  3. Tiering on delivery: each lead arrives with its tier and case type, matching the redacted alert you claimed.
  4. Monthly billing in arrears. Claimed leads are invoiced on the 1st of each month, itemised against the previous month's deliveries, and collected by Direct Debit. Payment is due within $payment_days days; credits from the section above are netted off the same invoice.
  5. No lock-in. Rolling arrangement, terminable by either side on short written notice.

Methodology. Arrival trend: Poisson regression (log-linear) fitted to the most recent eight complete calendar weeks, excluding the launch ramp so the trend is not overstated; the shaded band in Figure 1 is a 90% interval on the trend mean via the delta method. Trailing 30-day interval: nonparametric bootstrap of daily arrival counts, 5,000 resamples. Engagement values: per-lead good-faith estimates of first-year fees based on the work described in each enquiry; concentration measured as the estimated value share of the top quintile of assessed leads. Tier display: historical leads were graded on an internal value scale; for this document those grades are mapped to the published case-type tiers (the top two internal grades shown as Advisory, the middle grade as Standard, the lowest not sold) until case-type grading is backfilled. The observed series is short ($n_weeks complete weeks), so interval estimates are reported alongside every point estimate.
