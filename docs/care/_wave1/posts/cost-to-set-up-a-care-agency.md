<p>Setting up a care agency costs whatever it costs to keep the business alive from your first commitment until CQC registration is granted and the first invoice can lawfully be raised, because none of that period earns anything. Two lines carry published figures. The CQC regulatory fee for community social care is £239 plus £54.305 for each service user at each location, capped at £92,558, under the 2024/25 fee scheme. Pay is the other, at £12.71 an hour for workers aged 21 and over from 1 April 2026, with employer National Insurance at 15% above the £5,000 secondary threshold. Everything else is provider specific, and the total depends far more on how long you carry staff before income than on any one purchase.</p>

<h2>Why is the setup cost a cash question rather than a price list?</h2>

<p>Because the sequence forces it. Carrying on a regulated activity before CQC registration is granted is a criminal offence under the Health and Social Care Act 2008, so the lease, the registered manager, the recruitment, the insurance and the systems are all committed while the revenue line is zero. The question is not what the individual items cost but how many months of them you have to fund.</p>

<p>That is why two agencies with identical shopping lists can need very different opening capital. The one that hires a registered manager early and waits four months for a decision has funded four months of salary against nothing. We model that window separately in <a href="/blog/cqc-and-financial-compliance/cqc-registration-timeline-cash-burn-before-trading">the cash you burn before you can trade</a>.</p>

<h2>What is the CQC fee, and when does it start?</h2>

<p>For a domiciliary agency the annual regulatory fee is £239 plus £54.305 for each service user at each registered location, added together across locations and capped at £92,558 where a location reaches 1,700 or more service users. Those are the figures in CQC's published fee scheme for 2024/25, which was still the current scheme at the last check, and CQC's invoice to you is the definitive amount. Our <a href="/calculators/cqc-fee-calculator">CQC fee calculator</a> runs the formula for community, residential and nurses agency registrations.</p>

<p>Two points matter for a setup budget. The fee is recurring, not a one-off purchase you clear before opening, so it belongs in the ongoing overhead as well as the opening cash plan. And because it scales with service users rather than with revenue, a new agency starts at close to the base figure and the line grows as the caseload does, which makes it one of the few costs that behaves predictably in the first year.</p>

<h2>What does the first payroll actually cost an hour?</h2>

<p>More than the wage, and the gap is where new agencies underestimate. The National Living Wage is £12.71 an hour for workers aged 21 and over from 1 April 2026, and £10.85 for those aged 18 to 20. On top of that sits employer National Insurance at 15% on earnings above the £5,000 a year secondary threshold, which is £96 a week, offset for most new agencies by the Employment Allowance of up to £10,500 a year. For a small agency in its first year the allowance often removes the employer National Insurance bill entirely, which is worth modelling rather than assuming.</p>

<p>Then the hours you pay for exceed the hours you bill. Travel between one client and the next is working time for minimum wage purposes, mileage reimbursed at the approved rate is 55p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles from 6 April 2026, and holiday accrues for irregular hours workers at 12.07% of the hours worked in each pay period. The registered manager and the coordinators are paid for hours that are never chargeable at all. The <a href="/calculators/true-cost-care-hour-calculator">true cost of a care hour calculator</a> assembles those lines into the loaded figure your charge rate has to clear.</p>

<p>Engaging carers as self employed does not remove any of this. In a rota based model where workers cannot send a substitute and work under your direction they are employees in substance, and HMRC audit activity in the sector is concentrated on that exact pattern.</p>

<h2>Why does VAT exemption make every setup cost higher?</h2>

<p>Welfare services supplied by a CQC-registered provider are exempt from VAT under Group 7 of Schedule 9 to the Value Added Tax Act 1994. Exemption sounds like relief and behaves like a surcharge, because an exempt supplier cannot recover input VAT on the purchases used to make those supplies. The VAT on your office, vehicles, equipment, software and professional fees is a permanent cost, so every line in a setup budget should be entered VAT inclusive from the start.</p>

<p>Exempt fee income also does not count towards the £90,000 VAT registration threshold, which tests taxable turnover only, so most agencies never register. If you plan taxable activity alongside the care, selling training to other providers for example, track it separately, because partial exemption recovery is usually lost once exempt input tax exceeds the de minimis limits, which allow full recovery only where exempt input tax averages no more than £625 a month and is less than half of total input tax in the period.</p>

<p>One relief runs the other way. Qualifying plant and machinery attracts the Annual Investment Allowance at 100% on up to £1,000,000 of spend a year, and expenditure incurred in the seven years before trading begins is treated as incurred on the first day of trading, so pre-registration purchases are not stranded.</p>

<h2>What figure does CQC actually want to see?</h2>

<p>Not your total spend. The financial viability statement submitted with a new provider application tests whether the capital you hold covers the month by month cost base until fee income overtakes it, with a contingency for a request for further information. Build the model from the first commitment to the first fee received, treat the registration duration as a variable input, and a specialist reviews the assumptions before the pack goes in. Our <a href="/blog/cqc-and-financial-compliance/cqc-financial-viability-statement-walkthrough">walkthrough of the financial viability statement</a> covers what belongs in each section.</p>

<p>England is the default position here. Providers in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland register with the Care Inspectorate, Care Inspectorate Wales and the Regulation and Quality Improvement Authority respectively, each with its own fee scheme, so the CQC figures above do not apply. The minimum wage, National Insurance, VAT and capital allowance positions are the same across the United Kingdom.</p>
