<p>Setting up a care agency costs whatever it costs to keep the business alive from your first commitment until CQC registration is granted and the first invoice can lawfully be raised, because none of that period earns anything. Two lines carry published figures. The CQC regulatory fee for community social care is £239 plus £54.305 for each service user at each location, capped at £92,558, under the 2024/25 fee scheme. Pay is the other, at £12.71 an hour for workers aged 21 and over from 1 April 2026, with employer National Insurance at 15% above the £5,000 secondary threshold. Everything else is provider specific, and the total depends far more on how long you carry staff before income than on any one purchase.</p>

<h2>Why is the setup cost a cash question rather than a price list?</h2>

<p>Because the sequence forces it. Carrying on a regulated activity before CQC registration is granted is a criminal offence under the Health and Social Care Act 2008, so the lease, the registered manager, the recruitment, the insurance and the systems are all committed while the revenue line is zero. The question is not what the individual items cost but how many months of them you have to fund.</p>

<p>That is why two agencies with identical shopping lists can need very different opening capital. The one that hires a registered manager early and waits four months for a decision has funded four months of salary against nothing. That window is modelled separately in <a href="/blog/cqc-and-financial-compliance/cqc-registration-timeline-cash-burn-before-trading">the cash you burn before you can trade</a>.</p>

<h2>How does the CQC fee behave in a setup budget?</h2>

<p>Differently from the way most founders enter it. It is not a one-off purchase you clear before opening, so it sits in the ongoing overhead as well as the opening cash plan. And because the community social care calculation scales with service users rather than with revenue, a new agency starts close to the base charge and the line grows behind the caseload, which makes it one of the few costs that behaves predictably in a first year model. The <a href="/blog/cqc-and-financial-compliance/cqc-registration-domiciliary-care-finance">finance side of registering a domiciliary agency</a> sets out how the calculation works and what happens to it as you add locations. Our <a href="/calculators/cqc-fee-calculator">CQC fee calculator</a> runs it for community, residential and nurses agency registrations, and CQC's invoice to you is the definitive amount.</p>

<h2>What does the first payroll actually cost an hour?</h2>

<p>More than the wage, and the gap between the two is where a setup budget usually breaks. Start at the statutory floor of £12.71 an hour for workers aged 21 and over from 1 April 2026, or £10.85 where the worker is 18 to 20. Add employer National Insurance at 15% on earnings above the £5,000 a year secondary threshold, which works out at £96 a week. Then apply the Employment Allowance, worth up to £10,500 a year, which at the headcount a new agency carries before registration will often take the employer National Insurance line to nothing. That is worth modelling on your own numbers rather than assuming in either direction, because it changes the opening capital figure materially.</p>

<p>The second gap is between paid hours and billable hours, and in the setup period every hour is unbillable anyway. Once you are trading, travel between one client and the next counts as working time for minimum wage purposes, reimbursed mileage runs at the approved rate of 55p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles from 6 April 2026, and irregular hours staff accrue holiday at 12.07% of the hours worked in each pay period. The registered manager and the coordinators are paid throughout for hours nobody is ever invoiced for. The <a href="/calculators/true-cost-care-hour-calculator">true cost of a care hour calculator</a> assembles those lines into the loaded figure your charge rate has to clear.</p>

<p>Treating carers as self employed does not shrink any of this, and in a rota based agency it will not survive a status test: where a worker cannot send a substitute and works under your direction, the substance is employment whatever the contract says. HMRC audit activity in care concentrates on exactly that pattern, so a setup model built on self employed rates is a model built on a liability.</p>

<h2>Why does VAT exemption make every setup cost higher?</h2>

<p>Welfare services supplied by a CQC-registered provider are exempt from VAT under Group 7 of Schedule 9 to the Value Added Tax Act 1994. Exemption sounds like relief and behaves like a surcharge, because an exempt supplier cannot recover input VAT on the purchases used to make those supplies. In a setup budget that lands as an uplift on almost every line you have costed, from the office and the vehicles to the software, the equipment and the professional fees. Enter each of them at the VAT inclusive figure, because the VAT inclusive figure is what leaves the bank and never comes back.</p>

<p>Nor does the threshold rescue you. The £90,000 registration test looks at taxable turnover only, and exempt fee income is not taxable turnover, so most agencies never register and never have a return to reclaim on. Where a taxable sideline is planned, selling training to other providers for example, keep it in separate ledger codes from the first transaction. Partial exemption then decides how much input tax you keep, and full recovery survives only where exempt input tax averages no more than £625 a month and is less than half of total input tax in the period.</p>

<p>One relief runs the other way. Qualifying plant and machinery attracts the Annual Investment Allowance at 100% on up to £1,000,000 of spend a year, and expenditure incurred in the seven years before trading begins is treated as incurred on the first day of trading, so pre-registration purchases are not stranded.</p>

<h2>What figure does CQC actually want to see?</h2>

<p>Not your total spend. The financial viability statement submitted with a new provider application tests whether the capital you hold covers the month by month cost base until fee income overtakes it, with a contingency for a request for further information. Build the model from the first commitment to the first fee received, treat the registration duration as a variable input, and a specialist reviews the assumptions before the pack goes in. Our <a href="/blog/cqc-and-financial-compliance/cqc-financial-viability-statement-walkthrough">walkthrough of the financial viability statement</a> covers what belongs in each section.</p>

<p>England is the default position here. Providers in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland register with the Care Inspectorate, Care Inspectorate Wales and the Regulation and Quality Improvement Authority respectively, each with its own fee scheme, so the CQC figures above do not apply. The minimum wage, National Insurance, VAT and capital allowance positions are the same across the United Kingdom.</p>
