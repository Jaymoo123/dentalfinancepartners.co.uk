<p>Bid with one trading company: the entity that already holds the CQC registration, the filed accounts and the bank history. A framework tests the supplier in front of it, not the group behind it, and the structures providers reach for before a bid tend to cost more than they gain. A second company in the picture is an associated company, so the corporation tax thresholds of £50,000 and £250,000 divide between them and the group meets the 25% main rate on half the profit, against 19% below £50,000. Settle the entity first, then the rent and care split, then the profit policy, and only then write the submission.</p>

<h2>Which entity should the bid be submitted in?</h2>

<p>The one with the history. A contracting authority may set conditions of participation only where they are a proportionate means of ensuring that suppliers have the legal and financial capacity to perform the contract, under <a href="https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/54/section/22">section 22 of the Procurement Act 2023</a>. Capacity is evidenced with filed accounts, an operating track record and a registration. A company incorporated for the bid has none of those, and its balance sheet is the share capital you put in last month.</p>

<p>Two useful limits sit in the same section. An authority cannot require a supplier to provide audited annual accounts where that supplier is not otherwise required by law to have them, and it cannot require insurance to be in place before the contract is awarded. Small providers routinely spend money on both because a portal asked. Check the requirement against the statutory limit before you buy.</p>

<p>Where the group genuinely needs a new vehicle, for example because an existing company carries something the authority will not accept, expect the authority to look through to the parent instead and ask for a guarantee. That is a real liability, so it belongs in the decision rather than in the signature page.</p>

<h2>Should rent and care sit in separate companies?</h2>

<p>Start from separate ledgers, not separate companies. Supported living income arrives as two streams under two contracts: the accommodation charge under a tenancy or licence, and the support element commissioned by a local authority or an NHS body. Everything the finance work needs, the VAT analysis, the commissioner reporting and the cost evidence for a fee review, comes from separate nominal codes and matched cost allocations inside one set of books. The <a href="/for/supported-living">supported living hub</a> sets out how that split is built.</p>

<p>Splitting the streams into two legal entities adds a second corporation tax return, a second set of accounts, intercompany charges that have to be priced and documented, and the associated company effect on the thresholds. It also creates a supply between the two companies that did not exist before, which is where the VAT problem starts.</p>

<h2>Does a group structure improve the VAT position?</h2>

<p>No, and the version of it that circulated before 2025 is now under active challenge. Personal care supplied by a CQC-registered provider is exempt welfare, so the VAT on the cost of making those supplies is a permanent overhead rather than a recoverable item. <a href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/revenue-and-customs-brief-2-2025-the-use-of-vat-grouping-within-the-care-industry/use-of-vat-grouping-within-the-care-industry">Revenue and Customs Brief 2/2025</a> sets out HMRC's position that VAT group arrangements bringing an unregulated entity into the care supply chain, so that otherwise irrecoverable input VAT can be recovered, are avoidance. HMRC will refuse new group registrations built that way and will use its power to remove parties from existing groups.</p>

<p>The registration threshold question is separate and simpler. Only taxable turnover counts toward the £90,000 registration threshold, and exempt care fees are not taxable turnover. A provider whose income is entirely exempt support may have no registration obligation at all. Where there are taxable supplies alongside the exempt ones, a partial exemption calculation applies and recovery turns on the de minimis test, which is met only where exempt input tax averages no more than £625 a month and is less than half of total input tax in the period. A <a href="/services/care-vat-review">VAT review</a> before the bid is cheaper than a correction after it.</p>

<h2>What does a second company cost in corporation tax?</h2>

<p>It costs you threshold headroom. Corporation tax is 19% on profits up to £50,000 and 25% above £250,000, with marginal relief between, and <a href="https://www.gov.uk/corporation-tax-rates">HMRC divides both thresholds by the number of associated companies</a>. One trading company and one property company each reach the main rate at £125,000 of profit rather than £250,000. For a provider running a single framework contract at modest margin, that is a real rate rise bought in exchange for a structure the bid never asked for.</p>

<p>Dormant holding companies are generally outside the count, so a plain holdco over one trading company is usually neutral. A propco holding the accommodation is not neutral, and it carries a second consequence at exit, because separating property from trade can break Business Asset Disposal Relief eligibility.</p>

<h2>How should you take profit while a bid is live?</h2>

<p>Conservatively. The accounts the authority reads are the evidence of financial capacity, and distributable reserves you have taken out are reserves that are no longer on the balance sheet. From 6 April 2026 dividends are taxed at 10.75%, 35.75% and 39.35% above the £500 dividend allowance, so deferring a distribution past a year end rarely saves or costs much personally, while the retained reserves change how the submission reads.</p>

<p>Cost modelling belongs in the same conversation, because the price you bid is set by your loaded cost per support hour rather than by the rate on the framework. The floor is the National Living Wage of £12.71 an hour for workers aged 21 and over from 1 April 2026, plus employer National Insurance at 15% above the £5,000 secondary threshold, less the Employment Allowance of up to £10,500 where you are eligible, plus holiday pay and management overhead. The <a href="/blog/payroll-and-workforce-costs/care-worker-pay-rates-2026-27">current pay rates</a> are the starting point for that model.</p>

<h2>Does the CQC registration have to be in the bidding entity?</h2>

<p>For the support element, yes. Personal care is a regulated activity, registration attaches to a named legal person rather than to the service or to you, and providing it unregistered is a criminal offence under the Health and Social Care Act 2008. That is why the structure decision comes before the registration and not after it: moving the trade into a different company after registration means applying again, as the wider <a href="/blog/business-structure-and-acquisition/care-structure-before-cqc">structure decision before a CQC application</a> sets out. Accommodation only support, where no personal care is provided, sits outside CQC registration and follows a different route.</p>

<h2>The order to settle it in</h2>

<p>Fix the trading entity, register it with CQC and let it build a filing history. Split rent and care in the ledger. Leave the group flat unless something outside the bid requires otherwise. Build the cost model, then price. Prepare the <a href="/blog/cqc-and-financial-compliance/cqc-financial-viability-statement-walkthrough">financial viability statement</a> and the bid from the same numbers, so the accounts, the registration and the submission all describe one company. A specialist reviews the structure against the framework's conditions of participation before the submission goes in, and your accountant prepares the accounts that carry it.</p>
