## 1. The answer in three lines

1. **The equivalent is not a vertical. It is the outcome.** Nobody in the UK owns "the website
   that produces enquiries" as a productised, recurring service. The demand for that sits in
   three service tails, not in "web design for X".
2. **Search on service, convert on sector proof.** The keyword demand is service-shaped
   (redesign, conversion, local and productised SEO, maintenance and migration). DWC's unfair
   advantage is sector-shaped (17 live professional-services sites, lead counts attached).
   Rank for the first, close with the second.
3. **AI search is the wedge, not the site.** "Found in ChatGPT and Bing" is real, unclaimed and
   18 months early on volume. DWC has proof today that no UK agency publishes. Lead with it
   inside the offer; do not build a "GEO agency".

Decision needed at the end: section 8.

"Winnable" = on-intent, 50/mo or more, KD under 20, close variants collapsed. Full tables and
traps in SWEEP.md.

What matters:

- **Verticals are open because nobody wants them.** Across 360 classified SERP slots only 4
  were held by a niche specialist. "Web design for solicitors" is winnable at KD 0 and worth
  almost nothing. Accountancy, at 2,440/mo and five incumbents, is the exception. The
  Property-style vertical play does not exist for agencies because buyers do not search by
  their own profession; they search by the service they need.
- **The Property shape exists across three service tails together.** Productised SEO plus
  maintenance plus redesign/CRO = 196 winnable terms, 39,110/mo at KD under 20. Head terms
  (`seo agency uk` KD 54, `web design agency uk`, `web design london` KD 27-77) are closed and
  irrelevant; the tail is where the estate model lives.
- **The best single ratios:** `local seo services` 1,600/mo KD 0 on a 10-of-10 displaceable
  SERP; `website redesign services` 1,300/mo at £21.68 KD 0; `website migration agency` £257
  CPC KD 0 (highest CPC of all 17,688 keywords); `website maintenance services uk` 210/mo at
  £67 KD 0; `ai seo services` 390/mo at £72 KD 0; `geo agency` 320/mo KD 0.
- **No marketplace lock.** Clutch, Bark, PeoplePerHour and Fiverr hold at most 2 of 10 slots
  anywhere in the sweep, unlike the trades verticals where Checkatrade owns the page.

## 3. What the outside signals add

- **100 inbound leads a month is not an agency shape.** Every benchmark (SparkToro 2025, 376
  agencies; Promethean 2025; AgencyAnalytics 2025) says referrals dominate, partner referrals
  run ~15%, and only 14% of agencies call their pipeline healthy. No source shows a small
  agency at 100+ inbound from any channel. The number requires a productised offer with search
  volume behind it, or a partner that supplies many leads. The estate is both.
- **Buyers are leaving DIY for humans.** UK "website builder" searches fell 33% year on year
  while "freelance website builder" rose 40% (Nomada Digital via Workplace Journal, 30 Sep
  2025).
- **WordPress turmoil creates migration buyers.** WordPress share fell for the first time in a
  decade after the WP Engine dispute (TechCrunch, Oct 2025). Migration buyers are urgent and
  cannot use a template, which matches the £257 CPC on `website migration agency`.
- **AI search: agency demand proven, SME demand unproven, measurement unsolved.** SEOFOMO
  (Aug 2026): 98% of search professionals are asked about GEO by clients, 69% have budget,
  5% call the impact significant. UK "GEO agencies" are SEO shops with a new service page; no
  UK agency was found publishing an audited before/after AI-visibility number.
- **Regulation:** the European Accessibility Act (in force 28 June 2025, UK firms selling to
  EU consumers, WCAG 2.1 AA) is the only rule with a dated, design-shaped forced wave. Cookie
  and DMCC rules are audit wedges, not builds.
- **What SME buyers complain about, in frequency order:** not owning their domain and CMS, no
  deliverable trail, no attributable enquiries, 12-month lock-in, work that damaged the site.
  Every one inverts into a product decision DWC already makes on the estate (client owns
  everything, monthly one-page read, lead counts not traffic counts).
- **DWC's own site** is priced as a project studio (Spark £1,500, Surge £2,500-3,500), shows
  logos not numbers, and does not lead with its regulated professional-services portfolio
  (LexisNexis, Tolley, RELX, Accounts for Lawyers, Property, Emplifex). No recurring product on
  the page.

## 5. Positioning

**The website that books the calls. Design, content and AI-search visibility for professional
services firms, run as one engine.**

- **Who:** UK professional-services and B2B firms with £500k+ turnover: accountancy, legal,
  financial advice, recruitment, consultancies, agencies. Sector is the credibility axis, not
  the search axis.
- **What:** one productised, recurring engine. Redesign on the kit, four posts and a rewrite a
  month, lead capture and nurture, lead ops, one-page monthly read. This is
  `docs/_engines/DWC_PITCH/OFFER.md` already; the change is that it stops being "for
  accountants" and becomes "for firms that sell expertise", with accountancy as the flagship
  case study.
- **Why us:** the only UK studio that runs 17 of its own sites on the same engine and publishes
  the numbers. Named sector logos. Audited Bing and ChatGPT figures nobody else has.
- **Wedge:** "Are you visible in ChatGPT and Bing?" It is early on volume, KD 0, unclaimed, and
  DWC has the one thing the category lacks: a published, dated, re-derivable result.
- **Trust inversions from the complaints:** client owns domain, hosting and CMS from day one;
  dated deliverable log; enquiry counts reported monthly with the method stated; exit terms
  that are not punitive.

## 6. What to build

The laziest version that works is the estate's own playbook pointed at doublewiredcreative.com.
Nothing new is invented.

1. **Rebuild doublewiredcreative.com on the kit** as the showcase. The site is the portfolio.
   Tanj owns brand; the build reuses Next.js kit, blog engine, lead pipeline, nurture, lead ops.
2. **Three pillar clusters on the winnable tails**, written to the estate's A* bar:
   redesign and conversion (`website redesign services`, `cro agency uk`,
   `conversion optimisation services`); productised and local SEO (`local seo services`,
   `seo packages for small business`, `b2b seo agency`, `saas seo agency`, second-city terms);
   maintenance, migration and security (`website maintenance services uk`,
   `website migration agency`, `wordpress maintenance`).
3. **Three tools, the Property calculator equivalent:** a UK website cost calculator (the
   `how much does a website cost uk` SERP gives 2 of 10 to GoDaddy and Wix, the rest is
   displaceable), a redesign ROI calculator (enquiries per month at your traffic and lead
   rate), and an AI-visibility check (does ChatGPT, Copilot or Bing mention your firm for your
   service and town). The third is the wedge and the lead magnet; nobody in the UK offers it
   with a method.
4. **The proof wall:** a live page pulling the estate's counts (sites, enquiries this month,
   Bing vs Google CTR, AI-search enquiries) from the same `leads` and analytics tables the
   console uses. Every number re-derivable, dated, no rounding up.
5. **Case study:** Ecommerce Finance, two months at zero, redesign, first enquiry in hours from
   ChatGPT. Property as the flagship. Fill the `[NUMBER: ...]` placeholders in
   `CASE_STUDY_PROPERTY.md` from the proof sheet.
6. **Offer page** = OFFER.md widened from accountants to firms that sell expertise, price
   unchanged, founding rate for the first three.

Skipped: a separate lead-gen domain (the brand is the credibility; umbrella leverage is nil
either way), a standalone GEO agency (12 winnable terms), city pages for London (closed),
trades verticals (£10 CPC clients), Bark and Yell (price-shoppers, reputational damage).

## 7. How to get eyes on it

In order of yield per hour, cheapest first.

1. **The estate is the referral machine.** 17 sites, 457 human sessions a day, every kit site
   carries the footer credit. Add a "Want a site that does this for your firm?" page linked
   from every footer credit, pointing at DWC. The buyer-demand research already recommended a
   `/for-accountants` page for the practice owner checking their own ranking; this is the same
   page with DWC as the destination.
2. **Estate lead buyers and partners are the warmest list.** Every firm that has bought a lead,
   or is in the Aswatax and Sidekick orbit, has watched the engine work. Tanj calls them first.
3. **The 5,000-firm contact-form engine**, already built and parked on mock quality. Unpark it
   once the DWC site is the mock: the preview becomes "your site, on the engine that runs
   these 17". Same message, real proof instead of a template.
4. **Inbound from the three tails and three tools**, compounding over 12 months as Property did.
   Realistic capture of 10-15% of 39,110/mo winnable volume is 4,000-6,000 visits a month; at
   Property's 1.4% lead rate that is 55-80 enquiries a month from search alone by month 12.
   Plus referral and outreach, 100 a month is reachable. Not in month three.
5. **LinkedIn**, Tanj's voice, one post a week off the proof wall. 73% of agencies that use
   social rate it the most effective channel; it is free and it is where accountancy and legal
   partners are.
6. **Directories, only the free ones:** Google Business Profile for DWC (a real agency with a
   real address, unlike the estate sites; still an owner call because of the standing GBP
   rule), Framer Experts if any DWC work is on Framer. Clutch is pay-to-play at $1,500-1,800 a
   year plus sponsorship; skip until there is revenue to defend.
