# Adjacent-Vertical Lead-Gen Opportunity Research

**Prepared for:** Ashfield Trading Ltd — new lead-gen site strategy
**Date:** 2026-07-30
**Scope:** Six adjacent, non-competing verticals into which the estate's existing enquirers (landlords, doctors, dentists, solicitors, construction subcontractors) could be captured or resold.
**Method:** Six parallel research passes, web-search led. Every volume/competition claim is cited inline. Estimates are marked `[ESTIMATE]`. Where no source was found, that is stated explicitly rather than filled silently.

---

## Executive summary — read this first

**The single most important finding is structural, not per-vertical:** the accounting lead-gen model does **not** port cleanly to any of these six except one. The accounting sites work because introducing an accountancy client is **unregulated** — you can enrich a lead freely and sell it to a *panel* of competing firms in an auction. Five of these six verticals are **FCA-touched**, which breaks that model in two ways:

1. **The website itself becomes a financial promotion under s21 FSMA** (a criminal offence if communicated by an unauthorised person without approval). Since the Feb 2024 approver-gateway reform, getting a third party to sign off your promotions is harder and costlier.
2. **The compliant fix — becoming an Introducer Appointed Representative (IAR) of an authorised firm — ties you to one principal.** That principal both approves your promotions and (usefully) can be the lead buyer. But it collapses the "sell one lead to many firms" auction into a negotiated single-buyer/exclusive arrangement. Different economics, weaker pricing power.

**The only vertical that fully escapes this** is **dental commercial practice finance** (goodwill loans, commercial mortgages, business asset finance): lending to a *business* is outside the FCA's regulated-mortgage and financial-promotion regime entirely — no authorisation, no s21, no IAR needed. **SPV/Ltd-co BTL** is the next cleanest: the *introduction* is unregulated (business lending), but the *site content* is still a s21 financial promotion of "qualifying credit," so it needs an IAR — cleanly solved because the BTL broker who buys the leads can be the principal.

**Bottom line ranking (best → worst return per unit of effort):**

1. **Ltd-company / SPV & portfolio BTL mortgages** — direct adjacency to the existing property vertical (already ~40 landlord enquiries/month, many weighing incorporation), unregulated introduction, s21 solved via one BTL-broker IAR who also buys leads, strong structural tailwind (Section 24). **Build this first.**
2. **Dental commercial practice finance** — best-in-class regulatory ease (fully outside FCA scope), highest lead value, shares audience with the existing dentist site; capped by thin, un-growable volume.
3. **Construction plant / van / asset finance** — shares the CIS-site audience, fragmented beatable competition in the long tail, decent lead value; but content-heavy and the sole-trader slice is regulated.
4. **Non-resident / expat landlord mortgages** — premium leads, thin volume, entrenched specialists, s21 gate; best as a *cluster on the property site*, not a standalone.
5. **Solicitors' PII** — best unit economics of all six, but the worst regulatory fit for the model plus billion-pound incumbents and a Law Society moat; demand in two annual spikes.
6. **Medical financial advice** — highest regulatory risk (Avacade, DB-transfer minefield) and a doctor-trust incumbent (Medics' Money) a faceless operator cannot replicate. Deprioritise.

**A recurring data gap:** no free/public keyword-volume source resolved for the specific money terms in *any* vertical (Keyword Planner / Ahrefs / Semrush are login-gated). Every keyword-level volume below is `[ESTIMATE]` and must be validated in a paid tool before any build sign-off. Market-level volumes and all economics/competition/regulatory claims are sourced.

## 1. Limited company / SPV BTL & portfolio landlord mortgages  — RECOMMENDED FIRST BUILD

**Bottom line:** One of the more viable verticals on the regulatory axis, and uniquely, the one that maps directly onto the estate's existing strength. SPV/ltd-co/portfolio BTL is business lending, so introducing those borrowers is **not a regulated activity** and needs no FCA authorisation. But the site's promotional content **is** caught by s21 FSMA as "qualifying credit," so the clean structure is to become an **IAR of an authorised BTL broker** who both approves the promotions and buys the leads — one relationship solves compliance and monetisation together.

### Demand
No citeable per-term volume found for "limited company buy to let mortgage" / "SPV mortgage" / "portfolio landlord mortgage" — all `[ESTIMATE]` (mid-hundreds to ~1–2k/mo for the ltd-co head term; low–mid hundreds for SPV/portfolio). The one hard market figure: "buy to let mortgages" + "rates" ≈ **850,000 searches/year** ([archimediaaccounts.co.uk](https://archimediaaccounts.co.uk/buy-to-let-statistics/)). Structural tailwind is well-evidenced: the Section 24 interest-relief restriction is driving landlords into company structures ([habito.com](https://www.habito.com/buy-to-let-mortgages/spv-mortgage-buy-let), [commercialtrust.co.uk](https://www.commercialtrust.co.uk/buy-to-let-buy-to-let-mortgages-for-limited-companies/)); new BTL lending 59,000+ loans in Q4 2025, +18.2% YoY ([finder.com](https://www.finder.com/uk/mortgages/buy-to-let-statistics)). **This is the same audience the property site already captures (~40 landlord enquiries/month, many weighing incorporation).**

High-intent long-tail clusters (winnable, near-zero competition):
- **Criteria:** "SPV mortgage no income", "ltd company btl no minimum income", "day one remortgage SPV", "newly formed company btl mortgage"
- **Portfolio:** "portfolio landlord remortgage 4+ properties", "portfolio landlord stress test / ICR", "20+ property portfolio mortgage"
- **Structuring (accounting-adjacent, natural fit):** "transfer property to limited company mortgage", "incorporation relief btl mortgage", "SIC code SPV mortgage 68209"
- **Product × company:** "HMO limited company mortgage", "holiday let SPV mortgage", "MUFB ltd company mortgage"

Seasonality: tax-year-end (Feb–Apr) incorporation cluster + rolling 2/5-yr remortgage cycles.

### Competition
Head terms owned by aggregators (money.co.uk, Uswitch, Forbes, Charcol) and specialist brokers with deep content/tools (Commercial Trust, The Buy to Let Broker, SPV Mortgages, Fox Davidson, Habito). **Head terms unrealistic year one.** Genuine gaps: ultra-specific criteria long-tail; interactive tools (ltd-co ICR/stress-test calculator, personal-vs-company tax comparison — aligns with the estate's calculator-as-anchor pattern); SIC-code/SPV set-up guidance (accounting × mortgage intersection); portfolio-segment pages.

### Buyer economics
- Proc fee 0.35–0.5% of loan (BM Solutions 0.36%, Kent Reliance 0.37% — [mortgagestrategy.co.uk](https://www.mortgagestrategy.co.uk/cover-feature/cover-feature-paying-the-price-proc-fees-back-in-the-spotlight/)); avg BTL loan ≈ £180k ([UK Finance](https://www.ukfinance.org.uk/data-and-research/data/buy-to-let-lending) via [lenvi.com](https://www.lenvi.com/en-gb/latest-thinking/resources/uk-mortgage-market-quarterly-data/)) → **£630–£900 gross proc fee/case**, plus a £500–£1,000+ client fee → **£1,200–£2,500+ broker revenue per completed ltd-co case**, recurring at each remortgage.
- Lead prices (sourced): shared £10–18 (Bark); exclusive £26–36; BTL specifically "£30+" ([mortgagesbooked.co.uk](https://www.mortgagesbooked.co.uk/blog/mortgage-lead-costs)). Specialist ltd-co/portfolio exclusive lead `[ESTIMATE]` **£40–£75**. Broker paying £50/exclusive lead breaks even at ~1-in-20 completion → economics comfortably favour the buyer.

### Regulatory (the crux)
- **Introduction: not a regulated activity.** SPV/company/portfolio BTL is business lending, not a regulated mortgage contract ([FCA PERG 4.10B](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg4/perg4s21)), so introducing needs no FCA authorisation. Guardrail: stay strictly business/company-side; the moment you capture consumer-BTL or residential, "arranging a regulated contract" engages and you need the Article 33 introducer exclusion or full IAR.
- **Site content: IS a financial promotion.** s21 catches "qualifying credit" = credit secured on land where the lender is an authorised regulated-mortgage lender, *regardless of whether the specific loan is regulated* ([FCA Glossary](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/glossary/G1315), [PERG 8.17](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg8/perg8s17)). SPV BTL lenders (TMW, Paragon, Aldermore, etc.) are all authorised → the loans are qualifying credit → a site inducing enquiries is communicating financial promotions.
- **Route (recommended): IAR of an authorised BTL broker.** An IAR's permitted scope is exactly "introductions + distributing financial promotions" ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/principals-appointed-representatives/become)). The broker who buys the leads can be the principal and approve the site — one relationship, no FCA authorisation, no gateway application, no own PI insurance. Trade-off: channel exclusivity (one/few principals, not an open auction). Do **not** run conversion content unauthorised — s21 breach is criminal, and *Avacade* shows the FCA enforces against introducers who overstep ([kennedyslaw.com](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/case-review/clarification-on-the-regulatory-position-of-unregulated-introducers/)).

### Existing players
Mortgage lead-gen is crowded for *general* leads (Unbiased ~£85 effective, Bark £10–18, Lead Pronto £26, Digital Roo £10–65, MortgagesBooked £110/appointment) but **specialist ltd-co/SPV/portfolio lead-gen is thin-to-absent** — the white space. A tool-led ltd-co/portfolio site feeding exclusive leads to one BTL broker (who doubles as IAR principal) sidesteps the crowded general market and fits the estate's constraints.

## 2. Dental practice finance, mortgages & protection

**Bottom line:** Lowest-volume but highest-value-per-lead, and the **commercial-finance slice is the single most compliance-light opportunity in the whole set** — practice-purchase goodwill loans, commercial mortgages and business asset finance sit **outside FCA scope entirely** (not a regulated mortgage contract, not a controlled activity, so **not even caught by s21**). Build the estate around the unregulated commercial core; bolt on residential mortgages and protection only through an authorised partner.

### Demand
Population ceiling is the binding constraint: **47,916 registered dentists** ([GDC Jan 2026](https://www.gdc-uk.org/news-blogs/news/detail/2026/01/19/gdc-register-increases-to-over-47-000-dentists)), **~12,223 practices** ([Christie & Co](https://dentist-times.co.uk/dentist-times-owners-club-insights/dental-market-gains-ground-with-stronger-confidence-and-deal-activity); cross-checked [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-kingdom/number-of-businesses/dental-practices/4560/)). Thin and un-growable. No public keyword-volume source — all `[ESTIMATE]`: head terms ("dental practice finance", "dentist mortgage") 200–600/mo; long-tail ("goodwill loan dental practice", "associate dentist mortgage", "dentist income protection", "dental chair finance") 10–150/mo each. Evergreen, not seasonal. The vertical only works by covering *every* sub-product and the full ownership lifecycle to aggregate thin streams.

### Competition
Densely occupied by capable specialists with real authority: Rangewell (`/dentists` hub), Samera, Dental Elite, PFM Dental, Fifty Nine Financial, Fox Davidson/FD Commercial, Alpha Capital, Clifton PF; lenders Braemar Finance and Wesleyan (now HTB-owned). Difficulty **high** on money head terms. Gap: incumbents publish product pages, not exhaustive guide content — the informational long-tail and the *whole-lifecycle-in-one-place* coverage is under-served.

### Buyer economics — highest value per lead
- Practice deals combine goodwill loans (£300k–£1m+) and commercial mortgages, up to 100% LTV for the "professional" category ([Fifty Nine Financial](https://fiftyninefinancial.co.uk/commercial-finance/healthcare-finance/dental-practice-finance-broker/), [PFM Dental](https://pfmdental.co.uk/practice-sales/dental-practice-finance/)).
- Commercial proc fee ≈ 1% of facility (£5,000 on £500k; some ~0.5% — [Swoop](https://swoopfunding.com/uk/business-glossary/procuration-fees/)), plus client fee → **£3,000–£10,000+ to the broker per completed case** `[ESTIMATE]`. Residential dentist mortgage ~0.35–0.5% (£1,400–£2,000). Protection pays indemnity commission (~£1,600+ upfront).
- Generic commercial-finance leads sell from ~£50 ([Digital Roo](https://digitalroo.co.uk/leads/secured-loan-leads/)); a qualified exclusive dental-practice-purchase lead `[ESTIMATE]` **£150–£600+** given case value × plausible conversion. No dental-specific lead price found.

### Regulatory — best in the set
| Sub-product | FCA regulated? | s21 promotion? | Solo route | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Goodwill / commercial mortgage / business asset finance | **No** (business borrower) | **No** — not a controlled activity | Bare commercial introduction | **Near-zero FCA overhead** |
| Dentist **residential** mortgage | Yes | Yes | Article 33 introducer **or** IAR | Moderate |
| Income protection / locum insurance | Yes (IDD) | Yes | IAR of authorised adviser | Moderate |

Commercial lending to a business is excluded from FCA regulation ([FCA PERG 4.4](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg4/perg4s4)) and, not being a controlled activity, is **not a financial promotion under s21** ([PERG 8.17](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg8/perg8s17)). Watch-out: keep copy strictly business-purpose — a sole trader buying premises >40% used as a dwelling can flip it into a regulated mortgage contract.

### Existing players
Specialist brokers double as their own lead-gen (Rangewell, Samera, PFM, Fifty Nine). Generic commercial-finance vendors sell from ~£50/lead. Incumbent finance brands (Wesleyan/HTB, Braemar) are natural **buyers**. Model to emulate: Rangewell's `/dentists` hub, but as a lead-gen estate selling to a partner broker, staying unauthorised on the commercial slice.

**Net:** Prioritise for regulatory ease, not traffic. Pair with the existing dentist-accounting site (shared audience, cross-sell) rather than a standalone bet — the thin volume gives little margin for error.

## 3. Construction / CIS insurance & plant/asset finance

**Bottom line:** Classic high-volume, low-value, high-competition vertical. The insurance half is a **closed door** owned by Simply Business and Quotezone/Seopa — a zero-authority domain will not rank on head terms. The **finance half (plant/van/asset) is the attractive slice**: higher lead value, fragmented beatable competition in the long tail, cleaner regulatory path if scoped to companies. Regulation is solvable; out-ranking Simply Business is not. **Competition, not regulation, is the blocker.**

### Demand
Insurance: large but aggregator-owned — "public liability insurance" 90,500, "business insurance" 90,500, "small business insurance" 33,100 ([keysearch.co](https://www.keysearch.co/top-keywords/insurance-business-keywords), English-language, treat as upper bound). "tradesman insurance"/"tools insurance" — no volume found, SERP density implies mid-thousands `[ESTIMATE]`. TAM: **~748,000 self-employed construction workers** ([Statista/ONS](https://www.statista.com/statistics/318406/united-kingdom-self-employed-type-of-work-industry-section/)).
Finance: "asset finance" 3,600, "asset finance brokers" 170, "construction asset finance" 50 ([embryo.com](https://embryo.com/blog/100-of-the-most-searched-keywords-for-the-finance-industry/)); "digger/excavator/plant/van finance" — no volume, but deep SERP → low hundreds/mo each `[ESTIMATE]`. The plant/equipment long-tail is fragmented across small brokers.

### Competition
Insurance: **Simply Business** (VC/PE-backed, ~4,252 referring domains, sustained SEO program — [Blue Array](https://www.bluearray.co.uk/portfolio/simply-business/)) and **Quotezone/Seopa** (300+ provider panel, hundreds of white-label brands — [seopa.com](https://www.seopa.com/)). **Near-hopeless on head terms.** Finance: no dominant aggregator; many small-mid brokers (Rangewell, Portman, Charles & Dean, Liberty Leasing, Hard Asset Finance) — **moderate difficulty, beatable in the long tail**.

### Buyer economics
Insurance = low value: PL premiums £60–£600/yr, commission ~20% (£30 gross on a £150 premium), lead `[ESTIMATE]` £5–£15 shared / £15–£30 exclusive. Finance = higher: sourced UK asset-finance leads £25+£40 ad (~£65) or "from £50" exclusive ([leadcrowd.com](https://www.leadcrowd.com/lead-types/financial-services/commercial-finance/)); broker commission ~4% avg → £900–£1,200 on a £30k digger deal; lead `[ESTIMATE]` £40–£100+ exclusive.

### Regulatory
- **Insurance:** arranging/introducing = regulated insurance distribution (IDD, [PERG 5.6](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg5/perg5s6)). Routes: IAR of an insurance principal, or **affiliate/white-label under an authorised aggregator** (Seopa/Quotezone) — lowest friction, but you inherit their competitive wall.
- **Finance:** credit broking (RAO Art 36A). **Company/large-partnership borrower → unregulated; sole trader / small partnership → regulated** ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/authorisation/consumer-credit-brokers/primary-credit-brokers)). The CIS audience is heavily sole traders — the catch. Routes: scope site to limited companies only (clean but leaky at the form), or **AR of one authorised asset-finance broker** (best).

**Net:** Do **not** build insurance-first. If built, go **plant/van/asset-finance-first** riding the CIS-site adjacency, insurance as a secondary affiliate module. Flag: content-heavy for a solo operator — weigh against lighter verticals.

## 4. Non-resident / expat landlord mortgages

**Bottom line:** Regulatorily the hardest of the mortgage verticals and volume-sufficiency is genuinely marginal. Premium-per-lead, thin-volume niche defended by entrenched specialists and big banks. Buildable only through an **IAR/s21-approved** structure — a bare unauthorised site here is a criminal-liability trap. **Treat as a cluster on the existing property site, not a standalone domain.**

### Demand
No reliable per-keyword volume ([mpamag](https://www.mpamag.com/uk/mortgage-types/buy-to-let/expat-buy-to-let-a-growing-market-hiding-in-plain-sight/566568) names UAE, Hong Kong, USA, Singapore as demand hubs). Head "expat mortgage" plausibly low-thousands/mo `[ESTIMATE]`; long-tail (country-specific "British expat mortgage Dubai", "overseas landlord remortgage", "expat SPV BTL") very low individual volume. Very high commercial intent (62% of intermediaries report too few lender options for non-UK borrowers). **The expat/non-resident slice is not separately quantified in any source — realistically tens of leads/month at national scale for a category leader, single-digit for a new entrant.** Too thin for a standalone content site; worthwhile as a property-site cluster.

### Competition
Split field: banks (Skipton International, HSBC Expat, Santander/NatWest International) + entrenched specialists (Offshoreonline 25 yrs, Liquid Expat Mortgages, John Charcol, Enness) + Online Mortgage Advisor occupying the aggregator slot. **High difficulty.** Gaps: country-specific long-tail, non-resident *remortgage*, SPV+expat intersection, foreign-national (non-British) content.

### Buyer economics
Larger loans (£300k–£500k) at ~0.5% proc + broker fee → **£2,000–£4,500+ per completed case** `[ESTIMATE]`. Expat leads unpriced publicly; `[ESTIMATE]` **£75–£200+ per exclusive lead** (multiple of standard BTL). Economics reward quality/exclusivity over volume — suits an organic solo operator.

### Regulatory
Most expat BTL is unregulated ([FCA PERG 4.10B](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/PERG/4/10B.html)), but consumer-BTL and expat-residential are regulated, and an expat site unavoidably attracts those. Site = s21 financial promotion; breach = criminal (s25 FSMA, up to 2 yrs). Cross-border promotion to overseas consumers adds a layer. **Best route: IAR under an authorised Principal**, gate the whole thing on securing that first. Lean into unregulated investor-BTL framing + country-specific long-tail.

### Existing players
Online Mortgage Advisor (archetype SEO→adviser funnel, authorised/AR structure); Liquid/Offshoreonline are vertically-integrated competitors, not buyers. No unauthorised expat pure-play found — consistent with the s21 barrier. Standard BTL leads £30–60; expat premium unpriced (est. £75–200).

## 5. Solicitors' Professional Indemnity Insurance (PII)

**Bottom line:** The best unit economics of any vertical (premiums £2,500–£50,000+, commissions in the hundreds-to-thousands, recurring annually), but the two things that make the accounting sites work — an unregulated introduction and freedom to sell an enriched lead to many buyers — are **both absent**. Insurance introducing is FCA-regulated; the exemption a solo operator would need (art.33B) is specifically unlikely to apply *when lead-gen is your main business*; the compliant fallback (single-broker IAR, bare contact only) neuters the marketplace model. **Deprioritise, or pursue only as one authorised broker's IAR after a compliance opinion.**

### Demand
No keyword-volume source — all `[ESTIMATE]`. Strong, confirmed seasonality: ~75% of firms still renew **1 October** despite the common date's 2014 abolition ([recoveryfirst.co.uk](https://www.recoveryfirst.co.uk/news/solicitors-facing-issues-with-professional-indemnity-insurance-renewal/)), plus a secondary **1 April** cluster ([Howden](https://www.howdengroup.com/uk-en/april-2026-solicitors-pii-renewal-review)) → a hard Aug–Oct spike and thin demand the other ~9 months. Very high intent (PII is mandatory, 90%+ buy through brokers, online instant quotes essentially unavailable). Long-tail: "solicitors PII quote", "SRA minimum terms", "run off cover solicitors", "high risk conveyancing PII", "sole practitioner PII". Caveat: market has *softened* (record ~52 insurers 2025/26), dampening switching intent — peak lead value is in hard markets (cyclical).

### Competition
Billion-pound national brokers (Lockton, Howden, Marsh/JLT, Aon, Gallagher) + specialist mid-market (Prime Professions, Hera Indemnity, Lonsdale) + the **Law Society endorsement moat** a new domain cannot replicate + QuoteRack occupying the comparison slot. **Very high difficulty.** Only wedge: informational long-tail (run-off, startup-firm PII, SRA MTC explainers, SIF).

### Buyer economics — best in the set
Premiums median ~5% of turnover ([SRA research](https://www.sra.org.uk/sra/research-publications/professional-indemnity-insurance-market-law-firms/)): sole practitioner ~£2,500, £1m-turnover firm ~£50,000 ([Butler Evans](https://butlerevans.co.uk/solicitors-professional-indemnity-insurance-cost/)). PI commission ~15–25% `[ESTIMATE]` → **£375–£500** on a small firm, **£7,500–£10,000** on a large one, **recurring annually**. No UK PII-lead price found; `[ESTIMATE]` **£40–£150 exclusive**, higher for pre-renewal/large/hard-to-place.

### Regulatory — the blocker
Introducing insurance = regulated insurance distribution ([FCA PERG 5](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg5)). The art.33B "ordinary introducer" exemption lets you pass *only* contact details and *be paid* — **but FCA guidance says a firm whose main business is sourcing insurance leads is unlikely to qualify** ([RWA](https://insight.rwabusiness.com/blog/posts/2018/april/introducer-appointed-representatives-iars-and-the-insurance-distribution-directive-idd/)), which is exactly the Ashfield model. It also forbids enriching the lead (risk/renewal detail) — i.e. the £100+ lead you'd want to sell is the one you're not allowed to produce. Compliant version = single-broker IAR, bare contact, broker-approved promotions → single buyer, weak pricing power.

### Existing players
QuoteRack (closest aggregator), MoneySuperMarket/Simply Business (general PI), broker-owned guide content (Butler Evans, Howden, Lockton) that funnels to their own authorised broking. No lead-price transparency; the market monetises as authorised intermediaries, not raw lead sellers — consistent with the constraints.

## 6. Medical / NHS clinician financial advice

**Bottom line:** Highest-regulatory-risk vertical and the worst fit for the model. Demand is real and per-client economics are the best of any vertical, but every route that lets you take a *firm-side* fee (IAR, s21 approval) either ties you to a single principal or imposes per-promotion compliance a part-time operator can't run, and the "just introduce" exemptions forbid the third-party fee that is the entire business. Pensions/investment content also risks straying into regulated *advising* and, at the extreme, DB-transfer territory (the FCA's most-enforced area). **Rank last. Only the doctor-mortgage slice under a broker IAR is survivable.**

### Demand
Strong, recurring, deadline-driven (Scheme Pays 31 July, Self-Assessment 31 Jan, tax-year-end, autumn statement season — [NHSBSA](https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/nhs-pensions-annual-allowance-and-self-assessment-tax-returns)). No keyword-volume source — all `[ESTIMATE]`. Clusters: "NHS pension annual allowance", "scheme pays NHS", "consultant pension tax charge", "GP pension advice", "doctors mortgage" (likely highest-volume). Demand is not the problem.

### Competition
Well-funded content-led specialists, most authorised and capturing enquiries in-house: Wesleyan, Chase de Vere Medical, Cavendish Medical, Legal & Medical, Sanlam. The one to worry about: **Medics' Money** — doctor-founded, GMC-verified-review directory/matching service for medical IFAs/accountants, huge organic + podcast reach ([medicsmoney.co.uk](https://medicsmoney.co.uk/find-a-specialist-medical-ifa/)). It owns the doctor-trust signal a **faceless non-clinician operator cannot replicate** — and the estate's constraint is exactly that the operator is not a clinician and must stay faceless. **Very high difficulty**, gaps thin.

### Buyer economics
Pension/investment advice: ~2.4% initial (£1,500–£3,000+) + ~0.8% recurring AUM ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/investing/financial-advice/how-much-financial-advice-costs-aODa70J6nYs7)) — highest lifetime value, but the buyers (Wesleyan etc.) capture their own enquiries and least need a lead broker. Doctor mortgages: 0.35–0.5% proc (£1,400–£2,500/case) — lower value but far cleaner. Pension lead `[ESTIMATE]` £50–£150; mortgage lead £20–£60. Higher pension value is partly theoretical given who the buyers are.

### Regulatory — the decisive constraint
Three FSMA hazards: (1) **s21 financial promotion** — steering doctors to advice is an inducement, caught, and post-Feb-2024 the approver must hold gateway permission. (2) **The introducer exemptions collapse on the third-party fee** — FPO art.15 requires "no pecuniary reward from any person other than the recipient" ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2005/1529/made)); the Ashfield model is paid by the firm, voiding it. (3) **Straying into arranging/advising** — *FCA v Avacade* read "arranging" as merely creating the *opportunity* for a transaction, and a commission incentive tied to transactions pushed a mere "introducer" into unauthorised regulated activity ([Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/arranging-deals-in-investments-revisited)); DB/NHS-pension-transfer advice is the single highest-risk area and content must never touch it. IAR is workable only for the doctor-mortgage or single-IFA slice, and channels leads to one principal — incompatible with the multi-buyer auction. **Downside is criminal FSMA breach + personal liability, and the FCA litigates against introducers who overstep.**

### Existing players
Medics' Money (dominant, doctor-run directory — structurally the two things a faceless per-lead operator can't copy); VouchedFor/Unbiased (general directories); generic pension-lead vendors (~£500/mo, same s21 risk). Specialist IFAs run their own content and are competitors, not buyers.

## Final recommendation — build Ltd-company / SPV & Portfolio BTL mortgages first

**Reasoning:**

1. **It is not really a new site — it is a mortgage cluster on the vertical you already dominate.** The property site already produces ~40 landlord enquiries/month, and the memory notes a large share are portfolio owners weighing incorporation. That is *exactly* the SPV/ltd-co BTL mortgage buyer. You capture native mortgage intent from an audience you already own, on a domain that already has authority — the single biggest lever against the "zero domain authority" constraint that handicaps every other option. Head terms stay hard, but the criteria/portfolio/structuring long-tail is winnable, and the SIC-code/incorporation-relief cluster sits on the accounting × mortgage seam that plays to your existing content strength.

2. **The regulatory path is clean and self-funding.** The introduction is unregulated (business lending); the only obligation (compliant financial promotions) is discharged by becoming an **IAR of the BTL broker who buys your leads** — one relationship solves compliance *and* monetisation. No FCA authorisation, no gateway application, no own PI cover. Contrast: solicitors PII and medical advice can't be run this way at all, and expat needs the same IAR gate for thinner volume.

3. **Structural tailwind + recurring value.** Section 24 is actively pushing landlords into companies; BTL refinances every 2–5 years, so an acquired client recurs. Lead value (£40–75 exclusive `[ESTIMATE]`) on £1,200–£2,500 broker case revenue supports a healthy exclusive price.

4. **Lowest marginal effort for a solo part-time operator.** It reuses the existing niche-site template, the shared `optimisation_engine`/`blog_generator` (one config entry), faceless EEAT, the lead-consent form and routing — plus the property vertical's existing content and links. Dental commercial finance is the honourable mention (best regulatory ease, highest lead value) but its thin un-growable volume and high-authority incumbents make it a slower burn; run it *second*, paired with the dentist site.

**The one genuinely new muscle to build:** the IAR / financial-promotion-approval layer the accounting sites never needed. Secure a BTL-broker principal *before* publishing conversion-driving content — that single step is what separates a compliant launch from an s21 criminal exposure. Get a one-off compliance/solicitor sign-off on the IAR agreement and promotion wording; it is cheap insurance.

**Sequence:** (1) SPV/ltd-co & portfolio BTL as a property-site cluster + IAR principal → (2) dental commercial finance paired with the dentist site → (3) construction plant/van finance paired with the CIS site, finance-first. Park expat as a small property-site sub-cluster; deprioritise solicitors PII and medical advice.

### How this adapts the existing niche lead-gen system

The estate's niche-site machinery ports directly for the *build*: Next.js template, shared `optimisation_engine`/`blog_generator` (add one `--site` config + `SITE_RULES`/`CORE_PAGES` entries per [ENGINE_MAP_AND_ONBOARDING](ENGINE_MAP_AND_ONBOARDING.md)), faceless credential-free EEAT, anonymised social proof, no pricing, "book a call" CTA, the mandatory data-sharing consent checkbox, and lead→email/Sheets routing (add a new lead-source identifier per vertical).

What does **not** port, and must be added for the five regulated verticals: (a) a **financial-promotion approval layer** — an IAR principal or s21 approver signs off page copy before publish; (b) a shift from **multi-buyer lead auction to single-principal/exclusive** economics; (c) copy guardrails that keep content on the unregulated side of the line (business-purpose only for BTL/dental commercial finance; company-only for construction finance). Dental *commercial* finance is the sole vertical that needs none of this — it can run on the existing unregulated model unchanged.
