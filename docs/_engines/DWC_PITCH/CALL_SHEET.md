# Call sheet

One page. 15 minutes booked, 20 is fine, 40 is a bad sign.

## Two hard rules

1. **Never promise lead counts.** Not a number, not a range, not "firms like you usually see".
   If pushed: "I will not give you a number I cannot stand behind. What I can show you is what
   the system does and what we measure."
2. **Never give tax advice.** Not even an easy one. "That is your territory, not mine. I only
   know enough tax to write about it accurately and to know when to check."

## Opening (30 seconds)

"Thanks for the reply. I run a small design studio, we build and run websites for accounting
firms, and I spend most of my time inside tax content so I am not going to ask you what your
brand values are. Can I ask you five questions about how work reaches you at the moment, and
then I will tell you what we do and you can decide if it is interesting?"

## Five discovery questions

1. Where does new work come from at the moment, roughly, in order?
2. When somebody does find you online, what happens next? Who sees it, and how fast?
3. What kind of work do you actually want more of, and what do you get too much of?
4. Has anyone written anything for the site, and what happened?
5. If nothing changed for another year, is that a problem or is it fine?

Listen for: referrals plateauing, one person handling every enquiry by hand, wanting advisory
work and getting compliance work, a burnt SEO experience. Those are the four openings.

## Presenting the offer

Say it in this order, and stop after the price.

1. "A site on our design system. We built it for a property tax site last August, then put the
   same system on eight more sites, all live last week. Every one credits us in the footer,
   you can go and look."
2. "Four posts and one rewrite a month, written to what people actually type, with the tax
   facts kept current."
3. "Capture and nurture, so an enquiry that goes quiet gets followed up without you doing it."
4. "Lead ops. Every enquiry in your inbox and a spreadsheet inside a minute, deduplicated,
   spam filtered."
5. "One page a month telling you what happened and what we are doing next."
6. "£1,500 a month, twelve months, plus £2,500 to build the site. You are one of the first
   three firms so it is £1,000 a month if you let me write up what we did."

Then be quiet.

## Objections

**"That is expensive."**
It is about the price of two days of a junior's time a month, and it is the whole function:
site, content, follow-up and reporting. Compare it to hiring, not to a one-off website.

**"We get all our clients from referrals."**
Good, that is the strongest channel there is and nothing here replaces it. The question is
what happens when a referral looks you up before calling, because most do. And referral flow
is somebody else's decision, not yours.

**"We tried SEO and it did nothing."**
What did you get, and for how long? Usually it is either thin pages on a monthly package, or
six months and a stop. We publish content that answers real questions and keeps its facts
current, and we tell you every month what moved. If nothing is moving at month six you will
know, from me, in writing.

**"Twelve months is a long commitment."**
It is, deliberately. Content is at its weakest the week it goes live. Twelve months is about
48 posts working together and it is the shortest window where either of us learns anything.
Three months would be me taking your money for pages that never got found.

**"Who actually writes the content?"**
We do, to a brief built from what people search for, and every tax figure is checked against
source and kept current as rates change. You get the final say before anything publishes.

**"Is it AI?"**
We use tools, like everybody does. What you are paying for is the research, the fact checking
and the fact that somebody is accountable when a rate changes in April. You will see drafts
before they publish, and if one reads like a robot, reject it.

**"Can you guarantee leads?"**
No, and I will not. Anyone who does is guessing with your money. What I guarantee is the work:
the site, four posts and a rewrite every month, the follow-up running, every enquiry in front
of you within a minute, and a page every month telling you the truth about what happened.

**"We are a sole trader / not a limited company."**
Does not matter here. This is your practice's website, not your accounts. If anything it is
easier, because there is one person to sign off and one person to talk to.

## Close

"Shall I send you the proposal? It is one page, it is what I just said with your name on it,
and you can sign it online. If you want to think about it, I will send it anyway and follow
up on Thursday."

## Do not

- Do not name the portfolio owner or the other sites beyond "sites we build and run".
- Do not negotiate the price live. Founding rate is the only flexibility and it is already on
  the table.
- Do not offer Google Business Profile work. The wrong edit gets a profile suspended.
- Do not offer paid ads. Not in this offer.
