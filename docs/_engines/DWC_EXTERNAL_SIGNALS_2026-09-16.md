# DWC external signals: everything that is not Google keyword volume

Research date: 2026-09-16. UK focus. Every figure carries a source URL and the date of the source.
Keyword-volume evidence is deliberately excluded (handled by a separate agent).

**Read-me on confidence.** Figures are labelled by what they actually are:
- OFFICIAL = government or regulator statistic.
- VENDOR = published by a company with an interest in the number.
- SURVEY = named survey, sample size stated.
- UNVERIFIED = could not be confirmed from a primary or named source. Not guessed.

A recurring problem in this space: most "web design cost UK 2026" and "SEO pricing UK" pages are
agency marketing content citing each other. Where that is the only source, it is marked VENDOR and
should be treated as a price signal, not a measured average.

- **98%** have been asked about AI search visibility by clients or managers.

## 5. Regulation-driven demand

### 5.1 European Accessibility Act (strongest of the five)

- In force **28 June 2025** for all new digital products and services placed on the EU market;
  existing services have until **28 June 2030**.
  https://hamlins.com/insight/uk-business-with-eu-customers-the-european-accessibility-act-comes-into-force-from-28-june-2025-how-to-ensure-you-are-compliant/
- Applies extraterritorially: a UK business offering in-scope services to EU consumers must comply
  post-Brexit. Same source, plus https://reciteme.com/news/european-accessibility-act-in-the-uk/
- Standard is WCAG 2.1 AA via harmonised standard EN 301 549.
  https://accessibe.com/blog/knowledgebase/eaa-uk-compliance-impact-for-ecommerce
- Exemption: microenterprises with fewer than 10 staff **and** under EUR 2m turnover.
- Penalties vary by member state; commonly cited administrative fines up to EUR 20,000 per violation
  and daily fines up to EUR 1,000, plus EU market access restriction. VENDOR summary, accessiBe link
  above. UNVERIFIED per member state.

**Is there a forced wave?** Yes, and it is the cleanest one. In-scope: e-commerce, banking, ticketing,
e-books, transport and telecoms services sold to EU consumers. Any UK firm above the micro threshold
selling into the EU has a dated legal obligation with a specific technical standard attached. That is
a defined, findable buyer list and a scoped piece of work. It is also directly in DWC's skill area
(UX, design systems, front-end). **UNVERIFIED: how many UK businesses are actually in scope. No count
was found.**

### 5.2 UK Public Sector Bodies Accessibility Regulations 2018

- SI 2018/952, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/952
- GDS monitors on behalf of the Cabinet Office; enforcement sits with the EHRC in Great Britain and
  ECNI in Northern Ireland. https://www.gov.uk/guidance/accessibility-requirements-for-public-sector-websites-and-apps
- GDS began monitoring the extra WCAG 2.2 criteria from October 2024, with a 12-month grace period
  from the WCAG 2.2 release.
  https://www.lexdis.org.uk/digital-accessibility/digital-accessibility-regulations/monitoring-and-enforcement/
- Function transfers under the Transfer of Functions (Digital Government) Order 2025, 3 June 2025.

**Is there a forced wave?** Only for public sector bodies and their suppliers. It is a procurement
market (frameworks, tenders, panel appointments), not an inbound-lead market. Low fit for a
100-leads-a-month inbound goal.

### 5.3 DMCC Act 2024: fake reviews and drip pricing

- Consumer provisions commenced **6 April 2025** via the Commencement No.2 Regulations 2025.
  https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/the-dmcc-act-consumer-elements-come-into-force-from-6-april-2025
- Drip pricing and fake reviews are now in the list of practices unfair in all circumstances.
  https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2025/april/new-cma-consumer-protection-comes-into-force
- CMA said its first three months on reviews would focus on supporting compliance rather than
  enforcement, and that drip pricing enforcement would initially target clear breaches. Same source.
- CMA one-year-on review: https://competitionandmarkets.blog.gov.uk/2026/04/17/direct-consumer-enforcement-one-year-on/

**Is there a forced wave?** Partial and mostly e-commerce. It forces changes to pricing display
(all mandatory fees in the headline price) and review widgets. Real work, but it is a compliance
patch, not a redesign trigger, and it is usually handled by legal and platform settings rather than a
design agency.

### 5.4 Data (Use and Access) Act 2025: cookies

- PECR maximum fines raised from GBP 500,000 to **GBP 17.5m or 4% of global annual turnover**.
  https://www.mfmac.com/insights/data-protection/data-use-and-access-act-2025-increased-maximum-fines-for-cookies-and-direct-marketing-practices/
- Three new consent exemptions introduced. From **5 February 2026** certain low-risk cookies,
  including first-party analytics and appearance preferences, no longer require consent.
  https://www.policypros.co.uk/cookie-consent-changes-duaa-guide/
- ICO cookie/storage-and-access guidance published after two consultation rounds.
  https://www.stevens-bolton.com/insights/102mrve/the-data-use-and-access-act-2025-ico-guidance-on-cookies-and-the-use-of-storag/
- The ICO 2025 Online Tracking Strategy names tracking misuse as an enforcement priority.

**Is there a forced wave?** Every UK website needs its cookie banner and policy reviewed, and the fine
ceiling moved by 35x. That is a genuine universal trigger. But it is a low-ticket, high-volume job
that consent-management vendors (Usercentrics, CookieYes, OneTrust) already own. Poor standalone
product; usable as a door-opener or an audit hook.

### 5.5 Online Safety Act

- Children's safety duties and "highly effective age assurance" enforceable from **25 July 2025**, no
  phase-in. https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/age-checks-to-protect-children-online
- Penalties up to GBP 18m or 10% of global turnover, plus business disruption measures.
  https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/2025-uk-online-safety-act-round-up

**Is there a forced wave?** Yes but narrow: user-to-user services, platforms hosting pornographic or
harmful content, dating and gaming. Not a general SME website market. Ignore for DWC.

### 5.6 Ranking of the five

1. **EAA** (dated obligation, technical standard, design-shaped work, identifiable buyer list).
2. **DUAA cookies** (universal, low ticket, crowded vendor market, useful as an audit wedge).
3. **DMCC** (e-commerce narrow, mostly legal/platform work).
4. **PSBAR 2018** (procurement market, not inbound).
5. **OSA** (out of scope).

## 6. Platform shifts that create redesign waves

- **WordPress peaked.** WordPress reached 43.6% of all websites by mid-2025, its first decline after
  a decade of growth. https://superbthemes.com/blog/is-wordpress-losing-market-share-or-holding-steady/
  (2026). The nuance in that source matters: most competitor growth is new projects and migration
  from smaller CMSs, not a mass exodus.
- **Webflow vs Framer.** Webflow at 0.98% SaaS market share by adoption (#12 overall), Framer at 0.18%
  (#80), as of August 2026. Webflow powers roughly 822,550 active sites; Framer over 232,026.
  https://data.cledara.com/compare/webflow-vs-framer
  Framer's search-interest curve crossed Webflow's in November 2025. Same source.
  Positioning: Webflow dominates agencies and scaling businesses; Framer is strongest with UI/UX
  designers and early-stage startups. https://www.flowninja.com/blog/framer-vs-webflow (2026).
- **WP Engine vs Automattic.** Began Sept 2024 with Mullenweg's demand for 8% of WP Engine annual
  revenue (~GBP 26m) for trademark use; escalated through 2025 with counterclaims filed 24 Oct 2025.
  https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/12/wordpress-vs-wp-engine-drama-explained/,
  https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/24/automattic-files-counterclaims-against-wp-engine-in-wordpress-lawsuit-alleging-trademark-misuse/
  Over 150 Automattic employees left, several core contributors, slowing WordPress core development.
  https://wpvswpe.report/
  Agency-side effect: agencies that build client sites on WordPress are reassessing platform risk and
  contingency. https://brewdigital.com/resources/article/understanding-the-impact-of-the-automattic-and-wp-engine-legal-battle
- **Shopify.** UK Shopify stores grew 37% year on year in Q1 2024; the UK is Shopify's second largest
  market; EMEA merchants +31% in 2025. https://www.charle.co.uk/articles/shopify-statistics/ (2026).
- **Core Web Vitals pressure.** Only 48% of mobile pages and 56% of desktop pages pass all three CWV
  (2025 Web Almanac); 43% of sites still fail the 200ms INP threshold in 2026; mobile pass rates trail
  desktop by 7 points. https://thestacc.com/blog/core-web-vitals-statistics/ and
  https://nitropack.io/blog/most-important-core-web-vitals-metrics/ (2026).
- **DIY builder decline, and the crucial corollary.** UK "website builder" searches fell 33% year on
  year while "freelance website builder" searches rose 40%. Nomada Digital analysis of Google data,
  reported 30 Sep 2025:
  https://workplacejournal.co.uk/2025/09/a-third-of-uk-businesses-remain-offline-despite-digital-growth/

**Which shift creates buyers who need an agency rather than a template?**

The DIY-to-outsourced swing is the clearest single signal in this whole report: the same dataset shows
DIY intent falling a third while "get someone to do it" intent rises 40%. That is a buyer moving from
template to human.

Second is the WordPress governance risk. It creates a *migration* buyer, not a redesign buyer, and
migration buyers are higher intent, more urgent, and cannot be served by a template, because the
existing site has to be rebuilt on a new platform. Webflow and Framer are the destinations. Framer in
particular is where a design-led studio has an edge over a dev shop, since the platform rewards
design craft rather than plugin engineering.

Core Web Vitals is a technical retainer, not a redesign wave. Shopify UK growth is real but the
Shopify agency market is already saturated and price-anchored.

### The pattern underneath the quotes

Five distinct grievances, in frequency order:
1. **Loss of control.** Client does not own the domain, hosting, registrar or CMS login. This is the
   oldest and most consistent complaint and it has never been solved by the industry.
2. **No evidence of work.** No reports, no link lists, no deliverable trail.
3. **No attributable outcome.** Money spent, no enquiries anyone can point to.
4. **Lock-in.** 12-month terms, exit fees, no cooling-off.
5. **Method risk.** Work that actively damaged the site (dodgy links, penalties).

### What a buyer would trust instead

Directly inverted from the list, and each of these is a concrete, cheap product decision, not a brand
message:
- Client owns the domain, registrar, hosting and CMS from day one, in writing, with credentials
  handed over at launch.
- A named, dated deliverable log the client can see without asking.
- Lead counts, not traffic counts, reported monthly, with the measurement method stated.
- No minimum term, or a stated exit that is not punitive.
- Named clients in the buyer's own sector who will take a phone call. Referral is already how this
  market buys (58% find their accountant by referral against 3% by advertising, section 2.5), so
  reference-ability is the highest-value asset, not the highest-value message.

## 8. Where agency inbound leads actually come from

### SparkToro "2025 State of Digital Agencies"
Run by Rand Fishkin and Paddy Moogan. **376 respondents**, fielded 5 September to 31 October 2025.
63% North America, 25% Europe. Most respondents at agencies under 50 people.
https://sparktoro.com/blog/the-2025-state-of-digital-agencies-its-still-rough-out-there-but-slowly-improving-and-is-ai-really-a-threat/
and the new-business cut published 17 February 2026:
https://sparktoro.com/blog/digital-agency-new-business-still-a-concern-referrals-still-rule-for-lead-gen-and-are-sales-pipelines-finally-recovering/

Channel findings:
- Referrals from existing and past clients: **the single largest driver by a wide margin**.
- Partner company referrals: **15%**.
- Then, in descending order: speaking at events and conferences, outbound sales, social media, paid
  networking events, industry awards.
- Of agencies that use social, **73%** rate LinkedIn the most effective channel; **20%** say social
  media is not part of their marketing at all.
- Pipeline health: only **14%** describe their pipeline as very healthy (13% in 2024); **36%** say it
  is better than a year ago (30% in 2024).
- Conversion: the largest group, **39%**, convert 25% to 49% of leads.
- **29%** say deals now take longer to close than a year ago.
- **53%** now see AI as a significant threat, up from 44% in 2024.
- **64%** expect revenue growth in the next 12 months; half grew revenue last year, 23% declined.

**Exact per-channel percentages beyond the 15% partner figure are not published in the free write-up.
UNVERIFIED.**

### Promethean Research
2025 Digital Agency Industry Report and the 2025 Referral Playbook.
https://prometheanresearch.com/2025-digital-agency-industry-report/ and
https://prometheanresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/2025-Promethean-Research-Digital-Agency-Referral-Playbook-From-Random-to-Repeatable.pdf

- **75%** of agencies cite referrals as their primary source of new business.
- The average digital agency spends **7% of revenue** on sales and marketing, salaries and media
  included.

- Promethean's own framing: referrals are fine, but agencies stall at 30 to 50 people without a

### AgencyAnalytics 2025 Marketing Agency Benchmarks
**220+ agencies.** Full report gated; only three figures are public.
https://agencyanalytics.com/p/agency-benchmarks-2025
- **68%** say paid ads now outperform SEO.
- **73%** believe AI has flipped the SEO script.
- **81%** cite client relationships as the top retention factor.

### What this means for a 100-leads-per-month inbound target

Every credible agency benchmark says the same thing: agencies do not get leads from inbound at scale.
They get them from referral and partnership. **UNVERIFIED but worth stating plainly: no source found
in this research shows a small agency generating 100+ inbound leads per month from any channel.** A
100/month inbound target is not a normal agency shape. It is a *productised or lead-gen* shape, which
is the shape the owner's own estate already runs. The realistic routes to that number are:
(a) a productised low-ticket offer with volume search behind it, or
(b) a partner/referral network where one partner supplies many leads,
not conventional agency business development.
