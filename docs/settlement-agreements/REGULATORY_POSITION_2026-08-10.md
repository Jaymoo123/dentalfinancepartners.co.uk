# Regulatory position: settlement-agreements site (employment claims / FCA CMC regime)

Date: 2026-08-10. Phase 0 regulatory gate for the settlement-agreements niche build.
Research method: primary sources only (legislation.gov.uk, FCA Handbook, SRA, Law Society), fetched 2026-08-10.

## VERDICT: CONDITIONAL (scope limits below). The build can proceed, but the estate's STANDARD per-lead sale model is NOT usable for this niche without FCA authorisation. The site must launch on an advertising/sponsorship model (boundaries in section 5), or the owner must choose one of the structural options in section 6 before any lead capture goes live.

## 1. The regime and why this niche is inside it

The Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Claims Management Activity) Order 2018 (SI 2018/1253) inserted claims management activity into the FSMA Regulated Activities Order 2001 (SI 2001/544) as articles 89G to 89W. Employment-related claims are one of the six regulated sectors (alongside personal injury, financial products, housing disrepair, specified benefits and criminal injury).

- "Employment related claim" is defined broadly: it "includes a claim in relation to wages and salaries and other employment related payments and claims in relation to wrongful or unfair dismissal, redundancy, discrimination and harassment" (RAO art 89F(2), inserted by SI 2018/1253).
- Article 89G makes the following a regulated activity when carried on by way of business in Great Britain, in relation to an employment-related claim:
  - "seeking out persons who may have a claim" (unless that activity constitutes the communication of an invitation or inducement, which is then caught by the financial promotion restriction instead, see section 4);
  - "referring details of a claim or potential claim, or a claimant or potential claimant, to another person";
  - "identifying a claim or potential claim, or a claimant or potential claimant".
- Article 89M separately regulates advice, investigation or representation in relation to an employment-related claim.
- Territorial scope (art 4 / art 2 of the 2018 Order): caught if either the firm or the claimant is in Great Britain. We are both.

Sources: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/1253/made ; https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/544/article/89G

## 2. The crux: settlement agreement sign-off vs seeking out claims

The hoped-for distinction was: an employee who has been OFFERED a settlement agreement is not pursuing a claim; they need the statutory independent advice on "terms and effect" under s.203(3) Employment Rights Act 1996 for the agreement to be valid, so referring them to a solicitor is not referring "a claim".

Finding: NO published FCA (PERG), SRA or Law Society guidance supports that distinction. Nothing in PERG, the RAO or the 2018 Order carves settlement-agreement advice or referrals out of the employment sector. On the statutory wording the referred employee is squarely a "potential claimant": the entire function of a s.203 settlement agreement is to waive potential statutory claims (unfair dismissal, discrimination, wages claims), so the person by definition may have a claim, and the details passed to a solicitor (employer, circumstances, sums offered) are details of a potential claim. Article 89G does not require that a claim ever be issued or even intended.

The distinction DOES help the advising solicitor commercially (advice on terms and effect is routine legal practice), but the solicitor never needed it: legal practitioners acting in the ordinary course of legal practice are excluded anyway (art 89N). It does not rescue an unauthorised introducer.

Position taken: assume the referral of a settlement-agreement recipient to a solicitor for payment IS regulated claims management activity (art 89G, employment sector). The contrary argument is untested, unsupported by any regulator statement found, and carrying on a regulated activity without authorisation is a criminal offence (FSMA s.19/s.23), so this is not a boundary to gamble on. Commercial reinforcement: SRA guidance tells solicitors to verify that any introducer purporting to carry on claims management activity is FCA authorised, so buyer firms would not buy unauthorised leads anyway (https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/guidance/claims-management-activity/).

## 3. Exclusions examined (and why they do not cover a dedicated lead site)

- Art 89N legal practitioners: excludes solicitors, barristers and legal executives acting in the ordinary course of legal practice. This is how the proven competitor settlementagreement.com operates: it is Settlement Agreement Solicitors Ltd, an SRA-regulated law firm (SRA 801426) advising clients directly. The "single-purpose niche site" precedent in the sweep is therefore a LAW FIRM in site clothing, not an unregulated lead generator. This materially changes the competitive read: the model is proven, but the operators are inside the 89N exclusion and we are not.
- Art 89V certain providers of referrals: excludes referrals to authorised persons or legal practitioners only if ALL of: (a) the introducer carries on no other regulated claims management activity; (b) the activity is INCIDENTAL to the introducer's main business; (c) referrals go only to authorised persons or legal practitioners; (d) the introducer is paid for NO MORE THAN 25 claims per calendar quarter; (e) data protection compliance (waived for referrals to legal practitioners). A dedicated settlement-agreements referral site fails (b) outright (referral IS the business) and (d) at any commercial scale. 89V is a safe harbour for, e.g., an accountant occasionally passing a client to a solicitor, not for us. Source: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/544/article/89V
- Arts 89O to 89U (charities, trade unions, students' unions, MIB, medical defence unions, insurance intermediaries): none applicable.

## 4. Financial promotions (the advertising route)

Where "seeking out persons who may have a claim" takes the form of an invitation or inducement, it is carved out of art 89G and handled under the FSMA s.21 financial promotion restriction instead. "Controlled claims management activity" for promotion purposes covers the same six sectors including employment (FPO 2005 Schedule 1 Part 1A; FCA Handbook glossary "controlled claims management activity", https://www.handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/glossary/G3596c.html).

- FPO art 73B (inserted by SI 2018/1253 art 36): the restriction does not apply to a communication relating to controlled claims management activity made in England and Wales BY a legal practitioner, by a firm carrying on the activity through legal practitioners, or by an individual supervised by one, provided the activity is in the ordinary course of legal practice. This is why law firms advertise settlement agreement services freely.
- The exemption says made "by" the firm, not "on behalf of". So promotional CTAs AUTHORED BY US inviting readers to use a named firm's claims-related services would be our communication, not the firm's, and would need s.21 approval by an FSMA-authorised person (law firms are not FSMA-authorised persons). The compliant pattern is: the partner firm's own advertisement (its creative, its identity, its call to action) carried on our pages, with us as publisher/conduit; plus our own content remaining informational rather than an inducement to engage a specific firm's claims services.
- Pure information and journalism is not an invitation or inducement and is outside s.21 entirely. Calculators that compute generic figures (tax on a settlement, notice pay) are information, not claims management activity, provided they do not assess an individual's case and route it to a firm.

## 5. Boundaries that keep the site OUTSIDE the regulated perimeter (the CONDITIONAL scope)

Allowed (outside the perimeter):
- Publishing information and guides on settlement agreements, tax treatment, notice pay, redundancy, tribunal process, employee rights. No restriction.
- Generic calculators (settlement tax, PENP/notice pay, redundancy comparison) with information-only disclaimers and no per-case routing.
- Carrying partner law firms' OWN advertisements (their creative and identity), including click-outs to the firm's site and the firm's phone number, paid by flat sponsorship, flat advertising fee or per-click. FPO art 73B covers the firm's promotion; we are the publisher.
- Directory-style factual listings of settlement agreement solicitors (name, location, contact), without inducement or case assessment.
- Referral fees as such are LAWFUL in employment matters on the solicitor's side: the LASPO 2012 s.56 referral fee ban is limited to personal injury and death claims (s.56(4); the Lord Chancellor's power to extend it has not been exercised for employment). Solicitors must disclose referral arrangements (SRA Codes 5.1). The bar here is FSMA authorisation of the introducer, not LASPO. Source: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2012/10/section/56
- Nurture/email content that is informational; B2B marketing to solicitors of our advertising inventory (firms are not claimants).

NOT allowed without FCA authorisation (inside the perimeter):
- Capturing an employee's details (identity, employer, dispute, settlement offer) via a form or callback and passing them to a solicitor for payment per lead. That is art 89G referral of a potential claimant. This is the estate's standard leads model and it is OFF for this site.
- "Do I have a claim?", "how strong is my case?", "am I eligible?" checkers or quizzes that assess an individual's position and route them onward (art 89G identifying claims/claimants; potentially art 89M advice).
- Tribunal-claim marketing ("sacked unfairly? we will connect you with a no-win-no-fee lawyer") and any per-claim or per-lead invoicing to firms.
- Authoring our own promotional inducements to use a specific firm's claims services (s.21 breach absent approval; FPO art 73B does not cover communications made by us).
- Holding ourselves out as advising on any individual settlement agreement.

## 6. Structural options if the owner wants the classic lead model later

1. FCA authorisation as a claims management lead generator (permission: seeking out, referrals and identification of claims or potential claims, employment sector). CMCOB conduct rules then apply (call recording, pre-contract disclosures, marketing rules). Standard route used by CMC lead firms; cost and compliance overhead, not a prohibition.
2. Operate the site as the marketing property of a single SRA-regulated partner firm (the firm is the advertiser and data controller, intake flows directly into the firm's own systems, we are paid marketing/agency fees not per claim). The seeking out is then carried on by the firm through legal practitioners (art 89N / FPO art 73B). Needs solicitor sign-off on the arrangement design before build; the line between marketing agency and introducer is where the claimant's details flow and how payment is calculated.
3. Acquire or partner with an FCA-authorised CMC. Not explored.

## 8. Notes and open items

- No FCA statement was found either including or excluding settlement-agreement sign-off referrals by name; the position in section 2 is a conservative reading of the statutory words. If the owner wants to push the contrary argument, that is a question for a regulatory solicitor, not for the build.
- gte-settlementagreements.co.uk operator CONFIRMED in the competitor corpus pass (2026-08-10): former Gordon Turner Employment Lawyers, SRA 563689. Both proven single-purpose competitors are therefore SRA-regulated law firms operating inside the art 89N exclusion; neither is a precedent for an unregulated lead-gen site.
- The FPO art 73B "made by" limitation is the sharpest compliance edge in the advertising model: partner-firm creative must genuinely be the firm's communication. Template the ad slots accordingly at build time and have the DSA/commercial terms reflect flat-fee or per-click payment only.
