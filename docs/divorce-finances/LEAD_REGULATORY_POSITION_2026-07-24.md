# Divorce / Family-Finance Lead Generation, Regulatory Position (England and Wales)

Date: 2026-07-24. Research basis: primary sources (legislation.gov.uk, sra.org.uk, fca.org.uk) fetched or search-verified on this date. Anything not confirmed against a primary source is marked UNVERIFIED.

## Verdict: PERMITTED (conditional on standard disclosures)

Generating and selling consumer leads to solicitors or mediators for family law matters (divorce, financial remedy, children arrangements) is not a regulated activity in England and Wales. There is no referral-fee ban for family law, no claims-management permission requirement, and no reserved legal activity engaged by information, calculators and warm handoffs. Conditions: consumer-law disclosure of the commercial relationship (already in place via consent checkbox and data-sharing disclosure), information-only stance on pensions, and a partner agreement that supports the receiving solicitor's SRA Code 5.1 duties.

## 1. Referral fees and SRA rules

- LASPO 2012 ss.56-60 bans referral fees only for "prescribed legal business": claims for damages for personal injury or death, plus other damages claims arising out of the same circumstances, plus anything the Lord Chancellor prescribes by regulations. Source: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2012/10/section/56 (definition confirmed by direct fetch).
- No Lord Chancellor regulations have extended "prescribed legal business" to family law. SRA guidance treats the ban as personal injury only. Source: https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/guidance/prohibition-of-referral-fees-in-laspo-56-60/ and https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/guidance/ban-personal-injury-referral-fees/. (Absence of extension regulations verified via SRA guidance and Parliament research briefing; a periodic re-check of legislation.gov.uk secondary legislation is prudent.)
- Family law referral fees are therefore lawful. The compliance burden sits with the receiving solicitor under SRA Code of Conduct for Solicitors paras 5.1-5.3 (see section 5). Source: https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/standards-regulations/code-conduct-solicitors/
- SRA Transparency Rules govern solicitors' own price/service publication (they apply to the firm, not to us). We are not SRA-regulated and the Transparency Rules impose nothing on an unregulated lead generator. UNVERIFIED nuance: none found suggesting otherwise.

## 2. Claims management (FCA)

- FCA claims-management regulation (Financial Guidance and Claims Act 2018; FSMA 2000 (Claims Management Activity) Order 2018, SI 2018/1253) covers exactly six claim sectors: personal injury; financial services or financial products; housing disrepair; specified benefits; criminal injury; employment. Sources: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/1253/part/2/made and https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/claims-management-companies-enter-fca-regulation-today
- "Seeking out, referrals and identification of claims or claimants" is a regulated activity only within those six sectors. Family law, divorce and financial remedy proceedings are not claims for damages and are not in scope. No FCA permission needed.
- Guard rail: never drift into content that generates leads for financial-product mis-selling claims (e.g. "mis-sold pension in divorce" claims angles). That WOULD be in scope of SI 2018/1253.

## 3. Adjacent regimes

- Pensions and pension sharing: providing generic information about how pension sharing orders work, CEVs, offsetting etc is not "advising on investments" under article 53 RAO, which requires advice on the merits of a particular investment (personal recommendation or specific-investment advice). Information-only stance is fine. Do not recommend transferring, taking, or choosing any specific pension product or provider; always signpost to a regulated adviser (and MoneyHelper). Source: FCA Perimeter Guidance PERG 8.24-8.25, https://www.handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/PERG/8/25.html
- Legal Services Act 2007 reserved activities (rights of audience, conduct of litigation, reserved instruments, probate activities, notarial, oaths): none are engaged by information, calculators or introductions. No issue.
- Mediation: not a reserved legal activity. The Family Mediation Council is a voluntary self-regulatory body; only FMCA-accredited mediators can conduct MIAMs and sign court forms, but there is no restriction on who may refer or introduce clients to mediators. Sources: https://www.familymediationcouncil.org.uk/faqs-about-1st-january-2016-changes/ and https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Guide-to-Family-Mediation-for-the-Courts-2024.pdf. When routing leads to mediators for MIAM purposes, route only to FMCA-accredited mediators.
- MIAM / legal aid signposting: no statutory signposting obligation applies to us (the MIAM requirement binds the court applicant, not information sites). Good practice and helpful-content alignment: state that most court applicants must attend a MIAM first, and that legal aid may be available for mediation and for domestic abuse cases. UNVERIFIED as an obligation; verified as accurate fact.
- McKenzie friend concerns: only arise from providing case assistance or courtroom support to litigants. We provide none; not applicable.

## 4. Consumer protection (DMCC Act 2024)

- The CPRs 2008 were replaced by the unfair commercial practices provisions of the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, in force from 6 April 2025, with CMA direct enforcement and fining powers (up to 10 percent of global turnover). Sources: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/part/4/chapter/1 and CMA guidance https://gov.uk/government/publications/unfair-commercial-practices-cma207/unfair-commercial-practices
- Practical requirements for the site:
  - Disclose the commercial nature of the service: state clearly that leads are passed to partner solicitors/mediators and that we may be paid for the introduction. Failing to identify a commercial intent is a misleading omission.
  - No fake reviews or fabricated social proof (now blacklisted practices under DMCC).
  - No misleading urgency or dark patterns in the handoff flow.
  - Existing mandatory consent checkbox plus data-sharing disclosure satisfies the transparency baseline; add a plain-English "we may receive a fee from the firm we introduce you to" line if not already present.
  - UK GDPR: consent-based sharing already in place estate-wide; no change needed.

## 5. Receiving solicitor's obligations (for the partner agreement)

Under SRA Code of Conduct for Solicitors 5.1-5.3 (mirrored in the Code for Firms), the receiving firm must:
- Inform the client of any financial or other interest the introducer has in referring them (i.e. that a referral fee was paid).
- Have any fee-sharing agreement in writing.
- Not pay referral fees for clients in criminal proceedings (not relevant here).
- Not allow the arrangement to compromise independence or the client's best interests (SRA Principles 3 and 7).
- 5.2: a payment is presumed to be a referral fee unless shown otherwise.
Source: https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/standards-regulations/code-conduct-solicitors/

Partner agreement should therefore state: written terms, per-lead or fixed fee stated, firm's obligation to disclose the arrangement to the client, no obligation on the firm to accept any lead, and confirmation the arrangement does not affect the advice given.

## 6. Differences vs wills-probate

- Same core position: unregulated for lead generation, no reserved activity, referral fees lawful, Code 5.1 disclosure by the receiving firm.
- New in family law: (a) claims-management perimeter is nearby, keep off financial mis-selling claim angles; (b) pensions content is heavier, so the information-not-advice line and MoneyHelper/regulated-adviser signposting matter more than on wills; (c) mediation channel exists, unregulated to refer into, but route MIAM-bound leads to FMCA-accredited mediators only; (d) MIAM and legal-aid facts should be stated for accuracy, though not legally required of us. Probate-specific risk (probate activities are a reserved activity) has no family-law equivalent.
