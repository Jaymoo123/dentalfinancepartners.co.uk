## 1. Business structures: sole trader, partnership, limited company (locked figures, DRAFT 2026-06-12)

**Position.** Three core structures for an owner-managed UK business. **Sole trader:** the individual and the business are the same legal person; profits taxed as income (income tax + Class 4 NIC, §2); unlimited personal liability; simplest to run; registers for Self Assessment. **Partnership (general):** two or more persons carrying on a business in common; **tax-transparent**, each partner taxed individually on their profit share per the profit-sharing arrangement (ITTOIA 2005 s.850, §6); unlimited liability (joint and several). **LLP:** members taxed as partners (transparent) but with limited liability; a separate legal person registered at Companies House. **Limited company:** a separate legal person; the company pays corporation tax (§3) on profit, and the owner extracts via salary and dividends (§4); limited liability; more administration (statutory accounts, confirmation statement, corporation tax return). No structure is universally best: the answer turns on profit level, liability exposure, reinvestment needs, and extraction plans.

- **Statutory hooks:** Partnership Act 1890 (general partnerships); Limited Liability Partnerships Act 2000 (LLPs); Companies Act 2006 (companies); ITTOIA 2005 Part 2 (trading income, sole traders/partners) and s.850 (partner profit allocation) https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/5/section/850 ; CTA 2009 (company profits charged to corporation tax).
- **HMRC manual anchors:** BIM (business income manual, trade computation); PM (partnership manual) PM130000+ (profit allocation).
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** lead with "structure is a trade-off, not a ranking". Always present sole trader / partnership / company as alternatives with their own tax, liability and admin profiles. State the partnership is **tax-transparent** (partners taxed individually, the firm does not pay income tax). Do NOT claim a limited company is "always more tax-efficient" (at typical owner-manager profits the saving is modest and the 2026/27 dividend rise narrows it, §4). Cross-ref §1.A (incorporation), §3 (CT), §4 (extraction).

### 1.A Incorporation: moving from sole trader to limited company (DRAFT 2026-06-12)

**Position.** Incorporating transfers the trade and its assets to a new company in exchange for shares (and possibly other consideration). Two reliefs commonly apply. **TCGA 1992 s.162 incorporation relief** defers the CGT on chargeable business assets (notably goodwill) where the **whole business is transferred as a going concern, with all its assets (other than cash), wholly or partly for shares**: the deferred gain rolls into the base cost of the shares and crystallises on a later share disposal (cash consideration restricts the relief pro rata). Alternatively, **s.165 gift holdover relief** can defer a gain on a transfer of business assets. **Goodwill amortisation relief** for the company is restricted: relief on purchased goodwill is generally unavailable for acquisitions between 8 July 2015 and 31 March 2019, and from 1 April 2019 is given at a **fixed 6.5% per year** but only where the goodwill is acquired with qualifying intellectual property and capped at 6x the qualifying-IP spend (CTA 2009 Part 8 Ch 15A). Stamp duty, VAT (transfer of a going concern, TOGC), PAYE re-registration and capital-allowance continuity (assets transferred at tax written-down value via election, or at market value) all need handling. Incorporation is rarely a pure tax win at typical owner-manager profit; weigh administration, the dividend-rate rise (§4) and the loss of simplicity.

- **Statutory hooks:** **TCGA 1992 s.162** (incorporation relief) https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/12/section/162 ; **s.165** (gift holdover relief); **CTA 2009 Part 8 Ch 15A / s.879A+** (corporate intangibles / goodwill, 6.5% restricted relief, FA 2019); VATA 1994 / VAT Notice 700/9 (TOGC); CAA 2001 s.266/s.267 (succession election, transfers at tax written-down value).
- **HMRC manual anchors:** **CG65700+** (s.162 incorporation relief); **CIRD44050+** (goodwill/relevant-assets relief, restrictions); VTOGC (transfer of a going concern).
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** present incorporation as a calculation, not a default. State s.162 defers (does not cancel) the gain into the shares. On goodwill relief use 6.5% only for post-1-April-2019 acquisitions and surface the qualifying-IP / 6x-cap condition (a blanket "buy after April 2019 and you always get 6.5%" is wrong). Flag the capital-allowance election and the TOGC point. Do NOT present incorporation as automatically tax-saving. Cross-ref §3, §4, §5 (CGT/BADR on a later sale).

## 2. Sole-trader / partnership taxation: income tax, Class 4 NIC, self-assessment (locked figures, DRAFT 2026-06-12)

**Position.** A sole trader or partner pays **income tax** on trading profit at the individual's marginal rate plus **Class 4 NIC**. **Income tax (2025/26, rUK rates):** personal allowance **£12,570** (tapered away £1 for every £2 above £100,000, gone at £125,140); basic **20%** to £50,270; higher **40%** to £125,140; additional **45%** above. (Scotland sets its own non-savings/non-dividend bands and rates, so date-and-jurisdiction tag any rate for a Scottish trader.) **Class 4 NIC (2025/26): 6%** on profits £12,570 to £50,270, then **2%** above £50,270. **Class 2 NIC** liability was **removed from 6 April 2024**: a self-employed person with profits at or above the Small Profits Threshold is treated as having paid and keeps state-pension entitlement; those below can still pay voluntary Class 2. **Basis of assessment:** the **tax-year basis** applies from 2024/25 (basis-period reform, §2.A).

- **Statutory hooks:** ITTOIA 2005 Part 2 (trading income); ITA 2007 Part 2 (income tax rates and personal allowance); **SSCBA 1992 s.15** (Class 4 NIC) and s.11 (Class 2, reformed by the National Insurance Contributions Act 2024 from 6 Apr 2024).
- **HMRC manual anchors:** BIM (trade profit computation); NIM24000+ (Class 4); self-assessment SAM.
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** use Class 4 6%/2% and the 2025/26 bands, each date-and-jurisdiction tagged. State plainly that **Class 2 is no longer payable from 6 Apr 2024** for profits above the Small Profits Threshold (do NOT tell a sole trader to pay a weekly Class 2 charge). Flag Scotland's separate income-tax bands. Cross-ref §2.A (basis-period reform), §2.B (self-assessment deadlines), §8 (expenses).

### 2.A Basis-period reform: the tax-year basis (locked, DRAFT 2026-06-12)

**Position.** From **2024/25** unincorporated businesses are taxed on the **tax-year basis**: profits for the year to 5 April (or 31 March, treated as coterminous), regardless of the accounting date. **2023/24 was the transition year**, in which a business with a non-March/April year end was taxed on its normal 12 months **plus** the "transition part" up to 5 April 2024, with **overlap relief** (from earlier years) deducted. Any net **transition profit** is **spread over five tax years (2023/24 to 2027/28)** by default, with an option to accelerate. From 2024/25 onward, a business not using a 31 March / 5 April year end must apportion two sets of accounts (or use provisional figures) to land on the tax-year basis.

- **Statutory hooks:** Finance Act 2022 Sch 1 (basis-period reform); ITTOIA 2005 Part 2 Ch 15 as amended.
- **HMRC manual anchors:** BIM81200+ (basis periods, tax-year basis, transition and overlap relief).
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** state the tax-year basis applies **from 2024/25**, 2023/24 was the transition year, and transition profit spreads **five years to 2027/28**. Recommend a 31 March / 5 April accounting date to avoid annual apportionment. Use overlap relief on the transition. Do NOT describe the old "current year basis" / prior-year rules as still operating. Cross-ref §2, §8.

### 2.B Self-assessment deadlines and payments on account (locked, DRAFT 2026-06-12)

**Position.** Self-assessment filing and payment deadlines: register by **5 October** after the end of the first tax year of trading; **paper return 31 October**; **online return and balancing payment 31 January** after the tax year. **Payments on account (POAs):** two interim payments toward the next year's liability, due **31 January** and **31 July**, **each 50% of the prior year's income-tax-plus-Class-4 liability**, required where the prior liability **exceeded £1,000 and less than 80% was collected at source**. The first self-employed year creates the well-known "double bill" (the balancing payment for year one plus the first POA, both on 31 January). Late filing: an automatic **£100** penalty, then daily and further penalties; late payment carries interest plus 5% surcharges at 30 days / 6 months / 12 months.

- **Statutory hooks:** **TMA 1970 s.59A** (payments on account), s.59B (balancing payment), s.7/s.8 (notice to file); FA 2009 Sch 55 (late-filing penalties) and Sch 56 (late-payment penalties).
- **HMRC manual anchors:** SAM (self-assessment manual); payments on account SAM1000+.
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** state the 31 Jan / 31 July POA dates and the 50%-of-prior-year mechanic, the £1,000 / 80%-at-source trigger, and the first-year "double bill". Use the £100 late-filing penalty. Cross-ref §2, §9 (MTD changes the in-year obligations from April 2026).

## 3. Corporation tax: rates and marginal relief (locked figures, DRAFT 2026-06-12)

**Position.** A company pays **corporation tax** on its taxable profit. **Small profits rate 19%** where augmented profits do not exceed the **lower limit £50,000**; **main rate 25%** where they exceed the **upper limit £250,000**; **marginal relief** tapers the effective rate between £50,000 and £250,000 (FY2025 and FY2026). The limits are **divided by the number of associated companies** and **time-apportioned** for short periods. Marginal relief uses the standard fraction (3/200). The effective marginal rate on profits in the £50,000 to £250,000 band is **26.5%** (the slice between the limits is taxed at the margin to blend 19% up to 25%). The corporation tax return (CT600) and payment are due **9 months and 1 day** after the period end (large companies pay by quarterly instalments).

- **Statutory hooks:** **CTA 2010 Part 3** (ss.18 to 19 small profits rate and marginal relief; associated-company and limit-apportionment rules); CTA 2009 (charge to CT on income and the loan-relationship/intangibles regimes).
- **Authority anchors:** https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/rates-and-allowances-corporation-tax/rates-and-allowances-corporation-tax (19%/25%, £50k/£250k, marginal relief, FY2025 and FY2026).
- **HMRC manual anchors:** CTM01000+ (charge and rates); marginal relief CTM03900+; associated companies CTM03940+.
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** use 19% / 25% with the £50,000 / £250,000 limits and note marginal relief between them (effective ~26.5% in the band). Always flag that the limits are **shared between associated companies** and time-apportioned (a common trap for owner-managers with two companies). State CT is due **9 months and 1 day** after the period end. Do NOT state a single flat CT rate. Cross-ref §4 (extraction), §7 (capital allowances reduce taxable profit).

### 3.A Corporation tax payment timing: credit interest and quarterly instalments (locked, 2026-07-09, Wave 4 gate, verified gov.uk/CTM)

**Position.** A company that pays its CT **early** earns HMRC **credit/repayment interest at 2.75% (base minus 1%, from 9 Jan 2026)** from the payment date to the normal due date; late payment costs **7.75% (base + 4%)**. **Quarterly instalment payments (QIPs)** apply to **large** companies (augmented profits > £1.5m, divided by associated companies + 1, prorated for short APs; SI 1998/3175) on the 6m13d/9m13d/12m13d/3m14d-after-end schedule, and **very large** companies (> £20m) on the 14th of months 3/6/9/12 of the AP; QIP-specific rates are **3.50% credit / 6.25% debit from 29 Dec 2025**. Growth relief: not large in the preceding 12 months and profits <= £10m; de minimis: CT liability <= £5,000. Rates float with base rate (3.75% as of Jan 2026) - date-tag on every use and fetch current figures at write time.

## 4. Profit extraction: salary, dividends and the employer-NIC position (locked figures, DRAFT 2026-06-12)

**Position.** A director-shareholder of an owner-managed company extracts profit through a mix of **salary** (deductible for the company, taxable as employment income, subject to NIC), **dividends** (paid from post-tax profit, no NIC, taxed at dividend rates) and **employer pension contributions** (§10). The usual owner-manager pattern is a **modest salary plus dividends**, with the salary set either at the secondary threshold or at the personal allowance depending on whether the company can claim the Employment Allowance (see the employer-NIC block below).

**Corporation tax on the profit first:** §3 (19% / 25% / marginal relief).

**Dividend tax:**
- 2025/26: ordinary **8.75%**, upper **33.75%**, additional **39.35%**; **dividend allowance £500**.
- From **6 April 2026 (FA 2026 s.4):** ordinary **10.75%**, upper **35.75%**; additional **39.35%** unchanged; allowance **£500**. **Tag the tax year on every dividend figure.**

**Employer (secondary Class 1) NIC, the resolved position (verified gov.uk 2026-06-12):** the company pays employer NIC at **15%** on salary above the **secondary threshold of £5,000 a year (£96/week)**, with effect **from 6 April 2025** (the Autumn 2024 Budget cut the threshold to £5,000 and raised the rate to 15%). **This supersedes the old 13.8% rate and £9,100 threshold, which applied up to 5 April 2025 and must NOT be used for 2025/26 onward.** The **Lower Earnings Limit is £6,500/year (£125/week)** for 2025/26, preserving the director's state-pension entitlement even where little NIC is due. The **Employment Allowance is £10,500** but is **not available to a company whose only employee is a single director**: that is why a one-director company often sets salary at the secondary threshold (£5,000), while a company with at least one genuinely employed non-director (for example a genuinely employed spouse on a market-rate wage) can claim the £10,500 allowance and lift the director's salary to the personal allowance (£12,570) more efficiently.

> ESTATE-WIDE NOTE (prominent). The corpus warning that pages may state employer NIC at **13.8% / £9,100** is a stale-corpus artefact for any period from 6 April 2025. The verified law for 2025/26 (and continuing into 2026/27) is **15% above a £5,000 secondary threshold**. Every generalist salary/dividend/extraction page, every "optimal director's salary" page, and every "cost of employing staff" page (§9) must use 15% / £5,000 and must NOT use 13.8% / £9,100 except as a clearly date-tagged historic figure (to 5 April 2025). Treat any 13.8%/£9,100 token in a live page as a corrective-rewrite hit.

**Optimal-salary logic (2025/26):** with no Employment Allowance (single-director company), a salary at the **secondary threshold (£5,000)** avoids employer NIC entirely; some set it at the **personal allowance (£12,570)** accepting employer NIC of 15% on the £7,570 band (about £1,135) because the salary is corporation-tax-deductible and the director still has personal allowance to use. With the Employment Allowance available (a genuine second employee), a £12,570 salary is usually optimal because the EA absorbs the employer NIC. Always model both; do not assert a single "best salary" figure without stating the EA assumption.

**Employer pension contributions** are a tax-efficient extraction route: deductible for corporation tax on a **paid basis** (FA 2004 s.196, wholly and exclusively), with **no NIC**, subject to the §10 annual allowance and carry-forward.

- **Statutory hooks:** **CTA 2010 Part 3** (CT rates, §3); **ITA 2007 s.8** (dividend rates, amended by **FA 2026 s.4** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/11/section/4 ); ITTOIA 2005 Part 4 Ch 3 (dividend income); **SSCBA 1992** secondary Class 1 NIC (15% above £5,000 from 6 Apr 2025); National Insurance Contributions Act 2014 (Employment Allowance £10,500, single-director restriction); ITEPA 2003 (employment income, salary); **FA 2004 s.196** (employer pension deduction).
- **Authority anchors:** https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rates-and-thresholds-for-employers-2025-to-2026 (employer NIC 15% / £5,000, EA £10,500, LEL £6,500); https://www.gov.uk/tax-on-dividends (dividend rates + £500 allowance).

- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** never state a dividend rate without its tax-year tag (from 6 Apr 2026 use 10.75% / 35.75%). Always use employer NIC **15% / £5,000 secondary threshold (from 6 Apr 2025)** and **never 13.8% / £9,100** for current advice. State the **Employment Allowance £10,500 single-director exclusion** explicitly when discussing optimal salary. Spouse salary must be **genuine and market-rate** (HMRC disallows illusory/above-market pay). Do NOT present a single "optimal salary" without the EA assumption. Cross-ref §3, §4.A (director's loans), §9 (employing staff), §10 (pensions), §5 (CGT/BADR on exit).

### 4.A Director's loan accounts and the s.455 charge (locked, DRAFT 2026-06-12)

**Position.** An owner-managed company is normally a **close company**. An **overdrawn director's loan account** (drawings ahead of declared salary or dividend) triggers a **CTA 2010 s.455 charge**: the company pays an amount equal to a percentage of the loan outstanding **9 months and 1 day** after the period end, at a rate equal to the **dividend upper rate** for the year the loan is made, that is **33.75% for loans made in 2025/26** and **35.75% for loans made on or after 6 April 2026** (the rate tracks ITA 2007 s.8 as amended by FA 2026 s.4). The charge is **temporary**: **s.458 relief** repays it when the loan is repaid, released or written off, but the relief is **deferred to 9 months and 1 day after the end of the accounting period in which repayment occurs** (4-year claim window). Anti-avoidance blocks repay-and-redraw: the **30-day rule (s.464ZA)** and the **arrangements rule (s.464C/464D)** for balances of £15,000 or more. A loan **over £10,000** at any point in the year is also a **beneficial-loan benefit in kind** (ITEPA 2003 ss.173 to 191) unless interest is paid at the **official rate**, reportable on P11D with **Class 1A NIC at 15%** (the Class 1A rate aligns with the secondary Class 1 rate, so 15% from 6 Apr 2025).

- **Statutory hooks:** **CTA 2010 s.455** (charge on loans to participators, rate = dividend upper rate) https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/4/section/455 ; **s.458** (relief on repayment); **s.464ZA** (30-day rule), **s.464C / s.464D** (£15,000 arrangements rule); **ITEPA 2003 ss.173 to 191** (beneficial loans, £10,000 threshold, official rate).
- **HMRC manual anchors:** **CTM61500+ / CTM61630** (loans to participators, s.455/s.458, bed-and-breakfasting); **EIM26100+** (beneficial loans, official rate).
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** state the s.455 rate **with its date band** (33.75% on pre-6-Apr-2026 loans, 35.75% on/after), the 9-months-and-1-day timing, and that s.458 relief is **deferred**, not immediate. Flag the £10,000 BIK threshold. Do NOT describe s.455 as a permanent tax (it is repayable) or imply the relief is instant. Cross-ref §4.

## 5. Capital gains tax and Business Asset Disposal Relief (locked figures, DRAFT 2026-06-12)

**Position.** **CGT for individuals** on chargeable assets: from **30 October 2024** the main rates are **18%** (gains within the basic-rate band) and **24%** (gains above it), applied to **all chargeable assets** including residential property (the previously higher 28% / 24% residential rates were aligned). **Annual exempt amount £3,000** (2025/26 and 2026/27). Gains are reported through Self Assessment; UK residential-property gains have a separate **60-day** reporting-and-payment obligation.

**Business Asset Disposal Relief (BADR):** a reduced CGT rate on qualifying business disposals up to a **£1,000,000 lifetime limit per individual** (fixed since 11 March 2020, not indexed). Rate: **10%** to 5 Apr 2025; **14%** for disposals 6 Apr 2025 to 5 Apr 2026; **18%** from 6 Apr 2026. Conditions held throughout the **2-year period to disposal**: a trading business (sole trader/partnership disposing of the business or a qualifying asset on cessation) or, for a **share sale**, a **personal company** holding (at least **5% of ordinary share capital and 5% of voting rights**, plus a 5% economic entitlement) with the seller an **officer or employee** throughout, and the company trading.

**Asset sale vs share sale (a core owner-manager fork).** On a **share sale** the seller disposes of shares (one CGT disposal, BADR potentially available, buyer takes the company with its history and latent liabilities). On an **asset (trade and assets) sale** the **company** sells its assets (corporation tax on any gains/balancing charges inside the company), then the owner extracts the net proceeds (a second tax layer on extraction, or on a later liquidation where a **Members' Voluntary Liquidation** can deliver a capital distribution within BADR, subject to the Targeted Anti-Avoidance Rule on phoenixism). Buyers usually prefer assets (clean, step-up, no inherited liabilities); sellers usually prefer shares (single CGT layer, BADR). The structure drives very different tax, so model both.

- **Statutory hooks:** **TCGA 1992** s.1H and s.4 (CGT rates 18%/24% from 30 Oct 2024), s.1K (annual exempt amount); **ss.169H to 169S** (BADR) https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/12 ; CTA 2010 s.1030A and the TAAR (capital treatment on a distribution in a winding up) for MVLs.
- **Authority anchors:** https://www.gov.uk/business-asset-disposal-relief (10%/14%/18% schedule); https://www.gov.uk/capital-gains-tax/rates (18%/24%, AEA £3,000).
- **HMRC manual anchors:** **CG63950+** (BADR); CG10000+ (CGT general); CTM36300+ (distributions in a winding up / phoenix TAAR).
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** state the BADR rate **with its exact date band** every time (the 6 Apr 2026 step to 18% is a live planning lever for late-2025/26 completions). Use CGT **18%/24%** and AEA **£3,000**, both date-tagged. Always separate **asset sale** from **share sale** and note the double tax layer on an asset sale. Flag the **60-day** residential-property reporting rule. Do NOT state BADR is still 10%, and do NOT use the old 28% residential CGT rate. Cross-ref §1.A (s.162 to access BADR on a later share sale), §4 (extraction), §6 (partnership changes).

## 6. Partnerships and partnership changes (locked, DRAFT 2026-06-12)

**Position.** A partnership (and an LLP taxed as a partnership) is **tax-transparent**: the firm files a partnership return but does not pay income tax; each partner is taxed individually on their **profit share per the profit-sharing arrangement for the period** (**ITTOIA 2005 s.850**), and losses are allocated the same way. On a **change of partners** (an incoming equity partner buying in, or a partner retiring), three things move at once and must be kept separate: (1) the **income-tax** profit-share allocation changes from the date of change; (2) any **goodwill** payment is a **CGT** matter (a change in a partner's fractional share of the firm's chargeable assets is a part-disposal under **HMRC Statement of Practice D12**; where an incomer pays existing partners for goodwill outside the accounts, that is consideration for a disposal, potentially within BADR for the receiving partner if their §5 conditions are met; a no-payment reallocation at book value is typically no-gain/no-loss); and (3) **SDLT** arises only where the **partnership holds a chargeable interest in land**, in which case a transfer of a partnership interest is a land transaction under **FA 2003 Sch 15 para 14** with chargeable consideration by the **"sum of the lower proportions"** (Sch 15 para 20), which is fact-specific and can compute to a low or nil charge among connected partners. **LLP salaried-member rules** (ITTOIA 2005 s.863A onward) can re-characterise a "fixed-share" member as an employee for tax if the three conditions (disguised salary, no significant influence, no significant capital contribution) are all met.

- **Statutory hooks:** **ITTOIA 2005 s.850** (profit allocation, transparency) https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/5/section/850 ; s.863A+ (salaried members of LLPs); **HMRC SP D12** (partnerships and CGT); **FA 2003 Sch 15** paras 10/14/18/20 (SDLT on partnership land, transfer of interest, SLP); ITA 2007 s.398 (qualifying loan interest to buy into a partnership).
- **HMRC manual anchors:** **PM130000+** (profit allocation), PM250000+ (salaried members); **CG27000+ / CG27170** (SP D12 share changes); **SDLTM33000+ / SDLTM33730 / SDLTM33750** (Sch 15, para 14/20); BIM72000+ (changes in partners).
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** lead with "the partnership is tax-transparent; each partner is taxed on their profit share (s.850)". On a buy-in/buy-out separate the **three** strands (income-tax allocation, CGT on goodwill per SP D12, SDLT only if the firm holds land). Keep SDLT and BADR availability **hedged and fact-specific** (never a flat SDLT figure). Flag the **LLP salaried-member** trap for fixed-share members. Cross-ref §1 (structures), §5 (BADR), §8 (loss reliefs for a new venture).

## 7. VAT (locked figures, DRAFT 2026-06-12)

**Position.** A business must register for VAT when **taxable turnover exceeds £90,000** in any rolling 12 months, or is expected to exceed £90,000 in the next 30 days (raised from £85,000 on 1 April 2024). Voluntary registration below the threshold is possible (to reclaim input VAT). The **deregistration threshold is £88,000**. Standard rate **20%**; reduced **5%**; zero-rated and exempt categories apply per VATA 1994. **Making Tax Digital for VAT** has applied to **all VAT-registered businesses since April 2022** (digital records and MTD-compatible software for returns), regardless of turnover.

**Cash accounting scheme:** account for VAT on **payments received and made** rather than invoice dates (helps cash flow and gives automatic bad-debt relief); join if taxable turnover is **£1.35 million or less**, leave when it exceeds **£1.6 million**.

**Flat Rate Scheme (FRS):** pay a single flat percentage of **VAT-inclusive** turnover instead of tracking input VAT; join if expected taxable turnover is **£150,000 or less (ex VAT)**, with a **1% discount in the first year** of VAT registration. **Limited cost business (limited cost trader):** if goods cost **less than 2% of turnover, or less than £1,000 a year**, the flat rate is the high **16.5%**, which usually makes the FRS unattractive for labour-only/service businesses (most contractors are limited cost businesses).

- **Statutory hooks:** **VATA 1994** (charge, Sch 1 registration/deregistration thresholds); Value Added Tax Regulations 1995 (SI 1995/2518) (cash accounting Part VIII, flat rate Part VIIA); MTD for VAT under the VAT (Amendment) Regs.
- **Authority anchors:** https://www.gov.uk/vat-registration/when-to-register (£90,000); https://www.gov.uk/vat-flat-rate-scheme/eligibility (£150,000) and /how-much-you-pay (16.5% limited cost, 1% first-year discount).
- **HMRC manual anchors:** VATREG (registration); FRS7000+ (flat rate, limited cost trader); VCAS (cash accounting).
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** use **£90,000** registration / **£88,000** deregistration and never the old £85,000 for current advice. State **MTD for VAT applies to all VAT-registered businesses since April 2022**. For the FRS always flag the **16.5% limited cost business** rate and its 2%/£1,000 goods test (the reason most service contractors should not use the FRS). Use cash-accounting £1.35m join / £1.6m leave. Cross-ref §9 (MTD ITSA is separate, income tax not VAT).

## 8. Capital allowances (locked figures, DRAFT 2026-06-12)

**Position.** Capital allowances give tax relief on qualifying capital spend (plant and machinery, integral features), replacing non-deductible depreciation. **Annual Investment Allowance (AIA) £1,000,000 per 12-month period** (permanent), 100% relief on most plant and machinery, available to companies and unincorporated businesses, time-apportioned for short periods and **not carried forward** (use-it-or-lose-it). **Main-rate writing-down allowance: 18%, reducing to 14% from 1 April 2026 (CT) / 6 April 2026 (IT)** per **FA 2026 s.28** (a straddling period uses a hybrid time-apportioned rate). **Special-rate pool: 6%** WDA (integral features: electrical, cold/hot water, heating, lighting, air and ventilation systems, plus long-life assets), **unchanged**. **New 40% first-year allowance** on **new and unused** main-rate plant and machinery (not second-hand, not cars) **from 1 January 2026** per **FA 2026 s.29**, available to companies and unincorporated businesses. **Full expensing (companies only): 100% FYA** on new main-rate plant and **50% FYA** on new special-rate plant, permanent. **Cars** are excluded from AIA and the FYAs; relief is by WDA at 18% / 6% by CO2 emissions (or 100% FYA for new zero-emission cars). **Structures and Buildings Allowance** at **3% per year** on qualifying construction (not land, not pooled plant).

- **Statutory hooks:** **CAA 2001** s.38A/s.51A (AIA), **s.56** (main-rate WDA, 18% to 14% per FA 2026 s.28 https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/11/section/28 ), ss.104A to 104D (special-rate 6%), **ss.45U/45V** (40% FYA per FA 2026 s.29 https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/11/section/29 ), s.39/s.45S (full expensing), Part 2A (SBA 3%); s.198 (fixtures election on a property purchase, 2-year limit s.201).
- **HMRC manual anchors:** CA23080+ (AIA); CA22300+ (integral features); CA23150+ (full expensing); CA26800+ (fixtures elections); CA90000+ (SBA).
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** use AIA £1m; **date-tag the main-rate WDA every time** (18% to Mar/Apr 2026, then 14%; FA 2026 s.28); special rate 6%. For a **new** main-rate asset compare AIA (100%) with the 40% FYA (which leaves the balance in the pool) and note **full expensing is companies-only**. Do NOT claim AIA or the FYAs on cars. Do NOT state a flat 18% WDA for periods after April 2026. Cross-ref §3 (reduces CT profit), §2 (reduces trading profit), §5 (balancing charges on a sale).

## 9. Employing staff: PAYE/RTI, employer cost and auto-enrolment (locked figures, DRAFT 2026-06-12)

**Position.** Taking on an employee creates three connected obligations and a **loaded cost well above the headline salary**: **PAYE/RTI payroll**, the **employer cost of the wage**, and **pension auto-enrolment**.

- **PAYE and Real Time Information (RTI).** Operate PAYE (deduct income tax and employee Class 1 NIC), report **on or before each payday** via a Full Payment Submission, and pay HMRC monthly (quarterly for small employers). A first-time employer registers as an employer with HMRC. Late RTI filing carries monthly penalties scaled by headcount: **£100 (1 to 9 employees), £200 (10 to 49), £300 (50 to 249), £400 (250+)**.
- **Employer (secondary Class 1) NIC.** **15% above the secondary threshold of £5,000 a year, from 6 April 2025** (per §4; not the old 13.8% / £9,100). The **Employment Allowance £10,500** offsets employer NIC for a business with genuine non-director staff (not a single-director company). The **LEL £6,500/year** preserves the employee's contributory entitlement.
- **Pension auto-enrolment.** Automatically enrol eligible jobholders (aged 22 to State Pension Age, earning over the **earnings trigger £10,000**, 2025/26) into a qualifying scheme. Contributions are on **qualifying earnings** (the band **£6,240 to £50,270**, 2025/26): **minimum total 8%**, of which the **employer pays at least 3%** and the employee the balance (**5%**, including tax relief). Re-enrolment every ~3 years; a declaration of compliance is due. The employer contribution is **deductible** and carries **no NIC**.
- **The real cost of a hire.** Salary **plus** employer NIC (15% above £5,000, net of any Employment Allowance) **plus** employer pension (3% minimum of qualifying earnings) **plus** payroll running cost **plus** on-costs (holiday pay, statutory sick/maternity exposure). **Employer's liability insurance is compulsory.**

- **Statutory hooks:** Income Tax (PAYE) Regulations 2003 (SI 2003/2682) + ITEPA 2003 Part 11 (PAYE/RTI); FA 2009 Sch 55 (RTI late-filing penalties); SSCBA 1992 secondary Class 1 (15% above £5,000 from 6 Apr 2025); National Insurance Contributions Act 2014 (Employment Allowance £10,500, single-director restriction); **Pensions Act 2008 Part 1** (auto-enrolment); Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969.
- **Authority anchors:** https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rates-and-thresholds-for-employers-2025-to-2026 (15% / £5,000, EA £10,500, LEL £6,500); The Pensions Regulator employer-duties + thresholds.
- **HMRC manual anchors:** PAYE/RTI PAYE5000+; NIM06000+ / NIM24175+ (secondary NIC, Employment Allowance); auto-enrolment via The Pensions Regulator.
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** always build the **loaded cost** (salary + 15% employer NIC above £5,000 + 3% employer pension + payroll + on-costs), not the salary alone. Use the **2025/26** auto-enrolment figures (trigger £10,000, band £6,240 to £50,270, 8% / 3% / 5%) and date-tag them. Use the RTI penalty bands (£100/£200/£300/£400). **Do NOT state employer NIC at 13.8% / £9,100** (now 15% / £5,000). Cross-ref §4 (employer NIC, EA), §9.A (trivial benefits).

### 9.A Trivial benefits and tax-efficient staff perks (locked, DRAFT 2026-06-12)

**Position.** Small perks are exempt from tax and NIC under the **trivial benefits exemption (ITEPA 2003 s.323A)** where **all four** conditions are met: (1) cost **£50 or less** per benefit (a cliff edge: over £50 and the **whole** amount is taxable); (2) not cash or a cash voucher (a store gift card is fine); (3) not a reward for services or performance; (4) not contractual or under salary sacrifice. For a **director or office-holder of a close company** (most owner-managed companies), an **annual cap of £300** applies (s.323B), still subject to the £50-per-benefit limit. Separately, an **annual staff event** (for example a Christmas party) is exempt up to **£150 per head per year** (s.264, all-or-nothing per head), and one **mobile phone** per employee is exempt (s.319). The genuinely large tax-efficient lever remains **employer pension contributions** (§4, §10).

- **Statutory hooks:** **ITEPA 2003 s.323A** (trivial benefits) https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2003/1/section/323A ; **s.323B** (£300 close-company director cap); **s.264** (annual function £150/head); **s.319** (mobile phone).
- **HMRC manual anchors:** EIM21864+ (trivial benefits); EIM21690+ (annual parties).
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** state all four trivial-benefit conditions and stress the £50 cliff edge. Flag the **£300 annual cap** for the owner-director (close company). Keep the **£150** staff-event limit separate. Do NOT call a cash bonus or contractual perk a trivial benefit, and do NOT say trivial benefits are unlimited for a director. Cross-ref §4, §9.

## 10. Pensions: annual allowance and tapering (locked figures, DRAFT 2026-06-12)

**Position.** The pension **annual allowance is £60,000** (2025/26) for total tax-relieved pension input (employer plus personal, gross). It **tapers** where **threshold income exceeds £200,000 AND adjusted income exceeds £260,000**, reducing by **£1 for every £2** of adjusted income above £260,000, down to a **minimum tapered allowance of £10,000**. The **money purchase annual allowance is £10,000** (triggered by flexibly accessing a DC pension). **Unused allowance carries forward three tax years** (current year used first). Tax relief on personal contributions is limited to the higher of **100% of relevant UK earnings** or **£3,600 gross** (the £2,880 net non-earner floor). For an owner-manager, **employer (company) pension contributions** are the efficient route: corporation-tax-deductible on a paid basis (FA 2004 s.196), no NIC, and not limited by the individual's relevant earnings (only by the annual allowance and the wholly-and-exclusively test).

- **Statutory hooks:** **Finance Act 2004 Part 4** (annual allowance ss.227 to 228ZA; tapered annual allowance s.228ZA; MPAA; carry-forward); s.196 (employer contribution CT deduction, paid basis).
- **Authority anchors:** https://www.gov.uk/tax-on-your-private-pension/annual-allowance (£60,000; taper at threshold income £200,000 / adjusted income £260,000).
- **HMRC manual anchors:** PTM050000+ (annual allowance); PTM056000+ (taper); PTM055000+ (MPAA).
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** use £60,000 / £200,000 threshold income / £260,000 adjusted income / £10,000 floor, each tagged 2025/26. For owner-managers, route large contributions through the **company** (deductible, no NIC, not earnings-capped) and check the annual allowance and carry-forward. Do NOT use the old £40,000 allowance or the pre-2023 £240,000 adjusted-income threshold. Cross-ref §4, §9.

## 12. Mileage and travel (locked figures, DRAFT 2026-06-12)

**Position.** **Approved Mileage Allowance Payments (AMAP)** let a business pay (or an employee/sole trader claim) a tax-free per-mile rate for business use of a personal car or van: **55p for the first 10,000 business miles in the tax year, then 25p, from 2026/27 (6 April 2026)** (the first-10,000 rate rose from **45p to 55p**; 45p applied for 2011/12 to 2025/26). Motorcycles 24p; an extra 5p per passenger. **Ordinary commuting (home to a permanent workplace) is not business travel** and is not claimable. The **24-month rule** (ITEPA 2003 s.339): a workplace is **temporary** only while the employee expects to attend it for **24 months or less and for less than 40% of working time**; once attendance is expected to exceed 24 months (or 40% of time), it becomes a **permanent workplace** and travel to it becomes non-deductible commuting. Sole traders deduct genuine business mileage (the AMAP rates, or actual costs apportioned) under the wholly-and-exclusively rule.

- **Statutory hooks:** **ITEPA 2003 ss.229 to 236** (approved mileage allowance payments / mileage allowance relief); **s.338 / s.339** (travel for necessary attendance; permanent vs temporary workplace, 24-month rule); ITTOIA 2005 s.34 (sole-trader wholly-and-exclusively).
- **Authority anchors:** https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/rates-and-allowances-travel-mileage-and-fuel-allowances/travel-mileage-and-fuel-rates-and-allowances (55p/25p from 2026/27; 45p prior).
- **HMRC manual anchors:** EIM31200+ (AMAP); EIM32000+ (travel, temporary workplace, 24-month rule).
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** use **55p/25p (from 6 April 2026)** and date-tag it; use **45p** only for 2025/26 and earlier (clearly tagged). State the **home-to-permanent-workplace commuting** rule and the **24-month / 40%** temporary-workplace test. Do NOT treat ordinary commuting as deductible. ## 13. IR35 and off-payroll working (locked, DRAFT 2026-06-12)

**Position.** A contractor working through a **personal service company (PSC)** is within the **intermediaries legislation (IR35)**. For engagements with **medium or large clients** (private or public sector), the **off-payroll working rules in Chapter 10, Part 2 ITEPA 2003** apply: from **6 April 2021** the **client (or the fee-payer in the chain), not the PSC, determines status** and must issue a **Status Determination Statement (SDS)** with reasons, taking reasonable care. Inside IR35: the fee-payer deducts PAYE and NIC before paying the PSC (so tax-efficient salary/dividend extraction of that income is not available); outside IR35: the PSC is paid gross and extracts normally (§4). For **small clients** (broadly meeting two of: turnover not over £15m, balance sheet not over £7.5m, not over 50 employees, the Companies Act 2006 small-company test, uplifted in 2025), the **original Chapter 8 IR35 rules** apply and the **PSC itself determines its status**. Status rests on case-law factors (control, personal service/substitution, mutuality of obligation, financial risk, integration), assessed via HMRC's **CEST** tool and the Employment Status Manual; the contract label does not govern.

- **Statutory hooks:** **ITEPA 2003 Chapter 10, Part 2** (off-payroll, client-led, from 6 Apr 2021) and **Chapter 8, Part 2** (original IR35, small clients) https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2003/1 ; small-company size test in the Companies Act 2006.
- **HMRC manual anchors:** **ESM10000+** (off-payroll working); ESM0500+ (employment status factors); ESM11000+ (CEST).
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** for medium/large clients say the **SDS sits with the client**, not the contractor, with a client-led disagreement process; for **small clients** the **PSC decides its own status** (Chapter 8). State status is substance over the contract label and walk the factors. Do NOT write that "the contractor always decides their own IR35 status" (that is only the small-client/pre-April-2021 position). Cross-ref §4 (extraction blocked when inside), §12 (travel rules differ inside/outside).

## 14. Creative-industry reliefs: AVEC, VGEC, theatre and orchestra (locked figures, DRAFT 2026-08-25, creative_performers gate)

**Position.** The creative-sector reliefs are corporation tax reliefs claimed by the **production or development company**, not by individual performers. **Audio-Visual Expenditure Credit (AVEC)**, for expenditure from **1 January 2024**: **34%** expenditure credit for films and high-end TV; **39%** for animated films/TV and children's TV; from **1 April 2025** a **53% Independent Film Tax Credit** (principal photography on or after 1 April 2024, core costs under £23.5m, relief capped at £15m of core costs) and a **39% rate on visual-effects costs** for 34%-rate productions (VFX costs exempt from the 80% cap). **Video Games Expenditure Credit (VGEC)**: **34%** on qualifying expenditure from 1 January 2024; new productions must use VGEC from 1 April 2025 and all from 1 April 2027 (old VGTR ends). For both, qualifying expenditure is the **lower of 80% of total core costs or the UK core costs**, with a minimum **10% of core costs on UK activities**, and BFI cultural certification is required. The expenditure credit is itself **taxable** (RDEC-style mechanics), so the headline rate overstates the net benefit. **Theatre Tax Relief and Orchestra Tax Relief**: rates are **permanently 40% (non-touring) / 45% (touring productions and orchestral concerts) from 1 April 2025** (the planned taper to 35%/30% was scrapped, Spring Budget 2024); from 1 April 2025 eligible expenditure narrows to goods and services **used or consumed in the UK** (previously UK/EEA), with split calculations for straddling periods. **Scope fence (niche-map row 43, LOCKED):** Film & TV production is earmarked for its own future standalone site. The generalist covers these reliefs at **overview/informational level only** (what exists, headline rates, who claims), plus the position of **individual creatives** working on qualifying productions. It does NOT build production-company service pages, claim-preparation guidance, or company-level advisory depth; that ground stays clear so the row-43 build lands later with zero content overlap.

- **Statutory hooks:** CTA 2009 **Part 14A** (AVEC/VGEC expenditure credits, FA 2024); CTA 2009 **Part 15C** (theatre) and **Part 15D** (orchestra), rates amended to permanent 40%/45%.
- **Authority anchors:** https://www.gov.uk/guidance/claim-audio-visual-expenditure-credits-for-corporation-tax (34%/39%/53%, VFX 39%, 80% cap, 10% UK minimum); https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/corporation-tax-new-permanent-rates-of-relief-for-theatre-orchestra-museum-and-galleries-tax-relief/permanent-40-and-45-rates-for-theatre-orchestra-museum-and-galleries-tax-reliefs (40%/45% permanent from 1 Apr 2025).

## 15. Royalties: income tax and VAT for musicians, writers and their companies (locked, DRAFT 2026-08-25, creative_performers gate)

**Position.** **Income tax.** For a working musician, songwriter or author, royalties (PRS, PPL, MCPS, publishing, streaming, sync) are normally **receipts of their profession** and taxed as trading income under ITTOIA 2005 Part 2 (§2 rates, Class 4 NIC), on the tax-year basis (§2.A), with the trading allowance interaction per §17 where tiny. Where the recipient is **not carrying on a profession** (for example an heir or an investor holding a copyright), royalties are charged as **income from intellectual property under ITTOIA 2005 s.579** (Part 5, miscellaneous income, no Class 4). Royalties received **after the profession ceases** are post-cessation receipts (ITTOIA 2005 Part 2 Ch 18). A **company** holding the rights pays corporation tax under the intangibles regime (CTA 2009 Part 8). **Overseas royalties** commonly arrive with **foreign withholding tax deducted**; a UK resident is taxable on the gross and claims **treaty relief or foreign tax credit relief** (rate per the relevant double tax agreement, never assume zero). Mirror rule: a UK payer of IP royalties to an owner whose usual place of abode is **outside the UK** must **deduct basic-rate (20%) income tax at source** (ITA 2007 s.906), subject to treaty clearance. **VAT.** Royalty income is consideration for a **standard-rated (20%) supply of services** (licensing of rights) where the place of supply is the UK, and it **counts toward the £90,000 registration threshold** (§7). Royalties from overseas licensees are generally **outside the scope** under the place-of-supply rules (B2B: where the customer belongs). PRS for Music and PPL operate **VAT self-billing** for VAT-registered members: the society adds VAT to the royalty statement once notified, and the member must account for that output VAT. Tax point for royalties follows the special rules in VATTOS9500 (broadly, when payment is received or the amount is ascertainable).

- **Statutory hooks:** ITTOIA 2005 Part 2 (professional income), Part 2 Ch 18 (post-cessation receipts), **s.579** (royalties and other income from intellectual property) https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/5/section/579 ; **ITA 2007 s.906** (deduction at basic rate from IP royalties paid to owners abroad) https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2007/3/section/906 ; TIOPA 2010 (treaty relief / FTCR); CTA 2009 Part 8 (company IP); VATA 1994 (standard rating, place of supply of services).
- **HMRC manual anchors:** BIM50605+ (authors, composers, royalties as professional income); INTM630000+ (royalty withholding, incl. s.917A anti-treaty-shopping from 17 Mar 2016); **VATTOS9500** (VAT tax points for royalties and repeat fees); VAT self-billing per Notice 700/62.
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** for a working creative, royalties are **trading income of the profession**, not investment income; use s.579 only for non-professional holders. Always flag that **UK-source royalty income is VATable and counts toward the £90,000 threshold** (a musician with modest gig income plus growing royalties can cross it unnoticed), and that PRS/PPL self-billing does not remove the member's duty to account for the VAT. On overseas royalties, hedge to "treaty rate applies, check the DTA", never assert a specific foreign rate. Do NOT say royalties are always taxed the same way regardless of who receives them. Cross-ref §2, §7, §17.
- **Open question (not asserted on pages):** the exact BIM paragraph numbering for musicians' royalty income (BIM50605 family) was not fetched line-by-line here; confirm the precise paragraph before citing a specific BIM number in copy (cite "HMRC's Business Income Manual" generically until then).

## 16. Performer employment status, NIC and expenses (locked, DRAFT 2026-08-25, creative_performers gate)

**Position.** **Status and NIC.** Most actors, musicians and other performers engaged **under a contract for services are self-employed** for both tax and NIC, assessed under the ordinary case-law status factors (§13 factors; the contract label does not govern). The old special regime, under which the Social Security (Categorisation of Earners) Regulations 1978 treated many self-employed entertainers as **employed earners for Class 1 NIC**, was **repealed for entertainers from 6 April 2014** (SI 2014/635): since then engagers pay gross and the performer pays **Class 4 (and voluntary/credited Class 2 per §2)** as normal self-employed earnings. Performers on genuine employment contracts (for example some orchestral players or long-run theatre engagements) remain employees with PAYE and Class 1. **Expenses, the employed performer's special rule:** an employed **theatrical performer** (defined widely: "actor, singer, musician, dancer or theatrical artist", including TV and film work, per Madeley and Finnigan v HMRC (2006)) may deduct **agent's fees up to 17.5% of the employment earnings, VAT-inclusive** (ITEPA 2003 s.352), a rare statutory exception to the strict employee-expenses test; typical agent fees of 10-15% plus VAT fit inside it. Self-employed performers deduct agent commission under the ordinary **wholly-and-exclusively** rule (ITTOIA 2005 s.34), no percentage cap. **Clothing and appearance:** the controlling authority is **Mallalieu v Drummond [1983] 57 TC 330 (HL)**: ordinary everyday clothing is **not deductible** even if bought solely for work, because warmth and decency give it an intrinsic private purpose. **Costume and stage wear** that is not ordinary civilian clothing (a costume, a stage outfit not worn off-stage, protective or performance-specific items) is allowable; the line is the nature of the item, not the performer's intention. The same logic disallows ordinary grooming and cosmetic spend while permitting genuinely performance-specific items, keep this hedged and fact-specific on pages.

- **Statutory hooks:** SSCBA 1992 (Class 1 vs Class 2/4); **Social Security (Categorisation of Earners) (Amendment) Regulations 2014, SI 2014/635** (entertainers removed from the 1978 Categorisation Regulations from 6 Apr 2014); **ITEPA 2003 s.352** (employed entertainers' agent fees, 17.5% VAT-inclusive cap); **ITTOIA 2005 s.34** (wholly and exclusively).
- **HMRC manual anchors:** **BIM50150+** (actors and other entertainers: profession vs employment, allowable expenses); **ESM4148** (entertainers' NIC from 6 April 2014); **EIM62800+** (theatrical performers, s.352 agent fees, Madeley and Finnigan); **BIM37910** (Mallalieu v Drummond, clothing duality of purpose).
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** state plainly that **the entertainer Class 1 special treatment ended on 6 April 2014**; never describe producers as still deducting Class 1 from self-employed performers. Keep the **17.5% agent-fee cap for employed performers only**; a self-employed performer's commission is an ordinary business expense with no cap. On clothing, lead with Mallalieu: "bought only for work" is not the test, costume vs civilian wear is. Do NOT promise deductibility of ordinary clothes, haircuts or gym costs. Cross-ref §2 (Class 4), §12 (touring travel, 24-month rule), §13 (status factors), §15 (royalties).

## 17. Creator platform income, gifted products and the trading allowance (locked, DRAFT 2026-08-25, creative_performers gate)

**Position.** Income from platforms (YouTube/AdSense, TikTok creator programmes, Twitch, subscription platforms such as OnlyFans and Patreon, brand deals, affiliate commission) is **trading income** once the activity amounts to a trade under the badges of trade; regularity, commercial organisation and profit-seeking mark almost every monetised creator as trading. The platform's location does not change UK taxability for a UK-resident individual, and income counts **gross, before platform commission** (the commission is then an expense). **Gifted products and barter:** trading receipts include **money's worth**, not just cash (BIM40051, Gold Coast Selection Trust Ltd v Humphrey (1948)). A product or service received **in return for content, promotion or an agreed post** is a taxable trading receipt at its **market value**. Two hedges, both grounded in BIM40051: an item that is genuinely **not convertible into money** (cannot realistically be sold) may fall outside the charge, and **unsolicited freebies with no obligation or expectation of content** are not automatically trading receipts; both are fact-specific and pages must hedge rather than assert either way. **Trading allowance:** the first **£1,000 of gross trading income per tax year** is tax-free (full relief, no need to notify HMRC); above £1,000 the creator can deduct **either** the £1,000 allowance **or** actual expenses, never both (partial relief). Gross income for the £1,000 test includes the market value of contentful gifts, not just cash. The allowance is **not available** against income from the individual's own or a connected person's company, from a partnership, or from an employer, so a creator who incorporates cannot use it against company payments. **Registration:** register for Self Assessment by **5 October after the end of the tax year** in which gross trading income first exceeds £1,000 (or earlier voluntarily, for example to claim a loss or pay voluntary Class 2). VAT: platform and brand-deal turnover counts toward the **£90,000** threshold (§7); ad revenue and services supplied to overseas platforms are typically outside the scope under B2B place-of-supply rules but still watch UK-source brand work.

- **Statutory hooks:** ITTOIA 2005 Part 2 (trading income; badges of trade case law); **ITTOIA 2005 Part 6A (ss.783A onward)** (trading allowance £1,000, full and partial relief, exclusions for connected companies/partnerships/employers); TMA 1970 s.7 (notice of liability, 5 October); VATA 1994 (threshold, place of supply).
- **Authority anchors:** https://www.gov.uk/guidance/tax-free-allowances-on-property-and-trading-income (£1,000 allowance mechanics, exclusions, registration trigger).
- **HMRC manual anchors:** **BIM40051** (receipts in money or money's worth); BIM20200+ (badges of trade); TAM/registration via SAM.
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** never write "gifts are tax-free" and never write "all gifts are taxable": the locked line is **contentful gifts = taxable at market value; unsolicited no-strings freebies and genuinely unconvertible items = fact-specific, take advice**. Always state the trading allowance is **either/or against expenses** and that the £1,000 test uses **gross** income including gift value. Flag the 5 October registration deadline and the connected-company exclusion (the incorporated creator trap). Do NOT import employee benefit-in-kind valuation rules into a sole-trader creator's position (BIM40051 notes traders have no BIK equivalent). Cross-ref §2, §2.B, §7, §17's OnlyFans-adjacent pages stay tax-copy-only per the row-38 fence.
- **Open question (not asserted on pages):** HMRC's dedicated online-content-creator guidance pages (and any BIM paragraph specific to social-media income) were not individually verified here; professional commentary cites the principles above to BIM40051, which is verified. Cite BIM40051 and the gov.uk allowance page only until a creator-specific manual page is verified.

## 18. Trades and transport: gig platforms, drivers, hauliers (locked figures, DRAFT 2026-08-25, trades_transport gate)

**Position.** **18.1 Digital-platform reporting (GT-1).** The UK implemented the **OECD Model Reporting Rules for Digital Platforms** (the international counterpart of EU DAC7) via the **Platform Operators (Due Diligence and Reporting Requirements) Regulations 2023, SI 2023/817**, in force **1 January 2024**, first reports due **31 January 2025**. Platforms (Uber, Deliveroo, Amazon Flex, Etsy, Airbnb-type operators and the rest) must run due diligence on sellers, report each seller's **identity, tax reference and income from the platform to HMRC each calendar year by the following 31 January**, and give the seller a copy of what was reported. Occasional sellers of **goods** are excluded where they make **fewer than 30 sales of goods AND receive less than 2,000 euros (about £1,700)** in the calendar year; there is no equivalent exclusion for **services** (drivers, riders, taskers are reportable from the first pound). **Wording fence (locked): platform reporting is NOT a new tax.** It changes what HMRC sees, not what is taxable; the underlying rules (trading income §2, the £1,000 trading allowance §17, registration by 5 October §17) are unchanged. **18.2 Employment status fence (GT-2, locked).** **Uber BV v Aslam [2021] UKSC 5** decided the drivers were **"workers" for employment-law rights** (minimum wage, holiday pay). "Worker" is an **employment-law category that does not exist in tax law**: tax knows only employed or self-employed. Gig drivers and riders **remain self-employed for tax** (Self Assessment, Class 4 per §2) unless the ordinary §13 status factors point to employment on the facts. Never write that the Uber judgment made drivers employees for tax, and never infer PAYE from a worker-status finding. **18.3 Licence-renewal tax check (GT-3).** **Tax conditionality (FA 2021 Sch 33)**: an applicant **renewing** a taxi driver, private hire driver or PHV operator licence (England and Wales, from **4 April 2022**) or taxi/booking-office licences (Scotland and Northern Ireland, from **2 October 2023**), or re-applying after a lapse of a year or more, must complete an **HMRC tax check** and give the licensing body the resulting **tax check code**; the code is **valid for 120 days** and the authority cannot process the renewal without verifying it. First-time applicants only confirm awareness of HMRC guidance. The check confirms registration for tax; the licensing body sees pass/fail, not the tax affairs. **18.4 Private hire VAT (GT-4, litigation-shaped, hedge hard).** Stable ground: taxi and PHV fares are **standard-rated**; a **driver acting as principal** rarely breaches the £90,000 threshold (§7) so most drivers charge no VAT; an **operator acting as principal** must charge **20% on the full fare**. The principal-vs-agent question was litigated: **Uber Britannia v Sefton MBC [2023] EWHC 1975 (KB)** held operators outside London contract as principal, but the **Court of Appeal reversed it in July 2024 (Delta Merseyside and Veezu)** and the **Supreme Court upheld the reversal on 29 July 2025**: outside London an operator is **not** obliged by licensing law to contract as principal (London operators, under the separate TfL regime, do). Separately, the government's **November 2025 consultation response** confirmed PHV operators would be **excluded from the Tour Operators' Margin Scheme from 2 January 2026** (legislated via the Finance (No. 2) Bill 2026 cycle). Pages state the agent/principal fork and the driver-threshold point; everything else is date-tagged and hedged. **18.5 HGV driver subsistence (GT-5).** Employed drivers: HMRC's approved overnight subsistence rate is **£34.90 per night (from 1 January 2013, EIM66110)**, reduced to **75% (about £26.20) where the driver uses a sleeper cab (EIM66130)**, payable free of tax/NIC only where the **EIM66105 conditions** (genuine overnight absence, checking regime/approval) are met. Owner-drivers (sole traders) deduct **actual** reasonable accommodation and subsistence costs of an itinerant trade under wholly-and-exclusively rules, not the employed flat rate. **O-licence** costs: describe only, the licence obligation bites the client. **18.6 Vehicle-cost method choice (GT-7).** A sole trader chooses per vehicle between **simplified mileage rates (ITTOIA 2005 ss.94C to 94G, the §12 AMAP rates: 55p/25p from 6 April 2026, 45p/25p to 2025/26)** and **actual costs plus capital allowances (§8)**. The choice **sticks for as long as that vehicle is used in the business**: once mileage rates are claimed for a vehicle, no switch to actual costs or capital allowances for it (and mileage rates cannot be used for a vehicle on which capital allowances have already been claimed). Vans qualify for AIA/FYAs (§8); cars do not (WDA by CO2 only). **Driving instructors (GT-6):** franchise fees paid weekly/monthly to a driving school are ongoing revenue costs (deductible, §21.5 logic); DVSA ADI registration and test fees for an established instructor are deductible; the AMAP-vs-actual choice above applies to the tuition car. **18.7 Cleaning and security (GT-8).** Cite the existing sole-trader (§2), VAT (§7) and employer-cost (§9) positions. TUPE on cleaning-contract changeovers: **describe only**, employment law bites the client. **SIA licence fees**: deductible for a **self-employed** operative (wholly and exclusively); for an **employee** the strict ITEPA s.336 test generally blocks the deduction unless the fee appears in an HMRC-approved deduction list, so hedge the employed case.

- **Statutory hooks:** **SI 2023/817** (platform reporting, OECD model rules) https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2023/817 ; **FA 2021 Sch 33** (tax conditionality); **ITTOIA 2005 ss.94C to 94G** (simplified mileage); ITEPA 2003 ss.337 to 339 (employed travel/subsistence); VATA 1994 (fares standard-rated, §7 threshold).
- **Authority anchors:** https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-goods-or-services-on-a-digital-platform (reporting from 1 Jan 2024, 30-sales/2,000-euro goods exclusion); https://www.gov.uk/guidance/confirm-your-tax-responsibilities-when-applying-for-a-taxi-private-hire-or-scrap-metal-licence (conditionality dates and scope); the November 2025 government response on VAT and PHVs (TOMS exclusion from 2 Jan 2026).
- **HMRC manual anchors:** **EIM66105/EIM66110/EIM66130** (lorry drivers' overnight subsistence £34.90, sleeper-cab 75%); ESM (status factors, §13); BIM75005+ (simplified expenses, mileage rates).
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** on platform reporting always pair the mechanics with the fence: **reported does not mean newly taxable, and unreported does not mean tax-free**. On gig status always separate the three-status employment-law world (employee/worker/self-employed) from the two-status tax world, citing Uber v Aslam as employment law only. Date-tag the tax-check dates by nation and give the 120-day code validity. ## 19. Care and education: foster carers, childminders, nurseries, tutors (locked figures, DRAFT 2026-08-25, care_education gate)

**Position.** **19.1 Qualifying care relief (QCR).** Foster care, **shared lives** care, kinship/friends-and-family placements, **staying put** care and parent-and-child arrangements placed by a local authority, Health and Social Care Trust, fostering service provider or Shared Lives scheme are taxed under **qualifying care relief (ITTOIA 2005 Part 7 Ch 2)**. The carer's **qualifying amount** = a **fixed amount per household** plus a **weekly amount per person cared for**. Current figures (gov.uk foster-parent tax guidance, CPI-indexed yearly, date-tag on every use): fixed amount **£20,440 per household (2025/26)** plus **£435 a week per child under 11**, **£515 a week per child 11 or over**, **£515 a week per adult** (2024/25, per HS236 (2025): £19,360 / £405 / £485). Receipts at or below the qualifying amount = **nil profit, no tax** (a return may still be needed). Above it, the carer **elects each year** between the **simplified method** (taxable profit = total receipts minus the qualifying amount, no expense records) and the **profit method** (receipts minus actual expenses and capital allowances); the election is year-by-year, HS236 is the anchor. Foster carers are self-employed (§2 applies): since 6 April 2024 **Class 2 is treated as paid** above the small profits threshold (§2), and carers with low or nil profit can protect state pension via **voluntary Class 2 or NI credits (Specified Adult/foster-carer credits)**. **19.2 Childminders.** A registered childminder working from home is a sole trader (§2). **Outside MTD for Income Tax** the long-standing HMRC/PACEY-agreed simplifications apply: a **10% of childminding income wear-and-tear deduction** for household items and furniture (home-based income only), plus **hours-based apportionment** of household running costs: at **40+ hours a week, 33% of running costs (heating, lighting, food for the children etc.) and 10% of fixed costs (council tax, rent)**, pro-rated below 40 hours (hours/40 x 33% running; hours/4 = fixed-cost %). **Inside MTD ITSA the agreement's percentages fall away**: the childminder claims the **actual business proportion** of buying/repairing/replacing household items and of household costs instead (gov.uk childminder expenses guidance, updated 2026). With MTD phasing in from April 2026 by the §9 timetable, every childminder page must state **both regimes side by side, gated on MTD status**. Ofsted registration and inspection fees, DBS checks, training, toys and equipment are deductible (equipment via §8 where capital). **19.3 Nurseries and preschools.** **Funded-hours payments (the 15/30 funded childcare hours) from the local authority and receipts via Tax-Free Childcare are ordinary taxable trading income** of the provider, exactly like parent-paid fees: government-routed payment does not change its character. **VAT:** childcare by an **Ofsted-registered (state-regulated) provider is exempt** as welfare services **directly connected with the care or protection of children (VATA 1994 Sch 9 Group 7 item 9)**. Exempt means **no output VAT and no input-VAT recovery** on related costs; a provider with mixed taxable supplies (consumables sold, room hire) is into partial exemption, flag rather than compute. Staff costs, auto-enrolment and the loaded-cost frame per §9. **Scope fence (row 77, locked):** charity or committee-run preschools, academy trusts and anything needing an **audit or regularity-assurance engagement** are NOT this site's ground; they sit with the future charities/schools build. Generalist nursery pages address **privately run, fee-charging providers** only and never drift into academy or charity accounting. **19.4 Tutors.** Self-employed tutoring is ordinary trading income: the **£1,000 trading allowance and the 5 October registration trigger per §17**, platform reporting (tutoring marketplaces are reportable platforms, no goods exclusion for services) per §18.1. An **employed teacher tutoring on the side** keeps PAYE employment and reports the tutoring separately as self-employment on the same return; the trading allowance is available against it (the §17 connected-employer exclusion bites only if the tutoring is income from their own employer or own company). DBS, exam-board and marketplace commission costs are deductible (or swallowed by the allowance, either/or per §17).

- **Authority anchors:** https://www.gov.uk/support-for-foster-parents/tax-arrangements (£20,440 / £435 / £515 current figures); HS236 qualifying care relief helpsheet (method election, care types; 2025 edition carries £19,360 / £405 / £485 for 2024/25); https://www.gov.uk/guidance/claiming-expenses-and-keeping-records-if-youre-a-childminder (10% wear and tear, 33%/10% hours tables, MTD actual-cost switch).
- **HMRC manual anchors:** BIM52750+ (foster carers / qualifying care); BIM52205+ (childminders: wear and tear, household-cost apportionment); VATWELF (welfare exemption); VAT Notice 701/2 (welfare).

Cross-ref §2, §7, §8, §9, §17, §18.

## 20. Personal care and fitness: salons, chair rental, gyms, PTs (locked, DRAFT 2026-08-25, personal_care_fitness gate)

**Position.** **20.1 Chair and room rental.** Renting a chair, station or room to a self-employed stylist is **standard-rated for VAT, not exempt land letting, since 1 October 2012**: **VATA 1994 Sch 9 Group 1 para (ma)** (inserted by FA 2012) excludes from the land exemption "the grant of facilities to a person who uses the facilities wholly or mainly to supply hairdressing services". Splitting the invoice into "rent" plus "services" does not create an exempt element; it is one standard-rated supply of facilities. Chair-rental income counts toward the salon's **£90,000** threshold (§7) alongside its own takings. A genuinely separate room let with **no services** attached can still be exempt land, but that is the exception and is fact-specific, hedge it. The **stylist's status** is decided on the §13 factors as applied to the sector (own clients, own prices, own products and kit, keeps own takings and pays the rent, freedom to work elsewhere): a properly run rent-a-chair stylist is **self-employed** (§2, Class 4, post-2024 Class 2 position); a stylist paid a percentage by the salon on salon-set prices with salon-controlled hours looks like an **employee** (PAYE, §9), and mislabelling is the sector's classic failure. **20.2 Disaggregation fence (locked wording rule).** Legitimate positions: a salon and a genuinely independent stylist are separate persons with separate turnovers; a business may time capital spend or price to stay below the threshold. The fence: **artificial separation of one business into several entities to stay under £90,000 (salon/stylists, his-and-hers salons, beauty vs hair "businesses" sharing till, staff and premises) is disaggregation**, which HMRC counters with a **direction treating the parties as a single taxable person (VATA 1994 Sch 1 para 1A / para 2)**, prospective, plus registration failure exposure on the facts. Pages **describe** the boundary and the direction power; they never provide a splitting recipe, name a target structure, or frame separation as a planning technique. **20.3 Gym membership and fitness costs.** Employer-paid membership of a **public** gym is a **taxable benefit in kind** (P11D, Class 1A NIC at the §4 rate); putting it through **salary sacrifice** does not help because the **optional remuneration arrangements rules (ITEPA 2003 ss.69A/69B, from 6 April 2017)** tax the higher of the salary given up and the benefit value. The exemption that does work is **ITEPA 2003 s.261 (in-house sporting or recreational facilities)**: available to employees generally, **not available to the public**, used mainly by employment-related users, and not on domestic premises, the classic compliant case is an on-site gym, not a paid-for club membership. A **sole trader's own** gym or fitness spend is **generally disallowed** (duality of purpose, the §16 Mallalieu logic: personal fitness serves the person, not only the trade); narrow exceptions (for example a specific medical/professional requirement tied to the trade) are rare and fact-specific, hedge hard. A **limited company** paying the director's membership creates the same BIK, not a deduction trick. **20.4 Personal trainer status.** An **employed instructor** (gym payroll, gym's classes, gym's clients) is PAYE. A **freelance PT paying the gym a licence/rent fee** to train their own clients on the floor is self-employed (§2), with the fee deductible. **Mixed status in one tax year is normal** (employed instructor by day, freelance PT evenings): PAYE employment plus self-employment on the same return, each taxed under its own rules. **IR35 (§13) is only in play where the PT operates through a PSC** and the end client is not a private consumer; consumer-facing PT work through a company is outside the off-payroll rules' typical bite but the company still runs §3/§4 mechanics. **20.5 Salon basics** (tips/tronc, product resale at 20% VAT while services ride the same registration, MTD ITSA phasing): cite §2, §7, §9 at pack time; no new lock.

## 21. Retail and product businesses: margin schemes, dealers, retail VAT, franchises (locked figures, DRAFT 2026-08-25, retail_product gate)

**Position.** **21.1 VAT margin schemes.** A dealer in **second-hand goods, works of art, antiques or collectors' items** bought without VAT (typically from the public) may account for VAT at **one-sixth (16.67%) of the margin** (selling price minus purchase price) instead of 20% on the full price. No registration for the scheme is needed, but **record-keeping is constitutive**: invoices both ways plus a **stock book** tracking each item; margin-scheme eligibility is lost item-by-item where records fail or where the item was bought on a VAT invoice. **Excluded: precious metals, investment gold and loose precious stones** (finished second-hand jewellery qualifies; bullion and unmounted stones do not). **Used cars** run the margin scheme with the strictest stock-book discipline (VAT Notice 718/1: per-vehicle stock numbers, purchase and sale entries); losses on one vehicle cannot net against gains on another (each margin stands alone, no negative margins). **Global accounting** (Notice 718 sub-scheme) lets high-volume low-value dealers net purchases against sales per period for **items bought at £500 or less**, not available for vehicles. **21.2 Motor-finance fence (row 70, locked wording rule).** Motor-trade pages may cover margin-scheme VAT, stock finance accounting and dealer tax mechanics. They may NOT contain: motor-finance **commission or mis-selling claims content** (discretionary-commission or any successor redress scheme), any invitation to check eligibility or start a claim, any **referral of a claimant** to a CMC or law firm, or links whose purpose is claims traffic. Managing such claims is **regulated claims management activity in the financial services sector (RAO art 89G; FSMA s.19/s.23)** and the estate holds no such permission. One neutral factual sentence that redress litigation exists, with a gov.uk/FCA link, is the ceiling. **21.3 High value dealers (MLR).** A business accepting (or making) **cash payments of 10,000 euros or more** (or equivalent, single payment or linked instalments for one transaction) for **goods** must **register with HMRC as a high value dealer under the Money Laundering Regulations 2017 BEFORE the first such payment**, run AML policies and pay the annual fee; the clean alternative most dealers choose is a hard cash-acceptance cap below the trigger. Services-only receipts and card/cheque payments do not trigger it. **21.4 Retail VAT schemes.** Retailers who cannot itemise every sale use a **retail scheme** to compute output VAT: **point of sale** (identify the rate at the till, no turnover cap below the bespoke line), **apportionment** (split takings by the ratio of purchases at each rate) and **direct calculation** (mark up minority-rate goods to expected selling prices). Eligibility: **Apportionment Scheme 1 and Direct Calculation Scheme 1: retail turnover up to £1 million**; **Apportionment Scheme 2 and Direct Calculation Scheme 2: up to £130 million**; **above £130 million a bespoke scheme agreed with HMRC is mandatory**. Mixed-rate food retail (zero-rated groceries vs standard-rated confectionery/hot food) is the classic use case; the scheme choice materially moves the VAT bill, so present it as a calculation. **21.5 Franchise fees.** For the **franchisee**, the **initial franchise fee is capital** (payment for the right to trade under the system, **BIM57620**), still capital when paid by instalments, and not deductible against income, though a contractually **identified element for training or other revenue items can be apportioned out and deducted** on the facts. **Ongoing royalties and management-service fees are revenue, deductible** as incurred. A **company** franchisee may instead obtain relief for the initial fee through the **corporate intangibles regime (CTA 2009 Part 8)** where the franchise is an intangible fixed asset amortised in the accounts, a company-vs-sole-trader asymmetry worth flagging (hedged, facts and accounting treatment govern). Sole-trader franchisees get no amortisation route: the initial fee waits for CGT base-cost treatment on disposal. **21.6 R&D restatement (page-level rule).** Manufacturing/product pages state the merged-scheme facts already locked in the verification log (**merged RDEC 20%, ERIS at the 30% intensity threshold, periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024**) and nothing more aggressive: **no claim-farm positioning, no "most businesses qualify" framing, no sector-percentage teasers** (standing constraint). **21.7 CBAM/exporters:** not authored; conditional on the dossier's D3 volume check, lock before any N7 page.

## 22. Specialist professions: seafarers, flight crew, sports professionals, niche practices (locked figures, DRAFT 2026-08-25, specialist_professions gate)

**Position.** **22.1 Seafarers' Earnings Deduction (SED).** A **100% deduction** from seafarer employment earnings (ITEPA 2003 ss.378 to 385) for an **eligible period of at least 365 days** made up mainly of days absent from the UK. Machinery, all locked: a day counts as absent if the seafarer is **outside the UK at midnight**; return visits are tolerated if **no single UK visit exceeds 183 consecutive days** and total UK days stay within **one half** of the days elapsed since the eligible period began (the half-day rule, tested at each return); duties must be performed wholly or partly on a **ship** on a voyage that **begins or ends outside the UK** (the UK North Sea sector counts as UK); **offshore installations are not ships**, so rig, FPSO and installation workers are excluded however seagoing the job feels; the claimant must be **an employee** (self-employed crew cannot claim SED, they are just §2 traders) resident in the UK or an EEA state; claim via Self Assessment, **HS205** is the anchor and carries the year-by-year worksheet. **Mariners' NIC is a separate regime**: special Class 1 rules by ship's flag, employer's place of business and domicile of the employment (NIM29000+), never assume the SED answer decides the NIC answer, state and signpost. **22.2 Tonnage tax fence (locked, one line).** Tonnage tax (FA 2000 Sch 22) is an **elective ring-fenced corporation-tax regime for ship-operating companies**; "ship tax" queries get a factual paragraph and this boundary, and **operator-level tonnage-tax advisory is out of scope** for the generalist. **22.3 Flight crew.** Most pilots and cabin crew are **PAYE employees**; the standing deductions are the **flat rate expenses**: **£1,022 a year for uniformed flight deck crew plus £110 for travel-related expenses (EIM50050, BALPA agreement, from 2013/14)** and **£720 a year for uniformed cabin crew (EIM50070, from 2013/14)**, claimed through Self Assessment or PAYE. **Repayment-agent fence (row 67, locked):** the pages **describe** what is claimable and how to claim it yourself from HMRC; the estate **never processes claims, takes assignments or nominations, or charges contingent fees**, and never links to rebate firms (the HMRC repayment-agent regime, including the Income Tax (Repayment Agents) registration rules from 2023, plus the standing no-rebate-service rule). Contractor/self-employed pilots (instructors, ferry pilots) route to §2/§13. **22.4 Sports professionals, image rights and agents' fees (row 63 fence).** Facts that may be stated: a club commonly pays the agent under a **dual-representation** contract; HMRC's **Guidelines for Compliance GFC6 (football agents' fees and dual representation, 2024)** rejects a default 50/50 club/player split, requires the split to reflect **commercial reality with contemporaneous evidence**, and treats the **player-services element paid by the club as the player's taxable benefit, reportable on P11D** with Class 1A. **Image-rights structures** (payments to a player's image-rights company) are described only as an area HMRC actively challenges on commercial substance. **Hard fence:** no scheme promotion, no structure recipes, no investment or pension advice, no "keep more of your image rights" framing; the sector's DOTAS/POTAS/enablers history (FA 2004 Pt 7, FA 2014 Pt 5, FA 2017 Sch 16) is exactly why the tone stays compliance-first. **22.5 Architects and professional practices.** Structure content cites §1/§6 (LLP vs company is the live fork for practices; salaried-member rules §6.A for fixed-share members). **ARB registration, professional subscriptions and PII premiums are deductible** for the practice (wholly and exclusively; employed architects claim ARB/approved subscriptions under the approved-list rules). **R&D position: conservative by design.** Competitors sell architecture R&D claims hard; the house position states only the merged-scheme facts (§21.6 restatement) and that **routine design, standard-compliance and aesthetic work is not R&D** (the advance must be in science or technology, BEIS/DSIT guidelines); no claim-encouragement framing. **22.6 Renewables capital-allowances boundary (row 85 + property fence).** Energy and renewables **businesses** get the ordinary §8 machinery: AIA, full expensing (companies), the 40% FYA from 1 January 2026, WDA 18% to 14%, special rate 6%. No energy-broking or TPI monetisation anywhere (§6.1 estate lock). **22.7 Neurodivergent business owners (row 84 tone fence, locked).** Content is **business-tax content in plainer form**: systems, deadlines (§2.B), MTD readiness (§9 timetable), record-keeping routines, and **signposting to gov.uk reasonable-adjustment and Access to Work pages**.
