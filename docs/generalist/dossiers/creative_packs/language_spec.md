## 1. Measured answer patterns (what the winners do)

- **Length is not the lever.** Top-10 holders run 650 to 3,200 words; the p3 actor page is 1,400 words and the p4 musician page 1,100. Depth of FACT beats depth of word count: the entire SERP set carries almost no current figures.
- **Direct answer first under question headings.** The Q&A winners open each H2 with the ruling sentence, number embedded: "Once your YouTube earnings exceed £1,000, you must register as self-employed with HMRC, even if YouTube is just a side hustle." Context comes second, never first.
- **Question-form H2s on informational pages** ("When do UK YouTubers pay tax?", "Do Influencers Pay Tax on PR Gifts?"); noun-phrase H2s on service pages. Our informational pages (N1, N2, and FAQ blocks everywhere) use question H2s; service-choice pages (R1, N3) mix.
- **Second person throughout tax explainers** ("You're in charge of reporting your income"), short declarative sentences (winners average roughly 15-20 words), one idea per sentence. Service brochures run first-person-plural firm-voice ("We work with film, TV..."); that is a brand register we do not copy.
- **Numbers live inside sentences, not in asides.** Winners embed thresholds mid-sentence (£1,000, £12,570, 20%) and the best page uses named-person worked examples ("James's £25,000 gaming channel yields £3,605"). Tables appear on exactly two winning pages (rates table; royalties source table) and both rank; use them.
- **"It depends" is handled by naming the fork,** not by hedging into mush: "Gifts tied to posting requirements: probably taxable" — a classification, then the caveat. Copy the fork-naming, not their wrong answers.

## 2. House voice constraints (binding, from VOICE_STANDARD parity + standard terms)

- Plain cost-conscious British English, consumer register, reader in the sentence (the farming finding: "you pay", not "the relief applies").
- **No em-dashes** anywhere in copy. Commas, parentheses, full stops.
- **No "this guide"/"in this article" intros**, no "let's dive in", no "navigating the world of", no "unique needs of creative professionals" filler. H1 poses the real question or names the real service; sentence one answers or asserts.
- **No AI tells:** no rule-of-three adjective stacks, no "it's important to note", no mirrored "not only... but also", no section that summarises the previous section.
- **Worked examples with recomputable figures**, 2026/27 rates from house_positions.md sections cited by number (§2, §7, §14-§17). Every number re-derivable by the arithmetic floor; every rate date-tagged.
- Hedges are house-locked wordings, not vibes: the §16 clothing line, the §17 gift lines, the §15 treaty line. Use them verbatim in substance.

## 3. Do-not-copy list (measured on the same sample)

- pulse: em-dashes, salesy inversions ("Influencers aren't like traditional businesses—and your accountant shouldn't treat you like one"), 5-section firm-sell tails.
- yourinfluenceraccountant: stale rates (Class 2 £3.45, Class 4 9%, 2024/25 PA) and the invented "£50 PR gift threshold". Structure good, facts poisonous.
- srlv/weareband: brand-story service prose with zero tax content ("35-years", "financial confidant").
- creativeandnumbers: platitude H2s ("Understanding the Creative Mindset"), zero figures.
- greenandpeter: thin listicle framing ("3 Key Insights") and filler technology sections.
- performanceaccountancy: the "PPL income does not count toward your VAT threshold" claim (contradicts house §15; flag, never reproduce).
