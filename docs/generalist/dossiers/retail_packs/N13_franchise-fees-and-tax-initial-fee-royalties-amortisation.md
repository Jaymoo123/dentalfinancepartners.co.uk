## 5. Whitespace

- **The split table, done properly, in the first screen.** Every payment a franchisee makes, sorted
  into capital and revenue: initial fee (capital), any contractually identified training element
  (apportionable, revenue, on the facts), ongoing royalty (revenue), marketing or brand fund
  contribution (revenue), renewal fee (capital), transfer fee on sale. **No page in the field
  attempts a split.**
- **Capital means capital, sourced.** BIM57620, and the point livingstones misses entirely: paying
  the fee by instalments does not make it revenue.
- **The amortisation question answered on both sides of the incorporation line**, hedged per the
  gate: a company amortising the franchise as an intangible fixed asset may get relief through the
  intangibles regime; a sole trader gets no amortisation route at all and the fee waits as CGT base
  cost until disposal. That asymmetry is a genuine reason a franchisee might incorporate, it is
  worth real money, and it is unpublished by both competitors.
- **VAT on the fees**, which livingstones lists as a "consideration" and never answers.
- **The disposal end**, which nobody reaches: what happens to the capitalised fee when the franchise
  is sold or the agreement ends.
- A recomputable 2026/27 worked example: the same franchise fee run as a sole trader and as a
  company, to the tax difference.

## 6. Fences (binding)

  otherwise is an automatic QA fail. Never reproduce the "may need to be capitalised" hedge, which
  reads as though deduction is sometimes available against trading income.
- **The intangibles route stays hedged until CIRD verification (§21 open question 3).** The worked
  example may show the mechanic; it may not assert the outcome as certain for any given franchise.

- **The 4% to 12% royalty range, if used, is attributed as a market observation from published
