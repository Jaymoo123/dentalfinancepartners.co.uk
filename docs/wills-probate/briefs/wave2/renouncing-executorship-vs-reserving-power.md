## Gap/Angle
- No competitor leads with a direct side-by-side comparison table (reversibility, effort, cost, future involvement, when to use) — Laytons gets closest but has zero visual structure.
- Intermeddling rule (the thing that actually forecloses renunciation as an option) is buried mid-article on most sites rather than used as the decision gate up front — we can open with "check this before you choose" framing.
- Reversibility nuance is inconsistently stated: most say "irreversible," few explain the narrow court exception (fraud/undue influence) — we can be the clearest on this.
- No competitor ties the decision back to the de bonis non administratis risk scenario (what happens if the sole remaining acting executor later dies/loses capacity) as a concrete reason to prefer power reserved by default.
- None reference both PA15 (renunciation) and the Notice of Power Reserved procedure by name in one place with form/process specifics for each — an opportunity for a genuinely complete single-page answer.
