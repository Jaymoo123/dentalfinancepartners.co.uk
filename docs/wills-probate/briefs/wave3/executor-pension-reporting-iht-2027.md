## Competitor Coverage Outline
**Wilkins Southworth ("Executors / PRs and Pension Inheritance Tax: A Risky Inheritance")** (fetched):
- Responsibility shift: PRs (not scheme administrators) become legally liable for identifying, reporting and paying IHT on pension assets
- Core problem: PRs must pay tax on assets they don't control/can't access; funding gap because probate must be obtained before accessing pension funds
- Financial burden: potential 67% effective tax rate scenario combining IHT + income tax on some death benefits
- Government mitigation mechanisms: Direct Payment Scheme, installment payments over up to 10 years for illiquid assets, beneficiaries instructing administrators to pay IHT directly, grant on credit in exceptional cases
- Key vulnerabilities: minors/incapacitated beneficiaries lack workarounds; reimbursement from beneficiaries is difficult in practice
- Recommended actions (adviser-toned): maintain pension records, plan early, consider lifetime gifting, evaluate accepting executor roles carefully

**Coles Miller ("Pensions nightmare for executors")**:
- Similar liability-shift framing with a dramatic headline
- Practical steps for executors administering an estate with pensions

**Royal London / DMH Stallard (adviser technical pages)**:
- Reporting mechanics: information gathering, valuation, HMRC reporting, distribution
- Information-sharing requirements between PRs and scheme administrators before grant is issued

## Gap/Angle
- Every ranking result is adviser- or solicitor-firm-toned and largely written to unsettle rather than to walk a first-time executor through what to actually do step by step. A calm, practical "here is exactly what you as an executor need to do, and in what order" page is the clear content gap.
- No page in the set clearly separates "what the executor must legally do" from "what happens automatically via the scheme/HMRC" — conflating these creates unnecessary anxiety; this page should make the division of responsibility explicit.
- No page connects this new duty back to the executor's existing wider duties (asset-gathering, valuation, IHT400 process) in a way that fits a reader already following a general executor journey — this page should bridge from wave-2 executor content into the 2027-specific pension duty.
- The 50%/15-months withholding mechanism (confirmed via the L&G fetch in the pensions-iht-2027-changes brief) is directly relevant here as a practical funding-gap mitigation and should be explained factually as one option, without recommending it as advice.
- Content must state these as fact and must NOT give product/financial advice (e.g. no "you should take pension advice to do X", no specific product recommendations — informational only, general guidance to seek regulated financial advice where relevant). This applies with particular force here since several competitor pages already blur into advice-like recommendations ("consider lifetime gifting strategies," "evaluate accepting executor roles") — this brief's content must stay descriptive/informational and point to seeking regulated financial or legal advice rather than making the recommendation itself.
- Facts that must appear accurately: effective date 6 April 2027; personal representatives (PRs/executors) become legally responsible for reporting and paying IHT due on unused pension funds and death benefits; death-in-service benefits from registered pension schemes are excluded from the charge; HMRC estimates 10,500 estates newly become liable for IHT for the first time per year, and a further 38,500 pay more; the average increase for those paying more is around £34,000; up to 50% of the pension amount can be withheld by the scheme for up to 15 months to help fund the IHT liability; spouse/civil partner exemption still applies to pensions passing to a spouse/civil partner.
