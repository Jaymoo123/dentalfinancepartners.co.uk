**Figure/fact verification**: the £3,000 annual exemption figure surfaced consistently and matches ground truth. The "transfer of value" legal test (s.3 Inheritance Tax Act 1984 concept, though not always cited by name in consumer content) and "bad bargain" treatment were consistent across results — no discrepancy found. One nuance several results only mention in passing but is worth foregrounding: gifts with reservation of benefit (e.g. gifting a house but continuing to live in it rent-free) are NOT treated as effective gifts for IHT purposes at all — they stay in the donor's estate regardless of how many years pass. This is a materially important and under-explained trap.

## Gap/Angle
- Gift with reservation of benefit (GROB) is the single most consequential edge case and is underserved: most results mention it only briefly or not at all, despite it being one of the most common real-world mistakes (e.g. parents "gifting" a house to children but continuing to live in it) that fully defeats the 7-year planning strategy. This deserves a dedicated, prominent section with a clear example.
- The overlap/confusion between "deprivation of assets" (a means-testing/care-fees concept, assessed by local authorities) and IHT gift rules (an HMRC concept) is not distinguished anywhere in the result set — readers researching gifting ahead of care costs are likely to land on this page confused about which set of rules applies, so a short disambiguation paragraph is a genuine gap-filler.
