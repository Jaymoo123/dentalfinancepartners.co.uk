## Gap/Angle
- Most coverage is adviser/professional-facing and assumes the reader already has meaningful pension wealth and wants planning strategies. A genuinely plain-English "what is this, does it affect me, what do I do" explainer for an ordinary reader (not a wealth-planning client) is underserved — this is the clear angle to take.
- Several sources conflate "estimated 49,000 estates affected" (a rounded/combined figure some outlets use) with the HMRC-sourced 10,500 newly liable + 38,500 paying more breakdown — use the two precise HMRC figures rather than a rounded composite, and be explicit that they are HMRC estimates, not certainties for any individual.
- No result explains the 50% withholding/retention mechanism (up to 15 months) in plain terms for a general reader — this is a genuinely confusing practical point (how does the tax actually get paid if the money is locked in a pension) and is a strong differentiation opportunity.
- LOCKED POLICY FACTS to state as fact (informational only — no product/financial advice; direct readers to seek regulated financial advice where relevant):
  - Effective date: 6 April 2027.
  - Personal representatives become legally responsible for reporting and paying any IHT due on unused pension funds and death benefits.
  - Death-in-service benefits from registered pension schemes are excluded from the charge.
  - HMRC estimates 10,500 estates become newly liable for IHT for the first time each year, and a further 38,500 pay more.
  - The average increase for those paying more is around £34,000.
  - Up to 50% of the pension amount can be withheld/retained by the scheme for up to 15 months to help fund the IHT liability (describe factually; do not invent further mechanics).
  - Spouse/civil partner exemption still applies to pensions passing to a spouse or civil partner.
