## Gap/Angle
- Almost nothing in the top results is written for the ordinary consumer/executor audience — the ranking pages are adviser technical notes and law-firm briefings. A plain-English "what changes and what it means for you" page written for someone Googling this after seeing a headline is a clear gap.
- No competitor page ties the policy change to a concrete number people can act on — most describe direction ("more estates will pay IHT") without landing on HMRC's own 10,500/38,500 estate figures or the ~£34,000 average increase. Citing these numbers with source attribution differentiates this page and supports E-E-A-T.
- No page links straight through to a calculator that lets a reader estimate their own exposure — this is the natural next step and nobody is offering it as the CTA.
- Content must state these as fact and must NOT give product/financial advice (e.g. no "you should take pension advice to do X", no specific product recommendations — informational only, general guidance to seek regulated financial advice where relevant).
- Facts that must appear accurately: effective date 6 April 2027; personal representatives (PRs/executors) become legally responsible for reporting and paying IHT due on unused pension funds and death benefits; death-in-service benefits from registered pension schemes are excluded from the charge; HMRC estimates 10,500 estates become newly liable for IHT each year and a further 38,500 pay more; the average increase for those paying more is around £34,000; up to 50% of the pension amount can be withheld by the scheme for up to 15 months to help fund the IHT liability; spouse/civil partner exemption still applies to pensions passing to a spouse/civil partner.
