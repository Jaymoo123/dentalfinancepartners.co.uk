## Gap/Angle
- Every ranking result frames "who's affected" around high-net-worth or adviser-led estate planning ("substantial defined contribution pension pots," "multi-generational wealth transfer"). None of them clearly reassures or informs the much larger group of readers with modest pensions and modest estates who are anxious after seeing headlines but are, in fact, unlikely to be affected — this is a real underserved reader intent and a chance to be genuinely useful rather than alarmist.
- No page combines the two HMRC figures (10,500 estates newly liable + 38,500 estates already liable paying more) into one clear "who" breakdown with the average £34,000 increase attached to the second group specifically — most either cite one number or blend them.
- No page in the set walks through a simple self-check ("do you have a pension + an estate near the NRB/RNRB threshold + no spouse to leave it to?") before pointing to a calculator — this page can own that triage moment.
- Content must state these as fact and must NOT give product/financial advice (e.g. no "you should take pension advice to do X", no specific product recommendations — informational only, general guidance to seek regulated financial advice where relevant).
- Facts that must appear accurately: effective date 6 April 2027; personal representatives (PRs/executors) become legally responsible for reporting and paying IHT due on unused pension funds and death benefits; death-in-service benefits from registered pension schemes are excluded from the charge; HMRC estimates 10,500 estates newly become liable for IHT for the first time per year, and a further 38,500 pay more; the average increase for those paying more is around £34,000; up to 50% of the pension amount can be withheld by the scheme for up to 15 months to help fund the IHT liability; spouse/civil partner exemption still applies to pensions passing to a spouse/civil partner.
