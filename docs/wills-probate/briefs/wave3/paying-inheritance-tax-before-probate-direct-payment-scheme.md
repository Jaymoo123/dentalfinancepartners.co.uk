**gov.uk "Applying for a grant on credit for inheritance tax"** (implicit structure from search):
- IHT reference number required before submitting IHT400
- IHT423 sent to the bank/building society, not to HMRC
- HMRC's Direct Payment Scheme bank account (sort code 08-32-10, account 12001039)
- Scheme only works on accounts held in the deceased's sole name — not joint accounts

**UHY Hacker Young / UK Property Accountants** (from search snippets): both frame the DPS as the primary practical route, with the instalment option pitched as the fallback for illiquid assets (property, unlisted shares, certain business assets) rather than as a first choice.

## Gap/Angle
- No competitor clearly separates "cash sitting in the deceased's sole-name accounts → use DPS" from "value locked in property/business/illiquid assets → use the 10-year instalment option" as a decision tree; most present both options as a flat list. A clear "which route applies to you" framing is a genuine gap.
- The joint-account exclusion (DPS only works on sole-name accounts) is buried or omitted in several results despite being a common real-world blocker for grieving spouses — worth surfacing prominently and early.
- Interest accrual mechanics are inconsistently explained: interest runs from six months after death regardless of route, and the instalment option still accrues interest on outstanding balances (except on some agricultural/business property) — no result makes this trade-off (instalments ease cash flow but don't avoid interest) crystal clear.
- Probate loans are covered by only one competitor and treated almost as an aside; given the estate's own probate-cost-calculator and probate-timeline-estimator tools, this brief can connect the funding-gap problem to those existing decision-support assets rather than just describing commercial loans.
- No result links this topic forward to what happens once probate is granted (repayment/reconciliation of instalments as assets are sold) — a natural "what happens next" close that ties into the estate's probate-process cluster.
