**gov.uk technical note** (primary authority, could not be parsed cleanly via fetch but confirmed via search summary and cross-referenced by every secondary source): confirms the rule applies to deaths on or after 6 April 2012, the reduction is calculated against the "baseline amount" (broadly, the net estate after deducting exemptions, reliefs and the available nil-rate band), and HMRC's guidance describes a **component-based** calculation for estates split across survivorship property, joint property, settled property and the general estate — each component can, in some cases, be tested separately, with an election available to merge components to help reach the 10% threshold. This mechanic is the single most technical and easily-misstated part of the topic.

## Gap/Angle
- **Critical accuracy flag for the writer**: this topic has one clean headline rule (10% of net/baseline estate to charity → main rate drops from 40% to 36%) but a genuinely nuanced underlying mechanic — the "net estate"/baseline amount is calculated per **component** of the estate (general estate, survivorship property, trust/settled property, joint property), not simply "10% of the whole estate." Estates with assets split across these components may need to test each component separately, though HMRC allows electing to merge components in some circumstances. Do NOT oversimplify this into "just leave 10% of your estate to charity" without at least flagging that the calculation basis can differ for estates with joint or trust assets, and citing gov.uk as the authority for the precise mechanic. If the writer cannot verify the exact component rules against a current gov.uk/IHTM source at drafting time, the copy should say so plainly ("the calculation is done per component of the estate — for anything beyond a simple estate, get this checked") rather than inventing worked numbers for the component-split case.
- Most results are charity-fundraising pages ("leave a gift to charity and cut your tax bill") written to encourage giving, not neutral explainers of the mechanic — there is room for a genuinely balanced, mechanics-first page that neither pushes nor discourages charitable giving.
- No result clearly separates the **two distinct charity-related IHT benefits** — (1) gifts to charity are always fully IHT-exempt regardless of amount, and (2) leaving 10%+ additionally cuts the rate on the *rest* of the estate to 36%. Conflating these two is a common source of confusion worth explicitly untangling.
- No result connects this to a decision-support tool (calculator) to help a reader see whether their own estate is near the 10% threshold.
- This is a genuine tax-planning topic but must stay informational: no "you should leave X% to charity" personalised recommendation — flag that whether/how to structure a charitable legacy to access the reduced rate is a will-drafting and tax question for a solicitor or tax adviser, especially for estates with components beyond a simple single-component estate.
