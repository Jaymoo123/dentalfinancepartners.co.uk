**Figure verification against the brief's assumed numbers**: confirmed exactly as stated —
- Wedding/civil partnership gift exemption: £5,000 from a parent, £2,500 from a grandparent/great-grandparent or from one party to the other, £1,000 from anyone else.
- Small gift exemption: £250 per person, per tax year (but cannot be combined with any other exemption to the same person — e.g. cannot add £250 on top of using the £3,000 annual exemption on the same recipient).
- Annual exemption: £3,000 per tax year (can be carried forward one year if unused, giving up to £6,000 in a single year), and this can be used in conjunction with the marriage/wedding gift exemption for the same recipient.
No discrepancy found; all figures confirmed as-is via gov.uk-aligned sources (MoneyHelper, Royal London, Crowe).

## Gap/Angle
- Almost no result gives a clear worked example combining the wedding exemption with the annual exemption for the same recipient in the same year (a parent can give up to £8,000 tax-free to a marrying child: £5,000 wedding + £3,000 annual) — this is a strong differentiation opportunity with a simple table.
- The "in consideration of marriage" timing rule (gift must be made before/conditional on the marriage happening, not as a wedding-anniversary or belated gift afterwards) is inconsistently explained and is a common practical trap — worth a dedicated warning callout.
- The small gift exemption's "cannot stack with another exemption to the same person" rule is stated abruptly in most sources without explaining the practical consequence (e.g. you can't give someone £3,000 annual exemption AND £250 small gift in the same year — but you CAN use £250 small gifts to up to unlimited *different* people).
- No competitor connects this exemptions topic forward to what happens if gifts exceed these thresholds (falls back to being a PET, subject to the 7-year rule and taper relief) — a natural link to the wave-3 sibling pages on gifts and taper relief.
- Coverage of civil partnerships specifically (vs marriage only) is thin/inconsistent across results — worth confirming and stating explicitly that the same tiered exemptions apply equally to civil partnerships.
