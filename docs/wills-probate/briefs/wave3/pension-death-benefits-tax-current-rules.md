## Gap/Angle
- The single biggest gap across results: almost nobody cleanly separates the TWO different taxes that apply to a pension death benefit — (1) income tax on what the beneficiary receives (age-75 split: tax-free before 75, marginal rate at/after 75) and (2) inheritance tax on the pension's inclusion in the estate (currently generally exempt, changing from 6 April 2027). A clear two-column "income tax vs inheritance tax" framing is the structural win here.
- Content must state these as fact and must NOT give product/financial advice (e.g. no "you should take pension advice to do X", no specific product recommendations — informational only, general guidance to seek regulated financial advice where relevant).
- State plainly: effective date 6 April 2027; PRs/executors become legally responsible for reporting and paying any IHT due on unused pension funds and death benefits; death-in-service benefits from registered pension schemes are excluded from the charge; HMRC estimates 10,500 estates newly become liable for IHT for the first time per year, and a further 38,500 pay more; average increase for those paying more is around £34,000; up to 50% of the pension amount can be withheld/retained by the scheme for up to 15 months to help fund the IHT liability; spouse/civil partner exemption still applies to pensions passing to a spouse/civil partner.
- The transition rule is under-covered for a consumer audience: if death occurs before 6 April 2027, current rules apply in full even if the pension is actually paid out to the beneficiary afterwards — worth its own clearly flagged callout since it will be a common confusion point.
- The 2026/27 tax-free lump sum figures (standard £268,275; serious ill-health up to £1,073,100) are stated by only one lower-authority result (ukcalculator.com) — an opportunity to state this clearly and correctly sourced from HMRC/gov.uk framing.
