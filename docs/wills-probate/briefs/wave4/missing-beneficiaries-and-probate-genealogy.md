A Benjamin Order (from the 1902 case Re Benjamin) lets an executor distribute as if a missing beneficiary were deceased, protecting the executor from personal liability — though the beneficiary can still trace assets through those who received them.
- Genealogical research and public notices are expected before a court will grant a Benjamin Order; missing-beneficiary indemnity insurance is the lower-cost alternative for smaller/lower-risk shares.
