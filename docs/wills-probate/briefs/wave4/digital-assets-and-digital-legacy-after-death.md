## SERP Evidence
Query run: "digital legacy after death uk"

- 3 competitor domains cover this (probatebureau, the-probate-network, makeawillonline, onlineprobate.co.uk). No UK legislation currently governs digital assets directly — access is dictated by platform terms and conditions; the Property (Digital Assets etc) Act 2025 has been introduced to start addressing the gap.
- Cryptocurrency flagged as the highest-risk category — no customer service or recovery route if private keys/passwords aren't shared before death.

Verdict: **medium** competition.

## Competitor Coverage Outline
- What counts as a digital asset (social media, email, crypto, PayPal, domains, cloud storage)
- The legal gap — platform T&Cs govern access, not UK law (with the 2025 Act noted as an emerging change)
- Cryptocurrency-specific risk (private keys, no recovery)
- Practical steps: digital inventory, legacy contacts, storing access info with (not inside) the will
- How this affects executors' duties
- FAQ

## Gap / Angle
- Crypto-specific executor guidance is thin across the SERP — most pages give one line ('tell someone your password') without addressing the executor's practical problem of even knowing crypto assets exist if never disclosed; a short 'how executors can even discover crypto holdings' section (bank statements, email searches, known-exchange correspondence) is a genuine and currently-missing gap.
- The Property (Digital Assets etc) Act 2025 is very recent and under-explained on most competitor pages — flag it clearly as an emerging, not yet fully settled, area.
