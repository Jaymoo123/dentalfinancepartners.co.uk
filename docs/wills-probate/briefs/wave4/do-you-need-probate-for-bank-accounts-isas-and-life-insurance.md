## SERP Evidence
Query run: "do i need probate for premium bonds"

- Farra, AfterLoss and The Gazette rank alongside high-street bank bereavement pages (Halifax, Lloyds, Bank of Scotland, Virgin Money) rather than law firms — the SERP is dominated by bank 'what to do' hubs, not probate specialists.
- Consistent facts: each bank sets its own release threshold (roughly £5,000-£50,000); ISAs stay open as a 'continuing account of a deceased investor' for up to 3 years; life insurance in trust bypasses probate entirely and pays the named beneficiary directly.

Verdict: **soft** competition.

## Gap / Angle
- No single source tabulates the actual bank-by-bank thresholds — we can build a comparison table (Lloyds, Halifax, Barclays, NatWest, Nationwide, HSBC) as a genuinely useful asset none of the bank pages themselves provide neutrally.
- Premium Bonds/NS&I specific rules (its own £5,000-ish limit and process, distinct from bank accounts) barely feature despite being the literal ranked keyword — clear content gap to lead with.
- Weak coverage of what happens when life insurance is NOT written in trust (falls into estate, needs probate) — most pages only cover the in-trust case.
