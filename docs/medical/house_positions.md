## 1. Employment status of doctors (locked, 2026-06-03)

**Position.** A doctor's tax status follows the **substance of the working arrangement, not the label**, and the four common medical structures sit differently:
- **GP partner**: self-employed. The partnership files an **SA800**; each partner's share flows to the **partnership pages (SA104)** of their personal return and is taxed as trading income with **Class 4 NIC** (see §8). Partners are taxed on their **profit share, not their drawings**.
- **Salaried GP**: an **employee** of the practice, taxed under **PAYE with Class 1 NIC** deducted at source, and an active NHS Pension member.
- **GP locum**: usually a **sole trader (SA103)** or, less commonly, operates through a **personal service company (PSC)** (see §1.A for the off-payroll consequences). A sole-trader locum has no intermediary, so IR35 does not apply (status is judged on the employed-vs-self-employed factors instead).
- **Hospital consultant**: an **employee of the NHS** (PAYE, Class 1, NHS pension) who may also do **private work** as a sole trader, partnership or company. Only the NHS employment is pensionable (see §2.C).

Status rests on the established case-law factors (control, personal service / substitution, mutuality of obligation, financial risk, integration); no single factor is decisive. HMRC applies these via the **Check Employment Status for Tax (CEST)** tool and the Employment Status Manual.

- **Statutory / authority hooks:** there is no single statutory "self-employment" test; status rests on case-law factors derived from *Ready Mixed Concrete (South East) Ltd v Minister of Pensions* [1968] 2 QB 497 and later employment-status case law. Partnership taxation under the **Income Tax (Trading and Other Income) Act 2005** and partnership-return machinery under the **Taxes Management Act 1970**.
- **HMRC manual anchors:** **ESM0500+** (employment status, the irreducible minimum and the factors); CEST guidance at ESM11000+.
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** lead with "substance over paperwork". Map each of the four roles to its return (partner SA800 to SA104; salaried PAYE; locum SA103 or PSC; consultant PAYE plus private-work return). State that a GP partner is taxed on profit share, not drawings. Do NOT cite a fixed statutory section as the status test (there isn't one). Cross-ref §1.A for PSC locums, §4 (partnership capital), §2.C (consultant private work not pensionable).

### 1.A IR35 / off-payroll for locum doctors through a PSC (locked, 2026-06-03)

**Position.** A locum doctor operating through a **personal service company (PSC)** is within the off-payroll / IR35 framework. Who decides status depends on the hirer:
- **Public-sector hirers (NHS Trusts, NHS bodies)**: from **6 April 2017** the public-sector hirer (or, in a chain, the agency as fee-payer) **determines status** and operates PAYE/NIC on inside-IR35 engagements.
- **Medium or large private-sector hirers** (private hospitals, large clinics): from **6 April 2021** the hirer determines status, issues a **Status Determination Statement (SDS)** with reasons, and the fee-payer deducts.
- **Small private-sector clients** (within the Companies Act 2006 "small company" thresholds): the **old rule** survives, the **PSC itself decides** its IR35 status and accounts for any deemed payment.

Inside IR35, the fee-payer pays the PSC net of PAYE/NIC, so tax-efficient salary/dividend extraction of that income is not available; outside IR35, the PSC is paid gross and extracts normally. A locum across several hirers can hold a mix of inside and outside determinations. The **April 2024 PAYE-offset change** lets HMRC set off taxes the worker and PSC have already paid against the deemed employer's PAYE liability (removing the old double-counting in settlements); it does **not** change who decides status, who pays, or which engagements are caught.

- **Statutory hooks:** the original intermediaries legislation (IR35) is **Chapter 8, Part 2, Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003 (ITEPA 2003)**; the client-led off-payroll rules are **Chapter 10, Part 2 ITEPA 2003** (public sector from 6 Apr 2017, medium/large private from 6 Apr 2021). https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2003/1
- **HMRC manual anchors:** **ESM10000+** (off-payroll working / IR35).
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** state that for NHS Trust work the **SDS sits with the Trust/fee-payer, not the locum**; the locum can challenge within the client-led disagreement process. Distinguish the three hirer types (public from 2017, medium/large private from 2021, small private = PSC decides). Frame the April 2024 change as a set-off mechanic only. Do NOT write "IR35 was abolished", "IR35 doesn't apply to NHS work", "the off-payroll rules were repealed", or "the locum always decides their own status". Cross-ref §5 (whether the PSC route still earns its overhead), §2.C (PSC dividend income is not pensionable).

## 2. NHS Pension Scheme for doctors (locked, 2026-06-03)

**Position.** Doctors are in the **same NHS Pension Scheme** as dentists, a defined-benefit scheme in three sections. **1995 section** (final salary, 1/80th accrual + automatic 3x lump sum, normal pension age 60, closed to new entrants from 1 Apr 2008). **2008 section** (final salary, 1/60th, no automatic lump sum, NPA 65). **2015 section** (Career Average Revalued Earnings / CARE, **1/54th** of each year's pensionable earnings, active revaluation **CPI + 1.5%**, NPA linked to State Pension Age, or 65 if later). ### 2.A McCloud remedy (locked, 2026-06-03)

**Position.** The 2015 transitional protections were held age-discriminatory (*McCloud / Sargeant*). The remedy: service in the **remedy period 1 April 2015 to 31 March 2022** for eligible members was **rolled back into the legacy section (1995 or 2008) from 1 October 2023**, with a **deferred choice at retirement** (the Deferred Choice Underpin) between legacy and 2015 terms for that period. Eligibility: a member who **joined the scheme on or before 31 March 2012 and was an active member on 1 April 2015**. The mandatory (retrospective) remedy is complete; the live elements are the **in-retirement election** and any **annual-allowance / tax reconciliation** via the **NHS Digital Remedy Service** and HMRC. ### 2.C Pensionable pay, GP certification and the incorporation trap (locked, 2026-06-03; deadlines and contribution tiers added 2026-08-26)

**Position (the key medical divergence from the dentists doc).** The NHS pension distinguishes **practitioners** from **officers**, and the GP certification machinery is specific to medicine:
- **Type 1 medical practitioner** (a GP provider / partner, GMS/PMS contract-holder): pensionable earnings derive from net NHS-derived profit; the partner completes the **Annual Certificate of Pensionable Profits** (Type 1) each year via PCSE, including any locum or solo income.
- **Type 2 medical practitioner** (a salaried GP, and freelance/solo GP work): completes the **Type 2 self-assessment** form (submitted a year in arrears) so the correct tiered contribution is recorded.
- **Freelance GP locums** pension their income via **GP Locum forms A and B** and pay over via the Solo / PCSE route; they do not file an end-of-year certificate but must ensure the correct contribution tier is paid.

**Deadlines (verified at PCSE, 2026-08-26).** All three end-of-year routes run to a **28 February** deadline **a year in arrears**: the 2024/25 pension year was due **28 February 2026**, so the **2025/26 year is due 28 February 2027**. This applies to the **Type 1 Annual Certificate** (https://pcse.england.nhs.uk/services/gp-pensions/end-year-processes/gp-non-gp-partners-type-1-annual-certificate ), the **Type 2 self-assessment** (https://pcse.england.nhs.uk/services/gp-pensions/end-year-processes/salaried-and-solo-gps-type-2-self-assessment ) and, at practice level, the forward-looking **Estimate of Pensionable Profits** (https://pcse.england.nhs.uk/gp-practices/managing-pensions/estimate-pensionable-profits ), which practices file so that in-year contributions are collected at roughly the right tier and the year-end reconciliation is small. Use **14.38%** wherever the sentence computes what a practice, a PCN or any other employer actually bears, including every ARRS or staff-cost worked example. **The incorporation pension trap.** A **doctor's ordinary personal service company cannot hold a GMS/PMS contract** (the contract sits with GPs, their partnerships, or a company limited by shares whose shareholders all qualify, see the correction immediately below), and **income routed through a company is not NHS-pensionable**.

> **CORRECTION, 2026-08-26, and it applies everywhere this sentence appears in this file and on the site.** The first limb as written is **too absolute and is wrong as a flat statement**. **NHS Act 2006 s.86(1)** permits the Board to enter a GMS contract with "a medical practitioner", "two or more individuals practising in partnership where the conditions in subsection (2) are satisfied", **or "a company limited by shares where the conditions in subsection (3) are satisfied"**. Section 86(3) requires that "at least one share in the company is both legally and beneficially owned by a medical practitioner" and that any other share is owned by a person within s.86(2)(b) (broadly NHS employees, specified healthcare employees, healthcare professionals engaged in NHS service provision, and persons within s.93(1)(d)). Verified at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/41/section/86 on 2026-08-26.
>
> **The correct position:** a GMS contract CAN be held by a company limited by shares, but only a tightly restricted one whose shareholders are all qualifying persons with at least one medical practitioner among them. An ordinary personal service company, or a company with any outside shareholder, cannot hold one. **The pension limb is unaffected and remains correct**: income taken through a company as dividends is not NHS-pensionable, whatever the company is.
>
> **Practical writing rule.** Never write "a limited company cannot hold a GMS or PMS contract" flat. Write that the contract sits with GPs, their partnerships, or a company limited by shares meeting the s.86(3) shareholder conditions, and that a doctor's ordinary personal service company does not meet them. Then make the pension point, which is the one that actually decides the reader's question.
>
> **GMS and PMS are DIFFERENT PROVISIONS and s.86 does not cover both (added 2026-08-26, caught by a writer refusing to over-pin a citation).** s.86 is the GMS contracting power and is where the shareholder conditions live. **PMS agreements are made under s.92**, which reads "The Board may make agreements, other than arrangements pursuant to section 83(2) or general medical services contracts, under which primary medical services are provided", names no categories of eligible person at all, and delegates the detail to regulations under **s.94**. Verified at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/41/section/92 on 2026-08-26. **So cite s.86(3) only where the sentence is about GMS.** Where a sentence covers "GMS or PMS" together, use the unpinned accurate form ("a company limited by shares whose shareholders all qualify") rather than attaching the GMS subsection to both, which would be a smaller version of the same over-claiming defect. Nobody has yet read the s.94 regulations for the PMS limb; until someone does, do not assert a specific PMS shareholder test.
>

So private-practice or locum income taken as **dividends loses NHS accrual entirely**. ### 2.D Scheme Pays mechanics (locked, 2026-06-03)

**Position.** Where an annual allowance charge arises, **Scheme Pays** lets the NHS scheme settle it for a permanent reduction in benefits. **Mandatory Scheme Pays** is available where the charge **exceeds £2,000 AND** the pension input amount in the NHS scheme alone exceeds the **standard £60,000** annual allowance (a charge driven only by the taper below £60k is voluntary-only). The **election deadline is 31 July** in the year following the year the charge crystallised, per FA 2004 s.237BA, so a **2025/26 charge must be elected by 31 July 2027** and a **2026/27 charge by 31 July 2028**. Verified 2026-08-26 at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2004/12/section/237BA (notice "not later than 31 July in the year following the year in which the tax year ends") and https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/pensions-tax-manual/ptm056430 . Two qualifications on that deadline, both verified the same day: it is **extended** where a revised pension savings statement is issued **on or after 2 May**, to the earlier of **3 months from that statement** or **6 years from the end of the tax year** (this is the limb NHS members most often need, because NHSBSA statements are routinely late and revised); and it is **brought forward** where the member is about to become entitled to all their benefits, so there is **no election once all benefits are taken**. **Citation precision, resolved 2026-08-26 and then CORRECTED the same day:** the brought-forward limb IS statutory and is in **FA 2004 s.237B(6)**, verbatim "A notice may not be given after the individual becomes actually entitled to all of the individual's benefits under the pension scheme" (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2004/12/section/237B, re-fetched and read 2026-08-26). Cite s.237B(6) for it. An earlier note here claimed it was NOT statutory and sat only on HMRC PTM056430 plus the wider regulations. That claim was WRONG and is withdrawn. It arose because a writer searched s.237BA, the DEADLINE section, could not find the limb there and correctly declined to state it, and the coordinator then generalised that absence into 'not in the statute' without re-reading s.237B. The lesson is the estate's own rule applied to itself: a claim is not an input until re-derived, and that includes a coordinator's ruling. s.237BA carries the 31 July limb and the 2 May extended limb; s.237B(6) carries the brought-forward limb. The £2,000 and annual-allowance conditions were re-verified at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2004/12/section/237B ("exceeds £2,000" and input amount exceeding "the amount of the annual allowance specified in section 228(1) for the tax year"). The reduction is applied via scheme-set (NHSBSA/GAD) factors.

- **Statutory hooks:** FA 2004 **s.237B** (Scheme Pays liability), **s.237BA** (deadline). https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2004/12/section/237B
- **HMRC manual anchors:** **PTM056400+**.
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** distinguish mandatory (charge > £2,000 AND scheme input > £60k) from voluntary Scheme Pays; state the 31 July deadline with a worked example (**2026/27 charge to 31 July 2028**; use the 2025/26-to-31-July-2027 pairing only where the page is about the year just closed). ## 3. NHS GP contracts and practice income (locked, 2026-06-03)

**Position (the medical analogue of the dentists UDA section, but DOCTORS DO NOT USE UDAs).** NHS primary medical care in England is delivered under three contract types: **GMS** (General Medical Services, nationally negotiated, governed by the **Statement of Financial Entitlements**), **PMS** (Personal Medical Services, locally agreed) and **APMS** (Alternative Provider Medical Services). Core practice funding is the **Global Sum**, a per-patient payment **weighted by the Carr-Hill formula** (registered list size adjusted across six factors, per the shipped Carr-Hill page verified against the SFE 2026-08-26: age and sex, additional needs, list turnover, nursing and residential homes, rurality and geography, and a staff market-forces adjustment, to give weighted patients; corrected 2026-09-01, the earlier "age, sex, morbidity, list turnover and geography" paraphrase was wrong), **plus QOF** (the Quality and Outcomes Framework, a voluntary points-based quality scheme), **enhanced services**, and **PCN / Network Contract DES** funding (including the Additional Roles Reimbursement Scheme, ARRS). **Dispensing practices** also earn dispensing income. **Partner income is a profit share** (drawings taken against anticipated profit, trued up at year-end), not a salary. ## 4. Practice sale, goodwill, premises and CGT (locked, 2026-06-03)

**Position (CRITICAL medical divergence).** Unlike a dental practice, **the sale of NHS goodwill has been prohibited since 1 April 2004** for GMS/PMS (and now APMS) practices. The prohibition is currently in **The Primary Medical Services (Prohibition on the Sale of Goodwill) Regulations 2019 (SI 2019/251)**, in force from 1 April 2019, which **revoked and re-enacted** the original **2004 Regulations (SI 2004/906)** that first imposed the ban from 1 April 2004. A GP (or anyone on their behalf) **cannot sell the goodwill of an NHS medical practice**, and cannot sell company shares whose value includes that goodwill. So the **dentist-style "sell your NHS goodwill and claim BADR" playbook does NOT translate to GPs.**

**What actually changes hands** on a partner's entry or exit, or on a practice transaction, is:
- a share of the partnership's **tangible assets** (equipment, fixtures, working capital) and any **owned premises**;
- **partnership capital accounts** (a buy-in pays for the incoming partner's share of net assets; a buy-out returns the outgoing partner's capital);
- for any **private / non-NHS work**, transferable **private goodwill** (this is the only goodwill a GP can sell).

**Premises** are a bigger feature for GPs than for dentists. Premises are often held in a **separate property partnership or LLP** outside the medical partnership; income support comes via **notional rent** (owner-occupiers, current-market-rent basis assessed by the District Valuer), **cost rent** (the legacy borrowing-based scheme, closed to new schemes; **see the correction below on "converting on mortgage redemption"**) or **improvement grants**, under the **NHS (General Medical Services) Premises Costs Directions 2024** (in force 10 May 2024, replacing the 2013 Directions; verified at the HP-lock gate 2026-06-03). Notional-rent amounts are property-specific and District-Valuer-assessed, so do NOT lock a figure.

> **CORRECTION, 2026-08-26: there is no cost-rent conversion rule in the 2024 Directions, and this file previously implied there was.** The phrase "cost rent" appears exactly ONCE in the whole instrument, incidentally, in the mortgage-redemption and deficit-grant exclusions. Nothing requires a legacy cost-rent practice to convert to notional rent on redemption. What the Directions actually provide is the modern borrowing-cost route: **direction 40(1)(a)** runs borrowing-cost payments until "the loan is paid off", **direction 40(1)(b)** also ends them where notional rent is granted under direction 42, and **direction 42(2) to (3)** prevent notional rent running alongside borrowing costs while giving a contractor who elects to stop them the right to be granted notional rent on application. So the outcome is mechanically similar for the borrowing-cost route, but it is an election the contractor makes, not an automatic conversion, and it is not a statement about the legacy cost-rent scheme at all.
>
> **Practical writing rule.** Never write that cost rent "converts to notional rent on mortgage redemption". Write the borrowing-cost mechanism, name direction 40 and direction 42, and tell a legacy cost-rent practice to confirm its own scheme terms with the commissioner. **Found by a batch 3 writer that was asked to verify the claim before asserting it and reported that it could not be verified**, which is the correct outcome of the UNVERIFIED rule rather than a failure of it. The **"last man standing" risk** (a single remaining partner left holding the whole premises liability and lease) is a real planning point. Capital-account and premises-share valuation, not goodwill, drives the numbers.

**Business Asset Disposal Relief (BADR)** applies only to a **private practice disposal** or a **share sale of an incorporated private practice**, never to NHS goodwill (which cannot be sold). Rate (re-verified 2026-08-26 at https://www.gov.uk/business-asset-disposal-relief ): **10%** to 5 Apr 2025; **14%** 6 Apr 2025 to 5 Apr 2026; **18%** from 6 Apr 2026, which is **the rate in force today**, so write it in the present tense and treat 14% as last year's rate. **The rate BADR is saving against (added 2026-08-26, because a page needed it and it was not recorded here):** the main CGT rate on assets other than residential property is **24% for a higher-rate taxpayer from 6 April 2026**, and 18% for a basic-rate taxpayer up to the basic-rate band limit with 24% above it (https://www.gov.uk/capital-gains-tax/rates , read 2026-08-26). So for a GP partner, who is almost always a higher-rate taxpayer on a premises disposal, **BADR is worth up to 6 percentage points**, not the much larger saving the old 10% rate used to deliver. Say the saving, not just the relief rate. **£1m lifetime limit** per individual; conditions held throughout the **2-year period to disposal** (trading business/company; for a share sale, **5% of ordinary share capital and 5% of voting rights** plus **officer or employee** status and 5% economic entitlement).

**s.162 incorporation relief** (TCGA 1992 s.162) defers CGT on transfer of a **private practice** as a going concern to a company **wholly or partly for shares**. **FA 2026 s.39 (verified at primary source at the HP-lock gate, 2026-06-03)** changes s.162 for transfers **on or after 6 April 2026**: the relief is **no longer automatic**, it must be **claimed** (the claim, with the required information, by one year after the 31 January following the tax year of the transfer), and the old **s.162A election to disapply s.162 is removed** (relief is now opt-in by claim rather than automatic-with-an-opt-out). For transfers before 6 April 2026 the old automatic basis still applies. **Disposal timing**: the CGT date of disposal is the **contract date** where unconditional (TCGA 1992 s.28(1)); a contract conditional on a third-party consent is dated when the condition is met (s.28(2)), which is the lever for the 6 Apr 2026 BADR step.

- **Statutory hooks:** **The Primary Medical Services (Prohibition on the Sale of Goodwill) Regulations 2019 (SI 2019/251)** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2019/251/made (revoking **SI 2004/906** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2004/906/contents/made ); TCGA 1992 **s.169H to s.169S** (BADR) https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/12 ; TCGA 1992 **s.162** (incorporation relief, amended by FA 2026 s.39) https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/12/section/162 ; TCGA 1992 **s.28** (time of disposal).

## 5. Profit extraction and incorporation (locked, 2026-06-03)

**Position.** Incorporation is a **private-practice / private-work decision only**: a doctor's ordinary limited company **cannot hold an NHS GMS/PMS contract** (see the §2.C correction of 2026-08-26: NHS Act 2006 s.86(1)(c) does permit a company limited by shares to hold one, but only where every shareholder is a qualifying person under s.86(3) and at least one is a medical practitioner, which a personal service company is not) and company income is **not NHS-pensionable** (see §2.C). The relevant population is consultants' and GPs' **private income** (insurance medicals, medico-legal, occupational health, cosmetic, self-pay clinics) and **PSC locum work that is outside IR35** (§1.A). Structures: sole trader / partnership / **LLP** (members taxed personally, with limited liability) / **limited company** (corporation tax on profits, extraction via salary + dividend).

**Corporation tax (verified 2026-08-26, https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/rates-and-allowances-corporation-tax/rates-and-allowances-corporation-tax ):** **19%** small-profits (profits ≤ £50,000), **25%** main (> £250,000), **marginal relief** tapering between (standard fraction 3/200, FY2025 and FY2026, unchanged for the financial year beginning 1 April 2026). The effective marginal rate in the £50k to £250k band is ~26.5%.

**Dividend tax (verified 2026-08-26, https://www.gov.uk/tax-on-dividends , which is now headed "6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027"):**
- **2026/27, current** (FA 2026 s.4): ordinary **10.75%**, upper **35.75%**, additional **39.35%** unchanged; allowance **£500**.
- 2025/26 and earlier, historic: ordinary **8.75%**, upper **33.75%**, additional **39.35%**; allowance **£500**.
- Tag the year on every dividend figure. The 10.75% / 35.75% rates are **live now**, so do not write them as an upcoming change.

**Personal income tax / NIC (2026/27, verified 2026-08-26 at https://www.gov.uk/income-tax-rates and https://www.gov.uk/self-employed-national-insurance-rates ; all figures unchanged from 2025/26):** personal allowance £12,570 (tapered above £100,000); basic 20% to £50,270; higher 40% to £125,140; additional 45% above. **Class 4 NIC 6%** (£12,570 to £50,270) then **2%** above £50,270.

**Employer cost of salary (limited company):** the company pays **employer (secondary Class 1) NIC at 15%** on salary above the **£5,000 secondary threshold** (from 6 April 2025, unchanged for 2026/27; verified 2026-08-26 at https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/rates-and-allowances-national-insurance-contributions/rates-and-allowances-national-insurance-contributions , which gives 15% above a secondary threshold of £96 per week for 2026 to 2027). The **Employment Allowance is £10,500** (verified 2026-08-26 at https://www.gov.uk/claim-employment-allowance ) but is **not available to a company whose only employee is a single director**, which is why one-director medical companies often set salary at or near the secondary threshold (a genuinely employed spouse on market-rate pay can change that). **Employer pension contributions** are a tax-efficient route: **deductible for corporation tax on a paid basis** (FA 2004 s.196, wholly and exclusively), with no NIC, subject to the §2.B annual allowance and carry-forward.

**Director's loan / s.455:** a medical company is a **close company**, so an **overdrawn director's loan** triggers a **s.455 charge** at the dividend upper rate on the balance outstanding **9 months and 1 day** after the period-end: **35.75% on loans made on or after 6 April 2026** (the current rate, tracking the FA 2026 dividend upper rate) and **33.75% on loans made in 2025/26 or earlier**. Date-band by when the loan was **made**, not when it fell due. The charge is temporary, repaid under **s.458** once the loan is cleared, but the relief is **deferred** to 9 months and 1 day after the end of the accounting period of repayment (4-year claim window).

**Headline point:** at **2026/27** rates the pure tax saving from incorporating private work is modest at typical profit levels, and the dividend-rate rise that took effect on **6 April 2026** has already narrowed it (write this as a change that has happened, not one that is coming); the real drivers are managing the **annual-allowance taper** (routing private income outside pensionable pay), retained earnings, family-shareholder planning and asset protection. Always pair the comparison with the **NHS-pension-accrual loss** (§2.C).

- **Statutory hooks:** CTA 2010 Part 3 (small-profits rate and marginal relief, ss.18 to 19); ITTOIA 2005 Part 4 Ch 3 (dividends); ITA 2007 s.8 (dividend rates, amended by FA 2026 s.4); SSCBA 1992 s.15 (Class 4); CTA 2010 s.455 / s.458 (loans to participators) https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/4/section/455 ; FA 2004 s.196 (employer pension deduction).
- **HMRC manual anchors:** dividends BIM/CTM; **CTM61500+** (loans to participators); spouse-wages deductibility **BIM37700+** (wages wholly and exclusively for the trade and commensurate with work done).
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** never state a dividend rate without its tax-year tag; **for current advice use 10.75% / 35.75% / 39.35% (2026/27)** and treat 8.75% / 33.75% as historic. ## 6. VAT for medical professionals (locked, 2026-06-03)

**Position.** The supply of **medical care by a registered medical practitioner** (and by other registered health professionals) is **exempt** from VAT under **VATA 1994 Schedule 9 Group 7 Item 1** (Item 2 and the related items cover other registered professionals and persons supervised by them). The exemption turns on the **principal purpose** of the supply being the **protection, maintenance or restoration of health** of the individual, and the service falling within the professional's registered field. **NHS GMS/PMS income is outside the scope of VAT**; exempt and outside-the-scope income do not count towards the registration threshold.

**Standard-rated watch-items** (no, or insufficient, therapeutic purpose):
- **purely cosmetic / aesthetic procedures** with no therapeutic purpose (cosmetic Botox/fillers, some aesthetic clinic work);
- **medico-legal and expert-witness reports** and other services whose primary purpose is to enable a **third party to make a decision** (these are taxable even though a doctor provides them);
- some **occupational-health** services and certain certification/administrative services with no care element.

**Registration threshold £90,000** of **taxable (non-exempt) turnover only**; **deregistration threshold £88,000**. Both verified 2026-08-26 and **unchanged for 2026/27**: registration at https://www.gov.uk/register-for-vat/when-to-register ("your total taxable turnover for the last 12 months goes over £90,000") and both limits at https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/vat-notice-7001-should-i-be-registered-for-vat/vat-notice-7001-supplement--2 ("The current registration threshold for taxable supplies is £90,000", "The current deregistration limit is £88,000"), each in force since 1 April 2024. Registration is required **within 30 days of the end of the month** in which the threshold was crossed. A practitioner with both exempt and taxable supplies operates **partial exemption**: exempt input VAT is recoverable only if it passes the **de minimis** test (average **£625 per month / £7,500 per year AND no more than 50% of total input VAT**).

- **Statutory hooks:** **VATA 1994 Schedule 9 Group 7** (health and welfare; Item 1 registered medical practitioners) https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1994/23/schedule/9 ; registration / deregistration in **VATA 1994 Schedule 1**; partial exemption in the VAT Regulations 1995 (SI 1995/2518) regs 99 to 110.
- **HMRC manual anchors:** **VATHLT2000+** (VAT: health; the medical-care / principal-purpose test, VATHLT2010 doctors); partial exemption **PE21500+**.
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** lead with "medical care by a registered practitioner is VAT-exempt under Sch 9 Group 7 where the principal purpose is the protection, maintenance or restoration of health". Frame cosmetic-only work, medico-legal/expert-witness reports and some occupational health as the watch-items that can be standard-rated. Use the **£90,000** registration / **£88,000** deregistration thresholds and note they count **non-exempt** turnover only. Do NOT say "all medical income is exempt" without the cosmetic / medico-legal carve-out. Do NOT say "private medical services are standard-rated by default" (the default for genuine private medical care is exempt). Do NOT use the old £85,000 threshold for current advice. Cross-ref §5, §10 (medico-legal as private-work income), §6.A (dispensing practices).

### 6.A Dispensing-practice drug VAT (locked, 2026-06-03, HP-lock gate, Wave 2 C1)

**Position.** The VAT on drugs in a **dispensing GP practice** has three limbs (verified at primary source: HMRC Revenue and Customs Brief 2 (2020), VAT Notice 701/57, VATHLT6020). **Dispensed NHS prescription drugs** (qualifying goods dispensed to an individual for their personal use on the prescription of an appropriate practitioner, meeting the five RCB 2 (2020) conditions) are **ZERO-RATED** under **VATA 1994 Schedule 8 Group 12 Item 1**, NOT exempt. Because the supply is zero-rated rather than exempt, the practice can **recover the input VAT** on those drugs, which is why dispensing practices commonly register for VAT voluntarily. Drugs **personally administered** by the doctor during treatment (most vaccines and injections) are **exempt** under Sch 9 Group 7 (no input recovery). **Private-prescription** drugs are **standard-rated**.

- **Statutory hooks:** **VATA 1994 Schedule 8 Group 12 Item 1** (zero-rate, dispensing); Schedule 9 Group 7 (administered drugs, exempt). https://www.gov.uk/guidance/health-professionals-pharmaceutical-products-and-vat-notice-70157
- **HMRC manual anchors:** **VATHLT6000+** (drugs, medicines, aids); RCB 2 (2020).
- **Practical writing rule for sessions:** for a dispensing practice say dispensed NHS prescription drugs are **zero-rated (Sch 8 Group 12), not exempt**, so input VAT is recoverable; administered drugs are exempt; private-prescription drugs standard-rated. Do NOT call dispensed NHS drugs "exempt" (that loses the input-VAT recovery). Do NOT lock Drug Tariff prices or dispensing fees (they uplift). Cross-ref §3 (dispensing income), §6.

**Position.** **Annual Investment Allowance (AIA) £1,000,000** per year (permanent; verified 2026-08-26 at https://www.gov.uk/capital-allowances/annual-investment-allowance , "The AIA amount is £1 million"), giving 100% first-year relief on qualifying plant and machinery, which covers most clinical and consulting equipment (diagnostic kit, IT, consulting-room furniture, instruments) and excludes cars. ### 7.B Structures and Buildings Allowance (VERIFIED AT SOURCE 2026-08-26)

**Position.** Plant and machinery allowances do not reach the structure of a building, but **Structures and Buildings Allowance (SBA) does**. SBA gives **3% a year on a straight-line basis** over an allowance period of **33 and one third years**, where **all construction contracts were signed on or after 29 October 2018**, for non-residential use. A **written allowance statement is required** before any claim. The sting is at the other end: **the total SBA claimed is added to the disposal receipts** when the building is sold, which increases the chargeable gain. SBA is therefore a timing benefit against a later CGT cost, not a free deduction, and that interaction is the useful thing to write about it on a surgery-premises page.

**Why this section exists.** A live Medical page stated as fact that you cannot claim capital allowances on the structure of a building. That is wrong, and SBA is the reason. The error was found during batch 3 (BATCH3_INDEX D15) and corrected on the page the same day; this section is the ground truth that should have existed first.

**Source, verbatim.** https://www.gov.uk/guidance/claiming-capital-allowances-for-structures-and-buildings , read 2026-08-26: rate "3%" (from 1 April 2020 for Corporation Tax, 6 April 2020 for Income Tax); period "33 and one third years from the allowance period start date"; "All construction contracts must have been signed on or after 29 October 2018"; "You must add the total amount of structures and buildings allowance you've claimed to your disposal receipts to calculate your capital gain or loss"; "You must have an allowance statement", and a first user "must create a written allowance statement before you can make a claim".

- **Statutory hooks:** **CAA 2001 Part 2A** (SBA). The 2% to 3% uplift took effect 1 April 2020 (CT) / 6 April 2020 (IT).

**Amendment check: NOT RUN, and this is a known gap.** The standing method rule is to check a provision for amending instruments before locking a figure. That sweep has not been run against CAA 2001 Part 2A. The gov.uk guidance page is current as at 2026-08-26 and states 3% and 33 and one third years, so the position is safe to write today, but **the next session touching SBA should run the Part 2A amendment check and upgrade this block**, in the same form as §3.A and §3.B.

**Practical writing rule.** Where a page says plant and machinery allowances do not cover the fabric of the building, say so and then say SBA does, at 3% over 33 and one third years, with the 29 October 2018 contract condition and the allowance-statement requirement. ### 8.A Employment Allowance and GP practices: the answer is normally NO (VERIFIED AT SOURCE 2026-08-26)

**Position.** The Employment Allowance (**£10,500** for 2026/27) is **not available to a body whose activities are wholly or mainly of a public nature**, and HMRC treats NHS GP services as exactly that. A typical NHS GP practice therefore **cannot** claim it. The "wholly or mainly" test is 50% or more, so a practice whose private work is a minority, which is almost all of them, is caught.

**Source, verbatim.** HMRC **NIM06530**, listing examples of functions of a public nature: "NHS services, general practitioner (GP) services where NHS patients take up the majority of a GP's work". The same manual page lists, as functions that are NOT of a public nature, "independent pharmacies that include dispensing NHS prescriptions as part of their business", which is the contrast that makes the GP position unambiguous rather than inferred. gov.uk's eligibility page carries the same test in plain words: you can claim if "you do less than half your work in the public sector (such as for local councils and NHS services)". Statutory hook: **NICA 2014 s.2(1) to (2)**. https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim06530 and https://www.gov.uk/claim-employment-allowance/eligibility

**The exceptions, which is where the real work is.** A **registered charity** is not excluded even where its functions are of a public nature. A **separate PCN company or GP federation** is fact-sensitive and turns on what that entity actually does, so it is a question to answer for the entity in front of you, never a general yes.

**Why this section exists.** A batch 3 page stated the opposite four times, in the body, in a key takeaway and in an FAQ: that HMRC treats GP practices as falling outside the restriction so a practice can generally claim. That is backwards, and it had been carried into budgeting advice as "the one lever that reduces the employer National Insurance an ARRS post generates above the cap". Caught by the adversarial factual QA track before publication.

**Practical writing rule.** Never tell a GP practice it can claim the Employment Allowance. Say the practice normally cannot, give the wholly-or-mainly test, name the charity exception and say a separate PCN company or federation needs its own answer. Where a page models employer NIC on practice staff, do NOT net off £10,500. Cross-ref §2.C (the 14.38% employer pension split, the other half of any employer-cost calculation).

## 9. MTD for Income Tax Self Assessment (locked, 2026-06-03)

State most full-time locums and unincorporated private GPs **are already in scope**, having been caught from April 2026. ## 11. Uniform and laundry flat rate expenses, and how an employment expense claim is made (locked 2026-08-26)

**Position.** An employee who has to launder, repair or replace a uniform or specialist clothing at their own expense can deduct the cost under **ITEPA 2003 s.367** (cost of small tools and special clothing). Rather than prove actual spend, most employees take an **agreed flat rate expense** for their occupation. **There is no single "NHS rate".** There are five healthcare occupational rates, and using the wrong one is the error the competitor layer keeps making.

**The flat rate expenses for health and care staff (per tax year, verified 2026-08-26).** These apply to staff employed by the National Health Service, private hospitals, local authorities and independent care providers:

| Occupational group | Flat rate expense |
|---|---|
| Ambulance staff on active service | **£185** |
| Nurses, midwives, chiropodists, dental nurses, occupational, speech, physiotherapists and other therapists, healthcare assistants, phlebotomists and radiographers | **£125** |
| Plaster room orderlies, hospital porters, ward clerks, sterile supply workers, hospital domestics and hospital catering staff | **£125** |
| Laboratory staff, pharmacists and pharmacy assistants | **£80** |
| Uniformed ancillary staff: maintenance workers, grounds staff, drivers, parking attendants and security guards, receptionists and other uniformed staff | **£80** |

**Plus, for the nursing group, two shoe and hosiery allowances (CORRECTED 2026-08-26 on both the test and the group).** A nurse may add **£12 a year for shoes** and **£6 a year for stockings, tights or socks** on top of the £125, giving a maximum of **£143**. These are separate additional amounts, not alternatives.

**The test is a PRESCRIBED STYLE being obligatory, not self-provision.** HMRC **EIM67200**, verbatim: "**shoes: where the wearing of a prescribed style is obligatory in the hospital or other workplace in which they may work allow £12 per year**"; "**stockings/tights/socks: where the wearing of a prescribed style or colour is similarly obligatory, allow £6 per year**" (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim67200 , fetched 2026-08-26). So the question the page must ask the reader is **"does your employer mandate a style or colour"**, not "do you buy them yourself". An earlier version of this section tied the additions to nurses and midwives being *required to provide their own*, which is a different and narrower test and is wrong.

**And the group is wider than nurses and midwives, expressly including healthcare assistants.** EIM67200, verbatim: "**References to a nurse should be taken for this purpose to include nurses and midwives of all grades and includes auxiliaries, students, dental nurses, nursing assistants and healthcare assistants or workers.**" HMRC names healthcare assistants in the qualifying group, so **a page must not tell a healthcare assistant, nursing assistant, auxiliary, student nurse or dental nurse that the £12 and £6 additions are not for them.** That error has already reached a live page and is the reason this correction exists.

**Sources.** HMRC Employment Income Manual **EIM67200** (nurses, the £12 shoes and £6 stockings/tights/socks additions, the prescribed-style test and the definition of "nurse"), https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim67200 , fetched 2026-08-26. HMRC Employment Income Manual **EIM32712** (flat rate expenses table, health and care staff), https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim32712 , fetched 2026-08-26, which gives the statutory basis as "Section 367 ITEPA 2003" and cross-refers general laundry expenses not covered by an agreement to **EIM32485**. The same amounts are published for the public at https://www.gov.uk/guidance/job-expenses-for-uniforms-work-clothing-and-tools , fetched 2026-08-26.

**Year tagging, and an honest one.** These figures are **not annually uprated**. EIM32712 records the health and care rates as applying "for 2008 to 2009 onwards", with the amounts in the current table stated from 6 April 2014. They are therefore the rates for **2026/27** because they are the rates for every year since, not because they were set for 2026/27. Say "the current flat rate" and not "the 2026/27 flat rate", and do not imply the figure moves each April the way the Global Sum and mileage do. This is the opposite trap to §3 and §8.

**What blocks or reduces a claim.** Three rules, all of which matter to an NHS audience and all of which are verified:
- **Employer reimbursement.** "If your employer pays all of your expenses, you cannot claim any tax relief" (gov.uk job-expenses guidance, 2026-08-26). Where the employer pays part, the claim is the balance only.
- **Employer laundering facility.** You cannot claim "the cost of laundering your own uniform or specialist clothing if your employer provides a free laundering service, and you choose not to use it" (https://www.gov.uk/tax-relief-for-employees/uniforms-work-clothing-and-tools , fetched 2026-08-26). HMRC's own campaign restates it as a headline error: you cannot claim "uniform washing costs when your employer has a laundry facility that you choose not to use, such as by washing your uniform at home instead" (https://dontgetcaughtout.campaign.gov.uk/claiming-expenses/ , fetched 2026-08-26). **This is the single most common invalid NHS uniform claim and the page must say so.**
- **The initial cost of clothing is never allowable.** The relief is for cleaning, repairing and replacing, not for buying work clothing in the first place, and never for everyday clothing worn for work even where a colour or style is mandated.

**How the claim is made, and the four-year limit.** The claim routes are the **online service on gov.uk**, **form P87** by post, or, if the person already files one, the **Self Assessment return** ("If you complete a Self Assessment tax return, you must claim through your tax return instead"). The backdating limit is verbatim: **"You can claim for the current tax year and the 4 previous tax years"** (https://www.gov.uk/tax-relief-for-employees/uniforms-work-clothing-and-tools , fetched 2026-08-26). A flat rate claim is normally given as a **coding adjustment** going forward plus a repayment for the closed years, so a first claim commonly produces a lump sum and a permanently changed tax code.

### 11.A The repayment-agent position (locked 2026-08-26, and it is the point of the page)

**Position, stated plainly and without hedging.** A uniform and laundry claim is **free**, takes minutes, and is made directly to HMRC. A repayment agent will make exactly the same claim and keep a share of the refund. **There is nothing an agent can obtain that the taxpayer cannot obtain themselves, and the taxpayer remains liable for a wrong claim made in their name.** HMRC says both halves of that out loud, and both are quotable:

- **You keep everything if you do it yourself.** "If taxpayers think they are owed a tax rebate, they can claim directly from HMRC via the free and secure service on GOV.UK and will receive **100% of the money owed**" (https://www.gov.uk/government/news/greater-control-for-taxpayers-using-repayment-agents , fetched 2026-08-26). And: "It's fast, free and simple to claim work expenses with us directly via GOV.UK ... You won't have to pay any agent fees or commission if you do it yourself" (https://dontgetcaughtout.campaign.gov.uk/claiming-expenses/ , fetched 2026-08-26).
- **The liability does not transfer with the paperwork.** "Don't get caught out by online adverts and tax refund companies who offer to make work expense claims for you so they can claim a commission ... **If it turns out you aren't eligible to claim, you're still responsible for any claims made on your behalf**" (same campaign page, 2026-08-26). This is the sharper half of the argument and the one competitor pages never make: the agent takes a cut of the upside and none of the downside.
- **HMRC legislated against the mechanism.** Deeds of assignment of an income tax repayment are now void: "HMRC will now change this, by rendering void assignments, meaning they won't be legally recognised for income tax purposes ... Anyone who attempts to make an assignment for the repayment of income tax after the legislation comes into effect will find that it is invalid" (repayment-agents news release, 2026-08-26). Agents now use a nomination, which the taxpayer can withdraw, rather than an assignment, which they could not.

**Practical writing rule for sessions.** Say it in the reader's own words and early: **you can do this yourself, in minutes, for nothing, and you keep all of it.** Give the correct flat rate for the reader's occupational group from the table above, the £12 and £6 shoe and hosiery additions **with the prescribed-style test and the full EIM67200 group (nurses and midwives of all grades, auxiliaries, students, dental nurses, nursing assistants, healthcare assistants and workers)**, the four-year backdating, and the two rules that void a claim (employer reimburses, employer offers free laundering). Quote HMRC's own "100% of the money owed" and the "you're still responsible" line, because attributed regulator wording is more persuasive than our assertion and is verifiable by the reader. Do NOT state a single "NHS uniform allowance" figure, do NOT use £60 (that is the generic default for occupations with no agreed rate, not the healthcare rate), and do NOT imply the amounts uplift annually. Do NOT condition the £12 and £6 additions on the reader buying the items themselves (the test is a prescribed style or colour being obligatory), and do NOT exclude healthcare assistants, nursing assistants, auxiliaries, students or dental nurses from them (EIM67200 names them). Do NOT quote a refund in pounds without saying it is the flat rate multiplied by the reader's marginal rate: a £125 flat rate is worth **£25 to a basic-rate taxpayer and £50 to a higher-rate taxpayer**, not £125. **Every competitor page checked in the batch-2 packs got the flat rate figure wrong (£125, £100, a £6/£12/£125 split and a £60/£125/£185 split are all in the wild); take the number from the table above and nowhere else.** Cross-ref §12 (subscriptions, usually claimed on the same form), §8 (the general deduction list).

## 12. Professional subscriptions and registration fees: List 3 (locked 2026-08-26)

**Position.** An employee gets a deduction for a **statutory registration fee** under **ITEPA 2003 s.343** and for an **annual subscription to a body on HMRC's approved list** under **s.344**, where membership is relevant to the employment.

**Cite the statute and the manual for that framework, never List 3 (clarified 2026-08-26).** List 3 is a **list of approved bodies**, not authority for the two-section framework. Cite **ITEPA 2003 ss.343 and 344** and HMRC **EIM32880**, "Other expenses: professional fees and subscriptions: introduction", https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim32880 (fetched 2026-08-26), which sets out that such fees cannot qualify under s.336 because they are not incurred in performing the duties, and that relief instead comes from **s.343 for certain professional membership fees** and **s.344 for annual subscriptions to HMRC-approved bodies**. **List 3 will not support that framework if a reader checks:** searched as text on 2026-08-26, the list carries **"section 343 ITEPA 2003" around 30 times** in individual entries and **"section 344 ITEPA 2003" exactly once**, because entries annotate the statutory-fee cases and leave the ordinary subscription case unannotated. Use List 3 for one thing only, **whether a named body is approved**, and for the per-body restrictions quoted below.

The approved list is **List 3**, "Approved professional organisations and learned societies", https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/professional-bodies-approved-for-tax-relief-list-3 and the list itself at https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/professional-bodies-approved-for-tax-relief-list-3/approved-professional-organisations-and-learned-societies (fetched 2026-08-26; the list page records `dateModified` 2026-07-30, so it is actively maintained and must be re-checked, not remembered).

**Where the employer pays.** No relief. Verbatim from the guidance page: you cannot claim for "professional membership fees or annual subscriptions you have **not paid yourself** (for example, if your employer has paid for them)" (fetched 2026-08-26). Many NHS trusts and GP practices reimburse GMC or NMC fees or pay them directly, so **the first question on any subscription claim is who actually bore the cost**, and a reimbursed fee produces no claim. Where the employer reimburses and the reimbursement is taxed as earnings, the employee has borne it and the deduction is available; where it is paid direct or reimbursed free of tax, it is not.

**Interaction with the §11 uniform claim.** They are separate deductions with a common claim route. A nurse typically has both: the **£125 flat rate (plus £12 and £6 where applicable)** under s.367 and the **NMC registration fee** under s.343, and where relevant the **RCN subscription** under s.344. All go on the same **P87 or online claim**, and the same **four-year** backdating applies. Presenting them as one combined figure is fine arithmetic and bad writing, because the two have different rules about employer payment: the uniform rate is blocked by a free laundering facility, the subscription is blocked by employer payment. Keep them separate on the page.

**Fee amounts, and what is and is not pinned.**
- **NMC registration fee: £120 a year, rising to £143 a year from 1 October 2026, subject to Parliamentary approval.** Verified at the NMC's own announcement of the Council decision of 28 April 2026, https://www.nmc.org.uk/news/news-and-updates/nmc-approves-first-increase-in-registration-fees-for-11-years/ (fetched 2026-08-26), verbatim: "the main fee will increase by £23 per year, rising from £120 to £143 from 1 October, subject to Parliamentary approval." **Write both figures with the switchover date and the Parliamentary condition**, and do not write £143 as unconditional. ## 13. Maternity: SMP, Maternity Allowance, the NHS occupational scheme, pension accrual and SFE cover reimbursement (locked 2026-08-26)

**Position.** A doctor's maternity income depends on employment status, and the four layers are commonly confused. **Statutory Maternity Pay (SMP)** is an employer payment to an employee. **Maternity Allowance (MA)** is a state benefit for those who cannot get SMP, which is the self-employed and locum route. The **NHS occupational maternity pay scheme** sits on top of SMP for employed NHS staff. And the **Statement of Financial Entitlements** reimburses a **GP practice** for the cost of covering the absence, which is the layer that makes this a practice-accounting topic rather than a payroll one.

**13.1 Statutory Maternity Pay (2026/27).** **90% of average weekly earnings for the first 6 weeks**, then **£194.32 a week or 90% of average weekly earnings, whichever is lower, for the next 33 weeks**, so **39 weeks** in total. Verified at https://www.gov.uk/maternity-pay-leave/pay (fetched 2026-08-26), and the year is pinned by https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rates-and-thresholds-for-employers-2026-to-2027 (fetched 2026-08-26), which gives **£194.32** for **2026 to 2027** for SMP, Statutory Paternity Pay, Statutory Adoption Pay and Statutory Shared Parental Pay alike. **Qualifying tests**, verified at https://www.gov.uk/maternity-pay-leave/eligibility (fetched 2026-08-26): continuous employment with the same employer for **at least 26 weeks continuing into the qualifying week, being the 15th week before the expected week of childbirth**, and average earnings of **at least £129 a week**. **Where SMP is refused the employer must issue form SMP1, and the deadline has two limbs (CORRECTED 2026-08-26).** It is the **earlier of** (a) **7 days from the decision** that no liability to pay SMP exists, and (b) **28 days from the day the woman gave notice of her intended absence**, or of the confinement if the confinement came first. **Stating only the 7 days is half the rule**, and it is the wrong half where the decision is made late, because the 28-day limb can already have expired. Statutory source: the **Statutory Maternity Pay (General) Regulations 1986 (SI 1986/1960) reg 25A(4)**, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1986/1960/regulation/25A (fetched 2026-08-26), which requires the employer to return any maternity certificate and furnish written details of the decision and its reasons "**within 7 days of the decision being made, or, if earlier, within 28 days of the day the woman gave notice of her intended absence or of her confinement**", whichever of those events came first. SMP1 is the reader's route into MA, so a late or missing SMP1 is a delay to the benefit claim, not just a paperwork breach.

*Cross-check against estate ground truth.* The £129 a week SMP earnings threshold is the **Lower Earnings Limit**, and £129 x 52 = **£6,708**, which is exactly the 2026/27 LEL recorded in the estate memory `employer_nic_15pc_2025_ground_truth`. **No disagreement.** The two are the same figure expressed weekly and annually, and a page may use either provided it does not present them as two different tests.

**13.2 Maternity Allowance (2026/27).** Verified at https://www.gov.uk/maternity-allowance/what-youll-get (fetched 2026-08-26). Three routes:
- **Employed or recently stopped working:** **£194.32 a week or 90% of average weekly earnings, whichever is less, for up to 39 weeks.**
- **Self-employed:** **between £27 and £194.32 a week for up to 39 weeks**, and the amount depends on **Class 2 National Insurance**. The full rate requires registration with HMRC for at least **26 weeks** in the **66 weeks** before the due date and Class 2 paid for at least **13** of those weeks. With fewer than 13 weeks of Class 2, the award is **£27 a week**.
- **Unpaid work for the business of a spouse or civil partner:** **£27 a week for up to 14 weeks.**

**The Class 2 trap, and it is the sharpest genuinely useful point on the topic.** §8 records that **Class 2 has not been a required payment since 6 April 2024**: a self-employed doctor with profits at or above the Small Profits Threshold is *treated* as having paid. **Maternity Allowance is assessed on Class 2 actually paid.** A self-employed or locum GP who simply does not pay Class 2, which is now the default, can therefore find MA assessed at **£27 a week rather than £194.32**, a difference of **£167.32 a week over up to 39 weeks**. **Voluntary Class 2 is £3.65 a week for 2026/27** (§8, verified at https://www.gov.uk/self-employed-national-insurance-rates ). The planning point is that paying voluntary Class 2 ahead of a planned pregnancy is one of the highest-return elective NIC payments available to a self-employed doctor. **This is a house position on the interaction, derived from two verified primary sources (§8 for the Class 2 reform, MA guidance for the assessment basis). Where a page states the resulting MA award it must say the DWP assesses the claim and the claimant should confirm their Class 2 record**, and must not promise an outcome.

**13.3 The NHS occupational maternity pay scheme, and its relationship to SMP.** For employed NHS staff, the occupational scheme is **in addition to and inclusive of SMP**, not instead of it. Verified verbatim in the **NHS Terms and Conditions of Service Handbook, Section 15, "Leave and pay for new parents"**, read in the handbook PDF (Version 60, Amendment number 60, TCS Advisory Notice 01/2026), https://www.nhsemployers.org/system/files/2026-01/nhs-terms-and-conditions-of-service-handbook-Version%2060.pdf , downloaded with `curl` and extracted locally with `pdftotext -layout` on **2026-08-26**.

**Two source-handling notes, both added 2026-08-26.** First, the section URL previously cited here, `.../section-15-leave-and-pay-for-new-parents-england-and-wales`, now **301-redirects to the handbook landing page** https://www.nhsemployers.org/publications/tchandbook . Cite the landing page or the versioned PDF, not the old section path. Second, the landing page records a **later revision than the PDF read here: Amendment number 62, TCS Advisory Notice 03/2026, published 1 June 2026**, and lists the section in its contents as covering **England, Wales and Scotland** rather than England and Wales. The paragraph numbering and the pay arithmetic quoted below are taken from Version 60 and are **UNVERIFIED against Amendment 62**: what was tried on 2026-08-26 was fetching the landing page (which confirmed the amendment number and the title but served no Amendment 62 PDF link that resolved) and searching for a Version 61 or 62 PDF, which returned only Version 60. **Before a page quotes a paragraph number from Section 15, re-read the current handbook version**, per the standing method rule in the Verification log, which applies to a periodically amended handbook exactly as it does to Directions.

**Scoping, and this matters because the site is written for doctors (CORRECTED 2026-08-26).** The handbook is the **Agenda for Change** agreement and its Introduction states it "applies in full to all staff directly employed by NHS organisations, **except very senior managers and staff within the remit of the Doctors' and Dentists' Review Body**". **Most doctors are within the DDRB remit, so Agenda for Change does not apply to them directly.** An earlier version of this section scoped the occupational maternity scheme to Agenda for Change without saying so, which reads to a doctor as "this is not for you" and is wrong on the outcome. - **Doctors and dentists in training, specialty doctors and specialist grades** are on their own national contracts, which **apply Section 15 by reference** rather than restating it. The **12-month test is measured at the 11th week before the expected week of childbirth**, not at the leave start date, and it counts service with **one or more NHS employers**, so NHS service is aggregated across trusts and practices. The scheme is **conditional on an intention to return for three months**, which is a recoverable-overpayment risk the reader should be told about before they plan. ## 14. Opting out of the NHS Pension Scheme, refunds, and rejoining (locked 2026-08-26)

**Position, and the distinction the whole topic turns on.** The market uses "opting out", "getting a refund" and "a short service refund" interchangeably. **They are three different things and only one of them is reversible.** Batch 1 established that this confusion, not the arithmetic, is the reader's real problem.

1. **Opting out** stops future accrual. It is done on form **SD502**. Service already built up is **not** lost. If the member has **two or more years** of qualifying membership, the benefits become **deferred** and are payable at retirement.
2. **A refund of contributions** is a separate application on form **RF12**, available **only** where the member has **less than two years** of qualifying membership. It **extinguishes** the membership it relates to. **SD502 does not itself claim a refund.**
3. **A short service refund lump sum** is the **tax** name for what an RF12 refund is, and it carries its own tax charge, which is why a refund never returns the full amount paid.

**Forms and mechanics.** Verified at PCSE, https://pcse.england.nhs.uk/help/gp-pensions/opting-out-rejoining-nhs-pension-scheme (fetched 2026-08-26), which is the GP-facing administrator and is fetchable where NHSBSA's member hub is not:
- Opting out is done via **PCSE Online**, "select Opt Out from the GP Pensions dashboard", with form **SD502** completed from the NHS Pensions website and issued to PCSE.
- **The two-year rule**, verbatim: "You can only get a refund of contributions is you have **less than 2 years service** within the scheme. If you have **over 2 years** service, the pension pot will become **deferred** until your minimum or normal retirement age." (The typo is in the source; quote it corrected or paraphrase, do not reproduce it.)
- The refund application is form **RF12**: "an application to request a refund of pension contributions (RF12) form must be completed and sent to PCSE."
- **Timing of effect**, and it is the one deadline that matters: if the opt-out application is received **before the end of the first pay period**, the member is "treated as never having been included in the scheme" and contributions are returned. If received later, the opt-out takes effect "from the first day of the next pay period following receipt". So the practical deadline is **the end of the first pay period**, not a fixed number of days, and after it the member is an ex-member with a record rather than someone who was never in.
- **What is destroyed immediately.** Opting out makes members "**ineligible for any death in service**, along with other benefits available to active members". Death in service and ill-health cover are **active-member** benefits and they stop the day accrual stops, which is well before any pension is affected. That is the point most opt-out decisions are made without.
- **Rejoining (CORRECTED 2026-08-26, this was previously stated as a member form and it is not).** PCSE states that "a Joiner form must be completed and submitted" and that members "can rejoin the pension scheme at any time as long as you are **under the age of 75**", and for a GP working across several practices, "The joining date needs to be the same at each practice." **The Joiner form is an EMPLOYER form, not a member form, and the member does not submit it.** The **SS10**, **SS10GP** and **SS14** joiner forms sit in the **NHSBSA employer hub** and are completed and submitted by the employing authority, GP practice or Direction employer: SS10 for NHS Employing Authorities and Direction employers, SS10GP for GP practices and out-of-hours providers for non-medical employees, SS14 for Assistant, Principal and Locum Practitioners (https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/employer-hub/technical-guidance/joiners , fetched 2026-08-26).

  **What the member actually does**, verbatim from the NHSBSA member factsheet "Opting Out" (V6, 04.2025), https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/2025-07/Opting%20Out%20Member%20facsheet%20-%2004.2025%20-%20V6.docx , downloaded with `curl` and read locally on **2026-08-26**: "**You must apply in writing to your employer.**" Two conditions follow from the same factsheet and both were previously absent from this document:
  - **Membership restarts from the first pay period after receipt, and cannot be backdated.** Verbatim: "You will be entered into the NHS Pension Scheme from the **first day of the pay period following receipt of the application by the employer**." And where a specific date is asked for: "If you state that you wish to join or re-join from a specific date you should be entered into the NHS Pension Scheme from the first day of the next pay period following this date." And flatly: "**The date of joining / re-joining cannot be back dated.**" So a delayed application costs a whole pay period of accrual with no recovery, which is the mirror image of the opt-out timing rule above.
  - **A person cannot rejoin while absent from work, for any reason.** Verbatim: "Yes. You can join or rejoin the NHS Pension Scheme at any time as long as you satisfy the Scheme's normal eligibility criteria. However **you cannot re-join the NHS Pension Scheme if you are absent from work for any reason.**" This matches the §13.4 factsheet line that an employee "cannot join the NHS Pension Scheme during a period of absence for any reason", and it bites hardest on exactly the reader §13 is about: **a member who opted out and then goes on maternity leave cannot rejoin until they are back at work.**

  Also confirmed at NHSBSA https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/member-hub/joining-scheme (fetched 2026-08-26): "The maximum age for joining or rejoining the Scheme is 75", and "All members joining or rejoining the Scheme from 1 April 2023 will build up pension benefits in the **2015 Scheme**", which also covers those working under a contract for services including GPs.

**The tax on a refund (short service refund lump sum).** Verified at HMRC Pensions Tax Manual **PTM045000**, https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/pensions-tax-manual/ptm045000 (fetched 2026-08-26). The charge is **20% on the first £20,000 refunded and 50% on the remainder above £20,000**, and "It is the **scheme administrator** who is liable to the tax charge and not the person who receives the lump sum payment", so in practice the refund arrives **net**. The conditions include that the scheme is an occupational pension scheme, that pensionable service ended before normal pension age without entitlement to a short service benefit, that the member has not previously become entitled to benefits under that scheme, and that payment is made before age 75. The manual also records that "from 1 October 2015, section 36 Pensions Act 2014 shortened the two-year limit to **30 days** where the member's entitlement is entirely to **money purchase** benefits", which **does not apply to the NHS scheme's main defined-benefit accrual**; the two-year preservation limit is the one that binds an NHS member. Do not import the 30-day figure into an NHS page.

**What a refund destroys, stated plainly.** A refund returns the **member's own contributions**, net of the charge above. It does **not** return the **employer's 23.7%** (§2.C), which is simply lost. It extinguishes the **service** those contributions bought, including its **death in service** and **ill-health** value, and it removes that period from the calculation of qualifying service for everything downstream. Because the employer contribution is roughly double the typical member tier, a refund hands back well under half of what was paid in on the member's behalf, before the 20% charge. **That comparison is the honest centre of the page** and it does not require a projection to make.

**Interaction with the annual allowance, and the fence.** The common reason a doctor considers opting out is an **annual allowance charge** (§2.B) or the **taper** (§2.B, and §5 for the general adjusted-net-income taper). **This document's position is that opting out is rarely the right answer to a tax charge, because Scheme Pays (§2.D) settles the charge without giving up accrual, employer contribution, death in service or ill-health cover.** A page on opting out gets **one sentence and a link** on Scheme Pays and must not restate its deadlines or election mechanics, which belong to §2.D and to the frozen Scheme Pays pages.

**Practical writing rule for sessions.** Open on the three-way distinction (stop accruing, take a refund, and the tax name for a refund) because the reader's search does not distinguish them. Name **SD502 to leave** and **RF12 to claim a refund under two years' membership**, and say plainly that **SD502 is not a refund claim**. Give the **end of the first pay period** rule as the only genuine deadline. Lead the cost argument with **death in service and ill-health cover stopping immediately** and the **23.7% employer contribution** being forgone, not with a retirement projection. State the **20% / 50%** charge and that the refund arrives net. Say rejoining is possible at any time under 75, that the member **applies in writing to their employer** and the **employer** submits the Joiner form (SS10, SS10GP or SS14), that membership restarts from the **first day of the pay period after the employer receives the application and cannot be backdated**, that a person **cannot rejoin while absent from work for any reason**, and that rejoining does not restore refunded service. Do NOT tell a member to complete or submit a Joiner form themselves, and do NOT imply rejoining can be dated back to when they decided. Do NOT tell a member with over two years' membership that they can take a refund. Do NOT use the 30-day money-purchase limit. Do NOT restate Scheme Pays mechanics or deadlines. Do NOT extend into Form A, Form B or SOLO, which are the pension **certification** family and belong to §2.C and the frozen locum forms page. Cross-ref §2.B, §2.C, §2.D, §13.4.

## 15. Business relief, the £2.5m allowance, and why a company holding investments does not get it (locked 2026-09-01)

**Position.** Business relief (BR, still widely called BPR) is **not a general shelter for a company's surplus cash or for a family investment company**. Two separate gates have to be passed, and the second one is the gate that a cash-rich or portfolio-holding company fails outright.

**Gate 1, the qualifying gate. A business that consists wholly or mainly of making or holding investments is not relevant business property at all.** IHTA 1984 **s.105(3)**, verbatim: "A business or interest in a business, or shares in or securities of a company, are not relevant business property if the business or, as the case may be, the business carried on by the company consists wholly or mainly of one or more of the following, that is to say, dealing in securities, stocks or shares, land or buildings or making or holding investments." Source: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1984/51/section/105 , read 2026-09-01, current as amended (the only amendment noted is subsection (4A), inserted in 2012 for market makers and discount houses, which is irrelevant here). **A family investment company (FIC) whose business is holding a share portfolio is the paradigm case of a company caught by s.105(3), so it gets no BR on its shares, at any rate, before any allowance is considered.** HMRC applies "wholly or mainly" by looking at the business in the round: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm25265 , read 2026-09-01, "The rule excluding investment businesses ... from business relief applies if the business consists 'wholly or mainly' of the excluded categories, IHTA84/S105(3)", assessed on "main activities" and on "assets and sources of income or gains, over a reasonable period preceding the transfer".

**Gate 2, the excepted-assets gate, which is where surplus cash actually dies.** Even where the company IS a trading company that passes s.105(3), the value attributable to **excepted assets** is stripped out of the relief. IHTA 1984 **s.112(1)**, verbatim: "In determining for the purposes of this Chapter what part of the value transferred by a transfer of value is attributable to the value of any relevant business property so much of the last-mentioned value as is attributable to any excepted assets within the meaning of subsection (2) below shall be left out of account." **s.112(2)**, verbatim, gives the two-limb test: an asset is excepted unless it was "used wholly or mainly for the purposes of the business concerned throughout the whole or the last two years of the relevant period" or was "required at the time of the transfer for future use for those purposes". **s.112(6)**, verbatim: "an asset shall be deemed not to have been used wholly or mainly for the purposes of the business concerned at any time when it was used wholly or mainly for the personal benefit of the transferor or of a person connected with him." Source: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1984/51/section/112 , read 2026-09-01. **So a trading company's excess cash pile, held for no identified business purpose, is an excepted asset and carries no relief even inside an otherwise qualifying company.**

**Gate 3, the allowance, which only bites on property that already passed gates 1 and 2.** For deaths and chargeable transfers **on or after 6 April 2026**, 100% relief is capped at a **£2.5 million combined agricultural and business property allowance**, with **50%** relief on the value above it. Statutory source: **IHTA 1984 s.124D**, inserted by **Finance Act 2026 (c. 11), Schedule 12**, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1984/51/section/124D , read 2026-09-01, verbatim: "The 100% relief allowance available in relation to a chargeable transfer ('the relevant transfer') is equal to ... £2.5 million, less ... the total amount by which the values transferred by chargeable transfers made by the transferor in the allowance period were treated as reduced". HMRC's manual states the death limb and the transferable limb together: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm25520 , read 2026-09-01, verbatim: "For any individual who dies on or after 6 April 2026 the amount of 100% agricultural or business relief is limited to £2.5m", and the allowance "can be increased by up to 100%, i.e., up to £5m, if a pre-deceased spouse or civil partner did not use all their 100% relief allowance". The 50% rate above the allowance and the transferability are also stated on the current gov.uk policy page (last updated **3 March 2026**): https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/changes-to-agricultural-property-relief-and-business-property-relief/agricultural-property-relief-and-business-property-relief-changes , verbatim: "Relief at the lower rate of 50% will apply to the value of any qualifying relievable property over £2.5 million" and "Any unused amount of the £2.5 million allowance can be transferred to a surviving spouse or civil partner from 6 April 2026".

**AIM and other 'not listed' shares: 100% cut to 50%, in all circumstances, from 6 April 2026.** Same gov.uk page, read 2026-09-01, verbatim: the relief rate "will be reduced from 100% to 50% in all circumstances for shares admitted to trading on recognised stock exchanges designated as 'not listed'", and this also applies to "qualifying shares listed on foreign exchanges which are not a recognised stock exchange". Note the structure: those shares get **50% with no allowance consumption framing at the 100% rate**, so they are a separate rule from the £2.5m allowance, not a use of it.

> **TRAP, and it is a live one on gov.uk itself.** The **original** Autumn 2024 policy paper is still published and still says "**£1 million**" and "**Any unused allowance will not be transferable between spouses and civil partners**": https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/agricultural-property-relief-and-business-property-relief-reforms/summary-of-reforms-to-agricultural-property-relief-and-business-property-relief , read 2026-09-01. **That page is superseded and both of those statements are now wrong.** The allowance was raised from £1m to £2.5m and made transferable, announced 23 December 2025 and enacted by FA 2026 Sch 12. ## 16. The winding-up (phoenix) TAAR, and the s.464D citation the s455 pack flagged (locked 2026-09-01)

**Position, part 1: the winding-up TAAR.** A distribution made to an individual in a **winding up** is treated as **income, not capital**, where four conditions are all met. The provision is **ITTOIA 2005 s.396B** (with the trust equivalent at **s.404A**), inserted by **Finance Act 2016 s.35(1)**, and it applies to **distributions made on or after 6 April 2016**. Sources, both read 2026-09-01: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/5/section/396B (heading "Distributions in a winding up") and https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2016/24/section/35 , verbatim at s.35(3): "The amendments made by this section have effect in relation to distributions made on or after 6 April 2016." **The citation expected in the task is correct as read at source.**

The four conditions, one line each, quoted from HMRC **CTM36305**, https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/company-taxation-manual/ctm36305 , read 2026-09-01, which states the statutory reference as "ITTOIA05/S396B and 404A":

- **Condition A:** "The individual receiving the distribution had at least a 5 per cent interest in the company immediately before the winding up".
- **Condition B:** "the company was a close company at any point in the two years ending with the start of the winding up".
- **Condition C:** "the individual receiving the distribution continues to carry on, or be involved with, the same trade or a trade similar to that of the wound-up company at any time within two years from the date of the distribution".
- **Condition D:** "it is reasonable to assume that the main purpose, or one of the main purposes of the winding-up is the avoidance or reduction of a charge to Income Tax".

**Effect.** Where all four are met the distribution "will be treated as if it were a distribution" chargeable to **income tax**, so the **capital-gains treatment is lost and BADR with it** (CTM36305, read 2026-09-01). The relevant comparison for a doctor closing a private-practice company is therefore **income tax at dividend rates (§5, 10.75% / 35.75% / 39.35% for 2026/27)** rather than **BADR at 18%** (§4). **Condition C is the one that catches doctors**, because a consultant who liquidates a private-practice company and resumes similar private work within two years, in any vehicle, is squarely within it. HMRC operates a **clearance** route (CTM36350) and publishes **exclusions** (CTM36345); neither was read in full on 2026-09-01, so do not describe the clearance process in detail on a page until someone reads those two pages.

**Position, part 2: the s.464D reference the s455 pack flagged. VERIFY-FAILED as a live citation. It is repealed.** **CTA 2010 Chapter 3B of Part 10, which contained ss.464C and 464D, was omitted with effect from 30 October 2024** by **Finance Act 2025 (c. 8) s.81(3)(b) and (4)**. Sources, all read 2026-09-01: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/4/section/464D (page shows the text struck out, annotated "Part. 10 Ch. 3B omitted (30.10.2024) by virtue of Finance Act 2025 (c. 8), s. 81(3)(b)(4)"), https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/4/section/464C (same annotation), and https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/8/section/81 (heading "Loans to participators"; s.81(4) verbatim: "The amendments made by subsection (3) are treated as having come into force on 30 October 2024"). Section **464B** (relief in case of return payment) was omitted at the same time.

**What replaced it, and this is what a page should cite instead.** FA 2025 s.81(3)(a) inserted **CTA 2010 s.464ZA ("Treatment of certain repayments")** and **s.464ZB ("Section 464ZA: supplementary")**, in force from **30 October 2024**. Read at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/4/section/464ZA on 2026-09-01, s.464ZA carries the two anti-avoidance limbs that the old provisions carried, with their thresholds intact:

- **The 30-day rule:** within any period of **30 days**, where qualifying repayments total **£5,000 or more** and relevant chargeable payments total **£5,000 or more**, the repayment is matched against the new payment rather than the old loan.
- **The arrangements rule:** where the total owed is **£15,000 or more** and there are arrangements for replacement payments of **£5,000 or more**, the repayment is likewise matched against the replacement.

**So the substance the s455 pack wanted is still law; only the section numbers moved.** A page citing s.464C or s.464D is citing a provision that has not existed since 30 October 2024, which is the kind of citation that reads authoritative and is simply wrong.

- **Statutory hooks:** **ITTOIA 2005 s.396B** and **s.404A** (winding-up TAAR), inserted by **FA 2016 s.35**, effective for distributions on or after 6 April 2016. **CTA 2010 s.464ZA / s.464ZB** (repayment and replacement-payment matching, from 30 October 2024, inserted by FA 2025 s.81(3)(a)); **ss.464B, 464C, 464D omitted from 30 October 2024**. Base charge remains **CTA 2010 s.455**, relief **s.458** (§5).
- **HMRC manual anchors:** **CTM36300+** (winding-up TAAR: CTM36305 the rule, CTM36310 to CTM36340 conditions A to D, CTM36345 exclusions, CTM36350 clearance); **CTM61500+** (loans to participators).

Lead with **Condition C** for a medical audience, because resuming similar private work within two years is the normal case, not the exotic one. **Never cite s.464C or s.464D of CTA 2010**; use **s.464ZA** (30-day / £5,000, and £15,000 arrangements / £5,000) and date it from 30 October 2024. Cross-ref §5 (s.455), §4 (BADR).

## 17. Unused pension funds inside the estate for IHT from 6 April 2027 (locked 2026-09-01; ENACTED)

## 18. Normal minimum pension age: 55 now, 57 from 6 April 2028 (locked 2026-09-01; citation corrected)

**Position.** The **normal minimum pension age (NMPA)** is **55**, rising to **57 on and after 6 April 2028**. The increase is enacted in **Finance Act 2022 s.10 ("Increase of normal minimum pension age")**, which amends Part 4 of FA 2004, read at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/3/section/10 on 2026-09-01: the section redefines normal minimum pension age as **57 "on and after 6 April 2028"**, with **uniformed-services schemes** (firefighters, police, armed forces) left at the lower age.

**Protected pension age, one line.** FA 2022 s.10 inserts **paragraphs 23ZB and 23ZC into FA 2004 Schedule 36**, headed "**Protected pension age: scheme rights existing before 4 November 2021**": a member who on **4 November 2021** already had an **unqualified right** under the scheme rules to take benefits before 57 keeps a protected pension age, and the protection can survive a **block transfer** to another scheme. ## 19. Distributions exemption: a UK company receiving dividends (locked 2026-09-01)

**Position, and this is the paragraph the surplus-cash page's Option 3 needs.** A dividend received by a UK resident company is **chargeable in principle and exempt in practice**. **CTA 2009 Part 9A** sets the frame: **s.931A**, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2009/4/section/931A , read 2026-09-01, verbatim heading and subsection (1): "**931A Charge to tax on distributions received** ... The charge to corporation tax on income applies to any dividend or other distribution of a company, **but only if the distribution is not exempt**." Subsection (3) routes the exemption through **Chapter 2** (distributions received by **small companies**) or **Chapter 3** (distributions received by companies that are **not small**).

**The practical position for a doctor's company.** Where a medical private-practice company or a holding company holds shares and receives dividends, those dividends will normally be **exempt from corporation tax** under Part 9A, so the company is **not taxed again on income that has already borne corporation tax in the paying company**. That is a real feature of an intra-company investment route, and it is the honest half of Option 3. **The other half, which must appear on the same page, is that the exemption does nothing about the two costs that actually decide the question**: the money is still **inside the company** and comes out only through the **dividend or salary route at §5 rates**, and the shares are an **investment holding, which forfeits business relief on the s.105(3) and s.112 analysis at §15**. A page that gives the exemption without those two points is selling a benefit that does not survive the exit.

**One caution on scope, stated because it was not verified in detail.** The Chapter 2 (small company) and Chapter 3 (not small) exemptions are each **conditional**, and the conditions were **not read at source on 2026-09-01**. Only the Part 9A frame at s.931A was. **So a page may state the general position ("dividends received by a UK company are normally exempt from corporation tax under CTA 2009 Part 9A") and must not state that any particular dividend is exempt, or enumerate the exempt classes, until someone reads Chapters 2 and 3.** That is a deliberate ceiling on this block, not an oversight.

- **Statutory hooks:** **CTA 2009 Part 9A**, **s.931A** (charge, and the "only if not exempt" limb), **Chapter 2** (small companies), **Chapter 3** (companies that are not small).
- **HMRC manual anchors:** **INTM650000+** (distribution exemption).
- **Writers must:** say dividends received by a UK company are **normally exempt from corporation tax under CTA 2009 Part 9A (s.931A onwards)**, and in the same paragraph say (a) the cash is still trapped in the company and extraction is taxed at the **§5 dividend rates**, and (b) holding a portfolio makes the company an investment company, which **kills business relief entirely (§15)**. Do NOT list which dividends are exempt or state a particular holding qualifies (Chapters 2 and 3 unverified here). Do NOT present Option 3 as tax-free. Cross-ref §5, §15.

## 20. Corporation tax filing and payment machinery for a small medical company (locked 2026-09-01; penalty figure corrected)

**Position.** For a private-practice or PSC company well below the instalment threshold, the machinery is two dates that fall in the wrong order, which is the point worth making on a page:

- **Pay first.** Corporation tax is due **9 months and 1 day after the end of the accounting period**. https://www.gov.uk/pay-corporation-tax , read 2026-09-01, verbatim: "You must pay your Corporation Tax **9 months and 1 day after the end of your accounting period**" (for taxable profits up to £1.5 million). Corroborated at https://www.gov.uk/company-tax-returns , read the same day: "It's usually 9 months and one day after the end of the accounting period".
- **File second.** The **CT600 Company Tax Return** is due **12 months after the end of the accounting period**. https://www.gov.uk/company-tax-returns , read 2026-09-01, verbatim: "The deadline for your tax return is **12 months after the end of the accounting period it covers**."

**Quarterly instalments: the £1.5 million threshold, and augmented profits includes outside dividends.** A company pays by instalments where its profits exceed **£1.5 million** at an annual rate. https://www.gov.uk/guidance/corporation-tax-paying-in-instalments , read 2026-09-01, verbatim: "A large company is one whose profits for the accounting period in question are at an annual rate of **more than £1.5 million but less than £20 million**", with separate rules above £20 million. The measure is **augmented profits**, defined at **CTA 2010 s.18L ("Meaning of 'augmented profits'")**, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/4/section/18L , read 2026-09-01: augmented profits are the company's **taxable total profits plus any exempt distributions of a qualifying kind received** (the ABGH classes), **excluding** distributions received from a company that is a **51% subsidiary** of the recipient, or vice versa, or a quasi-subsidiary. **So dividends received from a company outside the group DO count towards the threshold, and dividends from a 51% subsidiary do not.** That is the point the task asked to be confirmed, and it is confirmed. It matters for §19: an investment holding that is exempt under CTA 2009 Part 9A still counts in augmented profits, so it can pull a company towards the instalment threshold (and, on the same definition, towards the marginal-relief limits at §5) even though it is not itself taxed. The threshold is **divided by the number of associated companies**, which is the trap for a doctor with more than one company.

> **CORRECTION to the expected figure. The first late-filing penalty is £200, not £100.** https://www.gov.uk/company-tax-returns/penalties-for-late-filing , read 2026-09-01, gives the table: **1 day late, £200; 3 months late, another £200; 6 months late, HMRC estimates the bill and adds a penalty of 10% of the unpaid tax; 12 months late, another 10% of the unpaid tax.** Same page: "If your tax return is late 3 times in a row, the **£200 penalties are increased to £1000 each**." **Any page stating a £100 first-step corporation-tax filing penalty is wrong** (£100 is the Self Assessment figure, and confusing the two is the likely origin). Note this is the **Companies House deadline's separate cousin**: the CT600 penalty above is HMRC's, and a late set of accounts at Companies House carries its own penalties, which were **not verified here** and must not be quoted from memory.

- **Statutory hooks:** **FA 1998 Sch 18** (company tax returns; para 3 notice to deliver, para 17 flat-rate penalties, para 18 tax-geared penalties); **TMA 1970 / CTA 2010 s.279A onwards** and the **Corporation Tax (Instalment Payments) Regulations 1998 (SI 1998/3175)** for instalments; **CTA 2010 s.18L** (augmented profits).

## 21. Employer pension contributions as an extraction route (locked 2026-09-01)

**Position.** For a doctor's private-practice or PSC company, an **employer contribution to a registered pension scheme** is usually the most efficient extraction route available, for two reasons that are verified separately.

**Reason 1, corporation tax deductibility, and the test is purpose, not a percentage.** HMRC **BIM46035**, https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/business-income-manual/bim46035 , read 2026-09-01 (page live, last updated 4 August 2026, statutory basis given on the page as "**S34 Income Tax (Trading and Other Income) Act 2005**" and "**S54 Corporation Tax Act 2009**"), verbatim: "A pension contribution by an employer to a registered pension scheme in respect of any director or employee will be **an allowable expense unless there is a non-trade purpose for the payment**", and "In cases where the contribution is part of a remuneration package paid **wholly and exclusively for the purposes of the trade**, then the contribution is an allowable expense." The same page flags the failure mode: a contribution can be disallowed where "the level of the remuneration package is excessive for the value of the work undertaken". **There is no statutory cap expressed as a multiple of salary**, and the common claim that a company contribution must be justified by reference to salary is not what the manual says; the test is the **total package against the value of the work**. The deduction is on a **paid basis** in the period of payment (**FA 2004 s.196**), which is why the contribution must actually leave the company bank account before the period end. Note for the conductor: the "paid basis" limb rests on FA 2004 s.196, which is cited in §5 of the live document but was **not re-read at source on 2026-09-01**; BIM46035 does not mention it.

**Reason 2, no National Insurance, on either side.** HMRC **NIM02716**, https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim02716 , read 2026-09-01, verbatim: an employer payment into a registered pension scheme within **s.308 ITEPA 2003** is "**disregarded in the calculation of earnings for Class 1 NICs purposes**", the statutory route being **s.308 ITEPA 2003** (substituted by FA 2004 s.201(2) from 6 April 2006) together with **paragraph 2(a) of Part 6 of Schedule 3 to the Social Security (Contributions) Regulations 2001**. **So the same money paid as salary would carry employer secondary Class 1 NIC at 15% above the £5,000 secondary threshold (§5) and employee NIC on top; paid as an employer pension contribution it carries neither.** That NIC gap, not the corporation tax deduction (salary is deductible too), is what actually makes the route win against salary.

**Annual allowance, one line, and do not restate the mechanics.** The contribution is only useful up to what the member's **annual allowance** permits, which for **2026/27 is £60,000** with the taper and the three-year carry-forward. **The mechanics live at §2.B and must not be re-derived on a page.** For an NHS doctor this is the sharp edge: the NHS DB **pension input amount** already consumes most or all of the allowance before any private company contribution is made (§2.B, §2.C), so a company contribution that looks free can generate an **annual allowance charge** at marginal rate. Model the input amount first, then the company contribution, never the other way round.

- **Statutory hooks:** **ITTOIA 2005 s.34** and **CTA 2009 s.54** (wholly and exclusively, per BIM46035); **FA 2004 s.196** (relief for employer contributions, paid basis); **ITEPA 2003 s.308** and **SSCR 2001 Sch 3 Pt 6 para 2(a)** (NIC disregard); **FA 2004 ss.227 to 228ZA** (annual allowance, see §2.B).
- **HMRC manual anchors:** **BIM46035** (employer contributions, wholly and exclusively); **NIM02716** (NIC disregard); **PTM043100+** (relief for employer contributions).
- **Writers must:** give the deductibility test as **purpose, not a salary multiple** (allowable unless there is a non-trade purpose; the risk is a total package excessive for the value of the work), say the deduction is **on a paid basis so the money must leave the account before the period end**, and say there is **no NIC on either side**, which is the real advantage over salary. Then point at **§2.B** for the £60,000 annual allowance, taper and carry-forward instead of restating them, and warn that an **NHS pension input amount can already have used the allowance**. Do NOT state a salary-multiple cap. Do NOT present a company pension contribution as unlimited or automatically tax-free. Cross-ref §2.B, §2.C, §5.
