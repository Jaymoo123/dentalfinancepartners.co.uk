<p>Decide the scope first and the fee second. A contractor accountant fee only means something next to the list of work it covers, so the real decision is which package shape fits how you work, then whether the quote in front of you carries that shape without extras surfacing later. Two quotes that look far apart usually differ on four things: the director's self assessment return, VAT returns, software licences and IR35 status work.</p>

<p>No single monthly figure answers the question, because the obligations behind a fee differ from one contractor to the next. A company registered for VAT once turnover passes £90,000, running RTI payroll and filing a CT600, is buying a different year of work from a company with one client and no VAT. Price the list, then read the quote against it.</p>

<h2>What should the fee cover for 2026/27?</h2>

<p>It should buy every filing your structure creates in the year, delivered on time, with someone accountable for each one. For a personal service company that means statutory accounts and the corporation tax return, the confirmation statement and Companies House filings, RTI payroll each time the director is paid, dividend vouchers and board minutes that exist before the money moves, VAT returns where you are registered, and the director's self assessment return covering salary and dividends.</p>

<p>Two 2026/27 items are worth naming in the quote. Dividend rates rose on 6 April 2026 to 10.75% ordinary and 35.75% upper under Finance Act 2026 s.4, so any salary and dividend planning has to run on current rates rather than last year's, which <a href="/blog/limited-company-tax/director-salary-dividend-split-guide">the director salary and dividend split guide</a> works through. Making Tax Digital for Income Tax started on 6 April 2026 for sole traders and landlords with qualifying income above £50,000, and it does not apply to company profits or to a director's dividends, so a contractor working through a company is generally outside it. Check whether a quote assumes otherwise, because <a href="/blog/mtd-and-compliance/do-i-need-an-accountant-for-mtd">whether you need an accountant for MTD</a> turns entirely on which income you have.</p>

<p>Status handling is the third item. Under Chapter 10 of ITEPA 2003 a medium or large client issues the Status Determination Statement and the fee-payer operates PAYE, while under Chapter 8 a small or overseas client leaves the assessment with your company. Ask what happens when a determination arrives that you think is wrong, and whether that work sits inside the fee.</p>

<h2>Fixed fee or hourly, and what changes between them?</h2>

<p>A fixed monthly fee suits recurring work with predictable volume, and it is the normal shape for limited company compliance. Hourly or project pricing suits events: incorporation, a mid-year switch, an HMRC enquiry, a share reorganisation, or closing the company down. The trade-off is not really price, it is certainty. A fixed fee removes surprises on routine work and usually carves the events out; an hourly arrangement does the reverse.</p>

<p>Where a fixed fee goes wrong is in the carve-outs nobody reads. Ask which specific events fall outside it, how those are quoted, and whether the monthly figure changes if you register for VAT, take on a second employee, or move from one engagement to several.</p>

<h2>Which questions should you ask before signing?</h2>

<p>Six questions separate comparable quotes from ones that only look comparable.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Is the director's self assessment return inside the fee, and does that cover income unconnected with the company?</li>
  <li>Are VAT registration and quarterly returns included, and is the Flat Rate Scheme decision modelled rather than assumed?</li>
  <li>Is bookkeeping software licensed by the firm or bought by you, and who holds the data if you leave?</li>
  <li>What happens when an engagement's status is in question, and what is quoted separately?</li>
  <li>Which events sit outside the monthly fee, and how are they priced when they happen?</li>
  <li>What are the notice period and the handover terms if you move on?</li>
</ul>

<p>Get the answers in writing. A quote and a scope list side by side is the only fair comparison, and sending the same list to two firms makes the difference between them visible in minutes.</p>

<h2>What should you watch for in how the service is run?</h2>

<p>Watch the boundary between a firm that guides while you decide and a packaged arrangement that runs your company's finances for you. The Managed Service Company rules in Chapter 9 of ITEPA 2003 do not depend on a status test, and where they bite, unpaid PAYE can transfer as a personal debt to the worker and to the directors. Providing accountancy services in a professional capacity is expressly carved out by s.61B(3), so an independent firm whose client makes their own decisions and could leave freely sits on the safe side of that line. The risk sits with standardised products that influence or control how you are paid. <a href="/blog/contractor-accounting-basics/msc-legislation-contractors">The MSC legislation guide</a> sets out the risk factors, and the well-known test cases were before the First-tier Tribunal during 2026 and remain undecided as at September 2026, so treat this as risk management rather than a verdict on any provider.</p>

A clean handover, professional clearance and prompt release of records cost nothing while the relationship works and matter a great deal when it stops working. <a href="/blog/contractor-accounting-basics/how-to-switch-contractor-accountant">How to switch contractor accountant</a> covers the sequence.</p>
