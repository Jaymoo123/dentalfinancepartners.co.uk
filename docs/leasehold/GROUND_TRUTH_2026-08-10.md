# Leasehold niche: GROUND TRUTH (verified 2026-08-10)

Status: LOCKED. This document is the hallucination_zones source for the leasehold site. Every content
writer and calculator must treat these positions as canonical. Re-verify before any figure is changed.
Jurisdiction: England and Wales only. Leasehold in this sense does not exist in Scotland (abolished by
the Long Leases (Scotland) Act 2012 conversion and feudal reform); Northern Ireland has its own regime.
All content is England and Wales unless explicitly tagged.

## 1. Marriage value: NOT ABOLISHED (the single most dangerous hallucination)

- Current law: on a statutory lease extension (LRHUDA 1993, Schedule 13) or collective enfranchisement
  (Schedule 6), where the unexpired term is 80 years or less, marriage value is payable and split 50/50
  with the landlord. The 80-year threshold is live and remains the key economic cliff edge.
- The Leasehold and Freehold Reform Act 2024 (LAFRA) abolishes marriage value on the statute book, but
  the valuation reforms (the Standard Valuation Method, Schedule 4) are UNCOMMENCED as of 2026-08-10.
- Why uncommenced: the new method needs secondary legislation prescribing deferment and capitalisation
  rates. The government consultation "Leasehold enfranchisement valuation rates" launched 15 July 2026
  and closes 23 September 2026 (gov.uk/government/consultations/leasehold-enfranchisement-valuation-rates).
  Options consulted on include retaining the Sportelli rates (4.75% houses, 5% flats), updating them
  with market evidence, or a different approach. Commencement realistically 2027 to 2028.
- Litigation risk: the freeholder groups' judicial review of LAFRA's valuation provisions was dismissed
  by the High Court in October 2025; the Court of Appeal granted permission to appeal on 1 April 2026,
  with a hearing expected late 2026 or early 2027. This adds further timing uncertainty.
- Editorial line: leaseholders near the 80-year threshold face a genuine wait-or-act decision. Waiting
  might make an extension cheaper (marriage value abolition) or dearer (rates could move against
  leaseholders and ground rent capitalisation treatment is unsettled). Never predict the outcome.

Sources: legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/22; gov.uk consultation above; Commons Library CBP-10653;
homehold.org/standard-article/leasehold-reform/ (cross-check, article current as of 15 July 2026).

## 2. Two-year ownership rule: ABOLISHED (in force)

- LAFRA s.27 removed the two-year ownership requirement for statutory lease extension and freehold
  purchase claims under the 1967 Act and lease extension claims under the 1993 Act.
- In force 31 January 2025 via The Leasehold and Freehold Reform Act 2024 (Commencement No. 2 and
  Transitional Provision) Regulations 2025 (SI 2025/57), reg 2. Verified on
  legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/22/section/27 (no outstanding effects).
- Practical consequence for content: buyers of short-lease flats can start a statutory claim
  immediately after completion; the old "assign the benefit of a s.42 notice" workaround is obsolete
  for new purchases. Do not publish the old rule as current.

## 3. 990-year lease extensions: NOT in force

- LAFRA provides for a standard statutory extension of 990 years (flats and houses) at a peppercorn
  rent, replacing the current terms. UNCOMMENCED as of 2026-08-10; tied to the same valuation
  commencement package as marriage value.
- Current law: flats get +90 years at a peppercorn on top of the unexpired term (1993 Act); houses get
  +50 years at a modern ground rent (1967 Act s.14) or, more commonly, buy the freehold.

## 4. Ground rent: 2022 Act covers NEW leases only; the £250 cap is a PROPOSAL

- Leasehold Reform (Ground Rent) Act 2022: peppercorn (zero financial) ground rent for most new
  qualifying residential long leases granted on or after 30 June 2022 (retirement properties from
  1 April 2023). Enforcement: fines £500 to £30,000 per qualifying lease.
- The 2022 Act does NOT touch existing leases. Existing ground rents, including doubling clauses and
  RPI-linked reviews, remain payable as drafted (subject to CMA-secured undertakings from some
  developers on doubling terms).
- The draft Commonhold and Leasehold Reform Bill (published 27 January 2026) proposes capping ground
  rents in existing flat leases at £250 per year, converting automatically to a peppercorn after 40
  years. This is a DRAFT BILL PROPOSAL, not law. Never present it as in force or certain.

## 5. Right to manage: qualifying and costs changes ARE in force

- In force 3 March 2025 via The Leasehold and Freehold Reform Act 2024 (Commencement No. 3)
  Regulations 2025 (SI 2025/131): LAFRA ss.49 to 52, plus s.64 insofar as it inserts s.20J Landlord
  and Tenant Act 1985 as regards RTM.
- Key changes now live: the non-residential floor-area limit for RTM claims rose from 25% to 50%
  (opens RTM to many mixed-use buildings); the costs regime changed so RTM claimants generally no
  longer pay the freeholder's non-litigation costs.
- RTM fundamentals unchanged: no-fault right under Commonhold and Leasehold Reform Act 2002; at least
  two-thirds of flats held by qualifying tenants; at least 50% of qualifying tenants must join; RTM
  company structure required.

## 6. Building safety overlaps (Building Safety Act 2022, as amended by LAFRA)

- BSA 2022 leaseholder protections (qualifying leases, the s.119 and Schedule 8 service charge caps
  for relevant defects, remediation orders and remediation contribution orders) are live and interact
  with service charge and enfranchisement content.
- LAFRA amendments to the BSA: ss.117 to 119 in force 24 July 2024 (two months after Royal Assent);
  ss.114 to 116 in force 31 October 2024. s.114 adds "relevant steps" (including interim measures such
  as waking watch); s.116 expands remediation contribution orders (codifying the Triathlon approach,
  with retrospective effect for s.124).
- Content rule: any enfranchisement or RTM article touching buildings over 11m/18m must flag the BSA
  interplay (liability for remediation transfers with the freehold; RTM companies and manager
  responsibilities under the safety regime) without giving safety-engineering advice.

## 7. Draft Commonhold and Leasehold Reform Bill: status as of 2026-08-10

- Draft Bill published 27 January 2026 alongside a ground rent policy paper and a consultation on
  implementing the ban on new leasehold flats (consultation closed 24 April 2026).
- Pre-legislative scrutiny: HCLG Select Committee report published 27 May 2026 (verdict: significant
  step, but government "must go further and faster").
- Headline proposals: mandatory commonhold for new flats, £250 existing ground rent cap converting to
  peppercorn after 40 years, commonhold conversion framework.
- NOT law. Independent commentary (Homehold, July 2026) estimates passage spring 2027 at the earliest
  and implementation late 2028 at the very earliest. Treat every provision as a proposal.

## 8. Service charges: current law still LTA 1985; LAFRA regime largely uncommenced

- Current law for all service charge content: Landlord and Tenant Act 1985 (s.19 reasonableness,
  s.20 consultation for works over £250 per leaseholder or contracts over £100 per year, s.20B
  18-month rule, s.21 summary rights, s.27A FTT challenge); Landlord and Tenant Act 1987 (s.47/48
  demand validity); Commonhold and Leasehold Reform Act 2002 (administration charges, Schedule 11).
- LAFRA Part 4 (standardised demand forms, annual reports, insurance transparency under s.60, costs
  recovery rebalancing, litigation costs) is largely UNCOMMENCED. Consultation "Strengthening
  leaseholder protections over fees, charges and services" ran 4 July to 25 September 2025; the
  government response confirms implementation by a series of statutory instruments, with the simpler
  measures (demand formats, annual reports) expected first via SIs from late 2026 and most changes
  taking effect from 2027 onwards.
- s.20J LTA 1985 (inserted by LAFRA s.64) is in force ONLY as regards right to manage (see §5).
