Spouse Exemption and the Second-Death Window for Landlord Estates

The headline IHT-on-first-death story for a landlord couple is short. Section 18 IHTA 1984 gives an unlimited inheritance tax exemption on transfers between long-term-resident spouses or civil partners; on first death the entire portfolio passes to the surviving spouse tax-free, and the IHT400 reports a chargeable estate of zero. The complication is the second death. The portfolio has consolidated in the surviving spouse's hands, and the next time the IHT door opens, it opens on the whole estate with no spouse to pass through. This page is the window between those two deaths. It covers the spouse-exemption mechanics that defer the first-death charge, the limited £325,000 cap that applies where the receiving spouse is not long-term resident (and the section 267ZA election that switches it off), the transferable NRB and RNRB claim mechanics on IHT402 and IHT436 within 2 years of the second death, and the surviving spouse's decision tree for the period between the deaths: gift down in lifetime under the 7-year PET clock, redirect via a deed of variation in the 2-year section 142 window, strip value through a charitable legacy, or accept the second-death exposure and plan liquidity with life cover in trust. The page closes on two worked examples for portfolios at £1,500,000 and £2,800,000.
