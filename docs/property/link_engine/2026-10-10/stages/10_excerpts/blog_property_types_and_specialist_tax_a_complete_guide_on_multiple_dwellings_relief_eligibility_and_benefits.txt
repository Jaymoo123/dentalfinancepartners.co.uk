A Complete Guide on Multiple Dwellings Relief: Eligibility and Benefits (Historical Architecture for Transitional Claims and Enquiry Defence)

If you still have a live MDR claim, or HMRC has opened an enquiry into one, the figures are not small: on a four-dwelling £2 million purchase the relief was worth roughly £50,000 to £60,000, and that is exactly the amount HMRC is trying to claw back when it challenges a dwelling count. Multiple Dwellings Relief let you, as a buyer acquiring two or more dwellings in a single transaction (or in linked transactions), compute the SDLT charge on the average per-dwelling consideration rather than the total transaction consideration. Introduced by Finance Act 2011 Schedule 22, it operated for transactions with effective dates from 19 July 2011 to 31 May 2024 inclusive. The full architecture sat in FA 2003 Schedule 6B (inserted by FA 2011), and remained in force in that form (with amendments through the Stamp Duty Land Tax Act 2015 and Finance Act 2016) until abolition commenced on 1 June 2024. Whether you are testing a transitional-cohort claim or defending one at enquiry, the same source text governs: Schedule 6B as it stood on 31 May 2024. What follows works through that operative version, then sets out how HMRC attacks pre-abolition claims and how to tell whether your pre-1-June-2024 contract still qualifies. Schedule 6B paragraph 2: the gateway claim mechanic Paragraph 2 was the gateway. You had to acquire two or more dwellings in a single transaction, or one dwelling and additional dwellings under linked transactions that aggregated under FA 2003 section 108. The relief operated by election, not automatically: you had to claim it in the SDLT return for the transaction. Without a claim, the standard residential SDLT rates applied to the full consideration (with the 5% additional-dwellings surcharge under Schedule 4ZA layered on where applicable).
