What Tax Do I Pay When I Sell a Rental Property in the UK?

When you sell a rental property in the UK, the main tax you face is Capital Gains Tax (CGT) on the profit, not income tax. This catches a lot of landlords out, because the income tax you paid on rent each year is a separate matter and does not reduce the bill when you sell. CGT applies to the gain, broadly the difference between what you sell for and what the property cost you, after deducting your allowable costs and any reliefs. There is also a tight reporting clock. If tax is due and you are a UK resident, you must report the sale and pay the CGT within 60 days of completion. Get the gain wrong, or miss that window, and you can face a penalty even when the eventual tax is modest. What you actually pay comes down to three things: the income tax you paid on rent during ownership, the CGT on the sale itself, and the 60-day return that carries it to HMRC.
