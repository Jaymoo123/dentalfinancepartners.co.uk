Welsh Land Transaction Tax (LTT) 2026/27: Rates, Bands, and What Welsh Buyers Need to Know

If you are buying property in Wales, the tax you pay is Land Transaction Tax (LTT), not Stamp Duty Land Tax. The two taxes share the same broad architecture, but LTT is administered by the Welsh Revenue Authority under the Land Transaction Tax and Anti-avoidance of Devolved Taxes (Wales) Act 2017, the band thresholds are different, the surcharge structure is different, the relief regime is leaner, and the return clock is more generous. Three of those differences work in your favour, and they shape the cost of every Welsh purchase before you even reach the 2026/27 rate table. If your purchase is in England or Northern Ireland, the regimes do not overlap, so use our SDLT rates and surcharge guide instead. Scotland operates a third devolved regime (Land and Buildings Transaction Tax administered by Revenue Scotland) with its own rate table and the Additional Dwelling Supplement at 8%. Three things Welsh buyers should know first These three points sit upstream of the rate table and shape every Welsh purchase decision. They are positive Welsh choices, not gaps in the SDLT regime. One. The nil band is £225,000, not £125,000. The Welsh nil-rate band has been £225,000 since 10 October 2022, materially higher than the SDLT £125,000 nil band that England and Northern Ireland restored on 1 April 2025.
