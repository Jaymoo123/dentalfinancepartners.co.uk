What Are the Major UK Landlord Tax Changes for 2026 and 2027?

Two consecutive tax years bring the biggest shift in landlord taxation for a generation. The 2026/27 year makes Making Tax Digital for Income Tax mandatory for most landlords, and the 2027/28 year introduces separate, higher property income tax rates. Both changes are now law. Making Tax Digital for Income Tax from April 2026 The most immediate change is Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax becoming mandatory from 6 April 2026. It applies to sole-trader landlords and self-employed individuals whose qualifying income is above £50,000. The threshold then drops to £30,000 from 6 April 2027 and £20,000 from 6 April 2028. Qualifying income is the figure that catches people out. It is your gross self-employment turnover plus your gross property rental income, measured before any deductions. A landlord with £52,000 of rent and £40,000 of allowable costs (a net profit of only £12,000) is still in scope at the April 2026 mandate, because the test is on the £52,000 gross figure. Where a property is jointly owned, each owner tests the threshold against their share
