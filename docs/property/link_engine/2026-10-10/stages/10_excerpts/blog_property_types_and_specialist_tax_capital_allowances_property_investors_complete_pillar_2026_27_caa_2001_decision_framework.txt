Capital Allowances for UK Property Investors 2026/27: The Four-Axis Decision Framework Under CAA 2001

Most capital-allowances guides start with the wrong question. They open with "what kind of equipment qualifies" and reach for a list of kitchens, boilers and carpets. For the majority of UK property investors that list is a trap, because for an ordinary residential letting the answer is mostly nothing: the Capital Allowances Act 2001 blocks plant-and-machinery allowances on plant inside a dwelling-house before the question of equipment type ever lands. Claim off that list anyway and you carry a penalty risk; ask the right questions and you find relief most landlords miss. There are four upstream questions, in order, before any list of qualifying assets is worth opening. The four questions: who is claiming, on what kind of property, on what kind of expenditure, and through which vehicle. Each is an axis. Together the four decide whether you have a claim at all, and if you do, which CAA 2001 route delivers the relief. The framework below is current for the 2026/27 tax year, with the post-FHL-abolition rules in CAA 2001 s.15, the permanent £1m Annual Investment Allowance, the company-only full-expensing route at s.45S, the 3 per cent Structures and Buildings Allowance, the in-force first-year allowances, and the s.35 dwelling-house restriction that disqualifies most residential lets from the start. The CAA 2001 s.35 dwelling-house rule sets the gate Before any allowance question matters, you have to clear the gate set by Capital Allowances Act 2001 s.35 (Exclusion for plant or machinery for use in a dwelling-house). It blocks plant-and-machinery allowances where expenditure is incurred on plant for use in a dwelling-house and the qualifying activity is an ordinary UK property business, an overseas property business, or special leasing of plant.
