First-Time Buyer Relief and the Deposit Challenge: How SDLT Savings Free Up Down-Payment Cash, and Where They Stop

When you are mid-affordability-calculation on your first home, the question that matters is blunt: how much cash does the SDLT first-time buyer relief actually put back in your pocket, and does it move the deposit you need. The honest answer is that it helps, but as a cost reduction at completion rather than a deposit grant, and only inside a tight set of conditions that can flip it against you if you trip the price cap or buy jointly with a partner who has owned before. Here is exactly how the relief translates into deposit availability, what it combines with, and where it stops. How the relief actually appears in your transaction SDLT is paid at completion via your conveyancer, who files the SDLT return on your behalf and pays the tax to HMRC out of the completion-monies. The first-time buyer relief reduces the SDLT figure on the return, which reduces the amount the conveyancer needs in the completion-monies, which reduces the cash you have to send before completion day. For a £400,000 purchase by a sole qualifying first-time buyer, the SDLT figure on the return drops from £10,000 (standard residential rate table) to £5,000 (first-time buyer relief). The £5,000 difference stays in your account. If you were assembling £40,000 of deposit-plus-completion-costs from a single savings pot, you now need £35,000 instead of £40,000.
