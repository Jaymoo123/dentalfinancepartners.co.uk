Stamp Duty on Property Incorporation UK: Do You Pay It Twice?

Landlords asking about stamp duty on property incorporation in the UK are usually asking one specific question: having already paid SDLT when they bought each rental, do they now pay it a second time to move those same properties into their own limited company? The blunt answer is that in economic terms you do. The transfer to your company is a separate land transaction and there is no credit for the stamp duty you paid the first time round. Whether the second charge actually lands is a different question, and three routes in the legislation answer it differently: genuine partnership incorporation under FA 2003 Schedule 15, the automatic six-dwellings deeming at section 116(7), and group relief at Schedule 7 for later intra-group moves. Each has a three-year rule attached that can pull the saving back after the return has been filed and accepted. Those routes, and the traps, are what follows. Stamp duty land tax is charged on acquisitions of chargeable interests, not on owners. When you bought a rental personally, that purchase was charged. When your company acquires the same property from you, that is a fresh acquisition by a different legal person, and it is charged again.
