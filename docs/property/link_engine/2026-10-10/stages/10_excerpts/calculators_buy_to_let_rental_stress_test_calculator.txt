How the buy-to-let stress test works Before offering a buy-to-let mortgage, lenders check that the rent would still cover the interest payment at a higher notional rate, not just the rate you are actually paying. This protects against rates rising and against the loan being unaffordable if the true pay rate proves optimistic. The check compares annual rent against annual interest at the stress rate, expressed as the interest cover ratio, or ICR. A lender might require 125% cover for a limited company or basic-rate taxpayer, and a higher 145% for an individual higher-rate taxpayer, because Section 24 leaves less of the rent available after tax. The maximum loan a property supports is therefore driven by the rent, not just the property value or your deposit.
