Business Rates or Council Tax: Which Applies to Your Property?

Most rental property in the UK pays council tax. A minority pays business rates instead, and getting the classification wrong, or missing a relief, costs real money. The split depends on the type of property and how it is actually used, not on what a tenancy agreement says or on the ownership structure behind it. One correction matters more than any other on this question, so it leads the guide. Houses in multiple occupation in England do not default to business rates. Since 1 December 2023 an HMO is treated as a single dwelling for council tax, with the owner liable for one bill. Older guidance that says HMOs with shared facilities become liable for business rates is out of date for England. This guide sets out which tax applies to each property type in 2026/27: standard residential rentals, HMOs, holiday lets and self-catering, and commercial or mixed-use units. It is led by the position in England, with the Wales and Scotland variations flagged where they differ. Northern Ireland uses a separate domestic and non-domestic rates system and is dealt with at the end.
