UK-Isle of Man Double Taxation Agreement: What Manx-Resident Landlords of UK Property Actually Face

If you are Manx-resident and let UK property, one quiet assumption costs people real money: that because the Isle of Man has a Double Taxation Agreement with the UK, you do not pay UK tax on your UK rental income. You do. The 2018 UK-Isle of Man Double Taxation Agreement allocates source-state taxing rights over UK immovable property income to the UK. It does not exempt you. UK Income Tax under ITTOIA 2005 Part 3 (individuals) or UK Corporation Tax under CTA 2009 Part 4 (companies) applies in full, and the same trap catches Jersey and Guernsey clients. Three UK obligations sit on top of that charge. The Non-Resident Landlord scheme under FA 1995 Schedule 23 and SI 1995/2902 makes your letting agent and tenants withhold 20% basic rate from your UK rent unless you hold NRL1, NRL2, or NRL3 gross-payment approval. The 60-day NRCGT return under TCGA 1992 s.1A and Schedule 1A is required on every UK land disposal by a non-resident, whether or not tax is due. If you hold a UK dwelling worth more than £500,000 through a Manx-incorporated company, you are inside the Annual Tax on Enveloped Dwellings (ATED) regime under FA 2013 Part 3, exactly as a UK or Jersey-incorporated company would be.
