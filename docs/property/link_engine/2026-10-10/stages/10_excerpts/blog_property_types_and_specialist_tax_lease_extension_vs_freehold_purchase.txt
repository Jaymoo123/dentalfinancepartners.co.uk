Lease Extension vs Freehold Purchase: The Decision-Architecture for Leaseholders of Houses and Flats Under the Post-LFRA-2024 Statutory Regime

If you own a leasehold flat with 70-99 years remaining on the lease, you have two statutory routes: extend the lease at a peppercorn rent under the Leasehold Reform, Housing and Urban Development Act 1993 Chapter II (currently for the existing term plus 90 years; LFRA 2024 legislates a 990-year term but that provision is not yet in force), or join with neighbours and buy the freehold under Chapter I. If you own a leasehold house, the parallel choice sits under the Leasehold Reform Act 1967: enfranchise (acquire the freehold) or extend the lease at a modern ground rent. The Leasehold and Freehold Reform Act 2024 recalibrates both choices: the abolition of the 2-year qualifying-ownership period is in force, while the abolition of marriage value, the 990-year statutory new-lease term and the regulation of the statutory deferment rate are on the statute book but not yet commenced. This page is the decision-architecture for the choice between the two routes. Two parallel statutory regimes: LRA 1967 (houses) and LRHUDA 1993 (flats) The English-and-Welsh leasehold reform statutes operate in parallel for the two main residential building types. Each regime offers the qualifying long-leaseholder both an extension right and an acquisition right; the strategic question (extend vs acquire) is symmetric across the two, but the operative mechanics differ.
