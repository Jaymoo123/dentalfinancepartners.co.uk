The Policy History of Making Tax Digital for Income Tax: How We Got From 2015 to the April 2026 Mandate

If you are a landlord now filing quarterly under Making Tax Digital, it helps to know that the rules landing on you in 2026 are not the rules anyone designed. The system did not arrive fully formed. It is the product of a decade of policy work, statutory drafting, redesign, delay and political accommodation, and the version that went live on 6 April 2026 is not the version the 2015 originators or the 2017 statutory drafters had in mind. The threshold that catches you, the penalties you face for paying late, the cohort you sit in: every one of those was moved at least once. Here is why. It traces back to the 2015 origins under the Office of Tax Simplification, the 2017 statutory basis, the 2018 design that proposed a £10,000 threshold, the years of delay through 2019 to 2022, the 19 December 2022 announcement that defined the current trajectory, the 2024 pilot launch, the Spring Statement 2025 penalty changes and the April 2026 mandate. Each of those moved the design, and the £50,000 phased mandate you face today is a substantially different beast from what was originally contemplated.
