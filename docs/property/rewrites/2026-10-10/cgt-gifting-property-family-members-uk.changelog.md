### Item 1 (BLOCK): s58 "practical use" figures, full recomputation

Facts used: residential CGT 18%/24% and AEA £3,000 (HP §5); basic rate band £37,700 = £50,270 less £12,570 (HP §21.4). Best case for the wife: no other income using her band.

| Step | Husband keeps all | Split: husband's half | Split: wife's half |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gain | £100,000 | £50,000 | £50,000 |
| Less AEA | (£3,000) | (£3,000) | (£3,000) |
| Taxable | £97,000 | £47,000 | £47,000 |
| Basic rate band used at 18% | £0 | £0 | £37,700 -> £6,786 |
| Remainder at 24% | £97,000 -> £23,280 | £47,000 -> £11,280 | £9,300 -> £2,232 |
| Tax | £23,280 | £11,280 | £9,018 |

Split total: £11,280 + £9,018 = £20,298. Saving: £23,280 - £20,298 = **£2,982**. My independent working agrees with QA (£20,298 / £2,982). Round-0 changelog fix #5 (£19,740 / £3,540) was wrong: it taxed all of her £47,000 at 18% when the band is only £37,700. Page now reads: "Even if her whole basic rate band is unused, only £37,700 of her £47,000 taxable gain is taxed at 18%, so the bill falls from £23,280 (24% of £97,000) to £20,298 (£11,280 on his half plus £9,018 on hers), a saving of about £2,982."
