## 4. How the DNS Associates one-page-per-city pattern is built

Google ranks this pattern (#1 "property accountant manchester" region, #8
"landlord accountant"), so the mechanics matter.

- **Scale**: 55 live `property-tax-accountants-in-<city>` URLs in
  `pages-sitemap.xml`, out of 133 `accountants-in-` pages in total. Cities are
  a mix of real cities and London micro-areas (canarywharf, chancerylane,
  camden, tottenham, wimbledon).
- **Coverage is selective, not exhaustive.** Birmingham and Leeds both return
  a **404**. They cover Aberdeen, Inverness, Whitehaven, Workington and Wigan
  but not Birmingham. So the pattern is not "every city".
- **Richness**: 2,166 to 2,615 words, 10 to 11 H2s, FAQPage + BreadcrumbList +
  WebPage JSON-LD. Not thin by word count.
- **How thin it really is**: Manchester versus London are **81% token-identical**.
  The differences are the city token swapped in, and light paraphrase of the
  same three or four paragraphs. The London page even leaks a stray heading
  "Accountants for buy to let landlords **Bournemouth**", which is template
  bleed from another city page.
- **Addresses**: no per-city address. Every city page carries the single
  Harrow HQ postcode **HA3 8DP** and one shared number **03300 886 686**. There
  is no `LocalBusiness` or `AccountingService` schema and no `address` in
  JSON-LD on any of them.
- **What each city page does carry**: the shared Trustpilot and Feefo review
  bars, a "we take care of..." scale block (13,000+ clients, 27 offices, 100+
  experts, 18+ years), a 3-step sign-up, a newsletter capture, a case-study
  link, a free-software offer, 133 images, ~177 internal links.

So the thing Google is rewarding here is **not** local proof. There is none.
It is a near-duplicate template at volume, hung off a rank-284 domain with
1,155 referring domains, plus platform review bars and a strong internal link
mesh. Our city pages are richer and more genuinely localised than theirs
(Article 4 directions, selective licensing, worked Section 24 examples) and
better marked up. We lose on domain authority and on contactability.

## 6. Could not verify

- **ukpropertyaccountants.co.uk** (ranks top 5 on three of six queries). WAF
  blocked every fetch route, including the summariser. No page attributes
  recorded. Backlinks were obtained (rank 271, 620 referring domains).
- **Page age for every URL.** archive.org was returning 429 then
  "Temporarily Offline" throughout. No first-capture dates. Domain-level
  `first_seen` from DataForSEO is in the table above as a weaker proxy.
- **haroldsharp, mccaccountants, gorillaaccounting** were read through the
  page summariser, not raw HTML. Their headings, word counts and on-page
  features are reported from that read; their JSON-LD is **unverified** and is
  excluded from every schema count in section 1. Treat their row in the CSV as
  lower confidence than the other 16.
- **Professional body badges** were counted from page text only. A badge that
  exists only as an image with no alt text would be missed, so 6 of 19 is a
  floor, not a ceiling.
- **Homepage link counts** are an href-pattern count, not a nav-versus-footer
  split. Directionally right, not exact.
- Whether our testimonials are from real clients is not something a page fetch
  can establish, so "4 of 6 carry anonymised testimonials" is a measure of what
  is published, not of provenance.
- Our live homepage form still carries the "specialist partner network"
  consent sentence. That sits inside consent copy, which is where the estate
  ruling permits it, so this is recorded as an observation and not as a defect.
