  - question: "Can I get legal aid for a divorce financial settlement in 2026?"
    answer: "Only in narrow circumstances. LASPO 2012 removed private family law, including divorce and financial remedy work, from the scope of legal aid from 1 April 2013. The main surviving route is the domestic abuse and child abuse evidence gateway: if you can produce evidence from the defined list that you or your children have been a victim of abuse by the other party, financial remedy work can come back into scope. There is also funding for family mediation, which is assessed on a separate and more generous basis, and legal aid remains available for protective orders such as non-molestation and occupation orders under Part 4 of the Family Law Act 1996. If none of those apply, legal aid will not pay for a solicitor to negotiate your settlement, and the realistic options are unbundled advice, a law centre, a litigation loan or a legal services payment order."
  - question: "What counts as evidence of domestic abuse for family legal aid?"
    answer: "The Legal Aid Agency works from a defined list rather than a general impression of the case. Accepted sources include the courts (a protective order, an undertaking or a finding of fact), the police (a caution, charge, conviction or a domestic violence protection notice), a multi-agency risk assessment conference referral, social services, a health professional such as a GP, nurse, midwife, health visitor or psychologist, a refuge manager or domestic abuse support organisation, an employer, an education or training provider, a benefits provider, or bank records evidencing financial abuse.   - question: "What are the legal aid income and capital limits?"
    answer: "For civil legal aid from April 2026 the gross income limit is £2,657 a month, rising by £222 a month for each dependent child beyond the fourth. If you pass that, disposable income after allowances must be no more than £733 a month, and disposable capital must be no more than £8,000. If you receive Income Support, income-based Jobseeker's Allowance, income-related Employment and Support Allowance, Universal Credit or the guarantee credit element of Pension Credit you are passported through the income test automatically, but your capital is still assessed. Equity in the family home counts towards capital subject to disregards, which is why homeowners frequently fail the capital test even on a low income. The free checker at gov.uk/check-legal-aid gives an indication before you approach a provider."
  - question: "What is the statutory charge and how much can it take from my settlement?"
    answer: "Section 25 of LASPO gives the Lord Chancellor a charge over any money or property you recover or preserve with the help of civil legal aid, including in a financial remedy case. In practice it means the cost of your legal aid is repaid out of your settlement, so legal aid in a money case behaves like a loan rather than a grant. If the recovery is cash, the charge is usually satisfied straight away. If it is a share of the family home, or money you are using to buy a new home for yourself or your dependants, enforcement can be postponed and registered against the property instead, similar to a second mortgage. Interest then accrues at 8% a year under the Civil Legal Aid (Statutory Charge) Regulations 2013 until the charge is paid, so a postponed charge left in place for a decade can grow substantially. Your provider must explain the likely charge before you start."
  - question: "I have evidence of abuse but I own half of the family home. Will I still get legal aid?"
    answer: "Possibly not, and this is the most common disappointment. Passing the abuse gateway only puts the case into scope. You then face the means test, and your share of the equity in the family home is capital, subject to the disregards the Legal Aid Agency applies to a main dwelling. A couple with a modest income but significant equity can be over the £8,000 disposable capital limit on paper while having no cash at all. It is still worth running the free eligibility check rather than assuming, because the calculation involves mortgage and equity disregards that are easy to get wrong by hand. If you are refused on capital, ask specifically about a legal services payment order under section 22ZA of the Matrimonial Causes Act 1973, which is designed for exactly this position: asset rich, cash poor, and unable to fund advice while the assets are locked up in the proceedings."
  - question: "Is the Family Mediation Voucher scheme the same as legal aid for mediation?"
    answer: "No, and mixing them up costs people money. Legal aid for mediation is means tested and, where you qualify, can cover the mediation information and assessment meeting and the mediation sessions themselves. The Family Mediation Voucher scheme is a separate government contribution of £500 per family, available for cases involving a dispute about a child regardless of your income, and the scheme has been extended to March 2027. The voucher is applied for by the mediator, not by you, and it goes towards the mediation fees rather than being paid to you. The two can interact: if one party qualifies for legal aid and the other does not, the party who does not qualify may still be able to use a voucher towards their own share of the cost."
  - question: "What can I do if I cannot get legal aid and cannot afford a solicitor?"

<p>Legal aid for the money side of a divorce in England and Wales now survives through one main door: evidence that you or your children have been the victim of domestic abuse or child abuse by the other party. The <a href="https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2012/10/contents">Legal Aid, Sentencing and Punishment of Offenders Act 2012</a> (LASPO) took most private family law out of scope on 1 April 2013, and financial remedy work went with it.</p>

<p>This guide sets out what legal aid still covers, the evidence gateway and the means test you have to pass, how funding for mediation differs, the statutory charge that quietly claws the money back, and the realistic alternatives if you do not qualify. Before you write legal aid off, compare the two funding routes most people actually choose with our <a href="/calculators/mediation-vs-solicitor-costs">mediation versus solicitor cost comparison</a>: if legal aid is out of reach, the gap between a mediated settlement and a solicitor-negotiated one is usually the largest single decision you will make about the cost of your divorce.</p>

<h2>What legal aid still covers in a divorce</h2>

<p>Three things survived the 2013 cuts and they are worth separating clearly.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Protective orders.</strong> Non-molestation orders, occupation orders and forced marriage protection orders under Part 4 of the Family Law Act 1996 remain in scope in their own right.</li>
<li><strong>Family finance and children work behind the abuse gateway.</strong> A financial settlement or child arrangements case comes back into scope where you can evidence domestic abuse or child abuse, subject to means and merits.</li>
<li><strong>Family mediation.</strong> Assessed on its own footing and much more widely available than representation.</li>
</ul>

<p>What legal aid does not do is pay the court fees for an ordinary divorce. That is a separate scheme, Help with Fees, applied for on form EX160, and it can reduce or remove the <strong>£628</strong> divorce or dissolution application fee (from 13 July 2026), the <strong>£62</strong> consent order fee and the <strong>£321</strong> Form A fee. Help with Fees is fee remission and nothing more. It will not pay a penny towards a solicitor. Our guide to <a href="/blog/process-and-costs/help-with-divorce-fees-ex160">help with divorce fees and form EX160</a> covers its own income thresholds (£1,420 for a single applicant, £2,130 for a couple) and the £4,250 disposable capital limit, which are entirely different numbers from the legal aid limits below.</p>

<h2>The abuse evidence gateway into legal aid for a financial settlement</h2>

<p>The gateway is documentary, not impressionistic. The Legal Aid Agency works from a defined list of acceptable evidence, and a provider cannot open a file without it. Accepted sources include the courts (a protective order, an undertaking or a finding of fact), the police (a caution, charge, conviction or domestic violence protection notice), a multi-agency risk assessment conference referral, social services, a health professional such as a GP, midwife, health visitor or psychologist, a refuge manager or domestic abuse support organisation, an employer, an education provider, a benefits provider, or bank records evidencing financial abuse.</p>

<p>Two points people miss. First, <a href="https://www.gov.uk/legal-aid/domestic-abuse">gov.uk publishes template letters</a> you can send to a doctor, the police or social services to request the evidence in the exact form required, which saves weeks. Second, the five-year time limit that once applied to how old the evidence could be was removed in January 2018 by the Civil Legal Aid (Procedure) (Amendment) Regulations 2017, so historic evidence still counts.</p>

<p>The capital test is where most separating homeowners fall out. Your share of the equity in the family home is capital, so a low earner with a substantial mortgage-free interest can be ineligible while having no accessible cash whatsoever. Check before you assume either way, using the free tool at <a href="https://www.gov.uk/check-legal-aid">gov.uk/check-legal-aid</a>.</p>

<h2>Legal aid for mediation in a divorce, and the £500 voucher</h2>

<p>Mediation funding is the part most people are wrongly told they cannot have. It is means tested on the same civil basis, but the crucial rule is that where <strong>one</strong> party is financially eligible, the mediation information and assessment meeting (MIAM) is covered for both of you. The eligible party's own mediation sessions are also funded. It is worth asking the mediator to run the check on both of you rather than only the person who raised the question.</p>

<p>Separately, and confusingly, the <strong>Family Mediation Voucher scheme</strong> provides a <strong>£500</strong> contribution per family for cases involving a dispute about a child, irrespective of income, and the scheme has been extended to <strong>March 2027</strong>. It is not legal aid, it is claimed by the mediator rather than by you, and the two can stack across a couple: where one party is legally aided and the other is not, the paying party may still use a voucher. Our <a href="/blog/process-and-costs/divorce-mediation-guide">guide to divorce mediation</a> covers how sessions actually run and how an agreement is made binding.</p>

<h2>The statutory charge: legal aid against your financial settlement</h2>

<p>Section 25 of LASPO gives the Lord Chancellor a charge over any money or property you recover or preserve with legally aided help. In a financial remedy case that is the whole point of the exercise, so the charge almost always bites. Legal aid in a money case is closer to a secured loan than a grant.</p>

<p>Take a composite example. Delyth qualifies through the abuse gateway, and her legally aided costs come to £7,000 by the time a final order is made. She receives a lump sum of £60,000 which she is using to buy a small flat for herself and her son. Because the money is going into a home for her and a dependant, enforcement is postponed and the charge is registered against the new property. Interest then accrues at <strong>8% a year</strong> under the Civil Legal Aid (Statutory Charge) Regulations 2013. Left in place for eight years, the £7,000 charge has attracted roughly £4,480 of interest, so around <strong>£11,480</strong> comes out of the flat when it is eventually sold or the charge is redeemed. Paying it off earlier, where you can, is usually the cheaper course, and your provider must explain the likely charge before work starts.</p>

<h2>Paying for divorce advice when legal aid is not available</h2>

<p>Most people reading this will not qualify, so the alternatives matter more than the scheme itself.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Unbundled or fixed-fee advice.</strong> You run the correspondence and forms, and pay only for the expensive-to-get-wrong parts, typically drafting or checking the consent order. See <a href="/blog/process-and-costs/divorce-solicitor-costs">divorce solicitor costs</a> for how firms price this, and <a href="/blog/process-and-costs/diy-divorce-without-solicitor">DIY divorce without a solicitor</a> for what you can safely handle yourself.</li>
<li><strong>Law centres, university law clinics and pro bono schemes.</strong> Free initial advice, often with a court duty scheme available on the day.</li>
<li><strong>Litigation loans.</strong> Specialist lenders advance fees against your expected settlement. The interest is real and is deducted from your final share, so compare the total cost against what the advice is likely to add.</li>
<li><strong>A legal services payment order.</strong> Under <strong>section 22ZA of the Matrimonial Causes Act 1973</strong> the court can order the wealthier spouse to fund the other's legal costs. The court must be satisfied you would not otherwise reasonably be able to obtain appropriate legal services, that you cannot reasonably secure a loan, and that you are unlikely to obtain services by granting a charge over assets recovered in the proceedings.</li>
<li><strong>Help with Fees.</strong> Court fee remission only, but it removes a fixed cost that falls due early.</li>
</ul>

<p>Legal aid for a divorce financial settlement in England and Wales is real but narrow: the abuse evidence gateway, then a means test with a £2,657 gross monthly income limit and an £8,000 capital limit, then a statutory charge that recovers the cost from what you win. Mediation funding is the more accessible route and is worth checking for both of you, and the £500 voucher sits alongside it for child disputes. Where none of that lands, unbundled advice, a law centre, a litigation loan or a section 22ZA order are the routes that keep a case moving, and our guide to the <a href="/blog/process-and-costs/cost-of-divorce-uk">cost of divorce in the UK</a> puts the total bill in context. For a clearer picture of what you are funding in the first place, start with our overview of <a href="/financial-settlements">financial settlements on divorce</a>.</p>
