<p>When a resident is assessed as eligible for NHS continuing healthcare, the NHS funds the full package of care and your customer for that bed changes from the local authority or the resident to the responsible NHS body. The separate NHS-funded nursing care payment, £267.68 per week at the standard rate and £368.24 at the higher rate from 1 April 2026, stops at the same moment. In the accounts for 2026-27 this is not a rate change; it is a change of payer, of contract and of the risk attached to that bed.</p>

<h2>What actually changes on the day a CHC award lands</h2>

<p>Three things change together. The payer becomes a single NHS body, the fee basis becomes whatever that body has contracted for the placement rather than your published rate or a local authority framework rate, and the nursing top-up disappears because the NHS is now paying for the nursing element inside the whole package.</p>

<p>The distinction underneath all of this is who is buying the bed. NHS-funded nursing care buys the nursing element only, and leaves whoever was already paying for the placement paying for the rest of it. Continuing healthcare buys the placement. It replaces the funding arrangement, which is why a CHC bed and an FNC bed cannot be modelled with the same line in a fee-mix sheet. The mechanics of running those streams side by side are set out in our guide to <a href="/blog/fees-fnc-and-local-authority-rates/fnc-chc-la-fee-mix-accounting">FNC, CHC and local authority fee-mix accounting</a>, and the <a href="/calculators/funded-nursing-care-fee-mix-calculator">funded nursing care fee mix calculator</a> will show you the shape of the mix before you change it.</p>

<h2>Where does CHC income sit in the ledger</h2>

<p>On its own line, with the responsible NHS body as a named debtor. Blending CHC receipts into a general fee income account is the error that hides the real position, because the four payer types behind a nursing home's revenue carry different rates, different payment behaviour and different levels of security.</p>

<p>A practical ledger structure for a mixed home separates self-funder fees, local authority fees, FNC receipts and CHC contract income, with the sales ledger carrying one account per NHS body and one per commissioning local authority. That structure costs nothing to set up and does two jobs. It lets you see at a glance how much of your revenue depends on decisions made by NHS assessors rather than by your own occupancy and pricing. It also means that when an award is reviewed or withdrawn, the affected income is already isolated and you can quantify the exposure in an afternoon rather than rebuilding it from invoices.</p>

<h2>Does a CHC award change the VAT position</h2>

<p>No, and the assumption that it might is common enough to be worth killing off. Group 7 of Schedule 9 to the Value Added Tax Act 1994 exempts the welfare service a CQC-registered provider delivers, and it looks at the service, never at the cheque. Sending the invoice to an NHS body instead of to a resident does not turn the same care into a taxable supply.</p>

<p>The consequence is the one that hurts. Because the supply is exempt rather than zero rated, the VAT on your consumables, equipment, building works and professional fees stays with you as a real cost. Where a home runs a small taxable activity beside the care, partial exemption decides what comes back, and the £625 a month de minimis limit is one a nursing home of any size is well past. Moving a resident onto CHC does not move it. The edge cases are covered in our post on <a href="/blog/vat-and-welfare-exemption/care-home-vat-exemption-edge-cases">care home VAT exemption edge cases</a>, and a <a href="/services/care-vat-review">care VAT review</a> is where a specialist reviews the attribution if you run a mixed activity.</p>

<h2>How should you forecast a CHC bed</h2>

<p>As reviewable income. The care and support package is normally reviewed within 3 months of the award and at least annually after that, and the review considers whether the resident is still eligible. If needs change, eligibility can change.</p>

<p>That makes CHC different from a self-funder bed, where the risk is the resident leaving, and different from a local authority bed, where the risk is the annual rate settlement. Here the bed can stay full while the funding route changes underneath it, and the home returns to invoicing the local authority or the resident with FNC on top. A forecast that treats every CHC week as certain for twelve months will overstate both revenue and cash. The safer approach is to schedule the next review date for every CHC resident alongside the income line, and to model the step down to the FNC position as the downside case so you know the size of the hole before it appears.</p>

<h2>What about backdated awards and closed periods</h2>

<p>Retrospective awards, including claims for previously unassessed periods of care, create a double recognition risk. The same weeks have already been invoiced to a local authority or a resident and now fall to the NHS. Raise the credit note and the replacement invoice in the same accounting period so the bed is recognised once, keep the correspondence with the award attached to both, and if the period reaches back over a year end your accountant prepares the adjustment rather than leaving it to net itself out in the current year.</p>

<h2>Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland</h2>

<p>The position above is England. Continuing healthcare, NHS-funded nursing care and local authority charging operate under different frameworks and different rates in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, so neither the £267.68 figure nor the FNC and CHC boundary described here can be read across. Confirm the applicable regime before applying any of this to a placement outside England.</p>

<h2>The practical order</h2>

<p>Split the income lines first, because everything else depends on seeing the streams apart. Then attach a review date to each CHC resident and run the downside case. Then check that nothing in the VAT position has been assumed to improve. Occupancy and staffing still drive the margin on a CHC bed exactly as they do on any other, and the cost side of that is set out in our guide to <a href="/blog/care-home-accounts-and-funding/agency-staff-cost-control-occupancy">agency staff costs and occupancy voids</a>.</p>
