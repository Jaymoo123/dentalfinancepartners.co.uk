<p>Ofsted, not the Care Quality Commission, registers a children's home in England, and the financial test it applies is not a one-off form. Regulation 47 of the Children's Homes (England) Regulations 2015 makes financial viability a continuing duty of the registered provider, and carrying on a home without registration is a criminal offence under section 11 of the Care Standards Act 2000. So four money decisions have to be settled before the application goes in: which legal entity holds the registration, how the home is financed, how the payroll floor of £12.71 an hour plus employer National Insurance at 15% is funded before a single placement fee arrives, and how you handle the VAT you will never get back.</p>

<h2>What does Ofsted actually test on the money?</h2>

<p>It tests whether the home can keep running. <a href="https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/541/regulation/47/made">Regulation 47</a> says the registered provider must carry on the home in such a manner as is likely to ensure it will be financially viable for the purpose of achieving the aims and objectives in its statement of purpose. That wording ties the money to the promise: if the statement of purpose commits to a staffing ratio, a therapeutic model or an education offer, the finances have to fund that specific model rather than a cheaper one.</p>

<p>Regulation 47 then gives the Chief Inspector a standing power to require annual certified accounts, a bank reference as to your financial standing, details of how the home is financed and what financial resources sit behind it, information about associated companies where the provider is a company, and evidence of liability insurance. Two points follow. Your records have to be current rather than reconstructed annually, because the duty is to supply copies on request. And a group structure is visible to the regulator, so a property company or a management company in the chain is something to be able to explain, not something to keep quiet about.</p>

<p>This is the practical difference from the adult side. There is no Ofsted version of the CQC financial viability statement template, so nothing tells you what good looks like. <a href="/blog/cqc-and-financial-compliance/ofsted-vs-cqc-money-paperwork">The regulator comparison</a> sets out how the two registration tracks diverge; what regulation 47 adds is that the Ofsted test carries on for as long as the home is open.</p>

<h2>Which entity should hold the registration?</h2>

<p>An organisation, in almost every case. Ofsted registers an individual, a partnership or an organisation, and registering as an organisation also means appointing a responsible individual who is the point of contact and is accountable for the quality of management. A limited company meets <a href="https://www.gov.uk/corporation-tax-rates">corporation tax at 19% on the first £50,000 of profit</a>, and you pay personal tax only on what you take out, at the <a href="https://www.gov.uk/tax-on-dividends">dividend rates</a> that apply from 6 April 2026.</p>

<p>A sole trader or partner is taxed on the whole profit in the year it is earned, drawn or not. In a business that has to hold cash back to meet a rota while local authority placement invoices settle, that is the sharper problem. Unincorporated providers are also pulled into <a href="/blog/care-home-accounts-and-funding/mtd-it-care-owner-operators">Making Tax Digital for Income Tax</a> from 6 April 2026 once combined trading and property income passes £50,000, which means quarterly updates on top of everything else a new registration demands.</p>

<p>If you split the property into one company and the operation into another, the associated company rules halve both corporation tax thresholds, so the group reaches the 25% rate on profits a single company would still be taxed at 19% on. That is a real cost to weigh against the reasons for splitting, and regulation 47 means Ofsted can ask about the associated company anyway.</p>

<h2>Are your placement fees VAT exempt?</h2>

<p>Yes, and that is a cost rather than a saving. Welfare services covering the care and protection of children, supplied by a state-regulated institution, are exempt under Group 7 of Schedule 9 to the Value Added Tax Act 1994, and <a href="https://www.gov.uk/guidance/welfare-services-and-goods-notice-7012">HMRC's welfare services notice</a> names Ofsted among the regulators whose registration makes a provider state-regulated. Your placement fees carry no VAT.</p>

<p>Because those supplies are exempt, the VAT on everything you buy to make them is irrecoverable. Building works, the fit-out, furniture, vehicles, utilities and professional fees all land at their VAT-inclusive cost, permanently. Budget from VAT-inclusive quotes, and compute capital allowances on the VAT-inclusive figures too.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.gov.uk/vat-registration/when-to-register">VAT registration</a> is compulsory only when taxable turnover passes £90,000 in a rolling twelve months, and exempt fees do not count towards it. A home with substantial fee income may have no registration duty at all. If you also sell something taxable, such as training or consultancy to other providers, measure that separately against the threshold.</p>

<h2>What does the staffing floor cost before any income?</h2>

<p>More than most first business plans assume, because the people come before the children. The registered manager needs two years of residential child care experience within the last five and a year supervising care staff, so that appointment is made and paid during the application period. Recruitment, vetting and induction for the rota follow, again before the first placement.</p>

<p>Both wage floors bite from 1 April 2026: <a href="https://www.gov.uk/national-minimum-wage-rates">£12.71 an hour once a worker turns 21</a>, and £10.85 for the 18 to 20 band. <a href="https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rates-and-thresholds-for-employers-2025-to-2026">Employer National Insurance runs at 15% above the £5,000 secondary threshold</a>, and <a href="https://www.gov.uk/claim-employment-allowance">Employment Allowance of up to £10,500</a> offsets that bill for eligible employers, which for a single home often removes most of it in the first year. Overnight cover follows the same National Minimum Wage rules as any care setting, and <a href="/blog/payroll-and-workforce-costs/sleep-in-pay-travel-time-nmw">the sleep-in and travel-time position</a> is where providers most often get the rota costing wrong. Treating any member of the rota as self-employed is the standing audit risk: direction, fixed shifts and no right of substitution make someone an employee whatever the contract says.</p>

<h2>What relief is there on the building and the fit-out?</h2>

<p>The <a href="https://www.gov.uk/capital-allowances/annual-investment-allowance">Annual Investment Allowance</a> gives a 100% deduction on up to £1,000,000 of qualifying plant and machinery in the year you spend it, which for most single-home conversions absorbs the whole equipment and fit-out spend. Above that, claim the 40% first-year allowance on new, unused main-pool additions, then writing-down allowances at 14% from April 2026 on the residual pool. New build or extension work attracts the <a href="https://www.gov.uk/guidance/claiming-capital-allowances-for-structures-and-buildings">Structures and Buildings Allowance at 3% a year</a> straight line. The split between plant and structure is worth getting right at the point of the works, and <a href="/blog/business-structure-and-acquisition/capital-allowances-care-home-fit-out">the fit-out capital allowances guide</a> covers the sequencing.</p>

<h2>Where do you start?</h2>

<p>Settle the entity, then build the cash model that regulation 47 is really asking about: every outgoing from the property commitment and the registered manager's start date through to the first settled placement invoice, funded by money you can evidence. A specialist reviews the structure and the projections behind that evidence before the application goes in rather than after. Where the same young people could instead be placed in housing with support, <a href="/blog/cqc-and-financial-compliance/supported-accommodation-registration-and-tax">supported accommodation registers on a different track</a> with a different VAT answer.</p>
