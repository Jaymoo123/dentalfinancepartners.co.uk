## Required structure (H2 skeleton)

Open with a 40-60 word BLUF in plain prose: zero-rated means VAT is charged at 0%. The supply is still taxable, so the seller registers, files returns and recovers the VAT on costs. That is what separates it from exempt, where no VAT is charged and none of the VAT on costs comes back.

**Boundary table (MANDATORY). Two columns: "Zero-rated (0%, input tax recoverable)" against "Exempt (no VAT, input tax blocked)".** Minimum four trade-anchored row pairs:

| Zero-rated: 0% on the invoice, VAT on costs recovered | Exempt: no VAT on the invoice, VAT on costs lost |
|---|---|
| A printer's 5,000 leaflets for a local estate agent | A broker's commission for arranging that estate agent's office insurance |
| A baker's cold loaves and uncut cakes sold over the counter | A funeral director's arrangements for the disposal of remains |
| A bookshop's paperbacks, and the ebook versions of the same titles | A private physiotherapy clinic's treatment sessions |
| A coach operator's 52-seat airport transfer | A sports club's playing membership subscription |
| A children's outfitter's coats inside the measurement limits | A commercial landlord's rent on a unit with no option to tax |

Under the table, in one sentence: both invoices show £0 of VAT, and only the left-hand column gets the VAT on its costs back.

**Worked examples (two, named to a trade):**
- A print shop turning over £140,000, almost all leaflets and brochures. Output VAT is nil. Input VAT on paper, ink and a press is recoverable, so registering puts the business in a repayment position rather than costing it anything. Show the arithmetic direction, not invented totals.
- A funeral director with the same turnover. Output VAT is also nil, but the VAT on the hearse, the premises and the stationery is not recoverable, so it sits in the cost base. Same zero on the invoice, opposite cash result.

**What people get wrong:**
- Using "zero-rated" and "exempt" interchangeably. They produce opposite cash outcomes.
- Assuming a zero-rated business should stay unregistered. Usually the reverse.
- Assuming insurance is zero-rated because there is no VAT on the premium. It is exempt, and there is IPT instead.
- Assuming the reduced rate (5%) is a kind of zero rate.
- Assuming everything printed is zero-rated. Reading matter is; stationery is not, and `vat-on-printing.md` carries that line.
