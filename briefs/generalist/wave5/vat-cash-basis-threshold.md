## THE TRAP THIS PAGE EXISTS TO FIX (read before the H1)

The picks data files this page under the `cash-business` mechanic alongside `cash-basis`, `cash-basis-allowable-expenses` and the accruals comparison. **That grouping is a research artefact, not a real relationship.** The income tax cash basis and the VAT Cash Accounting Scheme are different regimes under different statutes with different eligibility and different consequences. They share a word.

The page must **open by separating them**, in the first 60 words, before any preamble. A reader who has searched "vat cash basis threshold" may be holding either question in their head, and the most common failure is someone applying the £1.35m VAT figure to their income tax position, or looking for an income tax threshold that no longer exists.

## BOUNDARY TABLE (mandatory, exact columns)

Columns: **"VAT Cash Accounting Scheme"** against **"Income tax cash basis"**. This is the disambiguation table and it is the most important block on the page. Four row pairs minimum, trade-anchored:

| VAT Cash Accounting Scheme | Income tax cash basis |
|---|---|
| A limited company running a garage **can** use it | The same company **cannot** use the income tax cash basis at all; a company computes profits under GAAP |
| Turnover test: join at £1.35m or less, leave above £1.6m | **No turnover test of any kind** since 6 April 2024 |
| You opt in, and you must leave when you breach the exit figure | It is the **default**; you opt out by electing for traditional accounting |
| A salon's VAT return: output tax when the client pays | A sole trader salon owner's tax return: profit counted when the money arrives |
| Bad debt relief is automatic, because unpaid invoices never enter the return | An unpaid invoice is never income, so a builder's £40,000 bad debt is never taxed |
| Statutory home: VAT Regulations 1995, Part VIII | Statutory home: ITTOIA 2005 ss.24A, 25B, 25C, 33A |

## Worked examples (two, real figures)

1. A trade business with £1.28m of VAT taxable turnover joins the scheme, then grows to £1.65m. Show the point at which it must leave, and the catch-up: output tax on invoices issued but not yet paid becomes due.
2. A quarter's return under the scheme against the same quarter on invoice accounting, with one £12,000 sales invoice unpaid at the quarter end. Show the VAT difference and name it as cash flow, not a saving.
