## Asset type + play

Answer-first page whose entire value is a disappointment delivered early: **there is no VAT advantage to buying an electric car.** An EV is a car. The article 7 block is propulsion-neutral. Readers arrive expecting the opposite, because every other EV tax page on the estate and on the open web is about capital allowances, where EVs genuinely are favoured. Say it in the first sentence and then spend the page on the part that does differ, charging.

The charging rules are the real content and they split four ways: at work, at a public charge point, at home by a sole trader or partner, and at home by an employee. The last one is where employers are routinely wrong: the supply of electricity is made to the employee, so the employer cannot recover.

## BOUNDARY TABLE (mandatory, exact columns)

Columns: **"Blocked or not recoverable"** against **"Recoverable input tax"**. Four trade-anchored row pairs minimum:

| Blocked or not recoverable | Recoverable input tax |
|---|---|
| A consultancy buys an electric car available to the director privately: blocked, exactly as a diesel would be | The same consultancy charging that car at its own office charge point, on business mileage: recoverable |
| An employee of a builders' merchant charges the company EV on their home supply: not recoverable, the electricity is supplied to the employee | The same company's driver charging at a public rapid charger on a business journey: recoverable |
| A sole trader plumber's home charging with no mileage log: not recoverable, the business proportion cannot be evidenced | A sole trader plumber's home charging with a mileage log: the business proportion is recoverable |
| A cafe leases an electric car available for private use: 50% of the lease VAT blocked | The separately invoiced maintenance element of that lease: recoverable in full |
| An electric car bought on a margin-scheme invoice: no VAT on the invoice, nothing to recover | An electric van with a payload of one tonne or more: not a car for VAT, recoverable |

## HP GAPS (do NOT invent, omit or link gov.uk)

1. **Zero-emission company car percentages for 2027/28 onward are NOT confirmed as enacted** (Stage 1B B §4.4c). State **4% for 2026/27** as fact. Describe later years only as "announced increases, rising steeply from April 2028" with no figures. Do not state 5%, 18% or 19%.
2. **The domestic electricity VAT rate and the 5% versus 20% home-charging split.** Not locked in HP and not verified in Stage 1B B. The cannibalisation note mentions it as a gap the existing pages do not cover; that is a reason to be careful, not a licence to assert. Do not state a percentage. Describe the difference qualitatively and link https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-on-motoring-expenses-notice-70064 and the gov.uk fuel and power guidance.
3. **Do not assert that there is no road fuel scale charge for a fully electric car** (Stage 1B B §4.5b). If the question arises, say the scale charge is set by CO2 emissions and the published tables cover petrol and diesel, and route to HMRC.
4. **Advisory electricity rate for reimbursing business mileage in an EV.** Not locked. Do not state a pence-per-mile figure.
5. No fee figures, no named experts.
