## Asset type + play

One of the cleanest boundary pages in the wave, because the statute gives a genuinely usable organising idea: **the zero rate attaches to reading matter, not to paper.** A leaflet is something you read, so it is zero-rated. A letterhead is something you write on, so it is standard-rated. Lead with that and the whole page follows.

The wedge: a print shop's invoice routinely carries both rates, and the customer who queries it is usually told "that is just how it is". This page explains the rule, gives the boundary at item level, and covers the 2020 extension of the zero rate to electronic versions, which most print and design businesses still do not know about.

## Required structure (H2 skeleton)

Open with a 40-60 word BLUF in plain prose: printed matter is zero-rated when it is reading matter, so books, leaflets, brochures, newspapers and journals carry no VAT. Stationery and anything completed in use is standard-rated at 20%, which is why a single print invoice often shows both rates.

**Boundary table (MANDATORY). Two columns: "Zero-rated (0%)" against "Standard-rated at 20%".** Minimum four trade-anchored row pairs:

| Zero-rated: reading matter | Standard-rated at 20% |
|---|---|
| A restaurant's 2,000 takeaway menus printed as leaflets | The same restaurant's headed paper and compliment slips |
| An estate agent's property brochures | The estate agent's business cards |
| A school's printed prospectus booklet | The school's printed forms and exercise books |
| A charity's newsletter or journal | The charity's posters for public display at a fundraising event |
| A publisher's paperback, and the ebook version of it | A publisher's unused desk diary or account book |

Under the table, one sentence: the printer's question is never "is it printed", it is **"is it for reading, or is it for writing on and using up"**.

**Worked examples (two, named to a trade):**
- A print shop quoting a cafe: 2,000 A5 menu leaflets at £220 and 500 letterheads at £90. The £220 is zero-rated, the £90 is standard-rated so £18 of VAT arises if the price is VAT-exclusive (state the assumption). Total invoice £328. One customer, one delivery, two rates.
- The same print shop over a year: turnover £120,000, four fifths of it leaflets and brochures. Output VAT is small, input VAT on paper, ink and a press is fully recoverable, so registration is a repayment position rather than a cost. That is the paragraph a printer under the threshold needs.

**What people get wrong:**
- Assuming everything a printer produces follows one rate.
- Assuming a poster is a leaflet. A poster for public display is standard-rated.
- Assuming an ebook is standard-rated. Electronic versions of books, leaflets and newspapers have been zero-rated since 1 May 2020.
- Assuming an advertising-led publication is zero-rated. Item 7 excludes electronic publications wholly or predominantly devoted to advertising.
- Staying unregistered as a zero-rated printer when registering would generate repayments.
