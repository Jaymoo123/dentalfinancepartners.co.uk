## Asset type + play

Two-part page for two readers who both search the same phrase.

Part one, the funeral director's side: the disposal of remains and the arrangements for it are **exempt** under Schedule 9 Group 8, but a typical funeral package is not one supply. Flowers, headstones, memorials, plaques and newspaper announcements are standard-rated, so the package normally needs apportioning. That is the operative point for a funeral business and nobody publishes it clearly.

Part two, the executor's side: funeral expenses are deductible in computing the estate for inheritance tax. Hand the detail to the existing IHT page rather than re-authoring it here. See the HP GAP before writing a single word about IHT.

## Required structure (H2 skeleton)

Open with a 40-60 word BLUF in plain prose: the funeral itself is exempt from VAT, so no VAT is charged on the disposal of the remains or on arranging it. Flowers, memorials and announcements are standard-rated at 20%, which is why a single funeral invoice normally carries both treatments, and why the business cannot recover all of its input VAT.

**Boundary table (MANDATORY). Two columns: "Exempt (no VAT)" against "Standard-rated at 20%".** Minimum four trade-anchored row pairs, all sitting inside services the same businesses supply:

| Exempt: no VAT charged | Standard-rated at 20% |
|---|---|
| The funeral director's disposal of the remains | The florist's wreath supplied through the same funeral director |
| Arranging the funeral, including the director's professional fee for it | The newspaper announcement placed on the family's behalf |
| The crematorium operator's cremation fee | The stonemason's headstone, memorial or plaque |
| The coffin, bearers and transport of the deceased, as incidental to the exempt supply | A memorial bench or memorial vase at the cemetery |
| A funeral director's exempt service billed on to another funeral professional | Agency services billed alongside the funeral |

Under the table: the dividing line is **disposal against commemoration**. What gets the deceased buried or cremated is exempt; what remembers them afterwards is standard-rated.

**Worked examples (two, named to the trade, arithmetic only from the 20% standard rate):**
- A funeral director's invoice for a £2,900 funeral plus £180 of flowers and a £95 newspaper announcement. The £2,900 is exempt. The £275 of commemorative and ancillary goods is standard-rated, so £55 of VAT arises on that slice if the prices are VAT-exclusive (state the assumption). One invoice, two treatments.
- The same business over a quarter: most turnover exempt, so most of the VAT on premises, vehicles and stationery is not recoverable and sits in the cost base. Contrast with a stonemason, whose output is entirely standard-rated and who recovers everything.

**What people get wrong:**
- Treating the whole package as exempt because the funeral is exempt. The commemorative goods are not.
- Assuming exempt means the business is better off. It blocks recovery.
- Assuming a funeral director billing another funeral professional loses the exemption. They do not.
- Confusing exempt with zero-rated on a funeral invoice. Link `zero-rated-vat.md`.
- Treating the IHT deduction and the VAT treatment as the same question. They are unrelated.
