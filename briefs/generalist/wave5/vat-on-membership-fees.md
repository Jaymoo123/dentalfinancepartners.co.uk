## Asset type + play

The exemption is narrower than every club treasurer assumes, and the page's value is in showing exactly where it stops. Two conditions do the work: the body must be **eligible** (four cumulative limbs, all of which must hold) and the member must be **actively taking part in sport**. A social membership at the same club is standard-rated. A commercially run gym is not an eligible body at all.

The wedge no competitor writes: **the apportionment.** A club with playing and social members must split its subscription income, and that split then drags the club into partial exemption on its input tax. Say so, and hand the de minimis arithmetic to `vat-exemption.md`.

Second exemption in scope: **Schedule 9 Group 9**, subscriptions to trade unions, professional and other public interest bodies. That answers the "vat on membership" phrasing for a reader who is not a sports club at all, and it belongs on this page rather than a separate one.

## Required structure (H2 skeleton)

Open with a 40-60 word BLUF in plain prose: a membership subscription is exempt from VAT only where a non-profit making club supplies it to someone actually taking part in sport, or where it is a subscription to a trade union or professional body. Commercial gyms, social memberships and most other subscriptions carry VAT at 20%.

**Boundary table (MANDATORY). Two columns: "Exempt subscription" against "Standard-rated at 20%".** Minimum four trade-anchored row pairs:

| Exempt: eligible body, member taking part | Standard-rated at 20% |
|---|---|
| A members' cricket club's playing subscription | The same club's social membership, bar access and no cricket |
| A non-profit tennis club's court time for playing members | A commercial racquets centre's monthly gym membership |
| A rowing club's boat storage and changing room use for its crews | A privately owned health club's joining fee |
| An amateur golf club's competition entry fee | A retail loyalty or discount club's annual membership |
| A chartered institute's professional subscription (Group 9) | A trade magazine's subscription, or a networking group's membership sold for business promotion |

Under the table: the deciding question is never the word on the invoice. It is **who supplies it** and **whether the member is actually playing**.

**Worked examples (two, named to a trade):**
- A cricket club with 180 playing members at £120 and 60 social members at £45. The £21,600 of playing subscriptions is exempt; the £2,700 of social subscriptions is standard-rated, so £450 of that is VAT (£2,700 at 1/6 if the price is VAT-inclusive; state the assumption explicitly). Show the split, then say that the exempt side now blocks recovery on attributable costs.
- A commercially owned gym charging the same £45 a month. No exemption at any point, because the eligible-body conditions fail at the first limb. Every subscription is standard-rated and all input tax is recoverable. Same activity, opposite VAT treatment.

**What people get wrong:**
- Assuming "sport" is the test. The eligible body is the test, and a commercial operator fails it.
- Assuming a constitutional non-distribution clause alone is enough. It is one of four limbs.
- Treating a social membership as exempt because the club is exempt.
- Missing that being partly exempt blocks input tax, which is usually a bigger number than the VAT saved on the social subscriptions.
- Assuming any trade or professional subscription is exempt. Group 9 is narrower than "any body a business joins".
