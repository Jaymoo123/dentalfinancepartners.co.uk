## Asset type + play

Answer-first page built on timing. Under the cash basis an expense is deductible **when you pay it**, not when you are billed, and that single shift moves real money between tax years for a business with supplier credit. The second half of the page is capital spending, which is the part readers find genuinely surprising: a £1,200 tool is an ordinary expense when paid, with no pool and no Annual Investment Allowance, and a car is the one exception that stays in the capital allowances system.

## Required structure (H2 skeleton)

1. **First 60 words, the answer.** Under the cash basis you deduct an expense in the year you pay it, not the year you are billed for it. The ordinary wholly and exclusively test still decides whether an expense is allowable at all. What changes is timing, plus capital spending, which comes off as a normal expense when paid instead of going through capital allowances.
2. The test that does not change: wholly and exclusively.
3. The test that does: paid, not incurred.
4. **Boundary table** (see below).
5. Capital spending: deducted when paid, with no pool and no AIA.
6. What stays capital: cars, land and buildings, financial assets, intangibles, and the cost of buying or selling a business.

## BOUNDARY TABLE (mandatory, exact columns)

Columns: **"Cash basis: deducted when paid"** against **"Traditional accounting (accruals): deducted when incurred"**. Four trade-anchored row pairs minimum:

| Cash basis: deducted when paid | Traditional accounting (accruals) |
|---|---|
| A joiner's £3,000 timber invoice dated 20 March, paid 6 May: deducted in the later year | Deducted in the year of the invoice |
| A cafe's £1,400 espresso machine: the full cost is an allowable expense when paid, no pool, no AIA | Capital: AIA or writing down allowance, in a pool |
| A driving instructor's car: **not** deducted as an expense, it stays in capital allowances (CAA 2001 s.1A(4)) | Same, capital allowances by CO2 |
| A landscaper's £600 of stock bought in March and unsold at the year end: deducted in full when paid, no closing stock adjustment | Closing stock is carried forward and not deducted until sold |
| A mechanic's £900 of loan interest on a business loan: deductible in full, the old £500 cap was abolished | Deductible in full |
| A salon's purchase of the business it took over: **not** deductible on either basis (ITTOIA 2005 s.33A excludes business acquisition and disposal costs) | Not deductible |
