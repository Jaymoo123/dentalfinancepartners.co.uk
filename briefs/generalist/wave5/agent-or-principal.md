## Asset type + play

The highest-consequence question on this site that almost nobody writes about in public. Whether a business acts as agent or principal decides **what its turnover is**, and therefore whether it crosses the £90,000 VAT registration threshold at all. A booking agency handling £400,000 of client money on 12% commission is a £48,000 business if it is an agent and a £400,000 business if it is a principal. That is the commercial point and it should be the first worked example.

The second half of the page is disbursements, which is the same question wearing different clothes: a payment is only outside the value of your own supply if **all eight** HMRC conditions are met, and the condition most recharges fail is that the third party's supply was genuinely made to the client rather than to you.

The wedge over every competitor page: they give four or five conditions from memory. Give all eight, verbatim, with the source, plus the separate-records requirement that proves no input tax was claimed.

## BOUNDARY TABLE (mandatory, exact columns)

Columns: **"Agent, turnover is the commission"** against **"Principal, turnover is the whole amount"**. Four trade-anchored row pairs minimum:

| Agent, turnover is the commission | Principal, turnover is the whole amount |
|---|---|
| A letting agent collecting rent for a landlord and keeping 10%: the tenancy is between landlord and tenant | A letting agent who takes a property on a guaranteed-rent basis and sublets it: the agent is supplying the accommodation |
| A travel agent booking a hotel where the contract is between the hotel and the traveller | A tour operator selling its own package at its own price, buying the hotel in its own name |
| A driving instructor franchisee's fee collected by the franchisor on the instructor's behalf and disclosed as such | A driving school that contracts with the pupil, sets the price and pays the instructor |
| A garage passing on an MOT carried out by an approved test centre in the customer's name, itemised at exactly cost | The same garage buying the MOT in its own name and billing it on with a margin: a recharge, standard rated with the rest of the job |
| A salon booking platform charging a fee to the stylist for each booking | A salon selling the stylist's time to the client at the salon's price |

The garage MOT pair is the row to work hardest: it is the classic disbursement failure and it lets you carry the eight conditions in a real trade setting.

## Worked examples (two, real figures)

1. The threshold example. A booking business handles £400,000 of customer money at 12% commission. As an agent its taxable turnover is £48,000, below the £90,000 registration threshold. As a principal it is £400,000, comfortably over, and it must register and charge VAT on the full price. Same trade, same cash, two completely different businesses.
2. The disbursement example. A garage's invoice showing an MOT at exactly the amount the test centre charged, separately itemised, outside the value of the garage's own supply, against the same MOT bought in the garage's own name and marked up, which is part of a standard-rated supply. Show the VAT difference in pounds.
