## Asset type + play

Answer-first page on the single question every director asks and almost always gets wrong. The wedge is that the block turns on **availability for private use, not actual private use**. A business owner who genuinely never drives the car privately still cannot reclaim, because there is nothing preventing them from doing so. Article 7(2G) is explicit: intending to make the car available to any person for private use, for consideration or not, defeats the exclusive-business test. Quote it.

Second wedge: the things that **are** recoverable and that readers assume are not, repairs and maintenance in full, and the road fuel scale charge as the alternative to splitting mileage.

## BOUNDARY TABLE (mandatory, exact columns)

Columns: **"Blocked, no input tax"** against **"Recoverable input tax"**. Four trade-anchored row pairs minimum:

| Blocked, no input tax | Recoverable input tax |
|---|---|
| A garage buys a car for the owner, kept at home overnight with keys available: blocked, art. 7(2G) | The same garage's recovery truck or a van with a payload of one tonne or more: not a car for VAT, recoverable |
| A salon buys a car for the owner's business travel with no written bar on private use: blocked | A driving school's tuition car used for driving instruction: art. 7(2E) qualifying use, recoverable |
| A florist's delivery car available to staff at weekends: blocked | A florist's pool car kept at the shop, insured for business use only, never taken home: recoverable, subject to holding the evidence |
| A taxi firm buys a car on a margin-scheme invoice: nothing to reclaim because there is no VAT on the purchase (see `vat-on-second-hand-cars`) | A taxi firm buys a VAT-qualifying car for hire with a driver carrying passengers: recoverable |
| A cafe leases a car available for the owner's private use: 50% of the VAT on the lease charge is blocked | The separately invoiced maintenance element of that same lease: recoverable in full |
| Fuel put to private use with no scale charge and no mileage split | Repairs, servicing, tyres and MOT work on a business-used car: recoverable in full regardless of private motoring |

## Worked examples (two, real figures)

1. A company buys a car at £24,000 including £4,000 VAT for a director who takes it home. Nothing is recoverable, and the full £24,000 goes into the capital allowances pool (link `writing-down-allowance-cars`). Contrast the same £24,000 spent on a one-tonne-payload van, where the £4,000 is recoverable.
2. A quarter's motoring costs on one business-used car: £480 including VAT of repairs (VAT recovered in full) plus fuel where the business chooses the scale charge, so all the fuel VAT is recovered and output tax is accounted for by the CO2 band. Do not quote a scale charge amount (see HP GAPS).
