## Asset type + play

The page exists because of one change and one non-change, and the non-change is the more valuable half.

**The change:** private school fees became standard-rated at 20% on supplies made on or after 1 January 2025. Finance Act 2025 inserted a new Part 3 (Exceptions) into Schedule 9 of VATA 1994 and amended s.31(1) so a supply is exempt only if it is in Part 2 **and not** in Part 3. Part 3 removes the exemption from education by a private school, vocational training by a private school, and closely related board and lodging.

**The non-change, which is the page's wedge:** the private tuition exemption at **Schedule 9 Group 6 item 2 is untouched.** A self-employed tutor teaching a school subject personally is still exempt, and the obvious reader error is to assume tutors lost the exemption in January 2025. **They did not.** Say it in those words, prominently, because no competitor does and three of this site's own trade pages have readers who need it.

## Required structure (H2 skeleton)

Open with a 40-60 word BLUF in plain prose: education is exempt from VAT when supplied by an eligible body, but private school fees are not, and have been standard-rated at 20% since 1 January 2025. Private tuition by an independent teacher in a school subject remains exempt and was not affected by that change.

1. The 40-60 word answer (BLUF)
2. The rule before and after: VATA 1994 s.31 and Schedule 9 Group 6, item 1, education and vocational training by an eligible body. Then s.31(1) as amended by FA 2025, and the new Schedule 9 Part 3
3. What Part 3 removes: education by a private school, vocational training by a private school, closely related board and lodging. What it preserves: English as a foreign language, nursery classes, higher education courses
4. **Boundary table: exempt education against standard-rated education** (rows below)
5. The dates, laid out as a sequence, because the anti-forestalling rule is what people get wrong: s.48 catches prepayments made on or after 29 July 2024; per s.49 the provisions are treated as in force from 30 October 2024, with effect for supplies on or after 1 January 2025; fees invoiced or paid from 30 October 2024 for terms starting on or after 1 January 2025 are taxed at the time of the invoice or payment; fees invoiced or paid between 29 July and 29 October 2024 for such terms are taxed on the first day of that term
6. **Private tuition is still exempt.** Group 6 item 2, the three cumulative conditions, and the exclusions
7. Nurseries and childcare: the welfare route, not the education route. Ofsted-registered (state-regulated) childcare is exempt as welfare under Schedule 9 Group 7 item 9, and there is a timing trap (below)
8. What exemption costs a provider: no output VAT, no input tax recovery, exempt turnover outside the £90,000 test. Partial exemption handed to `vat-exemption.md`
9. Worked examples (two, below)
10. What people get wrong
11. FAQ block, 10 to 14 FAQs
12. Links out

**Boundary table (MANDATORY). Two columns: "Exempt (no VAT)" against "Standard-rated at 20%".** Minimum four trade-anchored row pairs:

| Exempt: no VAT charged | Standard-rated at 20% |
|---|---|
| A self-employed maths tutor teaching GCSE lessons personally | The same tutor's lessons delivered by tutors she engages, or billed through her limited company |
| An Ofsted-registered nursery's childcare fees (welfare, Group 7) | An independent school's termly fees, from 1 January 2025 |
| A private school's nursery class fees (preserved carve-out) | That school's boarding fees, closely related to the taxable education |
| An English as a foreign language course (preserved carve-out) | A private school's vocational training courses |
| A university's higher education courses (preserved carve-out) | A hobby or non-curriculum coaching course sold by an individual |

Under the table: after January 2025 the deciding question for a fee-charging provider is **what kind of body is supplying it**, not whether the activity looks like teaching.

**Worked examples (two, named to the trade):**
- An independent school charging £5,400 a term. From 1 January 2025 that is standard-rated, so VAT of £1,080 arises on a VAT-exclusive fee (state the assumption) and the school now makes taxable supplies, which means it can recover input tax it previously could not. Give both halves; the recovery side is the part schools underestimate.
- A sole-trader tutor billing £34,000 a year for lessons she teaches herself in school subjects. Entirely exempt, so no VAT is charged, none is recoverable, and none of the £34,000 counts toward the £90,000 registration threshold. Then vary it: she engages two other tutors who bill £18,000 between them. That £18,000 is standard-rated, and it is the slice the rolling 12-month test runs on.

**What people get wrong:**
- Assuming tutors lost the exemption in January 2025. They did not. This is the single most common error on this topic.
- Assuming the change hit nurseries. Nursery classes are a preserved carve-out, and registered childcare runs on the welfare exemption instead.
- Missing the anti-forestalling dates and treating a prepayment made in August 2024 as exempt.
- Assuming a tutor operating through a limited company keeps the exemption. The hook is an "individual teacher".
- Forgetting that a school that becomes taxable also becomes able to recover input tax.
- Assuming exempt fee income counts toward £90,000. It does not.
