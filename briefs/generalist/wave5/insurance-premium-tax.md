## Asset type + play

Answer-first reference page on a tax nobody explains in public. The wedge is the sentence no competitor writes: **IPT is not VAT, it is not recoverable as input tax, and the two never appear on the same premium.** Insurance is an exempt supply (VATA 1994 Sch 9 Group 2), so there is no VAT to reclaim; IPT sits on top instead and is a cost to the business, full stop. That single point is the page's commercial value, because it is the line that gets wrongly entered as reclaimable VAT in a bookkeeping system every day.

Second wedge: the higher rate. Readers assume 20% is "the VAT rate applied to insurance". It is not. It is the IPT higher rate, and it attaches to how the insurance was sold, not to what it covers. Ordinary motor insurance bought from an insurer is 12%; the same cover sold by the vehicle supplier can be 20%. That is the "car insurance premium tax" query answered properly.

**Boundary table (MANDATORY). Two columns: "IPT at 12% (standard rate)" against "IPT at 20% (higher rate)".** Minimum four trade-anchored row pairs:

| IPT at 12% (standard rate) | IPT at 20% (higher rate) |
|---|---|
| A driving instructor's motor policy bought direct from an insurer or through a broker | The same cover on a hire car, arranged by the vehicle supplier rather than an insurance company |
| A hairdresser's shop contents and public liability policy | The extended warranty the salon's till supplier sells with the till (insurance on a mechanical or electrical appliance, sold by the supplier of the goods) |
| A builder's van insurance arranged through his own broker | A travel policy sold to the same builder for a working trip abroad |
| A care home's employers' liability and buildings cover | The appliance cover a white-goods retailer sells alongside a washing machine to that care home |
| A private medical policy bought by a company for its staff (general insurance, 12%) | Not applicable, medical cover is not a higher-rate class; say so explicitly in the row or a note under the table |

The deciding test in the right-hand column is always **who arranged the sale**, not what is covered. Make that explicit in a sentence under the table.

**Worked examples (two, named to a trade, real figures from the locked rates only):**
- A courier's van policy quoted at £1,200 net. IPT at 12% is £144, total payable £1,344. No VAT appears on the document and none of the £144 is recoverable on a VAT return.
- The same courier buys a £180 travel policy for an overseas job. IPT at 20% is £36, total £216. Same business, same month, two different rates, and the reason is the class of insurance, not the customer.

**What people get wrong (write this section, it is the most valuable part):**
- Entering IPT in the VAT box as reclaimable input tax. It is not input tax and there is nothing to reclaim.
- Assuming 20% on an insurance document is VAT. It never is.
- Assuming ordinary motor insurance is higher-rated because cars "feel" like the vehicle class. It is 12% when bought from an insurer or broker.
- Assuming a rate changed in Finance Act 2026. Neither rate changed. Only scope moved.
- Treating the IPT charge as a reason not to insure. It is a cost line, and the premium is still deductible against profit on ordinary principles, which is where `can-i-claim-home-insurance-on-my-taxes.md` picks up.
