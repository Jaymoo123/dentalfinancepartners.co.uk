## BOUNDARY TABLE (explicit spec, load-bearing)

Two columns: **"Qualifies for an RHL multiplier"** against **"Pays the national multiplier"**. Trade-anchored on both sides, showing what actually moves a property across the line: use and rateable value band. Minimum four row pairs:

| Qualifies for an RHL multiplier (England, 2026/27) | Pays the national multiplier |
|---|---|
| A high street cafe with a rateable value of £28,000, paying 38.2p | An office above the same cafe, not retail, hospitality or leisure use, paying 43.2p at the same rateable value band |
| A 40 cover restaurant with a rateable value of £64,000, paying 43p | A food production unit at £64,000 that is not an RHL property, paying 48p |
| A town centre pub at £45,000, paying 38.2p | A distribution depot at £45,000, paying 43.2p |
| A 30 room hotel at £180,000, paying 43p | A large city centre hotel at £520,000: above the £500,000 rateable value threshold, so 50.8p and no RHL multiplier |
| A leisure operator's bowling site at £95,000, paying 43p | The same operator's £600,000 flagship venue, paying 50.8p |

The last two pairs carry the £500,000 ceiling, which is the single most commonly missed part of the new regime. Keep them.

## Worked examples (two or three, trade-named, real figures)

- A cafe with a rateable value of £28,000. RHL multiplier 38.2p gives a gross bill of £10,696 (£28,000 × 0.382). The national multiplier at that band would be 43.2p, giving £12,096. Difference £1,400 a year. Show the arithmetic.
- A restaurant with a rateable value of £64,000. RHL multiplier 43p gives £27,520. National equivalent 48p gives £30,720. Difference £3,200.
- A hotel with a rateable value of £520,000. Above the threshold, so 50.8p applies and there is no RHL multiplier: £264,160. Use this one to make the ceiling concrete.

State clearly that these are gross bills before any further relief, and that the rateable value comes from the operator's own valuation, not from a rule of thumb.

## What people get wrong (the section no competitor writes)

- "RHL relief gives you 40% off your business rates." It ended for new claims on 1 April 2026. The replacement is a permanently lower multiplier, not a discount you claim.
- "There is a cash cap I need to watch." There is no 2026/27 cash cap. Caps belonged to the old relief schemes. A multiplier has no cap.
- "I need to apply for the RHL multiplier." You do not. It is applied through the multiplier used to calculate the bill, not through a claim form.
- "Every hospitality property gets the lower multiplier." Not above £500,000 rateable value. At or above that, the property pays 50.8p and gets no RHL multiplier.
- "RHL and Small Business Rate Relief are the same thing." Two different schemes with different tests. SBRR is still a claim and is still live.
- "These figures apply across the UK." Business rates are devolved. Everything here is England.
