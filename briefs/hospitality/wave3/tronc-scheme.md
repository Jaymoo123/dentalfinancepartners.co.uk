## Asset type + play

The NIC mechanic, explained as a two-test problem. The entire commercial value of this page is one distinction that most hospitality content gets wrong:

**A tronc is a tax mechanic. The Tips Act is an employment law duty. They are two different tests and neither one discharges the other.**

The common error, and the thing to write against, is "set up a tronc and you comply with the Tips Act and save NIC" framed as one action with one test. It is two tests: independence of allocation (NIC), and fairness plus transparency plus no deductions plus timing (Tips Act). A compliant tronc does not automatically discharge the Tips Act duty. A Tips Act compliant distribution run by the employer does not earn the NIC disregard.

Second wedge: the statutory anchor. Nearly every page on this term cites NIM02922 alone. A manual is HMRC practice. The law is SI 2001/1004 Schedule 3 Part X paragraph 5. Cite both, and say which is which. That single move outranks the citation quality of the field.

## Required structure (H2 skeleton)

The boundary table never opens the page. Open in prose.

1. **First 60 words, the answer.** A tronc is a separate arrangement for pooling and distributing tips, run by a troncmaster who decides allocation independently of the employer. Where the employer is not involved in allocation, directly or indirectly, the payments are disregarded from earnings for National Insurance, so neither employer nor employee NIC is due. PAYE income tax is still due.
2. **The rule and its two sources.** SI 2001/1004 Schedule 3 Part X paragraph 5 (the law) and NIM02922 (HMRC practice). Quote the regulation's two limbs. Explain that in the tronc case the operative limb is the second: the employer does not allocate the payment, directly or indirectly, to the earner.
3. **What a tronc actually is, in operating terms.** The troncmaster, the tronc PAYE scheme, the money flow from card tips and service charge into the tronc and out to staff.
4. **BOUNDARY TABLE: NIC disregard survives against NIC disregard destroyed.** See the dedicated section below. Load-bearing.
5. **The two things that do NOT destroy the disregard.** Both are in the anchor and both are counterintuitive, so they are the most useful paragraphs on the page: (a) it being a term of employment that the employee is entitled to share in an independently run tronc, and (b) the employer reserving the right to make deductions from gratuities before they reach the tronc. Note the Tips Act interacts with (b), so cross-reference.
6. **The tronc is not the Tips Act.** The two-test section. This is the page's spine. Explain that the Tips Act duty is mandatory and applies whether or not a tronc exists, while the NIC treatment is optional in the sense that an employer can simply run tips through payroll and pay the NIC.

## BOUNDARY TABLE (explicit spec, load-bearing)

Two columns: **"NIC disregard survives"** against **"NIC disregard destroyed, NIC due"**. Each row is one trade on both sides of the independence line. Minimum four trade-anchored row pairs:

| NIC disregard survives | NIC disregard destroyed, NIC due |
|---|---|
| A restaurant where the troncmaster is a head waiter who sets the points allocation and the owner never sees the split before it runs | The same restaurant where the owner signs off the allocation each week before it is paid |
| A hotel where the tronc rules were written by the troncmaster and the tronc committee, and the general manager is not on it | The same hotel where the general manager sits on the tronc committee and votes on shares |
| A pub where the employment contract simply says staff are entitled to share in the independently run tronc | The same pub where the contract specifies each role's tronc percentage, set by the employer |
| A cafe where the employer deducts nothing and passes the full card service charge to the tronc, and the troncmaster allocates it | The same cafe where the employer pays a fixed top-up out of its own funds alongside the tronc; the top-up is contractual earnings and NIC-liable (HP 7, NIM02935) |
| A bar where an external tronc administrator runs the scheme under its own tronc PAYE reference | The same bar where the owner instructs the administrator on who should get more this month |

The third pair is the one that surprises operators, because they assume any contractual mention kills it. Keep it and cite the anchor.

## Worked examples (two or three, trade-named, real figures)

- A 20 seat restaurant distributing £4,000 of card service charge a month through an independent tronc. Show the employer NIC that would otherwise arise at 15% above the £5,000 annual secondary threshold (HP 12), and note that employee NIC is also disregarded. State clearly that PAYE income tax is deducted from the tronc payments regardless.
- The same £4,000 run through ordinary payroll instead, with the employer allocating it. Show the employer NIC bill. Be explicit that the difference is not a loophole, it is the price of genuine independence.
- A hotel that has a tronc but the GM approves the split. Show that the tronc's existence buys nothing: the allocation involvement destroys the disregard and NIC is due on the whole amount.

Every figure must show its arithmetic. Flag that Employment Allowance (HP 13, £10,500) may absorb some employer NIC for a small operator, so the saving is not the headline figure for everyone.

## What people get wrong (the section no competitor writes)

- "Set up a tronc and you have complied with the Tips Act." No. Two different regimes, two different tests. The Act applies whether or not a tronc exists.
- "The tronc saves tax." It saves National Insurance. PAYE income tax is still due on every tronc payment.
- "The owner can chair the tronc committee as long as they are fair." Employer involvement in allocation, direct or indirect, destroys the disregard. Fairness is the Act's test, not NIC's.
- "Mentioning the tronc in the employment contract kills it." It does not. Entitlement to share in an independently run tronc is expressly not employer allocation.
- "Tips can top staff up to minimum wage." They can never count toward NMW or NLW.
- "Cash tips kept by the waiter are the employer's problem." They are the worker's own to declare.
- "A tronc means we can deduct a card processing fee." The Act requires qualifying tips to be passed on without deductions. Read that duty from the Act page.
