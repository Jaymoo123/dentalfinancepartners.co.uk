## Asset type + play

A short, exact, correct answer page on a boundary that almost every source gets wrong in the same direction. The play is honesty about who the carve-out helps.

The answer is: usually yes, 20%. Animal feeding stuffs sit inside the Group 1 general items, but excepted item 6 pulls pet food back out. The working dog carve-out exists, but it is a **labelling** carve-out that sits with the manufacturer and how the product is held out for sale. It is not something a working dog owner can claim at the till. And there is a trap inside the carve-out: all biscuit and meal for cats and dogs is standard-rated regardless, because that limb of item 6 is unconditional.

Most competing pages write it as "working dogs are VAT free". That is wrong twice over. Writing it correctly, with the three-way split and the biscuit trap, is the whole differentiator.

## Required structure (H2 skeleton)

The boundary table never opens the page. Open in prose.

1. **First 60 words, the answer.** Yes, in almost every case. Dog food and pet food generally carry VAT at 20%, because pet food is an excepted item removed from the zero rate for food. A narrow exception exists for food specially formulated and held out for sale exclusively for working dogs, but never for biscuit or meal, which is always standard-rated.
2. **The rule and its source.** VATA 1994 Schedule 8 Group 1, excepted item 6, quoted. Then Notice 701/15 paragraphs 6.1, 6.4 and 6.5.
3. **The three-way split.** Set this out plainly as three cases: (a) all biscuit and meal for cats and dogs, standard-rated, unconditional; (b) canned, packaged or prepared food held out for sale as pet food, standard-rated; (c) food specially formulated and held out for sale exclusively for working dogs, zero-rated, unless it is biscuit or meal.
4. **"Held out for sale" is the test, not what the dog does.** Labelling, packaging, display, invoicing, advertising, catalogue listing. A product described as suitable "for all breeds, size and age of dog" is held out as pet food and is standard-rated, whatever the buyer's dog does for a living.
5. **BOUNDARY TABLE: 20% against zero-rated.** See the dedicated section below. Load-bearing.
6. **Which working categories qualify.** Sheepdog breeds, assistance dogs, gun dogs, racing greyhounds, per Notice 701/15 para 6.4. Name them, cite them, and say the list is the manufacturer's route to a zero-rated product, not the buyer's route to a refund.
7. **Cat food, bird food and the rest of item 6.** Packaged foods (not being pet foods) for birds other than poultry or game are excepted; and the biscuit-and-meal limb catches cats as well as dogs. This is where `is there vat on pet food` gets answered in its own words.
8. **Worked examples.** Two or three, trade-named, with real figures. See below.
9. **What people get wrong.**
10. **FAQ block, 10 to 14 FAQs**, in the words people typed.
11. **Links out.**

## BOUNDARY TABLE (explicit spec, load-bearing)

Two columns: **"Standard-rated at 20%"** against **"Zero-rated"**. Trade-anchored on both sides. Minimum four row pairs. The trades here are the retail and supply chain that sells the product, plus the working contexts that buy it:

| Standard-rated at 20% | Zero-rated |
|---|---|
| A pet shop's tinned dog food labelled for all breeds, sizes and ages | The same pet shop's sacked feed labelled and sold exclusively as working dog food, not biscuit or meal |
| A farm supply store's dog biscuits, even on a shelf marked "working dogs" | The same store's specially formulated working sheepdog food that is not biscuit or meal |
| A country store's gun dog "meal", because the biscuit and meal limb is unconditional | The same store's gun dog wet or extruded food held out exclusively for working gun dogs |
| A supermarket's cat food in any form, canned, pouched or dry | Not available: there is no working-cat carve-out; item 6 leaves no zero-rated route for cat food |
| A greyhound trainer buying a supermarket own-brand dog food for racing greyhounds, because it is held out as ordinary pet food | The same trainer buying a product specially formulated and labelled exclusively for racing greyhounds |
| A garden centre's packaged wild bird food | Not caught by the bird limb: poultry and game feed, which sits outside the excepted item |

The two "not available" rows are deliberate. They teach the reader that the carve-out is narrow and sits with the label, which is the whole point of the page.

## Worked examples (two or three, trade-named, real figures)

- A country store sells a 15kg sack of specially formulated working sheepdog food at £42.00. Held out exclusively for working dogs and not biscuit or meal, so zero-rated: the customer pays £42.00 and there is no VAT in the price.
- The same store sells a 15kg sack of dog biscuit at £42.00. Biscuit is standard-rated unconditionally, so the price carries £7.00 of VAT (£35.00 net plus 20%). Same shelf, same customer, same dog, different VAT, because of the product form.
- A pet shop sells a £24.00 tin case labelled for all breeds and ages. Standard-rated: £20.00 net plus £4.00 VAT. Note that a customer who genuinely owns a working dog cannot reclaim it, because the test is how the product was held out for sale.

## What people get wrong (the section no competitor writes)

- "Working dogs are VAT free." They are not. The relief attaches to a product that is specially formulated and held out for sale exclusively for working dogs. It attaches to the label, not to the dog.
- "I own a working dog so I should not be charged VAT." The test is how the product was held out for sale. Buying an ordinary pet food for a working dog does not zero-rate it.
- "It says working dogs on the bag, so it is zero-rated." Not if it is biscuit or meal. That limb of item 6 is unconditional and catches cats and dogs alike.
- "A product suitable for all breeds and ages can still be working dog food." No. That description holds it out as pet food, which makes it standard-rated.
- "Pet food must be zero-rated because food is zero-rated." Food for human consumption is the general item. Pet food is expressly excepted back out.
- "There is a working cat equivalent." There is not.
