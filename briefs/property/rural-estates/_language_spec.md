## 2. Answer patterns

**P1. The winners state what the relief does before they state what governs it.**
Them (W1, first sentence): "Business Property Relief (BPR) can significantly reduce inheritance tax
(IHT) payable when a person dies, or in some instances, when a person gifts assets to another person
or to a trust during their lifetime."
Them (W7, first sentence under its H1): "The simple answer is, No, agricultural land in the UK is
not automatically exempt from Capital Gains Tax (CGT), but there are specific conditions and reliefs
that can significantly reduce or defer the tax liability for landowners."
Us (APR key-benefits, first sentence): "Most landing pages on Agricultural Property Relief lead with
the relief rate."
Us (cap-impact, first sentence): "Note: the GOV.UK announcement-stage summary page still cites the
£1m headline figure announced 30 October 2024; the enacted FA 2026 figure verified against
legislation.gov.uk is £2.5 million."
Ours opens on a meta-argument about other people's content, and the cap page opens on an editorial
footnote about a GOV.UK inconsistency. Neither answers the query in the first sentence. W7's
"The simple answer is, No" is the pattern to take: the reader's yes-or-no, then the qualification in
the same sentence.

**P2. Our Pawson page already does P1 correctly. It is the model in our own set.**
Us (Pawson, first sentence): "If you own a buy-to-let portfolio and you have read that Business
Property Relief might shelter it from inheritance tax, save yourself the planning time: it does
not."
That is answer-first, second person, no statute in the sentence. It is also the only one of our four
pages with a second-person rate anywhere near a consumer winner (11.0 against W7's 10.9). Its
problem is what follows: 37 case-law citations and 14.4 statute references per 1,000 words from the
second paragraph onward.

**P3. A question becomes a heading only where the question is the reader's, and the heading is a
whole question.**
Them (W2): "Does a farmhouse qualify for Agricultural Property Relief?"
Them (W4): "Do you pay Stamp Duty Land Tax on agricultural land?", "How much stamp duty land tax
will you have to pay on agricultural land?", "Can you claim Stamp Duty Relief on agricultural land?"
Them (W6): "What Exactly Counts as Agricultural Property?"
Us (APR key-benefits): "The s.115(2) qualification gate", "Agricultural value versus market value
(s.115(3))", "The 100 per cent vs 50 per cent rate (s.116)", "The 2-year and 7-year minimum periods
(s.117)".
Us (maximising-BPR): nineteen headings, zero questions, thirteen of them containing a section
number.
No winner puts a statute reference in a heading. We put one in eleven headings across two pages. A
reader searching "do farmers pay inheritance tax" does not scan for "s.115(2)".

**P4. The number goes early in the sentence and stands alone, without its authority.**
Them (W3): "From 6 April 2026, a new £2.5m allowance will apply to the combined value of business
and agricultural property assets that qualify for 100% relief under APR or BPR."
Them (W6): "The government will cap this 100% relief to only the first £1 million of your combined
agricultural and business properties. Anything over £1 million will receive just 50% relief,
effectively meaning you'll pay a 20% inheritance tax on the excess."
Them (W5): "Any unused part of the £2.5 million allowance will be transferrable between spouses and
civil partners, enabling couples to benefit from a combined allowance of up to £5 million, and the
allowance will refresh every seven years."
Us (Pawson, same point): "The 100% rate (capped at £2.5m combined with Agricultural Property Relief
from 6 April 2026 under IHTA 1984 s.124D) and the 50% rate above the cap depend on the asset being
'relevant business property' under section 105 IHTA 1984."
Us (APR key-benefits, same point): "This page walks the gate first, then the rate, then the £2.5
million combined cap under IHTA 1984 s.124D as inserted by FA 2026 Schedule 12 paragraph 4."
W3 and our Pawson page carry the same fact. W3 spends 27 words on it and no citation. We spend 46
words and two citations, and the figure arrives inside a parenthesis. Note also that W6 converts the
cap into the number the reader actually wants, an effective rate: "you'll pay a 20% inheritance tax
on the excess". None of our four pages does that conversion in its opening.

**P5. "It depends" is one clause, attached to the thing it depends on, and it is never the closing
move.**
Them (W1): "Subject to various conditions, the relief is given at either 100% or 50% depending on
the type of asset."
Them (W2): "Relief can be given at either 50% or 100%, depending on whether certain conditions have
been met about the occupation and use of that land."
Them (W3): "Some business activities are borderline and whether they will qualify for relief depends
on the nature of services provided, typically these include: Holiday businesses. Property
development."
Them (W1) on case law, in one sentence and no citations: "There is a lot of case law to help
determine whether a business is more trading than investing, particularly whether it consists mainly
of making or holding investments."
Us (Pawson, same point): "If your operation sits near the boundary, the deeper case law (the
post-Pawson decisions Green [2015] UKFTT 334 and Vigne [2018] UKUT 0357, and the IHTM25277 to
IHTM25280 series) is set out in Serviced Accommodation and BPR: Clearing the Pawson Trading
Threshold."
W1 tells the reader that a boundary exists and moves on. We name three authorities and a manual
range inside the sentence. The winner behaviour is to name the boundary, then point at a page or an
adviser, never at a citation.

**P6. The hand-off to CTA is a short block with a named service and a first-person verb, and it
comes after the answer is complete.**
Them (W5): "How we can help. The legislation is complex, and the most appropriate next steps depend
on the specific circumstances and requirements of the individuals, trusts and beneficiaries in
question. If you would like to discuss how these changes could affect your business succession or
estate planning, or trust arrangements, please contact us."
Them (W1): "Get in touch with one of our experts today using the form below."
Them (W4): "The dns team have wide experience of advising clients on SDLT for agriculture and can
assist you in many ways. Call us or email for more help and advice on SDLT for agricultural
purchases." W4 also puts "Book a call with an expert" once, early, immediately after the intro.
Them (W6, closing): "Act Now to Secure Your Family's Future! With these upcoming changes, planning
now can save your family thousands."
Us (APR key-benefits and maximising-BPR, both): "Use the form at the foot of the page to get in
touch."
Us (cap-impact): "Get in touch through the form at the foot of the page to discuss the right
sequence."
Us (Pawson, the best of ours): "We can produce a written planning view tailored to your portfolio
composition, current age and risk appetite."
Three of our four hand off to a form the reader must scroll to find, with no service named and no
reason. Only the Pawson page names a deliverable. Every winner names either a team or an outcome.

**P7. The winners close on the reader's position. We close on our own corpus.**
Them (W2, closing): "It can often be the case that the family farming business has undergone
considerable changes since the last inheritance tax review was carried out and with ever-rising
property prices, it's wise to keep an eye on the value of the assets that are not relievable. With
good planning and advice there may well be ways of improving the situation before a chargeable event
(and thus a potential problem) takes place."
Us (APR key-benefits, closing): "The pages compound: this one gets the reader through the
qualification gate; the siblings handle the rate, the cap and the strategic allocation that
follows."
Us (Pawson, closing): "External authority cited above: section 105 IHTA 1984 (legislation.gov.uk);
Pawson v HMRC [2013] UKUT 050 (TCC); IHTM25000 (BPR overview); APR/BPR reform from 6 April 2026
(gov.uk); Autumn Budget 2024 (gov.uk)."
Our APR page's last sentence describes our own information architecture to the reader. Our Pawson
page's last block is a bibliography. No winner ends on either.

**P8. What the winners leave out, counted.**
Across 10,827 words of winner prose: **zero worked examples with a named person**, **zero
computation tables** (W3 and W4 have one table each and neither is a worked calculation), **one
case-law citation in total** (W2 refers to "cases" generically once), and **no section numbers
anywhere except a single "Finance Act" in W2**. They also leave out anti-forestalling detail
(only W5 touches it, and once), trust-charge mechanics below headline level, and the distinction
between announcement-stage and enacted figures. W6 wins its top-10 slot with 514 words, three
headings and no citations of any kind.

## 3. The register prescription for this cluster

The harvest shows the split clearly. The consumer head, "farmers inheritance tax" and "inheritance
tax farmers" at 4,400 a month each, plus "farming inheritance tax", "farms and inheritance tax",
"inheritance tax and farms" and "inheritance tax farm" at 2,900 each, is held by **nobody** in the
top ten. The best tracked position on the head pair is saffery's APR page at p50 and p51, and the
same page holds p4 on "agricultural property relief". One page, adviser register, winning the
adviser query and losing the consumer query by forty-six places. The only consumer-register page
holding a top-10 slot anywhere in the tracked set is W6, at 514 words, 40.9 second-person per 1,000
words, zero statute, zero case law and three headings.

**What the consumer query wants that saffery does not supply.** Saffery answers "what is this
relief and what are its conditions" for someone who already knows the relief exists and has an
adviser. A reader typing "how to avoid inheritance tax on farms" or "farmers inheritance tax" is
asking three things saffery never states plainly: will my family have to pay, how much, and what do
I do now. W6 answers all three in 514 words, in the second person, with the cap converted into an
effective rate the reader can apply ("effectively meaning you'll pay a 20% inheritance tax on the
excess") and a concrete couples number ("couples can still pass on a farm worth up to £3 million
free from inheritance tax"). Saffery states the same reform as "the full 100% relief for APR (and
Business Property Relief (BPR)) will be limited", with the reader nowhere in the sentence. The
register difference is not vocabulary and it is not sentence length, which are near identical
(saffery 25.4 words and Flesch 38.5, taxaccountant 19.7 and 41.9). It is **grammatical subject**:
who the sentence is about. Saffery's subject is the relief. W6's subject is you and your family.

**What our current register is, on the evidence of our own Bing rows.** Our single Bing-visible page
in this family earns 16 rows at positions 6 to 9 and every one of them is statute-shaped:
"ihta1984", "ihtm25000", "s124d ihta 1984", "ihta 1984 schedule 1", "ihta 1984 apr relief
percentage", "apr cap". One row is a verbatim paste of a sentence from our own summary. Those are
the queries of a practitioner checking a provision, or of another adviser's research, not of a
farmer. We are ranking for the register we write in, and we write in a register that is denser in
statute than any adviser winner in the set (14.8 per 1,000 against a winner median of 0.0). That is
consistent, not contradictory: the pages are working exactly as written, and what they are written
for is a market of a few impressions a month with no lead intent.

**The prescription, by planned page type.**

## 5. Hard rules for writers in this cluster

Standing (all clusters): zero em-dashes; UK English; no PropertyTaxPartners pricing on page. Four of
seven winners use zero em-dashes.

Cluster-specific, from the measurements above:

1. **Statute density at or below 2 references per 1,000 words on adviser pages, and 0 on consumer
   pages.** Winner median is 0.0 and winner maximum is 0.8. Ours run 13.1 to 20.1. Count section
   numbers, Act names, Schedule references and HMRC manual codes together.
2. **At most one case-law citation per page, and only where the case is the answer.** Winners carry
   0 to 1. Our Pawson page carries 37. Pawson itself can be named on the page that is about Pawson;
   Green, Vigne, McCall, McKenna and the IHTM25277-25280 series move to a linked sibling or come out.
3. **No statute reference in any heading.** Zero winners do it. We do it in eleven headings across
   two pages.
4. **Never open on our own corpus, on another site's content, or on a GOV.UK inconsistency.** The
   first sentence answers the query. Consumer pages: the reader's yes or no, then the number.
   Adviser pages: what the relief does, then the condition.
5. **Convert every cap and rate into the number the reader applies.** The £2.5m allowance becomes
   "an effective 20% inheritance tax on the value above it", the couples position becomes a single
   pound figure. W6 does this in its second paragraph and it is the only consumer top-10 in the set.
6. **Second person is the grammatical subject of every sentence that carries a figure.** Consumer
   pages at 15 per 1,000 or above, adviser pages at 6 or above. Our median is 0.9 and the APR page
   is at zero.
7. **Question headings on consumer pages: at least half of H2s, each a complete question in the
   reader's words.** W4 runs 7 of 15 and holds 7 of its 8 tracked keywords in the top ten. Adviser
   pages need only one or two.
8. **"It depends" is one clause inside the sentence about the thing that varies.** Never a closing
   paragraph, never a referral hedge mid-article, never a citation chain.
9. **The CTA names a deliverable and a doer.** "We can produce a written planning view tailored to
   your portfolio" is the model, and it is already ours. Never "use the form at the foot of the
   page" as the page's last move, which is currently how three of our four pages end.
10. **No closing bibliography, no "the pages compound", no disclaimer paragraph.** Close on the
    reader's next decision.
11. **Do not chase word count, tables, worked examples or reading ease as levers in this cluster.**
    Measured non-levers: the winner set spans 514 to 3,684 words, carries two tables between seven
    pages, contains no named worked examples at all, and its reading ease is indistinguishable from
    ours. Keep worked examples where they genuinely help an adviser-register page; do not add them
    to hit a target.
12. **Enacted figures only.** £2.5m combined allowance, £5m transferable between spouses, 50% relief
    above, per IHTA 1984 s.124D. Two winners and both contrast pages carry the superseded £1m
    figure. Being right here is free differentiation, provided the number is stated in consumer
    language and not wrapped in its citation.
