## 6. Whitespace / content: legitimate planning levers only

Core content mandate, all figures from house_positions.md §15.4 and §22.1:

1. **Lifetime gifting + the 7-year clock + the anti-forestalling interaction.** State plainly: a
   gift made now starts its own 7-year clock as normal (PET treatment). But if the gift was made **on
   or after 30 October 2024**, and the donor dies **on or after 6 April 2026** and within 7 years of
   the gift, the new APR/BPR-allowance rules apply to that transfer even though the gift itself
   predates the 6 April 2026 commencement. Gifts made **before 30 October 2024** are not caught by
   this rule even on a post-6-April-2026 death. This is the single most commonly misunderstood point
   in the whole cluster and no tracked competitor explains it in consumer language.
2. **Spousal transfers + the £5m couples allowance.** Full spouse exemption applies as normal to
   inter-spouse transfers (unlimited, subject to the long-term-residence test where relevant — cross-
   reference only, do not re-derive residence rules here). Each spouse's unused £2.5m allowance
   transfers to the survivor, producing the up-to-£5m combined position (arithmetic derivation from
   the locked £2.5m figure, not a separately-locked number — state it as such).
3. **Timing of succession** — passing the farm on while both generations are alive lets the family
   use the 7-year clock deliberately rather than leaving it to chance at death; state this as a
   general principle, not a specific structuring recommendation (this is not a substitute for advice
   on any individual family's position).
4. **AIM 50% sub-tier** — AIM-listed trading company shares get 50% relief from 6 April 2026 (down
   from 100% after 2 years' ownership previously), and **critically this 50% does NOT consume the
   £2.5m combined allowance** — it sits in its own separate tier. Where relevant to a mixed estate,
   this is a genuine planning consideration worth naming plainly.
5. **What does NOT work, stated honestly, required section:**
   - **Deathbed planning** — gifts made shortly before death do not achieve PET status in time; the
     7-year clock does not compress.
   - **Reservation of benefit** — gifting the farmhouse or land while continuing to live in it or
     benefit from it rent-free triggers the gift-with-reservation rules; the gift is treated as still
     in the estate. State this plainly; do not gloss it.
   - **Artificial fragmentation** — splitting land or a farming business across multiple entities or
     trusts purely to multiply the £2.5m allowance is blocked by the same-settlor anti-fragmentation
     rule (house_positions §15.4: trusts settled by the same settlor on or after 30 October 2024
     share a single allowance divided across the cohort, not one allowance each).
6. **The honest "sometimes the answer is pay it" close.** For estates well above what gifting and
   the allowance can shelter, the honest position is: pay the effective 20% on the excess, and
   consider **whole-of-life insurance written in trust** to fund the liability without forcing a land
   sale. This is a legitimate, unglamorous answer that the market's avoidance-framed content
   (including W6) does not offer, because "insure and pay" doesn't fit an "avoid" headline. State it
   anyway; it is the honest answer for a real subset of readers, and the language spec's whole
   thesis for this cluster is that honesty is the available differentiation.
