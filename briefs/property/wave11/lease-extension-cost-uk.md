# Lease extension cost UK: premium, marriage value and the full fee stack (2026)

## Statutory anchor
- Primary: Leasehold Reform, Housing and Urban Development Act 1993 (c. 28) ss.39, 42, 56, 60 and Schedule 13 (premium payable on grant of new lease, including the 50% marriage value split at para 4). WebFetch-verified at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1993/28/contents and /schedule/13 on 2026-08-15. NOTE: legislation.gov.uk shows Sch 13 "omitted by 2024 c. 22 s.36(5)" under "Changes and effects yet to be applied" - the repeal is PROSPECTIVE, Sch 13 remains the operative valuation schedule today. State this explicitly in the page.
- Supporting: Leasehold Reform Act 1967 (c. 88) ss.9, 14-15 (houses); LFRA 2024 (c. 22) s.27 (2-year rule removal, IN FORCE 31 Jan 2025 via SI 2025/57), s.33 (990-year term, NOT in force), Sch 4 (standard valuation method abolishing marriage value, NOT in force); FA 2003 Sch 17A para 9 (SDLT surrender-and-regrant, covered by existing page, forward-link only).
- House position reference: §31.3a (in-force table), §31.5 (valuation architecture: term, reversion, marriage value STILL PAYABLE), §31.6 (cost loading 1.3-1.8x of headline premium), §31.9 (do-not-write list).

It is not (§31.3a). A sub-80-year lease extended today pays marriage value in full, split 50/50 with the freeholder, and that can double the premium around the 80-year cliff edge. THE ACCOUNTANT'S ANGLE: the premium and associated professional fees are capital expenditure, not deductible against rental income, but they enter the CGT base cost of the lease as enhancement expenditure on a future disposal, and company-held flats get corporation tax treatment instead; flag the record-keeping point (keep the completion statement for decades). Competitors structurally cannot write that layer.
