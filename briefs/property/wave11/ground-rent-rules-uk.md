### Verify-at-write items resolved (Stage 2, 2026-08-15)
- **GATING: 2022 Act commencement (RESOLVED FOR LOCK §31.E state 1; conductor patches "verify at write against the commencement SI(s)" at Stage 2b).** SI 2022/694, the Leasehold Reform (Ground Rent) Act 2022 (Commencement) Regulations 2022, verified at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2022/694/contents/made, 2026-08-15: the Act commenced **30 June 2022** for leases other than leases of retirement homes, and **1 April 2023** for retirement-home leases. The commonly stated 30 Jun 2022 date is CONFIRMED from the SI, cite SI 2022/694 not secondary sources.
- **GATING: enforcement penalties (RESOLVED FOR LOCK §31.E; note the lock says "s.8/enforcement penalty figures", the figures actually live in s.9).** Verified at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/1/section/9/enacted, 2026-08-15: **s.9 "Financial penalties"**: an enforcement authority (trading standards, per s.8 "Enforcement authorities", heading lock-verified at Stage 1b) may impose a financial penalty where "satisfied beyond reasonable doubt" of a s.3(1) breach; the penalty is "not to be less than £500" and "not to be more than £30,000" per breach. Supporting: **s.10 "Recovery of prohibited rent by enforcement authority"** (verified /enacted 2026-08-15): the authority can order the landlord (or receiver of the payment) to refund prohibited rent not already refunded; s.7 automatically converts a prohibited term to a peppercorn. Writer states: £500 minimum, £30,000 maximum per qualifying breach, plus refund orders; do not attribute the figures to s.8.
- **GATING: the HA 1988 Sch 1 AST trap, current position INCLUDING Renters' Rights Act interaction (RESOLVED FOR LOCK §31.E; this is a correction-of-record for most of the SERP).** Two-part verified answer:
  1. The historic trap was real: HA 1988 Sch 1 para 3A excludes tenancies at a rent of "£250 or less a year" (£1,000 or less in Greater London) from assured status; a long lease with ground rent ABOVE those figures could technically be an assured (shorthold) tenancy, with ground-rent arrears exposing the leaseholder to mandatory possession (Ground 8) rather than mere forfeiture procedure.
  2. **The trap is now closed for leases over 21 years.** HA 1988 Sch 1 fetched 2026-08-15 (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/50/schedule/1, revised version dated 14 Aug 2026): paragraph **3D**, inserted by **RRA 2025 s.31** and IN THE APPLIED TEXT (inserted 27 Dec 2025; consistent with HP §20.3 and §20.12), excludes fixed-term tenancies exceeding **21 years** from assured status regardless of rent level (para 3E covers a closed 7-to-21-year transitional cohort). CONSEQUENCE: a residential long lease (over 21 years) with ground rent over £250/£1,000 can NO LONGER be an assured tenancy. Page frames this as: the trap existed, drove doubling-clause panic and lender caution, and was closed by the Renters' Rights Act 2025; historic warnings across the SERP are out of date. Cite HP §20.3 discipline; writer re-fetches Sch 1 at write per §16.35.
- **Deductibility fork (verified):** PIM2205 "Rent paid out", verified live 2026-08-15: "That treatment should apply to any ground rent, rent-charge, ground annual or feu duty." Ground rent paid on a let flat = revenue-deductible; the premium to extend and remove it = capital (CG71141, see A9). Freeholder side: ground rent received = property income (PIM1056 "rentcharges, ground rents and feu duties" as income; writer verifies the leaf page at write).
