## FINDINGS

### lease-extension-cost-uk

[ADVISORY] lease-extension-cost-uk: "Budget £2,600 to £4,400 for the stack as a whole" — the page's own
fee table does not sum to this. £800+£600+£800+£600+£65 = **£2,865** minimum; £1,300+£900+£1,300+£900+£65
= **£4,465** maximum. The headline is £265 below its own floor and £65 below its own ceiling, and the
£2,600-£4,400 figure is reused in the metaDescription, the summary, four FAQ answers and the all-in
column of the band table. Drop-in fix: replace every instance of "£2,600 to £4,400" with "£2,900 to
£4,500" and re-run the band table all-in column (+£300 / +£100 on each row).

[ADVISORY] lease-extension-cost-uk: "<tr><td>Land Registry</td><td>£65</td>" — £65 is not a Land Registry
fee for this transaction. Registering a new lease granted for a premium is assessed on **Scale 1 on the
premium plus rent** (HM Land Registry registration services fees guidance). For premiums up to £80,000,
which covers every worked example on this page, Scale 1 is **£45 by post / £20 through the portal**. The
only £65 in the fee order is the Scale 2 portal fee for the £500,001-£1,000,000 band, which cannot apply
here. The figure is traceable to the HOA pack page, but HMLR's own scales contradict it. Drop-in fix:
"£20 to £45" with cell 3 reading "HM Land Registry Scale 1, assessed on the premium; £20 lodged through
the portal".

[ADVISORY] lease-extension-cost-uk: "Take Marcus, whose SPV holds a £450,000 flat with 72 years
unexpired. His illustrative premium, with marriage value now firmly in play, lands in the low-to-mid
£40,000s" — not derivable from the page's own stated method. Re-derived at the page's own 5% deferment
rate: reversion = £450,000/1.05^72 = £13,413; term at £150-£200 pa ≈ £2,500; freeholder's diminution
≈ £15,900. Marriage value at 72 years on relativity of 87-90% gives a tenant's 50% share of
£12,300-£19,050, so a premium of **£28,000 to £35,000**. Reaching "low-to-mid £40,000s" requires
relativity near 84%, below any mainstream published graph at 72 years. This matters because the
paragraph's conclusion turns on the number: "because the premium exceeds £40,000, SDLT enters the
picture". Drop-in fix: either raise the flat value ("a £600,000 flat with 72 years unexpired") or restate
as "lands in the £30,000s, and on a higher-value flat comfortably past the £40,000 mark at which SDLT
enters the picture".

[ADVISORY] lease-extension-cost-uk: "<tr><td>80 to 85 years</td>...<td>No</td>" (marriage value applies?)
— wrong at the boundary. Sch 13 para 4(2A) sets marriage value at nil only where the unexpired term
**exceeds** eighty years, so at exactly 80 years marriage value applies in full. The page's own body text
and FAQ 3 state this correctly ("once it falls to 80 years or below"), so the table row contradicts the
prose. Drop-in fix: change the band label to "Just over 80 to 85 years" and the cell to "No, but the
exemption is lost at exactly 80 years; serve your notice before the term reaches 80".

[ADVISORY] lease-extension-cost-uk: "Term (capitalised ground rent): around £2,000" (both the 84-year and
79-year worked examples, £150 pa fixed) — understated. At a conventional capitalisation rate the YP for
79-84 years is 16.5 at 6% and 19.7 at 5%, giving **£2,480 to £2,950**; "around £2,000" implies a
capitalisation rate above 7.5%. The premium totals still reconcile internally because the reversion
ranges absorb the difference, but the component line itself is soft. Drop-in fix: "around £2,500".

[ADVISORY] lease-extension-cost-uk: "HMRC's Capital Gains Manual at CG71141 covers allowable expenditure
on lease disposals" — CG71141 is titled and scoped to **short leases** ("A short lease of land is a
wasting asset..."), i.e. the TCGA 1992 Sch 8 para 1 wasting restriction. The sentence citing it is about
a lease that after extension will be 174 years long, so a reader following the link lands on the page
that does not govern them. The following sentence about the sub-50-year restriction IS supported by
CG71141. Drop-in fix: cite CG71141 only for the wasting-asset sentence, and use TCGA 1992 s.38 (or
CG15150) for the base-cost proposition. Same fix applies on the solicitor page.

[ADVISORY] lease-extension-cost-uk: the 79-year marriage-value range ("uplift ... £10,000 to £14,000, of
which the freeholder takes 50%, so £5,000 to £7,000") sits at the tenant-pessimistic end of published
relativity. Re-derived: at 95.5% relativity the tenant's share is ~£1,600; at 94% ~£3,900; the page's
range implies 92-93%. Defensible (the page labels the whole block "illustrative ranges ... in line with
published market examples") but the resulting "roughly double" claim is doing heavy work on one
unstated assumption. No fix required if the label stays; consider adding "on a relativity assumption
around 92-93%, which varies by graph and by location".

### lease-extension-solicitor-what-they-do

[ADVISORY] lease-extension-solicitor-what-they-do: "your own solicitor at roughly £800 to £1,300 ...
your valuer at £600 to £900, and ... the freeholder's legal and valuation costs at £1,200 to £2,100 on
top ... Across both sides, £3,600 to £6,600 of professional costs before the premium" — the three
components named in that sentence sum to **£2,600 to £4,300**, not £3,600 to £6,600. The £3,600-£6,598
figure is the SAM Conveyancing pack total, which is VAT-inclusive and assumes the s.42 notice work and
the conveyancing are charged as two separate £600-£1,200 lines. That basis is never stated to the
reader, so the lede appears to contradict itself by up to £2,300. Drop-in fix: "...on top. Add VAT and
any valuer negotiation time and £3,600 to £6,600 of professional costs before the premium is a realistic
planning range." Same fix in FAQ 3.

[ADVISORY] lease-extension-solicitor-what-they-do: "<td>£800-£1,300 combined (itemised: £600-£1,200
notice work, £600-£1,200 conveyancing where charged separately)</td>" — the parenthetical contradicts the
headline in the same cell. If both lines are charged separately the total is £1,200-£2,400, which cannot
be "£800-£1,300 combined". Two different sources (HOA combined, SAM itemised) have been merged without
reconciliation. Drop-in fix: "£800-£1,300 where quoted as one fixed fee; £1,200-£2,400 where the s.42
notice work and the conveyancing are billed as separate lines".

[ADVISORY] lease-extension-solicitor-what-they-do: "Adding up a typical failed claim: £1,500 to £2,500 of
wasted professional costs on both sides" — an unlabelled precise range with no source and no derivation
on the page. It is plausible against the page's own table but is presented as a fact. Drop-in fix: prefix
"On the ranges above, a failed claim typically wastes £1,500 to £2,500...".

[ADVISORY] lease-extension-solicitor-what-they-do: "Freeholder's valuation (s.60) | £600-£899" — the £899
is a verbatim carry-over of a competitor's odd price point and reads as false precision next to the
page's other round ranges. Drop-in fix: "£600-£900", which also harmonises with the cost page.

### leasehold-reform-act-2024-what-is-in-force

[ADVISORY] leasehold-reform-act-2024-what-is-in-force: "a statutory extension premium would typically
land somewhere in the £14,000 to £20,000 range ... marriage value, which at 78 years might account for
roughly £4,000 to £7,000 of that total" — the top of the premium range is not reachable from the three
components the sentence names. Re-derived for a £320,000 flat at 78 years, £150 pa, 5% deferment:
reversion = £320,000/1.05^78 = £7,118; term ≈ £2,470; freeholder's diminution ≈ £9,570. Adding the
page's own marriage-value slice of £4,000-£7,000 gives **£13,600 to £16,600**. A £20,000 premium would
require a marriage-value slice above £10,000, outside the range the same sentence states. The
marriage-value slice itself is well derived and re-computes correctly (relativity 92-94% gives
£4,000-£7,200). Drop-in fix: "£14,000 to £17,000".

[ADVISORY] leasehold-reform-act-2024-what-is-in-force: "<td>Prescribed deferment and capitalisation
rates</td><td>ss.33-37 valuation package</td>" — loose attribution. s.33 is "Longer lease extensions" and
s.34 is the LRA 1967 peppercorn provision; neither prescribes a valuation rate. The rate-prescribing
machinery is s.37 ("Enfranchisement or extension: new method for calculating price payable") together
with Sch 4 ("Determining and sharing the market value"). The row also double-counts s.33, which already
has its own row. On a page whose entire authority claim is per-row section citation, this is the one row
a hostile reader could check and win. Drop-in fix: "s.37 + Sch 4 (valuation package)".

[ADVISORY] leasehold-reform-act-2024-what-is-in-force: "<td>Building-safety amendments</td><td>ss.114-116,
s.120</td><td>IN FORCE</td>" — s.120 was only part-commenced. SI 2024/1018 reg 2 commences s.120
"(interpretation of references to other Acts) **in relation to the definition of the Building Safety Act
2022**". The page makes a virtue of part-commencement elsewhere (the s.64 / s.20J footnote), so the
omission is inconsistent with its own standard. Drop-in fix: "ss.114-116; s.120 in part (BSA 2022
definition only)".
