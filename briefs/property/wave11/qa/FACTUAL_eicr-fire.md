## FINDINGS

[BLOCKER] eicr-certificate-cost-landlords: "Northern Ireland has taken powers for an equivalent scheme under the Private Tenancies Act (Northern Ireland) 2022, with the detail being phased in, so NI landlords should check current official guidance before assuming no duty applies." — stale by ~9 months. The Electrical Safety Standards for Private Tenancies Regulations (Northern Ireland) 2024 (SR 2024/201) were made under s.10 of that Act and are fully in force: new tenancies from 1 April 2025, all pre-existing tenancies by 1 December 2025. The duty is live, not "phasing in", and telling an NI landlord to check "before assuming no duty applies" invites exactly the wrong inference. Drop-in fix: "Northern Ireland now has its own regime: the Electrical Safety Standards for Private Tenancies Regulations (Northern Ireland) 2024, made under section 10 of the Private Tenancies Act (Northern Ireland) 2022, require a five-yearly inspection by a qualified person, with new tenancies caught from 1 April 2025 and all existing tenancies from 1 December 2025." (Same fix needed in the FAQ "Do the EICR rules apply in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland?", which repeats the stale line.)

[BLOCKER] landlord-electrical-safety-certificate: "the remedial and investigative work must also be complete by 31 March unless the report set a shorter period" — wrong by one day. reg 3(4) SI 2020/312, verified verbatim: work must be carried out within "(a) 28 days; or (b) the period specified in the report if less than 28 days, **starting with the date of the inspection and testing**". Inspection 3 March is day 1, so the 28-day window ends 30 March, not 31 March. (The 31 March date IS correct for the existing-tenant copy under reg 3(3)(b), which reads "within 28 days **of** the inspection and test" — the two clocks are worded differently and the page has merged them.) Drop-in fix: "the family, as existing tenants, must have their copy of the report by 31 March, and the remedial and investigative work must be complete by Monday 30 March, because reg 3(4) counts its 28 days starting with the inspection date itself."

[BLOCKER] landlord-electrical-safety-certificate: "Marcus then has until 21 April (28 days from 24 March, when the work including its confirmation was completed) to give the confirmation and report to the family and to the council." — the clock does not run from the confirmation date. reg 3(5)(b) SI 2020/312, verified verbatim: supply "within 28 days of completion of the further investigative or remedial work". The work completed on 20 March, so the deadline is 17 April. The page gives Marcus four days of deadline he does not have, and expressly justifies it with a reading of reg 3 the text does not carry. Drop-in fix: "His electrician completes the work on 20 March and issues written confirmation on 24 March. The supply clock runs from completion of the work, not from the confirmation, so Marcus has until 17 April to get the confirmation and the report to the family and the council: a reminder to chase the confirmation early, because a slow electrician eats your window."

[ADVISORY] landlord-electrical-safety-certificate: "most company lets where an individual occupies" (body) and "most company lets to occupiers" (FAQ "Do all landlords need an EICR?") — asserted as settled scope, but it is not. A "specified tenancy" under reg 2 requires the tenancy to grant "one or more persons" the right to occupy as an only or main residence; a corporate tenant does not occupy at all, and gov.uk guidance covers "tenancies and licences to occupy" without addressing company lets either way. Drop-in fix: "and, on the widely taken view, lets to a company where an individual actually occupies, though the Regulations do not address company lets expressly and the safe course is to comply."

[ADVISORY] landlord-electrical-safety-certificate: "an unsatisfactory report effectively suspends the clock until remedial work is confirmed" (FAQ "How often does a landlord need an electrical inspection?") — there is no suspension mechanism anywhere in reg 3. The next-inspection date on the report stands; what an unsatisfactory report starts is the separate reg 3(4) remedial clock. Drop-in fix: "and an unsatisfactory report starts a separate 28-day remedial clock alongside the five-year cycle rather than pausing it."

[ADVISORY] landlord-electrical-safety-certificate: "There is no separate document called an electrical safety certificate anywhere in the Electrical Safety Standards in the Private Rented Sector (England) Regulations 2020" (FAQ 1) — uses the superseded title while the body correctly explains the SI was renamed by SI 2025/1043 to "...Private Rented Sector and Social Rented Sector (England) Regulations 2020". Verified against the current legislation.gov.uk contents page. Drop-in fix: use "the Electrical Safety Standards in the Private Rented Sector and Social Rented Sector (England) Regulations 2020" in the FAQ too.

[ADVISORY] landlord-electrical-safety-certificate: "for a brand-new installation it [an EIC] can stand in place of a first EICR until the next inspection falls due" — nothing in SI 2020/312 says this; it comes from gov.uk landlord guidance. Unsourced statutory-sounding claim. Drop-in fix: attribute it ("government guidance accepts that where a new installation has an EIC, the next-inspection date on that certificate governs until the first EICR falls due").

[ADVISORY] landlord-electrical-safety-certificate: "<a href=...guidance-for-landlords-tenants-and-local-authorities>government guidance</a> points landlords to competent-person schemes" — the linked gov.uk landing page (title now "Electrical safety standards in the private and social rented sectors: guidance", last updated 1 November 2025) does not itself say this; the attached landlord guide does. [ADVISORY] fire-risk-assessment-cost: "so a £40,000 article 32 fine is paid out of income that has already been taxed" — an unlabelled precise figure with no source, and it is the exact statutory cap of two different regimes (SI 2020/312 reg 11 and Housing Act 2004 s.249A), which invites the reader to think article 32 has a £40,000 cap. ## VERIFIED CLEAN (checked and found correct, listed so the writer does not re-litigate them)

- SI 2020/312 reg 3: 5-year maximum interval; 28 days to existing tenants; before occupation for new tenants; 28 days to prospective tenants on written request; 7 days to the local housing authority on written request; retention until the next inspection and supply to the next inspector (reg 3(3)(ca), 3(3)(d)). All four supply deadlines in the obligations page's table match the text exactly.
- SI 2020/312 reg 11: "£40,000", "beyond reasonable doubt", "(or more than one penalty in the event of a continuing failure)", per breach, breaches of reg 3, 3B, 3C or 3D. Both pages state this correctly.
- SI 2025/1043: full title, reg 10(b) substitutes £40,000 for £30,000 with effect 1 November 2025 (reg 7 and pre-1-December-2025 registered-provider tenancies to 1 May 2026); reg 3(1) renames the SI; reg 12 removes the social-housing exclusion and adds the moveable structures/vehicles/vessels class. Every claim on both pages about the amendment is supported.
- SI 2020/312 Schedule 1 as in force: exactly 8 classes, paragraph 1 (registered providers) omitted 1 November 2025, paragraph 9 (moveable structures) added the same day. The obligations page's list of eight is correct and in the right order.
- SI 2020/312 reg 2: "qualified person" IS defined there ("a person competent to undertake the inspection and testing required under regulation 3(1) and any further investigative or remedial work in accordance with the electrical safety standards"), and "electrical safety standards" means BS 7671:2018, the 18th edition. The obligations page's reg 2 attribution is correct.
- SI 2020/312 regulation map on the obligations page is correct in every particular: reg 4 duty (not power) to serve a remedial notice, reg 5 landlord compliance within 28 days, reg 6 authority arranges remedial action, reg 7 appeals on remedial action, reg 8 recovery of costs, reg 9 appeals on costs, reg 10 urgent remedial action, reg 12 procedure and First-tier Tribunal appeal.
- SI 2020/312 reg 13 inserts into HA 2004 Schedule 4 the condition that every electrical installation be safe and that the licence holder "supply the authority, on demand, with a declaration by him as to the safety of such installations" - supports the HMO-licence claims on both EICR pages.
- HA 2004 s.249A £40,000 per offence from 1 May 2026 (Financial Penalties (Housing Offences and Breach of Banning Orders) Regulations 2026, SI 2026/319) - the obligations page's date is right, and the two-regime paragraph on both pages matches §26.10.
- Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005: art 3 "Meaning of 'responsible person'", art 9 "Risk assessment", art 26 "Enforcement of Order", art 32 "Offences". Contents page 200.
- Fire Safety Act 2021 s.1: amends RRFSO art 6 to cover structure, external walls (including doors, windows and anything attached such as balconies) and all doors between domestic premises and common parts; England commencement 16 May 2022 (SI 2022/544). Fire page's scope sentence supported.
- BSA 2022 s.156: in force 1 October 2023; removes "significant" from art 9(7)(a) so the full assessment must be recorded; inserts art 21A (resident information) and art 22A (handover to successor). Fire page's DIY/recording section supported, including the "five or more employees is dead law" line.
- SI 2022/547 reg 10, verified verbatim: information duty applies to any building with 2+ sets of domestic premises and common parts used for evacuation; the checking duties apply to buildings above 11 metres, at 12-monthly intervals for flat entrance doors and 3-monthly for common-parts fire doors. Fire page body and FAQ both match. reg 3 is "Meaning of high-rise residential building"; the 18m/7-storey threshold matches §26.12.
- SI 2015/1693 reg 4 as amended from 1 October 2022: smoke alarm on each storey with a room used wholly or partly as living accommodation; CO alarm in any such room containing a fixed combustion appliance other than a gas cooker; reg 4(1)(b) day-one working-order check still in force. Fire page's single-let paragraph is correct, and the §26.12 SI 2022/707 verify-at-write item is discharged.
- ITTOIA 2005 s.272: applies the trading rules to property business profits including s.34 (wholly and exclusively) and s.57 (pre-trading expenses, 7 years). Supports the deduction and pre-letting claims on all three pages.
- TCGA 1992 s.38(1)(b), verified verbatim: enhancement expenditure reflected in the state or nature of the asset at disposal. Supports the capital-rewire treatment on both EICR pages.
- BIM38515 ("Wholly and exclusively: fines, penalties and damages", CIR v EC Warnes & Co Ltd 12 TC 227) and BIM38500 (contents) both support the non-deductibility statements.

The strongest sourced claim across the three pages.
