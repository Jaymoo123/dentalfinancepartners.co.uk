## Findings

[BLOCKER] mees-regulations-landlords: "Letting without a required EPC to avoid a bad rating swaps one breach for another and blocks a Section 21 notice into the bargain." — Section 21 no longer exists. RRA 2025 s.2 (abolition of assured shorthold tenancies, the s.21 abolition mechanism) came into force 1 May 2026 via SI 2026/421 reg.2; house_positions §20 in-force table records it as in force and §20 do-not-write bans "Section 21 is still available". The page is dated 2026-08-15 and states repealed law as a live consequence. — Fix: delete the clause. "Letting without a required EPC to avoid a bad rating swaps one breach for another: the property is still being marketed and let unlawfully under regulation 7 of SI 2012/3118."

[BLOCKER] mees-regulations-landlords: "first-time loft or wall insulation, double glazing replacing single glazing, a heating system upgrade, typically improve the property beyond its previous state, which makes them capital under the tests in HMRC's Property Income Manual at PIM2030" (and the identical claim in FAQ "Is MEES improvement spend tax-deductible?") — the cited manual says the opposite for the one example named. PIM2030 verbatim: "alterations due to advancements in technology are generally treated as an allowable repair rather than an improvement, if the functionality and character of the asset is broadly the same. For example, when single glazing is replaced with double glazing." Citing PIM2030 as authority for double glazing being capital is a direct contradiction of the source. (Note: house_positions §26.7 carries the same error and needs a conductor patch; the lock is not a source.) — Fix: "first-time loft or wall insulation and a heating system upgrade of a higher specification typically improve the property beyond its previous state, which makes them capital under the tests in HMRC's Property Income Manual at PIM2030. Glazing is the exception HMRC calls out there: replacing single glazing with double glazing is normally an allowable repair, because the functionality and character of the asset is broadly the same." Mirror the change in the FAQ.

[BLOCKER] epc-certificate-cost-uk: "Works that improve the property beyond its previous specification, insulation where there was none, double glazing replacing single, a new heating system of a higher standard, are capital" — same error, uncited here but contradicted by PIM2030 (quoted above) and by the corrected sibling. Two Wave 11 pages must not ship the same wrong proposition. — Fix: drop "double glazing replacing single" from the capital list and add it to the repair sentence: "Like-for-like replacement, a modern condensing boiler replacing a failed one on a straight swap, or single glazing replaced with double glazing (HMRC treats the technological upgrade as a repair at PIM2030), is deductible against rental income."

[BLOCKER] epc-certificate-cost-uk: "TCGA 1992 s.38(1)(c) allows the incidental costs of making the disposal, and s.38(2) defines those costs to include fees for the professional services of any surveyor or valuer and the costs of advertising to find a buyer. A statutorily required certificate produced by an accredited assessor as a precondition of marketing sits within that frame" — the cited provision does not support the sentence as written. CG15250 opens: "TCGA92/S38 defines the incidental costs of acquisition and disposal. The definition is exhaustive." s.38(2) verbatim lists "fees, commission or remuneration paid for the professional services of any surveyor or valuer, or auctioneer, or accountant, or agent or legal adviser and costs of transfer or conveyance", plus "costs of advertising to find a buyer". A domestic energy assessor is none of the listed professionals, so "defines those costs to include" (which reads as a non-exhaustive list) is doing the work the argument needs. The FAQ compounds it with a flat "Yes ... sits comfortably within that frame". — Fix, body: "TCGA 1992 s.38(1)(c) allows the incidental costs of making the disposal, and s.38(2) sets an exhaustive list of what those are: fees for the professional services of a surveyor, valuer, auctioneer, accountant, agent or legal adviser, transfer costs, and the costs of advertising to find a buyer. An EPC is a statutory precondition of advertising a property for sale, which is where the fee normally belongs in the computation; HMRC's guidance on the exhaustive definition is at CG15250." Fix, FAQ: open with "Usually yes, as part of the costs of advertising to find a buyer" rather than "Yes".

[ADVISORY] mees-regulations-landlords: "with power under regulation 37 to serve a compliance notice ... and under regulation 38 to serve a penalty notice" — bare "regulation 38". house_positions §26.13 do-not-write is explicit: "never a bare 'reg 38' without naming its SI (two-reg-38 trap)". The risk is live on this page specifically because it cross-links epc-certificate-cost-uk, which discusses SI 2012/3118 reg 38 (£200 EPC penalty amounts) three sentences from an internal link back here. [ADVISORY] mees-regulations-landlords: "per <a href=".../regulation/36">regulation 36</a> the exemption runs from registration" — reg 36 does not say this. Fetched in full: reg 36(1) requires the Secretary of State to establish and maintain the register; reg 36(2) requires the landlord to register the Schedule information in order to rely on an exemption. The start date and five-year life sit in the exemption regulations themselves: reg 25(2) verbatim, "this regulation applies for a period of five years starting with the date on which the landlord registers information in accordance with regulation 36(2)" (regs 31(2), 32(3), 33 track the same pattern). — Fix: "per regulation 25(2) an exemption runs for five years starting with the date the landlord registers the information under regulation 36(2), and the enforcement authority checks the evidence later if it chooses to."

[ADVISORY] mees-regulations-landlords: "a £10,000 cost cap has been consulted on alongside it" and "The government has committed to look at a long-term trajectory ... with the stated aim of as many as possible reaching EPC band C or equivalent by 2030" — the hedge is safe but the framing is stale by seven months. The government response to "Improving the energy performance of privately rented homes (2025 update)" was published 21 January 2026: it settled on a £10,000 cost cap with 10-year exemption validity, a 1 October 2030 compliance date, and a dual-metric standard, and states the government "will seek to lay a statutory instrument with the aim of it coming into force in 2027". £10,000 is therefore the decided policy figure, not merely a consulted-on one (the consultation itself floated £15,000 with a £10,000 affordability exemption). The page's core claim survives verification: I checked legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/962 and the last amending instrument is SI 2026/325 (RRA consequentials, 1.5.2026), with "no known outstanding effects" — no EPC C amending SI has been made. — Fix: "the government's January 2026 consultation response settled on a £10,000 cost cap and a 1 October 2030 compliance date, and says it will seek to lay a statutory instrument with the aim of it coming into force in 2027. None of that is enacted: no amending Statutory Instrument has been made ..." Same staleness sits in the epc-c-2030 sibling and in house_positions §26.3/§26.8 (both verified 2026-05-24) - conductor call, not edited here.

[ADVISORY] mees-regulations-landlords: exemption table, "Property devaluation (reg 32) ... [ADVISORY] mees-regulations-landlords: exemption table, "Third party consent (reg 31) | 5 years, or until the current tenancy ends or is assigned where the refusal came from the tenant" — reg 31(3) verbatim ends the tenant-refusal variant "once that tenant's tenancy has come to an end"; it says nothing about assignment. [ADVISORY] epc-certificate-cost-uk: "estate agents arranging the certificate as part of a sale or letting package commonly charge £75 to £150" (opening paragraph) vs the table's agent-arranged column, which runs to "£100 to £200+" for a detached house, and the headline "between £35 and £120+".
