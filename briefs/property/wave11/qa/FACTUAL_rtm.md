## FINDINGS

### BLOCKERS

[BLOCKER] right-to-manage-process-steps: "not later than the end of the two months beginning with the day the counter-notice was given (s.84(3))" — the two-month tribunal-application deadline is in **s.84(4)**, not s.84(3). s.84(3) is only the power to apply ("Where the counter-notice... the RTM company may apply to the appropriate tribunal for a determination"); s.84(4) is the clock ("An application... must be made not later than the end of the period of two months beginning with the day on which the counter-notice... was given"). Verified verbatim at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2002/15/section/84. Right figure, wrong subsection — the exact trap the QA spec names — and it appears **three times**: (a) timeline table row 5 "CLRA 2002 s.84(3)"; (b) body, "the most dangerous clock" paragraph; (c) the contested worked example, "By 1 December". This is the page whose selling point is that every deadline carries its section number. Drop-in fix: replace all three occurrences of `s.84(3)` with `s.84(4)`. (FAQ 5's bare "(s.84)" is fine as is.)

[BLOCKER] right-to-manage-explained: "company formation is under £100" (FAQ 10) and cost table row "Forming the RTM company | Under £100 | Companies House fee plus any formation agent" — the Companies House digital incorporation fee is **exactly £100** (paper £124), effective 2 July 2026, verified at https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/companies-house-fees/companies-house-fees. "Under £100" is therefore wrong on its face, and wrong again once the table's own "plus any formation agent" is added. It also contradicts the sibling page, which states £100 four times (`right-to-manage-company-setup`: summary, intro, step 6, closing). Cross-page divergence on the one hard £ figure the trio shares. Drop-in fix: table cell → `£100 (plus any formation agent's fee)`; FAQ 10 → `company formation is £100 at Companies House`. The worked example arithmetic survives unchanged: £100 + £2,500 = £2,600 / 4 = £650 exactly.

[BLOCKER] right-to-manage-explained: "their share of the running costs of the claim and their ongoing service charges are revenue expenses against rental income" (costs section) and, repeated, "For Priya, her £650 share and her service charges sit against her rental income as revenue expenses" (worked example) — the service-charge half is right; the **claim-costs half is asserted flat with no source and is at best arguable**. PIM2120 (https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/property-income-manual/pim2120) allows routine professional costs but disallows "legal costs incurred in acquiring, or adding to, a property" and one-off fees producing an enduring benefit; a one-off legal spend to acquire a permanent statutory right over the block's management is squarely in that contested territory, and no HMRC guidance treats RTM claim costs as revenue. The brief said "**RTM running costs** and service charges = revenue" (i.e. the company's ongoing recharges); the page has drifted that into "running costs **of the claim**". An accountancy site telling BTL leaseholders to deduct one-off claim fees is a real exposure. Drop-in fix (both places): "their ongoing service charges are revenue expenses against rental income, while the one-off legal costs of the claim itself are not straightforwardly revenue: they buy a lasting right rather than service the letting, so treat them as a capital-risk item and take advice before deducting." Worked example: "For Priya, her service charges sit against her rental income as revenue expenses; her £650 share of the claim costs is the item to take advice on."

[BLOCKER] right-to-manage-company-setup: "The right to a summary of relevant costs and to inspect the underlying invoices under sections 21 and 22 of the Landlord and Tenant Act 1985 is exercisable against the RTM company once it manages the block" — **s.21 does not say this in its in-force form.** LTA 1985 s.21 was substituted (heading now "Service charge information") and the in-force text is a *regulation-making power*; legislation.gov.uk's own editorial note reads "The power to make such regulations has not to date been exercised" (verified at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1985/70/section/21 and the 2026-08-19 point-in-time version). There is no operative statutory right to a summary of relevant costs under the current s.21, and s.22 — which *is* in force, heading "Request to inspect supporting accounts &c." — is expressly predicated on the tenant having "obtained such a summary as is referred to in section 21(1)". Citing an uncommenced/unexercised provision as a live obligation is precisely the §31.3a in-force failure mode, here on LTA 1985 rather than LFRA. (Note §31.D labels s.21 neutrally as "service charge information"; the page went beyond the lock.) Drop-in fix: "**Leaseholders keep their information rights.** Leaseholders can require inspection of the accounts and receipts behind the service charge under section 22 of the Landlord and Tenant Act 1985, and can challenge reasonableness at the First-tier Tribunal under section 27A. Those rights run against the RTM company once it manages the block. The block that spent years demanding the freeholder's receipts is now the party that has to produce them..."

### ADVISORIES

[ADVISORY] right-to-manage-explained: "an uncontested claim commonly runs its course within a few months of the first notice" — the sibling process page derives a **statutory floor of about four and a half months** (14 days s.79(2) + one month s.80(6) + three months s.80(7)) and gives 4-6 months for real uncontested claims. "A few months" reads as shorter than the floor and is the trio's only duration divergence. Drop-in fix: "an uncontested claim commonly takes around four to six months from the first notice, against a statutory floor of about four and a half".

[ADVISORY] right-to-manage-explained: "Schedule 6 then excludes three categories" — Schedule 6 has **four** exclusions. Para 4 ("This Chapter does not apply to premises... if a local housing authority is the immediate landlord of any of the qualifying tenants") is missing. The FAQ hedges with "three main categories"; the body does not. Verified at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2002/15/schedule/6. Drop-in fix: change to "excludes four categories" and add a fourth bullet: "**Local authority landlords.** Premises where a local housing authority is the immediate landlord of any qualifying tenant."

[ADVISORY] right-to-manage-explained: "Converted buildings (not purpose-built blocks) with no more than four flats where the freeholder or an adult family member lives in one of them as their only or principal home" — Sch 6 para 3 says "do not contain more than four **units**", not flats (a unit is a flat or other separate set of premises, so a block of four flats plus a shop is five units and outside the exclusion), and the para also requires the resident landlord to have occupied for at least the preceding twelve months. Drop-in fix: "...with no more than four **units** (a unit being a flat or any other separate set of premises)... lives in one of them as their only or principal home, and has done so for at least the last twelve months."

[ADVISORY] right-to-manage-explained: table row "The freeholder's reversion and any right to be a member of the RTM company after acquisition" sits in the column headed "Stays exactly as it was" — the freeholder's s.74 membership right does not stay as it was; it is a **new** right arising on the acquisition date (s.74(1)(b), verified). Drop-in fix: "The freeholder's reversion (and, from the acquisition date, a new statutory right to join the RTM company under s.74)".

[ADVISORY] right-to-manage-explained: "commonly runs to five figures per flat plus both sides' professional costs" (FAQ 9, enfranchisement premium) — unlabelled market claim in the FAQ. The same figure is properly labelled in the body table ("Cost order of magnitude (illustrative)"). Drop-in fix: prefix with "as an order of magnitude,".

[ADVISORY] right-to-manage-explained: the opening link text "Part 2 Chapter 1 of the Commonhold and Leasehold Reform Act 2002" points at `/ukpga/2002/15/section/72` (premises to which the Chapter applies), not the right-conferring provision. Both resolve 200; the target just does not match the anchor. Drop-in fix: point it at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2002/15/section/71.

[ADVISORY] right-to-manage-explained: FAQ 8, "the First-tier Tribunal, which since 3 March 2025 is the first-instance forum in all cases" — s.52 LFRA 2024 (verified) bars High Court first-instance applications only "where jurisdiction in respect of a matter is conferred on the appropriate tribunal under Chapter 1 of Part 2 of the CLRA 2002", and expressly preserves High Court challenges to tribunal decisions. Drop-in fix: "in all cases where the Chapter gives the tribunal jurisdiction".

[ADVISORY] right-to-manage-company-setup: worked example "£28,000 demanded, £24,500 spent" — precise unlabelled figures. The neighbouring interest figure is tagged "(illustrative)"; these two are not. Drop-in fix: add "All figures in this example are illustrative." to the example's opening sentence.

[ADVISORY] right-to-manage-company-setup: "Older guides quoting £12 or £13 incorporation fees are out of date; fees rose substantially in May 2024" / "Guides still quoting £12 or £13 predate the May 2024 fee rises by some distance" — the page's own £100 figure does **not** date from May 2024. May 2024 took incorporation £12 → £50; the current £100 / £124 / £50 / £110 schedule is effective **2 July 2026** (verified on the gov.uk fees page). As written the page under-dates its own freshness signal. Drop-in fix: "...are out of date by two rounds of fee rises: May 2024 and again on 2 July 2026, when digital incorporation reached £100."

[ADVISORY] right-to-manage-company-setup: "should tell HMRC it has become active within three months" — correct in substance (Finance Act 2004 s.55(2)(e), verified: notice "must be given not later than three months after the beginning of the accounting period"), but the only source the paragraph offers is the gov.uk dormant-company page, which carries no three-month rule, and neither does gov.uk's restart-a-dormant-company page. Drop-in fix: cite it — "within three months of the start of the accounting period in which it happens (Finance Act 2004 s.55)".

[ADVISORY] right-to-manage-process-steps: "or both tenants where there are only two (s.79(5))" — the two-flat rule is **s.79(4)**; s.79(5) is the half-the-flats rule ("In any other case..."). Verified at /ukpga/2002/15/section/79. Drop-in fix: "(s.79(5), or s.79(4) where there are only two flats)".

[ADVISORY] right-to-manage-process-steps: "The single escape hatch: no deemed withdrawal occurs if the person who gave the counter-notice agrees in writing" — s.87(3) disapplies **only s.87(1)** (the no-application and withdrawn-application limbs). It does not rescue the s.87(4) insolvency and strike-off limb, which is the third bullet immediately above it. Same overbreadth in FAQ 6 ("The same happens if... the company hits an insolvency event... The only escape is..."). Drop-in fix: "The single escape hatch, and it covers only the first two bullets: no deemed withdrawal occurs under s.87(1) if the person who gave the counter-notice agrees in writing... A strike-off or insolvency event under s.87(4) cannot be cured this way."

[ADVISORY] right-to-manage-process-steps: worked example "specifying 2 October as the counter-notice date... and 5 January as the intended acquisition date (three months later, s.80(7))" — 2 October to 5 January is three months and three days. Valid ("at least three months") but mislabelled, and the page elsewhere insists the deadlines are exact. Drop-in fix: "and 5 January as the intended acquisition date (the three-month minimum falls on 2 January; the notice allows a short margin, s.80(7))".

[ADVISORY] right-to-manage-process-steps: "Section 87A, the general rule... and the section also blocks the landlord from recycling claim costs into the service charge" — s.87A itself does not impose that block; it signposts it ("See also sections 20CA and 20J of the Landlord and Tenant Act 1985, which prevent costs in connection with a claim under this Chapter being recovered by way of a variable service charge"). s.20J was inserted by LFRA 2024 s.64, commenced 3 Mar 2025 only "insofar as it inserts section 20J... as regards the right to manage" (SI 2025/131 reg 2(e), verified verbatim). Substance right, attribution loose. Drop-in fix: "...and, through sections 20CA and 20J of the Landlord and Tenant Act 1985 that it signposts, the landlord cannot recycle claim costs into the service charge either."

[ADVISORY] right-to-manage-process-steps: "As sourced estimates rather than statutory figures: a well-run uncontested claim typically completes in around four to six months" — labelled as sourced but no source is named on-page. Drop-in fix: attribute or relabel as "practitioner estimates".
