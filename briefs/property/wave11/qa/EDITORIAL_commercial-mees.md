## 2. ORIGINAL VALUE (2-3 nameable things a generic SERP page would not have)

No page is a BLOCKER on this test. Each is nameable:

- **commercial-epc-requirements**: (a) the reg 38 non-dwelling formula worked at three rateable values including the £750 no-rating-list default, where the SERP publishes a flat "£500 to £5,000"; (b) the correction that the listed-building exemption is conditional, not automatic, with the "unacceptably alter character" test quoted; (c) the two-reg-38s warning (SI 2012/3118 vs SI 2015/962) and the observation that the air-conditioning inspection is the only standing, non-transaction duty in the instrument.
- **commercial-energy-performance-certificate-cost**: (a) an aggregated four-source national benchmark table with assessor level and method as *driver* columns, plus the honest negative finding that no scheme publishes per-level pricing; (b) the two-landlord VAT comparison (opted vs exempt) that no assessor price list carries; (c) the deduction routing across individual / investment company / owner-occupier trader, including the sale-costs boundary.
- **commercial-property-mees-compliance**: (a) the reg 41 two-limb formula worked at £40,000 and £600,000 rateable values showing the "greater of" floor biting; (b) the comply-vs-exempt decision in cash with FA 2026 allowances (£60,000 gross to £45,000 net); (c) FRI-lease cost-bearing, service-charge exclusion of improvements and the s.198 exit position.
- **mees-regulations-landlords**: (a) the exemption table with evidence *and* duration per class, including the 6-month new-landlord class the SERP routinely lists as 5 years; (b) the reg 40 ladder with the correction that £5,000 is an aggregate cap, worked through a stacking example; (c) the tax layer: grants reduce base cost, the cap is not a tax category, penalties non-deductible.

## 3. FINDINGS

### BLOCKERS

`[BLOCKER] commercial-epc-requirements: "Regulation 7 of SI 2012/3118 requires that where a building is to be sold or rented out and no valid EPC exists, the relevant person must secure that one is commissioned before the building is put on the market. An agent acting for you must check that this has happened. You must then use all reasonable efforts to obtain the certificate within 7 days of marketing starting, with a further 21 days allowed if, despite those efforts, it has not arrived."` — this FAQ answer is a near-verbatim copy of the body paragraph at `<a href=".../regulation/7">Regulation 7</a> adds the marketing trigger...` (42 of 108 eight-word shingles match the body verbatim). The reg 5 exemption FAQ is worse: 44 of 128 shingles match the body bullet list, reproducing the seven categories in the same order and near-identical wording. Two FAQs restating the body word-for-word is padding, and duplicate answer/body text is exactly what a helpful-content assessment penalises. **Fix:** rewrite both FAQs to answer the *question* rather than restate the section — for the marketing FAQ, lead with the practical sequence ("Commission it before the listing goes live, not before completion; the 7-day and 21-day windows are grace for the assessor, not for you") and let the body carry the statutory recitation; for the exemptions FAQ, give the three categories a commercial owner actually hits (under 50m² stand-alone, low-energy industrial/workshop, conditional listed) and link down to the full list.

`[BLOCKER] commercial-energy-performance-certificate-cost: "The table below is a national benchmark assembled from four published assessor price lists and market guides, checked in August 2026."` — the page's central asset never states whether its bands are VAT-inclusive or exclusive, while the same page criticises the market for precisely that omission: `"Assessor fees carry VAT at the standard rate, and almost no price list says whether its figures include it."` A reader budgeting £350 for a 100-250m² office may be looking at £420. The page holds itself to a lower standard than the sources it benchmarks. **Fix:** add to the sentence introducing the table: "All ranges are quoted excluding VAT, which is how assessors publish them; see the VAT section below for who actually bears that 20%." Add "(ex VAT)" to the "Typical range (checked Aug 2026)" column header.

`[BLOCKER] commercial-energy-performance-certificate-cost: "paying for a certificate the building already has is the most avoidable cost on this page."` (FAQ) versus `"commissioning a certificate the building already holds is the most avoidable spend on this page."` (body, "Check the register first"). Same claim, same superlative, same closing rhythm, twice — and preceded in both places by the same twin: `"The certificate attaches to the building, not the owner"` / `"The EPC attaches to the building, not to you."` Self-plagiarism inside a single page reads as generated filler. **Fix:** delete the superlative from the FAQ and end it on the mechanic instead: "The register is free to search and the check takes minutes, so make it the first step of any instruction." Keep the "most avoidable spend" line once, in the body.

`[BLOCKER] commercial-property-mees-compliance: "Lighting, heating, air conditioning and other integral features attract the £1m annual investment allowance or, for companies, 100% full expensing on qualifying main-rate plant, so a large slice of a retrofit can be written off against profits immediately."` — as written this reads as if full expensing covers integral features. It does not: integral features are special-rate expenditure, and s.45S full expensing is main-rate only, as the page's own body bullet correctly says (`"Full expensing: 100% first-year allowance for companies only (CAA 2001 s.45S) on new and unused main-rate plant"`). FAQ answers are the text most likely to be extracted into a rich result, so the loose version is the one that travels. Flagging for Track 1 confirmation. **Fix:** "Lighting, heating, air conditioning and other integral features are special-rate expenditure, so the £1m annual investment allowance is the route to a 100% year-one deduction on them; full expensing (companies only) applies to new and unused main-rate plant elsewhere in the same works."

`[BLOCKER] commercial-property-mees-compliance: "Exemptions do not transfer to a new owner: a purchaser must register afresh."` (FAQ) and `"exemptions are personal to the registering landlord and do not pass to a purchaser, who must register afresh"` (body) — asserted twice as settled law. The domestic sibling, working from the same SI 2015/962 reg 36 machinery, hedges it correctly: `"Do not assume so. An exemption is registered by, and applies from the registration of, the landlord who registered it..."` The two pages therefore contradict each other on the same provision, and the unhedged version is the one without a cited source. Cross-refer Track 1. **Fix:** adopt the domestic page's formulation on the commercial page too: "Do not assume an exemption travels with the building. It is registered by, and runs from the registration of, the landlord who registered it, and the reg 33 temporary exemption gives a new owner their own six-month window — which is what the scheme plainly expects a purchaser to use."

`[BLOCKER] commercial-epc-requirements: "talk them through with your accountant or a property tax specialist before the spend is committed rather than after; the tax outcome of energy-efficiency work is largely fixed by decisions made at specification stage."` and `[BLOCKER] mees-regulations-landlords: "put the numbers in front of a property tax specialist before the works start, because the invoicing and evidence decisions made at that stage are the ones that fix the tax treatment for good."` — the same close, twice, down to the argument ("go before the spend, because decisions at that stage fix the treatment") and the cadence. The writer spec is explicit: "CTA close: topic-fitted, varied, no repeated stock phrase across pages." **Fix:** differentiate by topic. On the requirements page close on the transaction clock instead: "The register search costs nothing and the assessment takes days; the only expensive version of this is the one commissioned after the board goes up." On the domestic MEES page close on the evidence file: "Whatever route you take, the folder of quotes, reports and invoices is doing three jobs at once, and only one of them is MEES."
