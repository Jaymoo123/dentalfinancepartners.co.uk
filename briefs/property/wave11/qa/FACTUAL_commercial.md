### commercial-energy-performance-certificate-cost

[ADVISORY] commercial-energy-performance-certificate-cost: "SBEM (Simplified Building Energy Model) is the government-approved calculation methodology used for most non-domestic EPCs in England and Wales" (FAQ 3) — technically wrong on the SBEM/DSM relationship the dispatch flags. SBEM is BRE's calculation tool implementing the government's National Calculation Methodology; dynamic simulation models implement the same NCM by a different route. Calling SBEM "the government-approved methodology" implies DSM is a departure from the approved method, when both are approved. ### commercial-property-mees-compliance

[BLOCKER] commercial-property-mees-compliance: "The over-three-months limb applies: 20% of £40,000 is £8,000, so the 'greater of' floor lifts the penalty to **£10,000**. Now scale the same facts to a £600,000 rateable value office block: 20% is £120,000, within the £150,000 cap, so the penalty is **£120,000**." (plus FAQ 6 "a breach of less than three months attracts a penalty of the greater of £5,000 or 10% of the property's rateable value, capped at £50,000"; FAQ 7 "at a £40,000 rateable value the minimum £10,000 floor applies"; and the penalty table's "Financial penalty" column) — MISSTATES THE LEGAL EFFECT OF REG 41. Verified verbatim at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/962/regulation/41 and /41/made: the authority may impose "a financial penalty **not exceeding** whichever is the greater of—(i)£5,000, and (ii)10% of the rateable value of the property, provided that the financial penalty must not exceed £50,000" (under 3 months) and "**not exceeding** whichever is the greater of—(i)£10,000, and (ii)20% of the rateable value ... must not exceed £150,000" (3 months or more). The "greater of" test sets the CEILING, not a floor. £10,000 at a £40,000 rateable value is the maximum the authority may impose, not the amount it will impose, and calling it a "floor" / "minimum £10,000 floor" inverts the provision twice. The arithmetic is right; the characterisation is wrong, and it inflates stated exposure at the exact point the page tells the reader to base a board decision on it. It also contradicts the domestic sibling `mees-regulations-landlords.md`, which states its reg 40 equivalents correctly ("regulation 40 sets maximum financial penalties", "up to £5,000 per property"). Drop-in fix: table column header → "Maximum financial penalty"; cells → "Up to the greater of £5,000 or 10% of rateable value" and "Up to the greater of £10,000 or 20% of rateable value". Body → "The over-three-months limb applies: 20% of £40,000 is £8,000, so the 'greater of' test sets the ceiling at £10,000, and that is the most the authority may impose. Now scale the same facts to a £600,000 rateable value office block: 20% is £120,000, inside the £150,000 cap, so the maximum exposure is £120,000." FAQ 6 → "can attract a penalty of up to the greater of £5,000 or 10% ...". FAQ 7 → "Yes, up to that, where the breach has run for three months or more ... at a £40,000 rateable value the ceiling is £10,000 because 20% is only £8,000". Also adjust the following sentence "Same breach, twelve times the bill" → "twelve times the maximum" (the 12x arithmetic itself recomputes OK).

[ADVISORY] commercial-property-mees-compliance: "regulation 28 defines relevance for commercial property through a simple payback calculation: an improvement is relevant only if the value of the energy bill savings it is expected to achieve over the seven years from installation equals or exceeds its cost, calculated using the approved methodology, current energy prices and the cost of purchase and installation" — incomplete on two mechanics verified at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/962/regulation/28. First, the payback limb is a second gate: the improvement must first be a measure listed in the Schedule to the Green Deal (Qualifying Energy Improvements) Order 2012 or Table 6 of Approved Document L2B, AND be recommended for the property in "a green deal report, a recommendation report, or a report prepared by a surveyor". Second, the cost side ("R") is purchase and installation cost EXCLUDING VAT, multiplied by an interest-rate factor tied to the Bank of England base rate, so Joan's raw £28,000 ÷ £3,000 = 9.3 years flatters the payback (the conclusion that it fails is safe, the method shown is not the statutory one). Drop-in fix, add after the definition sentence: "Two mechanics the commentary drops: the improvement has to be a listed measure recommended in a green deal report, recommendation report or surveyor's report before the payback test is even reached, and the cost side of the test is the purchase and installation cost excluding VAT, uplifted by an interest factor tied to the Bank of England base rate, so a raw division of cost by annual saving understates the true payback."

[ADVISORY] commercial-property-mees-compliance: "Lighting, heating, air conditioning and other integral features attract the £1m annual investment allowance or, for companies, 100% full expensing on qualifying main-rate plant" (FAQ 10) — reads as though integral features can go through full expensing. They cannot: integral features are special-rate expenditure (CAA 2001 s.33A / s.104A, confirmed at CA22320, which cites "CAA01/S33A, S33B and S104A"), and s.45S full expensing is main-rate only; the special-rate companion is a 50% first-year allowance. The BODY gets this right ("Any element that is genuinely new, unused main-rate plant could go through 100% full expensing instead"), so the FAQ is the outlier. Drop-in fix: "...attract the £1m annual investment allowance, which gives 100% relief in year one. Full expensing does not reach them: integral features are special-rate expenditure and section 45S full expensing is main-rate only."

[ADVISORY] commercial-property-mees-compliance: "Timing note for unincorporated owners: chargeable periods straddling April 2026 use a hybrid writing-down rate between 18% and 14%" — scoped the wrong way round for a CT-first page. House position §38: the 14% applies to chargeable periods beginning on or after 1 April 2026 (CT) / 6 April 2026 (IT), and FA 2026 s.28(2)-(6) gives a hybrid time-apportioned rate for straddling periods on BOTH sides. The SPV in the page's own worked example is the most likely reader to have a straddling period. Drop-in fix: "Timing note: any chargeable period straddling 1 April 2026 for a company, or 6 April 2026 for an individual or partnership, uses a hybrid, time-apportioned writing-down rate between 18% and 14%, so the year the works are incurred can change the answer at the margin."

[ADVISORY] commercial-property-mees-compliance: "The government's own non-domestic MEES landlord guidance confirms there is no statutory timeline and no enacted provisions for EPC C or EPC B" (and the parallel FAQ 9 line) — over-attributes. Drop-in fix: "The government's own non-domestic MEES landlord guidance still states the minimum as EPC E and carries no EPC C or EPC B date anywhere in it; the EPC B work appears only as a linked consultation."
