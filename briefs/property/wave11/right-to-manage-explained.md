# Right to Manage explained: what it is, who qualifies, and whether it is the right move

## Statutory anchor
- Primary: Commonhold and Leasehold Reform Act 2002 (c. 15) Part 2 Chapter 1: s.71 (the right to manage), s.72 (premises to which Chapter applies), s.75 (qualifying tenants), s.76-77 (long leases). WebFetch-verified at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2002/15/contents on 2026-08-15.
- Supporting: LFRA 2024 (c. 22) ss.49-52: s.49 (non-residential limit raised from 25% to 50%), s.50 (costs of RTM claims), s.51 (compliance with Chapter 1 obligations), s.52 (no first-instance High Court applications), plus s.64 so far as affecting RTM. ## Key questions this page must answer
1. What is the Right to Manage in plain terms, and what statute gives it? (CLRA 2002 Part 2 Ch 1.)
2. Which buildings qualify (s.72), and what changed in March 2025? (Non-residential limit 25% to 50%, LFRA 2024 s.49.)
3. Who is a qualifying tenant (s.75), and how many must participate? (Members of the RTM company holding leases of at least half the flats.)
4. Is RTM no-fault? (Yes, no mismanagement needs proving, unlike an LTA 1987 manager appointment.)
5. What do we actually take over, and what stays with the freeholder?
6. Do we pay the freeholder's costs of the claim? (Changed by LFRA 2024 s.50, in force Mar 2025; Stage 2 states the amended position precisely.)
7. RTM vs buying the freehold vs appointing a manager: which fits which situation?
8. Does RTM change my lease, ground rent, or the length of my lease? (No; forward-link A9/A15.)
