# Estate agent fees for renting: what a letting agent charges a landlord, and which of those fees actually reduce your tax

## Statutory anchor
- Primary: **Consumer Rights Act 2015 Part 3 Chapter 3, ss.83-88** (duty of letting agents to publicise fees). s.83 requires a letting agent to publish a list of fees at its premises, on its website and on third-party listing sites, with the amount inclusive of tax and whether it is charged per dwelling or per tenant. **s.85(1) covers fees "payable to the agent by a landlord or tenant"**, so the landlord-side fee is inside the duty, not just the tenant-side one. **s.87(7) caps the local weights and measures authority's financial penalty at £5,000.** WebFetch-VERIFIED at legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/part/3/chapter/3 on 2026-08-21.

## CGT hook (mandatory, the wave's organising rule)
A letting agent's fees and a selling agent's commission sit in two different tax computations and landlords routinely put them in the wrong one. Everything the letting agent charges while the property is let (tenant-find, the monthly management percentage, renewal and inventory charges) is a **revenue expense against rental income** in the year it is incurred, via ITTOIA 2005 s.272 applying the s.34 wholly-and-exclusively test. It is not base cost and it cannot be saved up for the sale. What is CGT-deductible is the commission on the **disposal**, an incidental cost of making the disposal under **TCGA 1992 s.38(1)(c)** read with the exhaustive s.38(2) list. This bites harder for a landlord than an owner-occupier because letting is exactly what stops Private Residence Relief covering the whole gain, so there is a taxable gain for those disposal costs to reduce at the 18% and 24% rates in house_positions §5, and where tax is due a 60-day return follows (FA 2019 Sch 2 para 3, the 30-day period substituted for 60 days by FA 2022 s.23(2), verified 2026-08-21). One paragraph and one worked line only, then forward-link to `/blog/capital-gains-tax/tax-sell-rental-property-uk` and `/blog/capital-gains-tax/cgt-payment-deadlines-property-sales-2026`. Do not restate the CGT calculation here.

**MANDATORY CORRECTION TO THE SEED'S EXPLOITATION PLAN (Bug #3 clause).** The seed assumed no competitor tells a landlord the fee is deductible. That is now false. LettingAProperty states verbatim: "Landlord fees paid to letting agents are strictly allowable business expenses, reducing your taxable rental income", and links HMRC guidance. **Do not write "no one tells you these fees are deductible".** The differentiator moves up one level, to the split and the mechanism. Five beat-them elements, in descending order of value:

1. **The two-computation split.** One competitor says "deductible" and stops. Neither says which computation. The letting fee is revenue against rental income in the year incurred (ITTOIA 2005 s.272 applying s.34); the sale commission on the same property is capital, an incidental cost of making the disposal under TCGA 1992 s.38(1)(c). Same agent, same property, two different reliefs, two different years.
2. **s.38(2) is an exhaustive list and it names "agent".** HMRC CG15250: "TCGA92/S38 defines the incidental costs of acquisition and disposal. The definition is exhaustive." So the sale commission qualifies and the management fee can never be banked as base cost. This is the correction that stops a landlord double-counting.
3. **Fees paid before the first tenant are not lost.** ITTOIA 2005 s.57 imported by the s.272(2) table, seven-year window, PIM2505. Neither competitor mentions it. One sentence and a link to our own page.
4. **The published fee list is a statutory duty, not a courtesy.** CRA 2015 s.83 requires the list at the premises, on the website and on the third-party portal, each fee inclusive of tax and marked per dwelling or per tenant, with a local weights and measures authority penalty behind it. Both competitors discuss "make sure the quote includes VAT" without knowing there is a statute that requires it.
5. **Net-of-relief cost.** 12% plus VAT is 14.4% of rent gross; after relief it is materially less, and the number depends on the landlord's marginal rate. Neither competitor runs it. This is the page's worked example.
