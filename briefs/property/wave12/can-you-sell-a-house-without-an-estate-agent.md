# Can you sell a house without an estate agent? The legal answer, the five things that can stop you, and whether you should

## Statutory anchor
- Primary: **Estate Agents Act 1979 s.1(1)**, VERIFIED at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1979/38/section/1 on 2026-08-21. The Act regulates "estate agency work", which it defines as things done **in the course of a business** pursuant to **instructions received from another person** who wants to dispose of or acquire an interest in land. It regulates agents when you use one. **It imposes no obligation to use one.** That is the statutory basis for the page's headline yes, and it is the correct way to answer a "can you" question: not "there is no law against it", but "here is the Act that governs agents, and here is why it does not reach a seller acting for themselves". Also **s.1(4)** (a person whose only activity is publishing advertisements, disseminating information, or providing a means of direct communication between buyer and seller is outside the Act), which is what a portal-listing intermediary relies on.
- Primary: **Legal Services Act 2007 s.13** (a person may carry on a reserved legal activity only if authorised or exempt), **s.12(1)(c)** and **Sch 2 para 5(1)(a)** (reserved instrument activities include "preparing any instrument of transfer or charge for the purposes of the Land Registration Act 2002 (c. 9)"), **Sch 3 para 3(10)** ("The person is exempt if the person is an individual who carries on the activity otherwise than for, or in expectation of, any fee, gain or reward"). VERIFIED at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2007/29/section/12, /schedule/2 and /schedule/3 on 2026-08-21. This is the second "can you": can you also skip the solicitor. **Stage 2 must not overstate the exemption**; if it cannot be supported from primary law plus HM Land Registry practice guidance, frame it procedurally and flag it.
- Supporting: **Estate Agents (Provision of Information) Regulations 1991 (SI 1991/859)** reg 5 and the Schedule (prescribed explanations of **sole selling rights** and **ready, willing and able purchaser**), VERIFIED at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1991/859/contents/made on 2026-08-21. ## CGT hook (mandatory, the wave's organising rule)
The honest answer to "should you" changes depending on whether the sale is taxable, and that is the fork nobody else builds into the decision. Under **TCGA 1992 s.38(1)(c)** the incidental costs of making the disposal reduce the gain, and **s.38(2)** expressly covers the agent's fee and the costs of advertising to find a buyer. So for a seller whose gain is fully covered by private residence relief, every pound of commission avoided is a pound kept, and the DIY case is at its strongest. For a seller of a former rental, a second home or an inherited property, the state pays 18% or 24% of the commission anyway through the deduction, so the net saving shrinks by that fraction and the risk of achieving a lower sale price weighs proportionately heavier. One sentence changes the recommendation: **if the sale is chargeable, the agent is cheaper than it looks and a weaker sale price is dearer than it looks.** The second tax consequence of going it alone is procedural. Where tax is due, **FA 2019 Sch 2 para 3(1)(b)** requires the return and the payment on account within 60 days of completion, and a seller with no agent and often no conveyancer prompting them is the classic late filer.
