# How to sell a house without an estate agent: the process, the paperwork, and what the saving is worth after tax

## Statutory anchor
- Primary: **Estate Agents Act 1979 s.1(1)**, VERIFIED at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1979/38/section/1 on 2026-08-21. "Estate agency work" means things done **in the course of a business** pursuant to **instructions received from another person** who wants to dispose of or acquire an interest in land, for the purpose of introducing a third person or, after an introduction, securing the disposal. A person selling their own home is doing neither of those things, so the Act does not regulate them: **no s.18 fee disclosure duty, no redress scheme, no statutory client protections, in either direction.** Also **s.1(4)**, which puts outside the Act a person whose only activities are **publishing advertisements or disseminating information**, or **providing a means by which a prospective buyer and seller can communicate directly with each other**. That subsection is the legal mechanism behind portal-listing intermediaries and it is why some of them are not estate agents at all. Stage 2 quotes s.1(1) and s.1(4) verbatim.
- Primary: **Legal Services Act 2007 s.12(1)(c)** (reserved instrument activities are a reserved legal activity), **Sch 2 para 5(1)(a)** ("preparing any instrument of transfer or charge for the purposes of the Land Registration Act 2002 (c. 9)"), **s.13** (a person is entitled to carry on a reserved legal activity only if authorised or exempt), and **Sch 3 para 3(10)** ("The person is exempt if the person is an individual who carries on the activity otherwise than for, or in expectation of, any fee, gain or reward"). VERIFIED at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2007/29/section/12, /schedule/2 and /schedule/3 on 2026-08-21. **Stage 2 must handle this carefully: Sch 3 para 3(10) is the route by which DIY conveyancing on your own transaction is lawful, and the page must not overstate it into "anyone can do anyone's conveyancing".** If Stage 2 cannot support the self-conveyancing framing from primary law plus HM Land Registry practice guidance, frame it procedurally instead and raise a flag rather than guessing.
- Supporting: **Misrepresentation Act 1967 s.2(1)**, VERIFIED at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1967/7/section/2 on 2026-08-21 ("Damages for misrepresentation"). It applies to **"a person"** with no trader restriction, which is the point: a private seller carries full misrepresentation exposure on what they say about the property.
- Supporting: **DMCCA 2024 s.225(3)**, VERIFIED at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/section/225 on 2026-08-21: "trader" means a person acting **for purposes relating to P's business**, or a person acting in P's name or on P's behalf for those purposes. A private seller of their own home is not a trader, so **the unfair-trading regime in DMCCA 2024 Part 4 Ch.1 (in force 6 April 2025 by SI 2025/272 reg 2(1)(3)) binds the portal or intermediary, not the private seller**. The seller's exposure is contractual and under the 1967 Act instead. Do not cite the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008: revoked by **DMCCA 2024 s.251(1)** from 6 April 2025 (verified). Property Misdescriptions Act 1991 is repealed and must not be cited.
- Supporting: **TCGA 1992 s.38(1)(c) + s.38(2)** and **FA 2019 Sch 2 para 3(1)(b)** (60th day, substituted by FA 2022 s.23(2)), both VERIFIED 2026-08-21 at the URLs recorded in the A1 seed.

## CGT hook (mandatory, the wave's organising rule)
Selling without an agent removes a deduction as well as a cost. **TCGA 1992 s.38(1)(c)** allows the incidental costs of making the disposal, and **s.38(2)** lists the professional fees and the costs of advertising to find a buyer. Take the agent out and there is no commission to deduct, but the costs you do incur still qualify: the portal listing fee, the photography, the EPC, the solicitor's or licensed conveyancer's bill, and any valuation you pay for. So a seller who spends £1,500 doing it themselves rather than £7,200 on commission saves £5,700 of cash and gives up £5,700 of deduction, which on a chargeable disposal at the 24% higher residential rate means the real saving is nearer £4,332. That is the number the DIY-selling sites never print. It only matters if there is a chargeable gain at all, which is the fork the page must state plainly: fully covered by private residence relief and the whole saving is yours, a former let or a second home and roughly a fifth to a quarter of it goes back in tax. Where tax is due, **FA 2019 Sch 2 para 3(1)(b)** gives 60 days from completion to file and pay, and a seller with no agent and no chain of professional reminders is exactly the person who misses it. ### 1. Verify-at-write resolutions

- **LSA 2007 Sch 3 para 3(10) and the DIY-conveyancing framing: RESOLVED, and the primary law supplies its own limit (resolved for lock §26.14, proposed patch raised as F-168).** Full text of Sch 3 para 3 fetched https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2007/29/schedule/3 on 2026-08-21. **para 3(10) verbatim: "The person is exempt if the person is an individual who carries on the activity otherwise than for, or in expectation of, any fee, gain or reward."** The guard the lock asked for is inside the same paragraph: **para 3(3)(d)** makes a supervised individual exempt only where the supervisor "is entitled to carry on the activity, **otherwise than by virtue of sub-paragraph (10)**". So the statute itself says a person who is exempt only because they act unpaid cannot be the supervisor under whom someone else acts. **Safe framing, which the writer must not exceed: you may prepare the transfer of your own property because you are not doing it for fee, gain or reward; you may not do it for anyone else for payment, and the unpaid exemption gives you no standing to supervise anyone.** Do not write "anyone can do anyone's conveyancing unpaid".

**Worked examples (2, second person only).**
(a) *The saving, before and after tax.* You sell a £300,000 former rental. The agent route would have cost £4,260 at 1.42% including VAT. You spend £1,079 instead: a fixed listing fee, an EPC and photography. The cash saving is £3,181. But the £3,181 you did not spend is £3,181 you no longer deduct under **s.38(1)(c)**, so as a higher-rate taxpayer on a chargeable disposal the real saving is **£2,418**. If the property is fully covered by private residence relief instead, there is no gain to deduct against and the whole £3,181 is yours. **That fork changes the answer and it is the only thing on this page a fee-comparison site cannot copy.**
(b) *The step nobody schedules.* You exchange on 26 March and complete on 12 May. The disposal falls in the **earlier** tax year (s.28(1) fixes it at exchange) but the 60-day return runs from **completion**, so the filing deadline is in July for a gain that belongs to the tax year that ended in April. With no agent and no chain of professional reminders, that is the classic missed filing.
