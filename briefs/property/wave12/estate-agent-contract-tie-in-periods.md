# Estate agent tie-in periods: how long you are locked in, what it costs to leave, and which of those fees survive into your tax computation

## Statutory anchor
- Primary: **The Estate Agents (Provision of Information) Regulations 1991 (SI 1991/859), regulation 5** and its Schedule, which prescribe the explanations an agent must give of "sole selling rights", "sole agency" and "ready, willing and able purchaser". WebFetch-VERIFIED at legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1991/859/made on 2026-08-21. The prescribed **sole agency** explanation makes the client liable "if at any time unconditional contracts for the sale of the property are exchanged" with a purchaser introduced by the agent during the sole agency period or with whom the agent had negotiations during that period, or with a purchaser introduced by another agent during that period. The prescribed **sole selling rights** explanation is wider: liability arises if contracts are exchanged during the period "even if the purchaser was not found by us but by another agent or by any other person, including yourself", and also after the period ends where the purchaser was introduced or negotiated with during it. **This distinction is the whole page.**
- Supporting: **Estate Agents Act 1979 s.18** ("Information to clients of prospective liabilities"). The agent must disclose, before the client enters the contract, when remuneration becomes payable, the amount or its method of calculation, and other payments or charges, and must disclose any variation. On failure, "the contract or, as the case may be, the variation of it shall not be enforceable by him" unless a court so orders, and the court may dismiss the application or reduce or discharge the sum to compensate for the client's prejudice, having regard to the agent's culpability and the harm caused. WebFetch-VERIFIED at legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1979/38/section/18 on 2026-08-21.
- Supporting: **The Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013 (SI 2013/3134)**, regs 29, 30 and 36. reg 29(1): "The consumer may cancel a distance or off-premises contract at any time in the cancellation period without giving any reason, and without incurring any liability except under these provisions". reg 30: for a service contract the period "ends at the end of 14 days after the day on which the contract is entered into". reg 36: early supply requires the consumer's express request, on a durable medium for an off-premises contract; reg 36(2) the right is lost once the service is fully performed after such a request with an acknowledgement; reg 36(4) on cancellation after early supply the consumer pays a proportionate amount; reg 36(6) they pay nothing where the trader failed to give the required cancellation information. WebFetch-VERIFIED at legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134 regs 29, 30, 36 on 2026-08-21. **Most agency agreements are signed at the vendor's kitchen table, which makes them off-premises contracts and brings the 14-day right into play. No consumer guide leads with this.**

## CGT hook (mandatory, the wave's organising rule)
A tie-in dispute is a tax problem as well as a contract problem, and it is the one place in this wave where a selling cost can fail to be deductible. **TCGA 1992 s.38(1)(c)** allows the incidental costs of **making the disposal** and **s.38(2)** defines them exhaustively; HMRC's own **CG15250** puts it plainly: "TCGA92/S38 defines the incidental costs of acquisition and disposal. The definition is exhaustive." Commission to the agent who actually sold the property is squarely inside that list as a professional fee to an agent. A withdrawal fee, an early-termination charge, or a second commission to a first agent who did not sell is a different animal: a cost of getting out of a contract, not a cost of making the disposal, and it must satisfy the wholly-and-exclusively test on its own before it enters the computation. Anyone facing a dual-fee claim under sole selling rights should treat the second payment as contested and keep the paperwork accordingly. None of it matters where Private Residence Relief covers the sale; where tax is due, the 60-day return under FA 2019 Sch 2 follows completion. One paragraph and one worked line only, then forward-link to `/blog/capital-gains-tax/cgt-record-keeping-property-sales-what-to-save-how-long` and `/blog/capital-gains-tax/tax-sell-rental-property-uk`.
