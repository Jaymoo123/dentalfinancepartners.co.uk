# The cost of moving house in the UK: the full bill, itemised, and the three tax buckets it splits into

## Statutory anchor
- Primary: **TCGA 1992 s.38**. **s.38(1)(a)** allows the acquisition consideration "together with the incidental costs to him of the acquisition"; **s.38(1)(b)** allows enhancement expenditure reflected in the state or nature of the asset at disposal; **s.38(1)(c)** allows "the incidental costs to him of making the disposal"; **s.38(2)** defines those incidental costs exhaustively as professional fees to a surveyor, valuer, auctioneer, accountant, agent or legal adviser, transfer or conveyancing costs including stamp duty, advertising to find a buyer or seller, and valuation or apportionment costs reasonably incurred for the CGT computation. **s.38(3)**: "Except as provided by section 40, no payment of interest shall be allowable under this section." WebFetch-VERIFIED at legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/12/section/38 on 2026-08-21. **s.38 is the spine of this page: it is the provision that sorts a moving bill into deductible and non-deductible, at both ends of the chain.**
- Supporting: **The Land Registration Fee Order 2024 (SI 2024/931), Schedule 1 (Scale 1)**, the registration fee on a transfer of registered land by value band. Verified at legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/931/schedule/1/made on 2026-08-21: **£0-80,000 = £20 electronic / £45 otherwise; £80,001-100,000 = £40 / £95; £100,001-200,000 = £100 / £230; £200,001-500,000 = £150 / £330; £500,001-1,000,000 = £295 / £655; £1,000,001 and above = £500 / £1,105**, with a separate reduced scale for voluntary first registration. **The 2021 Order (SI 2021/1226) is revoked; cite the 2024 Order.** This is the only line on the whole moving bill that is fixed by statute, which makes it the page's most defensible table row and something no consumer competitor sources properly.

## CGT hook (mandatory, the wave's organising rule)
A moving bill looks like one number and is actually three. **Selling costs** (agent commission, the sale conveyancing, EPC, advertising to find a buyer) are incidental costs of making the disposal under **TCGA 1992 s.38(1)(c)**, deductible from the gain on the property you are leaving. **Buying costs** (SDLT, the Land Registry fee, survey, purchase conveyancing) are incidental costs of acquisition under **s.38(1)(a)**: worth nothing now, banked into the base cost of the new property for a disposal that may be twenty years away. **Everything else** (removals, storage, cleaning, redirection, new carpets, and mortgage interest, which s.38(3) rules out expressly) gets no relief at either end. The first bucket only pays off where Private Residence Relief does not cover the whole gain, and where tax is due a 60-day CGT on UK property return under FA 2019 Sch 2 follows completion. The second is worth receipts regardless, because nobody remembers a 2026 survey fee in 2046.
