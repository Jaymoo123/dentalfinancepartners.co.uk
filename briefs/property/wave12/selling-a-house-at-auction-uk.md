# Selling a house at auction in the UK: costs, timetable, and the tax date nobody mentions

## Statutory anchor
- Primary: **Law of Property (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1989 s.2**: s.2(1) requires a contract for the sale of an interest in land to be made in writing incorporating all agreed terms; **s.2(5)(b) disapplies that requirement for "a contract made in the course of a public auction"**. WebFetch-VERIFIED at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1989/34/section/2 on 2026-08-21 (latest revised version, 21 Aug 2026). This subsection is the legal engine of the whole page: it is why the fall of the hammer binds both parties immediately, with no exchange, no cooling-off and no gazundering.
- Primary (tax): **TCGA 1992 s.38(1)(c) + s.38(2)**: s.38(2) is an exhaustive list of deductible incidental costs and it **names "auctioneer" expressly** alongside surveyor, valuer, accountant, agent and legal adviser, plus "costs of transfer or conveyance (including stamp duty or stamp duty land tax)". VERIFIED at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/12/section/38 on 2026-08-21 (latest revised, 20 Aug 2026; s.38(2) amended by FA 2003 to insert SDLT).
- Primary (timing): **TCGA 1992 s.28(1)**: "Where an asset is disposed of and acquired under a contract the time at which the disposal and acquisition is made is the time the contract is made (and not, if different, the time at which the asset is conveyed or transferred)." s.28(2) puts a conditional contract's disposal date at the time the condition is satisfied. VERIFIED at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/12/section/28 on 2026-08-21.
- Supporting: **Estate Agents Act 1979 s.1** (definition of estate agency work: things done in the course of a business pursuant to instructions from a person wishing to dispose of an interest in land, with a s.1(4) carve-out only for bare advertising or a means of contact, so an auction house running the full sale is inside the Act; VERIFIED 2026-08-21); **s.18** (fees and prospective liabilities must be given to the client **before** contract, and by **s.18(5)** a non-compliant contract "shall not be enforceable by him except pursuant to an order of the court"; VERIFIED 2026-08-21); **s.23A** (redress-scheme power, inserted by CEARA 2007 s.66(2) from 12 Oct 2007; VERIFIED 2026-08-21). **DMCCA 2024 Part 4 Ch.1** ss.225-230 and **Sch 20** (32 always-unfair practices, incl. para 5 bait advertising and para 7 false scarcity), all in force **6 April 2025** by SI 2025/272; **s.251(1) revoked the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008 outright** from the same date (VERIFIED at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/section/251 on 2026-08-21). **FA 2019 Sch 2 para 3(1)(b)** (60-day return, "60th" substituted for "30th" by FA 2022 s.23(2); VERIFIED 2026-08-21).

## CGT hook (mandatory, the wave's organising rule)
An auction sale is a disposal like any other, and every pound the auction house takes is deductible under **TCGA 1992 s.38(1)(c)**: the auctioneer's commission, the entry or marketing fee, and the solicitor's cost of assembling the legal pack all sit inside the exhaustive s.38(2) list, which names the auctioneer by title. That only matters if the gain is not fully sheltered by private residence relief, so the page's tax section is written for the three vendors who actually turn up at auction with a taxable gain: the landlord selling a let property, the owner of a second home, and the beneficiary or personal representative selling an inherited house. The point no consumer auction guide makes is the **date**: under **s.28(1)** the disposal happens when the contract is made, and at a traditional auction the contract is made on the fall of the hammer, so a property knocked down on 2 April and completed on 30 April is a disposal in the **earlier** tax year, against that year's rates, allowance and losses. The 60-day clock is different again: **FA 2019 Sch 2 para 3(1)(b)** runs it from **completion**, not from the hammer. Forward-link `/blog/capital-gains-tax/capital-gains-tax-property-complete-guide-uk` for the mechanics and `/blog/capital-gains-tax/cgt-payment-deadlines-property-sales-2026` for the reporting deadline; this page states the auction-specific timing fork and stops.

## Framing differentiator (anti-templating, anti-cannibalisation)
Nearest existing page: `bridging-finance-for-auction-purchases`, which is written entirely for the **buyer** raising short-term finance against a 28-day completion. This page is the mirror image and never touches finance: it is the **seller's** economics of the same room. Nothing else on the site targets auction selling, and the cannibalisation check returned a top Jaccard of 0.12 against `inheriting-a-house-in-the-uk`, which is a false neighbour.

The accountant's angle is the house differentiator. HomeOwners Alliance, TheAdvisory, Which? and the auction houses' own guides all price the entry fee and the commission, then argue about whether the auction discount is 10% or 20%. None of them prices the tax, and none of them notices that auction is the one sale route where the disposal date and the completion date reliably fall in different months and can fall in different tax years. The page's spine is therefore a decision, not a description: **auction buys you certainty and speed and charges you a discount for it**, so quantify the discount against what the certainty is worth, then net both against the tax position. Two further seller-side points nobody covers: the legal pack is where an auction sale is won or lost (a missing lease, an absent building regs certificate or an unresolved title defect is what actually depresses the hammer price), and the reserve is the seller's only real control lever. Lane guards vs siblings: A11 owns the modern method entirely (this page gives it one contrast paragraph and a link, no more); A12 owns part-exchange; A1-A5 own estate agent fee structures, so this page compares auction cost to agent cost in a single table row and does not re-litigate commission models.

**NOT resolved, and the page must therefore not assert it - the auction discount.** There is no reliable published UK figure for a residential auction discount, and the auction houses publish the opposite claim. **Beat-them plan, five elements the ranking pages do not have:**
1. **The tax layer (the wave's mandatory hook).** TCGA 1992 s.38(2) names the **auctioneer** expressly in an exhaustive list, so auction commission, the entry fee and the legal-pack solicitor's fee are all deductible. None of the four competitors mentions tax at all.
2. **The disposal date.** s.28(1) puts the disposal at the fall of the hammer, not at completion, so an auction straddling early April moves the gain into the earlier tax year while the 60-day clock under FA 2019 Sch 2 para 3(1)(b) still runs from **completion**. This is the single most valuable sentence on the page and it appears nowhere in the SERP.
3. **Why the hammer binds, with the section.** LP(MP)A 1989 s.2(5)(b) disapplies the s.2(1) writing formalities for "a contract made in the course of a public auction". Competitors say "it is legally binding"; none says why.
4. **The fee-disclosure right, with the consequence.** EAA 1979 s.18 requires fees and prospective liabilities before contract and **s.18(5)** makes a non-compliant contract unenforceable by the agent except by court order. A seller reading that has leverage nobody else gave them.
5. **The guide-price rule as it is since 6 April 2025.** DMCCA 2024 Sch 20 para 5 (bait advertising) and para 7 (false scarcity to elicit an immediate decision), with CPUTR 2008 revoked by s.251(1). Every competitor page that touches guide prices is either silent or citing revoked law.
