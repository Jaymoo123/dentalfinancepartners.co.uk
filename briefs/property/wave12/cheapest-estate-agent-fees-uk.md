## CGT hook (mandatory, the wave's organising rule)
Chasing the cheapest fee is a rational move only until you compare it with the tax on the same sale. Under **TCGA 1992 s.38(1)(c)**, the agent's commission is an incidental cost of making the disposal and comes off the gain, and **s.38(2)** puts "agent" and "advertising to find a buyer" expressly in the list. So on a property that is not fully sheltered by private residence relief, the real cost of an agent is the fee **after tax relief**: at the 24% higher residential rate, saving £2,000 of commission saves you only about £1,520 net, because the £2,000 you did not spend is £2,000 you no longer deduct. That reverses the usual comparison logic in a way no fee-comparison site will publish, because their business is the comparison. The other half of the hook is the sale price itself: a cheaper agent who achieves £5,000 less on the sale price costs a taxable seller far more than the fee saving, and where tax is due the **FA 2019 Sch 2 para 3(1)(b)** 60-day clock from completion is running while you are still arguing about it. **Worked examples (2, second person only).**
(a) *The breakeven.* Take the 1.42% including-VAT average and a £1,079 upfront fixed fee including the £80 AML charge. The two cost the same at a sale price of £1,079 divided by 0.0142, about **£76,000**, so on cash alone the fixed fee is cheaper on essentially any house. Now take the pay-on-completion structure at £2,249 all in: the crossover moves to about **£158,000**, which is below the average house price but above a large part of the North East and Scotland. That is the honest answer to "which is cheapest", and it depends on one number the comparison sites never ask you for.
(b) *The after-tax reversal.* The property is a former let, so the gain is chargeable and you are a higher-rate taxpayer. Choosing the £1,079 fixed fee over a £4,260 commission on a £300,000 sale saves £3,181 of cash, but you also lose £3,181 of s.38(1)(c) deduction, so at 24% the real saving is **£2,418**. And if the cheaper route achieves £5,000 less on the price, you are £2,582 worse off before you count anything else. State it plainly: **the fee is the small number and the price is the big one.**
