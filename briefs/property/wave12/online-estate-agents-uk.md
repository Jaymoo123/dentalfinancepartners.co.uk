# Online estate agents in the UK: how the model actually works, what it costs, and where the saving goes

## Statutory anchor
- Primary: **Estate Agents Act 1979 s.1(1) and s.1(4)**, VERIFIED at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1979/38/section/1 on 2026-08-21. s.1(1) defines "estate agency work" as things done **in the course of a business** on **instructions received from another person** who wants to dispose of or acquire an interest in land, either to introduce a third person or, after an introduction, to secure the disposal. s.1(4) puts **outside** the Act a person whose only activities are **publishing advertisements or disseminating information**, or **providing a means by which a prospective buyer and seller can communicate directly with each other**. **This subsection is the page's central legal insight: an online agent that takes instructions and works the sale is doing estate agency work and is regulated as an estate agent; a pure portal-listing intermediary that only advertises and passes messages may sit outside the Act altogether, with everything that follows for the seller's protections.** No consumer competitor draws that line, because most of them sit on one side of it. Stage 2 quotes s.1(1) and s.1(4) verbatim and states the test rather than naming which side any particular brand falls on.

## CGT hook (mandatory, the wave's organising rule)
An online agent's fee is deductible on exactly the same footing as a high-street agent's, and the timing difference is where sellers get caught. **TCGA 1992 s.38(1)(c)** allows the incidental costs of making the disposal and **s.38(2)** covers the agent's fee and the costs of advertising to find a buyer. A percentage commission paid on completion is unambiguously a cost of making the disposal. **An upfront fee paid months earlier, on a sale that then falls through and never completes, is not: there is no disposal to attach it to.** That is the sharpest tax point available on this keyword and it follows directly from the words of s.38(1)(c), which the Stage 2 writer must quote rather than paraphrase. So on a chargeable disposal, a former let or a second home, the upfront model carries a tax risk the completion-fee model does not, and where the sale does complete the fee reduces the gain at 18% or 24% in the ordinary way. Where tax is due, **FA 2019 Sch 2 para 3(1)(b)** gives 60 days from completion to file and pay, and an online agent is not the party who will remind you. ### 1. Verify-at-write resolutions

- **SI 2013/3134 regs 31 and 36: RESOLVED, verbatim (the seed forbade stating either until opened; both now open; resolved for lock §26.14).** Fetched 2026-08-21.
  - **reg 31**, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/31/made. **reg 31(1)** applies where the trader does not give the consumer the cancellation information required by paragraph (l) of Schedule 2. **reg 31(2): "If the trader provides the consumer with that information in the period of 12 months beginning with the first day of the 14 days mentioned in regulation 30(2) to (6), but otherwise in accordance with Part 2, the cancellation period ends at the end of 14 days after the consumer receives the information."** **reg 31(3): "Otherwise the cancellation period ends at the end of 12 months after the day on which it would have ended under regulation 30."** **Decisive figures: the extension is up to 12 months, and cures at 14 days from late disclosure.**
  - **reg 36**, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/36/made. **reg 36(1): "The trader must not begin the supply of a service before the end of the cancellation period provided for in regulation 30(1) unless the consumer, (a) has made an express request".** **reg 36(4):** where the service was supplied on request the consumer "must (subject to paragraph (6)) pay to the trader an amount, (a) for the supply of the service for the period for which it is supplied, ending with the time when the trader is informed of the consumer's decision to cancel the contract, and (b) **which is in proportion to what has been supplied, in comparison with the full coverage of the contract**". **reg 36(6): "The consumer bears no cost for supply of the service, in full or in part, in the cancellation period, if, (a) the trader has failed to provide the consumer with the information on the right to cancel required by paragraph (l) of Schedule 2, or (b) the service is not supplied in response to a request in accordance with paragraph (1)."** **This is the sharpest consumer point available on this keyword: cancel inside 14 days and you pay only a proportionate amount for what was actually done, and if the agent never told you about the cancellation right, or started without your express request, you pay nothing at all.**
  - **reg 5 definitions fetched the same day** to confirm the regulations reach these contracts: "distance contract" is "a contract concluded between a trader and a consumer under an organised distance sales or service-provision scheme without the simultaneous physical presence of the trader and the consumer"; an "off-premises contract" is concluded in the consumer's presence away from business premises; "service contract" is "a contract, other than a sales contract, under which a trader supplies or agrees to supply a service to a consumer". **Both routes matter: signing on the website is a distance contract, signing at an in-home valuation appointment is an off-premises contract, and reg 30 gives 14 days either way.** That closes the obvious objection and no competitor makes the distinction.

**Worked examples (2, second person only).**
(a) *The fee that has no disposal to attach to.* You pay £999 upfront in March plus the £80 check fee, the buyer withdraws in June, and you take the house off the market. Under **s.38(1)(c)** the deduction is for "the incidental costs to him of **making the disposal**", and **CG15250** says the definition is exhaustive. There was no disposal, so there is nothing for the £1,079 to be an incidental cost of, and the money is gone in both directions: non-refundable under the contract and not allowable against a gain that never arose. Now relist and sell the following year: the fee you pay **then** is deductible against **that** disposal, and if you cannot recover VAT the gross figure is what goes in (**CG14300**).
(b) *The cancellation right, priced.* You sign on the website on the 1st, the agent takes photographs and lists on the 3rd at your express request, and you change your mind on the 9th. You are inside the 14 days under **reg 30**, so you may cancel, and **reg 36(4)** limits what you owe to an amount "in proportion to what has been supplied, in comparison with the full coverage of the contract", not the whole £999. If the agent never gave you the cancellation information, **reg 36(6)** means you owe nothing at all, and **reg 31(3)** stretches the cancellation window out to twelve months.
