# Modern method of auction explained: the reservation fee, the timetable, and who really pays

## Statutory anchor
- Primary: **Law of Property (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1989 s.2(1)**: a contract for the sale of an interest in land "can only be made in writing and only by incorporating all the terms which the parties have expressly agreed in one document or, where contracts are exchanged, in each". **s.2(5)(b)** disapplies that for "a contract made in the course of a public auction". WebFetch-VERIFIED at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1989/34/section/2 on 2026-08-21 (latest revised, 21 Aug 2026). This section is the whole page: the modern method's winning bid produces a **reservation agreement**, which is not a contract for the disposition of an interest in land, so **no land contract exists** and s.2(1) has not been satisfied by anyone. The land contract still has to be made, in writing, on exchange, inside the reservation period. Traditional auction leans on s.2(5)(b); the modern method does not, and that single difference generates every consequence on the page.
- Primary (tax): **TCGA 1992 s.38(1)(c) + s.38(2)**: exhaustive list of deductible incidental costs of disposal, naming "auctioneer" and "agent" expressly, plus costs of transfer or conveyance including SDLT. VERIFIED at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/12/section/38 on 2026-08-21.
- Primary (consumer): **DMCCA 2024 s.230** (material information in an invitation to purchase). **s.230(2)(b)** requires the total price; **s.230(4)** VERIFIED verbatim: "the total price of a product includes any fees, taxes, charges or other payments that the consumer will **necessarily** incur if the consumer purchases the product". **s.230(9)** treats information given "in a way that is unclear or untimely" or "in such a way that the consumer is unlikely to see it" as an omission. In force **6 April 2025** by SI 2025/272 reg 2(1)(3). VERIFIED at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/section/230 on 2026-08-21. This is the statutory hook for the reservation fee: a non-optional buyer fee is part of the advertised total price, not a footnote.
- Supporting: **DMCCA 2024 ss.225-229** (unfair commercial practices, misleading actions, misleading omissions, aggressive practices, professional diligence) and **Sch 20** "Commercial practices which are in all circumstances considered unfair", 32 entries, including **para 5** bait advertising and **para 7** falsely stating limited-time availability "in order to elicit an immediate decision" (all in force 6 Apr 2025 by SI 2025/272; VERIFIED 2026-08-21). **s.251(1): "The Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008 (S.I. 2008/1277) are revoked"**, same commencement (VERIFIED at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/section/251 on 2026-08-21). **Estate Agents Act 1979 s.1** (the modern method is estate agency work) and **s.18** (prospective liabilities disclosed before contract; s.18(5) makes a non-compliant contract unenforceable except by court order), VERIFIED 2026-08-21. **TCGA 1992 s.22(1)(c)** (capital sums received "in return for forfeiture or surrender of rights" are a disposal; s.22(2) times the disposal at receipt), VERIFIED at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/12/section/22 on 2026-08-21. **TCGA 1992 s.28(1)/(2)** (disposal date is contract date; conditional contract dates from satisfaction of the condition), VERIFIED 2026-08-21.

## CGT hook (mandatory, the wave's organising rule)
The modern method is sold to vendors as "no fee to you", and that framing is exactly what breaks the tax analysis. Where the vendor genuinely pays nothing, there is nothing to deduct under **TCGA 1992 s.38(1)(c)**: the buyer's reservation fee is the buyer's cost, sits outside the seller's s.38(2) list, and the seller's chargeable gain is computed on the full price achieved with no offset. The fee has not vanished, it has been priced into what the buyer was willing to bid, so the seller absorbs it as a lower gross price and gets no deduction for it, while an ordinary agent's commission on the same sale would have been fully deductible. Where the vendor does pay a fee, or the fee is netted off the completion monies, it is a deductible incidental cost and the page shows the one-line workings. Any of this matters only where the gain is not fully covered by private residence relief, so the tax section addresses second homes, let property and inherited property; the mechanics live on `/blog/capital-gains-tax/capital-gains-tax-property-complete-guide-uk` and the deadline on `/blog/capital-gains-tax/cgt-payment-deadlines-property-sales-2026`, and this page links rather than restates. One further point that is genuinely modern-method-only: because the reservation agreement is not the land contract, the **s.28(1)** disposal date is the later exchange, not the end of the online bidding, and a **reservation fee forfeited to the seller** when a buyer walks is a capital sum received for the surrender of rights within **s.22(1)(c)**, taxable in its own right and dated by s.22(2) to receipt.

Net-new: the cannibalisation check returned a top Jaccard of 0.09 and the site has no page on conditional auction. The nearest real neighbour is A10 `selling-a-house-at-auction-uk`, and the lane split is hard: **A10 is traditional auction, this page is the modern method**, and the page's job is to make the difference legible rather than to re-explain auctions. The pivot is a single legal fact, verified above, that almost no consumer coverage states correctly: at a traditional auction the hammer makes the contract because LP(MP)A 1989 s.2(5)(b) says a public auction contract does not need the s.2(1) formalities; under the modern method the winning bid makes a **reservation**, and the sale of the land is still an unexchanged, unbinding proposition. Everything vendors and buyers complain about (the fee that is not refundable, the 56-day pressure, the buyer who withdraws, the seller who accepts a higher offer) follows from that.

The accountant's angle is the house differentiator. Consumer coverage (HomeOwners Alliance, TheAdvisory, Which?, MoneySavingExpert, the property forums) argues about whether the reservation fee is fair. Nobody works out who bears it in the price, and nobody notices that the vendor's "free" sale is the one route where a real selling cost becomes non-deductible because it was charged to the other side. That single paragraph is the reason a tax firm is answering this query. Second differentiator: the page states the **s.230(4)** total-price rule and what it means for a listing that shows a bid level and buries a percentage-based reservation fee in the small print, which is a live compliance question since 6 April 2025 and is being covered nowhere accurately.
