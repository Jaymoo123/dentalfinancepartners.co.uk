# Average London estate agent fees: what commission you should be paying, and what it is worth back against Capital Gains Tax

## Statutory anchor
- Primary: **TCGA 1992 s.38(1)(c)**, "the incidental costs to him of making the disposal", read with **s.38(2)**, which defines those costs exhaustively as professional fees to a surveyor, valuer, auctioneer, accountant, agent or legal adviser, transfer or conveyancing costs including stamp duty, advertising costs to find a buyer, and valuation or apportionment costs reasonably incurred for the CGT computation. WebFetch-VERIFIED at legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/12/section/38 on 2026-08-21. This is the provision that makes an estate agent's commission a deduction at all.
- Supporting: **Estate Agents Act 1979 s.18** ("Information to clients of prospective liabilities"). The agent must give the client, **before the client enters into the contract**, the circumstances in which remuneration becomes payable, the amount or the method of calculating it, and any other payments or charges. If the agent fails, "the contract or, as the case may be, the variation of it shall not be enforceable by him" except on a court order, and the court may dismiss the application or reduce or discharge the sum to compensate the client's prejudice. WebFetch-VERIFIED at legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1979/38/section/18 on 2026-08-21. **There is no statutory cap on estate agent commission in England and Wales; say so explicitly, because a large part of the search intent is "am I being overcharged and is that even allowed".**

## CGT hook (mandatory, the wave's organising rule)
London is where the commission percentage matters least and the pound figure matters most, and the only thing that changes the net cost is whether any of it comes back. If the property was your only or main residence throughout, Private Residence Relief covers the gain and the commission is simply money gone. If it was a buy-to-let, a second home, a pied-a-terre or a former home you let for part of your ownership, there is a chargeable gain and the commission is an incidental cost of making the disposal under **TCGA 1992 s.38(1)(c)** that reduces it pound for pound. At the 24% higher residential rate in house_positions §5, every £10,000 of commission is £2,400 of CGT you do not pay, so a 1.5% London fee on a let property really costs about 1.14%. London gains also clear the £3,000 annual exempt amount routinely, so the 60-day CGT on UK property return under FA 2019 Sch 2 is the norm here, not the exception.
