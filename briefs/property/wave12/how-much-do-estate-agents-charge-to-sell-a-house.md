# How much do estate agents charge to sell a house? The fee, the VAT, and the cost nobody puts in the comparison table

## Statutory anchor
- Primary: **Estate Agents Act 1979 s.18** (information to clients). WebFetch-VERIFIED at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1979/38/section/18 on 2026-08-21. The agent must give the client, **before the client enters into the contract**, the circumstances in which remuneration becomes payable, the amount of that remuneration or the method of calculating it where it is not yet ascertainable, and the amount or estimate of any additional payments that are not part of the remuneration. **s.18(5): failure means the contract is not enforceable by the agent except by court order; s.18(6): the court may dismiss the application or reduce or discharge the sum payable** to compensate the client for prejudice suffered. This is the statutory spine of the whole page: the fee is not just a market price, it is a disclosure duty with a remedy attached.
- Supporting: **The Estate Agents (Provision of Information) Regulations 1991 (SI 1991/859)**, reg 5 and the Schedule "Explanation of certain terms" (prescribed explanations of **sole selling rights**, **sole agency** and **ready, willing and able purchaser**), reg 6 (prominence of those explanations). VERIFIED at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1991/859/contents/made on 2026-08-21; Stage 2 must open reg 5 and the Schedule and quote the prescribed wording verbatim before the page paraphrases it.
- Supporting: **TCGA 1992 s.38(1)(c) and s.38(2)** (incidental costs of disposal), VERIFIED at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/12/section/38 on 2026-08-21 (revised text current to 20 August 2026): s.38(1)(c) allows "the incidental costs to him of making the disposal"; s.38(2) confines those costs to fees, commission or remuneration for the professional services of a surveyor, valuer, auctioneer, accountant, agent or legal adviser, costs of transfer or conveyance, and, on a disposal, the costs of advertising to find a buyer.

## CGT hook (mandatory, the wave's organising rule)
The agent's commission is not just a cost, it is a deduction. Under **TCGA 1992 s.38(1)(c)**, the incidental costs of making the disposal come off the gain before tax, and **s.38(2)** names "agent" and "advertising to find a buyer" in the list of what counts. So on a property that is not fully covered by private residence relief (a former let, a second home, an inherited property, a place you moved out of years ago), a 1.5% plus VAT commission on a £400,000 sale is not £7,200 of pure cost: at the 24% higher residential rate it shelters roughly £1,728 of tax, which is the part of the fee the comparison sites never net off. The same paragraph carries the trap in the other direction. If the property is fully covered by private residence relief there is no gain to deduct against, so the fee is simply a cost and the negotiation is the only lever you have. And where tax is due, **FA 2019 Sch 2 para 3(1)(b)** gives you 60 days from completion to file and pay, which means the completion statement showing the agent's fee is a document you need for a return that is already running, not a receipt to file away. Forward-link target: `capital-gains-tax-property-complete-guide-uk` for the mechanics and `/calculators/capital-gains-tax-calculator` for the number. This page states the hook and one worked line, and does not restate rates, the annual exempt amount or the reporting rules, all of which the armed cgt1 batch owns.

### 1. Verify-at-write resolutions

- **Irrecoverable VAT in the s.38 computation: RESOLVED, and the seed pointed at the wrong manual page (resolved for lock §5.B, raised as F-167).** The rule is **CG14300** (`Computation: interaction with other taxes: income tax`), fetched https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg14300 on 2026-08-21, verbatim: **"If VAT is suffered on the expenses of disposal and this is available for set-off in the vendor's VAT account, the expense exclusive of VAT is to be deducted"**, and **"If no set-off is available, the expense inclusive of VAT is to be allowed"**. The same page also gives the two proceeds/base-cost limbs: "the gain is to be computed by reference to the proceeds of disposal exclusive of VAT" and "the cost of the asset for capital gains purposes should be the cost exclusive of VAT". **Decisive figure for this page: a private seller who cannot recover VAT deducts the GROSS commission.** On a £300,000 sale at 1.42% including VAT, the whole £4,260 is the s.38(1)(c) deduction, not the £3,550 net. The writer cites **CG14300**, never CG15250 or CG15260 for this point.
- **CG15250 exhaustiveness + the s.38(2) list: RESOLVED.** Fetched https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg15250 on 2026-08-21, verbatim: **"The definition is exhaustive. No other expenditure is allowable unless specifically provided for by TCGA92"**, then the four categories: professional fees (surveyors, valuers, auctioneers, accountants, agents, legal advisers); transfer or conveyance costs including Stamp Duty or stamp duty land tax; **"costs of advertising to find a buyer or seller"**; valuation or apportionment costs for the CGT computation. **Attribution precision (per the sibling batch's F-171): only the exhaustiveness sentence and the "including stamp duty or SDLT" gloss are CG15250's own contribution. Advertising to find a buyer and the valuation or apportionment costs are on the face of s.38(2) and must be cited to the statute, not to the manual.** The exhaustiveness sentence is the one the whole cost-of-selling cluster hangs on and it is quotable.
- **CG15260 does NOT carry the estate-agent treatment: RESOLVED as a negative.** Fetched https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg15260 on 2026-08-21. It covers transfer/conveyance examples ("packing, carriage and insurance in transit to an auctioneer's premises"), valuation and apportionment costs, and liquidator expenditure. No estate agent example, no advertising example, no VAT. **Delete CG15260 from the citation list for this page.** CG15160 (fetched same day) gives only the s.38(1) category headings and is not needed either.
- **SI 1991/859 reg 5 and the Schedule: RESOLVED, verbatim captured.** Fetched https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1991/859/regulation/5/made and https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1991/859/schedule/made on 2026-08-21. reg 5(1) requires the prescribed written explanation wherever the term is used; reg 5(1) proviso requires the explanation to be altered where the contract's own terms would make it misleading; reg 5(2) extends the duty to terms of similar effect; reg 5(3) requires the explanation to appear **in the contract document itself**. Prescribed wording, quotable on-page:
  - **Sole selling rights:** "You will be liable to pay remuneration to us, in addition to any other costs or charges agreed, in each of the following circumstances, if unconditional contracts for the sale of the property are exchanged in the period during which we have sole selling rights, **even if the purchaser was not found by us but by another agent or by any other person, including yourself**", and where contracts are exchanged after that period to a purchaser introduced or negotiated with during it.
  - **Sole agency:** liability arises where contracts are exchanged "with a purchaser introduced by us during the period of our sole agency or with whom we had negotiations about the property during that period", or "with a purchaser introduced by another agent during that period".
  - **Ready, willing and able purchaser:** "A purchaser is a 'ready, willing and able' purchaser if he is prepared and is able to exchange unconditional contracts for the purchase of your property", and the fee "must be paid **even if you subsequently withdraw and unconditional contracts for sale are not exchanged, irrespective of your reasons**".

**Worked examples (2, second person only, no personas).**
(a) *The completion-statement sort.* You sell a former rental for £300,000. The agent's invoice is 1.42% including VAT, £4,260. The premium portal listing is £300 and the removals firm is £900. The £4,260 and the £300 are incidental costs of disposal (s.38(2) names "agent" and, on a disposal, "the costs of advertising to find a buyer"); the £900 is not, because the s.38(2) list is exhaustive, as CG15250 says in terms. At the 24% higher residential rate the two allowable lines shelter £1,094 of tax and the removals line shelters nothing.
(b) *The VAT line, one paragraph.* You are not VAT registered, so you cannot set the VAT off. Per CG14300 the expense **inclusive** of VAT is allowed, so the deduction is the gross £4,260 and not the £3,550 net. If instead the property is fully covered by private residence relief, there is no gain to deduct against and the only lever left is the negotiation in H2 7.
