**Four things the numbers say, before any interpretation.**

1. **Second person is the register, and it is a much bigger lever here than in any prior cluster.**
   Winner median 54.4 per 1,000 words, floor 27.9, ceiling 76.1. Twelve of twelve winners sit above
   27. Our shipped cost page sits at **10.7**, which is a fifth of the winner median and below the
   floor by a factor of 2.6. Our rural pages, written to the rural spec, land at 24.6 and 48.8, so
   the house can already write at this rate when the spec asks for it.
2. **Sentence length IS a variable in this cluster, unlike the last one.** The rural pass found our
   sentences shorter than the winners' and killed that lever. Here it reverses: winner median mean
   sentence is **17.0 words** with a ceiling of 20.4, and all three of our pages sit at 20.0 to
   24.0. Our cost page is 3.6 words per sentence longer than any winner in the set. Flesch follows:
   winner floor is 62.0 across twelve pages, our cost page is **48.0**, fourteen points below the
   least readable winner.
3. **The statutory layer is uncited SERP-wide, and this is the sharpest confirmation of F-152 in
   any pass so far.** Across 35,385 words of winner prose there are **nine** hard statutory
   references, eight of them on two pages, both about the same provision (the Estate Agents Act
   1979 fee-disclosure duty). Ten of twelve winners cite no statute at all. Our EPC cost page runs
   5.5 hard references per 1,000 words, which is twenty-two times the winner rate.
4. **Nobody prices the tax.** Capital Gains Tax is mentioned on **3 of 12** winner pages (W2 thirteen
   mentions, W4 ten, W13 twenty-two) and on zero of the rest, including zoopla at p2. Exactly
   **two** winner pages state that selling costs are deductible from the gain, one sentence each,
   neither naming TCGA 1992 s.38 and neither listing which costs qualify. **Not one page in the
   measured set computes a CGT figure in pounds**, and not one shows a selling cost reducing a gain
   by a pound amount. The whole hook this wave is built on is unoccupied.

**One intuition the numbers killed.** "Winners open with the number" is only half true. A pound or
percentage figure appears within the first 60 words on **4 of 12** winner pages and within the
first 120 words on **9 of 12**. All three of our pages already carry a figure inside 60 words. We
are ahead of the winner set on answer-first and behind it on everything about how the sentence
around that figure is written. Do not spend brief effort on a lever we already hold.

## 2. Answer patterns

**P1. The cost answer is a total in pounds attached to a stated property value, in the same
sentence.** Not a range, not a percentage on its own.
Them (W12, opening sentence): "The average cost to sell a house in the UK is £4,621, based on a
property worth £270,000."
Them (W2, first line of its at-a-glance box): "Typical total cost: Around £5,000 to sell an average
UK home (£290,000), including estate agent, conveyancing and EPC fees."
Them (W1, third line): "The average estate agent fee in 2026 is 1.42% including VAT. So on a
£275,000 house this would mean estate agent fees of around £3,900."
Us (`epc-certificate-cost-uk`, opening sentence): "A domestic Energy Performance Certificate costs
between £35 and £120 for most homes in 2026, with large detached properties and agent-arranged
packages running to £200 or more."
Ours is a range with no anchor property and no single number the reader can carry away. W1's move
is the one to take: the percentage, then "So on a £X house this would mean £Y". The conversion
sentence is the pattern, and it appears on every high-density winner.

**P2. The range comes second and it is always bounded at both ends with a stated cause.**
Them (W1): "However, estate agent fees typically range from 0.9%-3.6% depending on which estate
agents you use, how many are selling your property, and how well you negotiate."
Them (W7): "Estate agent fees can vary from less than 1% to as much as 3.5%, depending on a number
of factors, including how many estate agents are selling your property."
Them (W14): "Estate agent fees are typically between 1% and 3.5% of your house sale, or a flat fee."
Them (zoopla, p2, fetched qualitatively): "With a sole agency contract, where just one agent markets
your home, the average fee is usually between 1% and 2% of your home's value. The average fee for a
multi-agency contract is around 3%."
The shape is invariant across four domains: **average first, range second, driver third**. None of
them presents the range without saying what moves it. Our EPC page gives the range and the drivers
but never gives the average, so there is no number for a reader to repeat.

**P3. Fee tables are two columns, Item and Cost, and the cost cell holds a range, not a point.**
W4 runs five tables and every one is `Item: | Cost:` with cells like "Estate agent fees |
0.5%-3%+VAT (of sale price)", "Conveyancing fees | £550-£1000 (inc.VAT)", "Removal costs |
£250-£4,000+VAT". Its third table repeats the same rows with a single worked scenario and a
"TOTAL COST TO SELL: | £4,621" row. W13 clones that structure and adds a third column, Source.
W6 runs one table only, `Type of cost | Average cost in England`, seven rows and a Total row of
£17,831.
Us (`epc-certificate-cost-uk`, our one table): five columns, `Property type | Direct to assessor |
Online broker | Agent-arranged | What moves the price`.
**The winner shape is two or three columns and a total row. Ours is five columns and no total.**
The total row is the load-bearing element: three of the four table-carrying winners have one, and
it is the number the SERP snippet quotes.

**P4. The highest-density domain writes cost tables as prose boxes, not tables.**
Three HOA pages hold five top-10 slots between them and carry **zero** `<table>` elements. HOA
presents the same data as a "KEY INFORMATION / At a glance" list: "Estate agent fees: Average
sole-agency high street fee is 1.42% incl. VAT, about £4,000 on a £290,000 house.", "Conveyancing
fees for selling: Usually £610-£950, with extra costs for leasehold properties.", "EPC costs:
£60-£120, unless you already have a valid certificate." Each line is label, figure, one condition.
Tables are therefore **not** the ranking lever in this cluster: the winner median is zero tables
and the domain with the most top-10 slots uses none. Build the table where it genuinely helps a
budgeting reader, and build the at-a-glance box regardless, because that is what the p1 page has.

**P5. Negotiation advice is a specific number to ask for, not an encouragement to haggle.**
Them (W2): "And be prepared to haggle on fees, estate agents will expect this and if you're
successful you'll reduce the cost of selling a house. If you are going for sole agency, you should
aim for a fee of 1.2% (this is 1% + VAT) or even less for high value properties."
Them (W1): "Estate agents generally aim for the top of their fee range so it's up to you to
negotiate them down."
Them (W7): "Estate agents will not want to lose your business, so you can often negotiate on the
fee or other aspects of the service (for example the notice or tie-in period). If one agent has
quoted you a lower fee but you'd prefer to use another firm, mention that you've found a cheaper
deal."
Them (W9): "Get at least three quotes to compare. Talk to a mix of local independents and bigger
chains. Ask about sliding-scale fees that reward a higher sale price."
Four of five fee pages carry negotiation advice and all four give the reader a script or a target
number. W7 adds the non-price lever, the tie-in and notice period, which is the whole subject of
pick A8. Nobody in the set says "fees are negotiable" and stops there.

**P6. The verdict on a disposal route arrives inside the first 120 words, in the writer's own
voice, and it is a judgement.**
Them (W11, holding p1 on a 2,900/mo head term): "The modern method of auction rarely delivers what
it promises sellers. A typical sale takes around four months, the buyer's fee of 4 to 6% scares
away most buyers, and all-in you can expect around 10 to 15% less than a good estate agent would
get you. It suits very few sellers, and most have better options."
Them (W11, opening): "If your estate agent has just recommended the modern method of auction, you
might be wondering if it's actually any good, or if you're about to get fleeced."
Them (W3): "If you're looking for a speedy sale and certainty that a buyer won't pull out of the
purchase, then selling a house at auction is a good way to go."
W11 is the single most instructive page in the set for picks A10, A11 and A12: Flesch 76.2, mean
sentence 14.0, headings written as tests ("Test 1: Is the modern method of auction fast?", "Test 4:
What does modern auction cost you?"), and the cost of the route quantified as a price haircut, "On
a £250,000 home, that's £25,000 to £35,000". It ranks p1 with the lowest second-person rate in the
set (27.9), which is the evidence that on route pages a **verdict** substitutes for direct address.

**P7. The CTA is a named free tool with a stated time cost, or a named comparison service. It is
never a form.**
Them (W12, closing block): "Cost of Selling a House Calculator. Get a clear, personalised breakdown
of your selling costs in under 60 seconds."
Them (W11, in the opening block): "If you want to cut right to the chase and find out whether
modern auction is right for you and your property, I've designed a free online quiz to help you
answer that very question. It takes about 60 seconds and you get your results straight away."
Them (W1, closing): "check their performance with our Best Estate Agent Finder tool."
Them (W2, closing): "Use our cost of moving calculator."
Them (W5, closing): "Get honest FREE valuations from your top local experts. Discover which local
agents will give you the most accurate FREE valuations."
Us (`epc-certificate-cost-uk`, closing): "The EPC is one of the cheapest documents in property, and
most of the money decisions around it are decisions not to spend."
The **"under 60 seconds" promise appears independently on two winner pages**, and every measured
winner hands off to a named tool rather than to contact details. Our cost page closes on a maxim.
This is the pattern the wave's calculator exists to satisfy, and the pillar plus all thirteen pages
should hand off to it in the winners' words: what it gives you, and how long it takes.

**P8. Question headings are the norm on fee and cost pages and they are the reader's exact query.**
Them (W1): 27 of 50 headings are questions. "How much are estate agent fees?", "What do estate
agent fees include?", "Do multi estate agency fees have to be more expensive?", "What are no sale,
no fee estate agents?"
Them (W13): 12 of 27. "What are the costs of selling a house in 2026?", "What fees do you pay when
selling a house?", "What Do I Need to Do to Sell My House?"
Us (`inheritance-tax-on-farms`): 7 of 7, and this is the model. "Will your family farm pay
inheritance tax?", "What does the £2.5 million allowance actually cover?", "Why do some pages still
say the cap is £1 million?"
Us (`epc-certificate-cost-uk`): **0 of 13**. "EPC cost by property type and booking route", "What
the price actually buys", "The tax treatment: landlord, seller, improver".
Our rural pages already beat the winner median on this metric. Our cost page has none. The lesson
is not that we cannot do it, it is that the cost-page precedent in our corpus is the wrong one.

**P9. The tax paragraph, where it exists at all, is short, plain and rate-shaped, and it never
opens the page.**
Them (W2, under the heading "Do you need to pay Capital Gains Tax?"): "You pay capital gains tax
when you sell an asset that has increased in value since you bought it. Usually, when you sell your
main home (or only home) you don't have to pay any capital gains tax (CGT) due to private residence
relief."
Them (W4, one sentence, the only deductibility statement in 2,292 words): "You can deduct the cost
of any improvement work you've carried out and also the transactional costs of buying & selling the
property (SDLT, solicitor fees, Estate Agent fees etc.)."
Them (W4, in a table cell): "Capital Gains Tax (CGT) | see notes below".
Us (`epc-certificate-cost-uk`, our equivalent): the tax section is three headings deep, arrives at
heading 8 of 13, and is written as "The seller: an incidental cost of disposal".
W4's sentence is the entire competitive field for this wave's hook, and it is loose: it lumps SDLT
(an acquisition cost) in with disposal costs, and it gives no figure. **Our differentiator is
correctness plus arithmetic, delivered in W4's register, not in ours.** One short block, plain
words, one pound figure showing the deduction working, then a link out.

the competitor set is consumer money media and the reader is a private seller nought to three
months from disposal, not a practitioner.

**Why the fee-page floor is 45 and not the winner median of 54.** Our house voice carries a factual
correction load the winners do not, and the highest second-person rates in the set (W14 at 76.1)
come from a page that is an advertisement for its own service, which we cannot copy. 45 to 60 puts
us inside the winner range at the bottom half, which is where W4, W9 and W13 sit, and all three
hold top-10 slots.

**The pillar specifically.** W2 is the p1 page on the pillar's own head term and it is 3,010 words,
zero tables, 7 of 29 question headings and 60.5 "you" per 1,000. Its structure is: an at-a-glance
box of six labelled figures, a "Calculate your total" line pointing at its own calculator, a "Top
tip" line, then one section per cost. The pillar should be built on that skeleton, with the
child-page links doing the work HOA's inline detail does. The pillar carries the three-bucket tax
split (deductible disposal costs, banked acquisition costs, no relief) because it is the only page
in the wave that sees the whole bill, and it carries it as **one block near the end**, not as a
frame.

**The calculator landing copy specifically.** Two independent winners promise a result in "under 60
seconds" (W12) and "about 60 seconds" (W11), and W12's line is the complete pattern: "Get a clear,
personalised breakdown of your selling costs in under 60 seconds. Whether you're selling only,
buying and selling, or just planning ahead, the tool helps you budget accurately and avoid hidden
costs." Copy the shape, not the words: what you get, how long, which situations it covers. Do not
put the methodology above the tool. W13 is the counter-example: it is a calculator site whose
cost page opens with 120 words about what the calculator can do before any number appears, and it
ranks p7 and p10 while HOA's calculator, mentioned in one line inside a cost guide, sits at p1.

**Where the CGT hook goes on every page type.** After the reader's cost question is fully answered
and before the CTA. One block. Plain-word statement that selling costs come off the gain, one
pound figure showing it working, the PRR carve-out in one sentence, then the link out to the CGT
pillar. Never in the opening. Never as an H1 or as the first H2. Never as a rate table, which the
armed cgt1 batch owns.

## 4. Do not copy

The contrast rows in this pass are drawn from inside the winner set, because the measured spread
runs from p1 to p10 and the low end is instructive. All four pages below hold a top-10 slot and all
four carry habits that must not enter our corpus.

**W13 movingcostscalculator.co.uk (p7 and p10, the weakest measured cost page).** Measured:
- **27 em-dashes**, the only page in the set with more than two, against a winner median of zero.
- Its two cost tables are a near-verbatim clone of W4's, down to the same "See notes below" cells
  and the same row order, with a Source column bolted on.
- Sixteen soft-authority name-drops used as sourcing ("according to HMRC", "the HomeOwners Alliance
  recommends"), which is citation theatre: it names a body instead of a rule.
- Duplicated sentences left in the body: its negotiation advice appears twice, once with a full
  stop and once with an exclamation mark.
- It is the only winner that attempts CGT seriously and it does so as a rate list plus a 60-day
  reporting line, which is exactly the material the armed cgt1 batch owns. Do not follow it there.

**W5 theadvisory.co.uk (p10 on its own head term, 9,801 words).** Measured:
- Seventy-two headings and seventy-one lists on a single page, and it still only reaches p10, where
  W7 reaches p1 on the same query with 1,477 words. **Length is not the lever.**
- First-person brand performance in place of an answer: "This is the only guide to estate agency
  fees (on the internet) written by 'serial' house sellers.", "As you can imagine, we have
  opinions!"
- It opens on credentials and a promise list, not on a number. No figure appears within 60 words.
- Its tail carried a forty-line directory of international estate agency associations, which is
  sitewide furniture on an editorial page.

**W14 purplebricks.co.uk (p6).** Highest second-person rate measured anywhere (76.1) and the reason
is that the page is an advertisement. "Estate agent fees are typically between 1% and 3.5% of your
house sale, or a flat fee (we'll sell your home for a fair, fixed fee, of course, but more on that
later)." and "Of course, if you sell with Purplebricks, you can choose how you pay. Three different
options. The choice is yours." Take the direct address, never the self-reference. Our standing rule
against pricing our own services makes this pattern unavailable in any case.

**W4 theadvisory.co.uk (p5).** Its tables are the model for the cluster and its opening is not:
"Insider tips and secrets from serial house sellers", "How to budget accurately and calculate your
'net' profit", "And lots more…". A promise list is not an answer, and this page carries no figure
inside its first 120 words despite having 41 pound figures further down.

**W6 which.co.uk (p6).** Opens on "Moving home can be an expensive business, with lots of different
fees to factor in.", which is the "cost varies" opening this spec's P1 rules out, and it closes
several hundred words away from the query on remortgaging and a mortgage-broker referral. One of
eight headings is a question. It is the lowest question-heading share in the set outside W12.

**Our own `epc-certificate-cost-uk`, which is the precedent this wave must not inherit.** It is our
only shipped consumer cost page and on five of the seven measured axes it is the outlier: mean
sentence 24.0 against a winner ceiling of 20.4, Flesch 48.0 against a winner floor of 62.0, "you"
10.7 against a winner floor of 27.9, zero question headings against a winner median of 4.5, and
5.5 hard statutory references per 1,000 words against a winner rate of 0.25. It also carries a
named worked-example persona ("Gwen lets a three-bedroom terrace"), which this wave's rule
prohibits outright. Its tax section is correct and well sourced, and that content is worth keeping;
the register around it is not.

**Not measured, and not a model.** telegraph.co.uk holds four top-10 slots across the harvest, the
second-highest density of any domain, on national-press paywalled feature writing. It was not
fetched and nothing in this spec is derived from it. It is a reminder that some of this SERP is won
by brand and distribution rather than by page craft, and that those slots are not addressable.

## 5. Hard rules for writers in this cluster

Standing (all clusters, restated because they are enforced at QA):
- **Zero em-dashes.** Ten of twelve winners use zero. W13 uses 27 and ranks p7.
- **UK English.**
- **No Property Tax Partners pricing on page, ever**, and no self-referential fee comparison. W14
  is the pattern to avoid.
- **No named worked-example personas.** The wave rule. Worked examples use a stated property value
  and no person.
- **The CGT hook is mandatory and it never opens the page.** The page opens on the reader's cost
  question. Tax arrives after the answer is complete.

Cluster-specific, each from a measurement above:

1. **Sentence length at or below 18 words mean, and at or below 17 on route pages.** Winner median
   17.0, winner ceiling 20.4. Our three pages run 20.0 to 24.0. This is the single most consistent
   gap and it is the one QA can check mechanically.
2. **Flesch at or above 62 on every page, and at or above 68 on route pages.** Winner floor is 62.0
   across twelve pages. Our cost page is 48.0.
3. **Second person at 45 per 1,000 or above on fee, cost and pillar pages; 28 or above on route
   pages.** Winner median 54.4. Our cost page is 10.7.
4. **Zero hard statutory references in body prose.** No section numbers, no SI numbers, no Act
   names, no `legislation.gov.uk` in a sentence. Ten of twelve winners cite none. The statutory
   anchors in each pack are for the writer's accuracy and for the fact-check trail, not for the
   page. Where a rule must be attributed, attribute it in plain words ("the rules require the fee
   quote to include VAT") and put the citation in the pack, not the prose.
5. **Every percentage gets a pound conversion in the same sentence or the next one**, against a
   stated property value. W1's "So on a £275,000 house this would mean estate agent fees of around
   £3,900" is the model.
6. **Every cost page opens with a total in pounds tied to a stated property value.** Not a range
   alone, not a percentage alone, not "it depends on".
7. **An at-a-glance box on every fee, cost and pillar page**: five to seven lines, each a label,
   a figure and one condition. This is what the p1 page does and it is the format the SERP quotes.
8. **Cost tables are two or three columns and carry a total row.** `Item | Cost` or
   `Item | Typical cost | What moves it`. Never five columns. Tables are optional (winner median is
   zero); the total row is not optional when a table exists.
9. **Question headings: at least 40% of H2s on fee and route pages, at least 30% on cost pages and
   the pillar**, each one a complete question in the reader's words. W1 runs 27 of 50 and holds two
   p2 slots. Our rural pages already run 7 of 7 and 8 of 12, so this is a solved problem in-house.
10. **Negotiation advice gives a target number or a script.** "Aim for 1.2% including VAT on sole
    agency", "get three quotes", "say you have a cheaper quote elsewhere", "negotiate the tie-in
    rather than the rate". Never "fees are negotiable" as the whole advice.
11. **Route pages carry a verdict inside 120 words and price the route as a pound haircut.** W11's
    "around 10 to 15% less than a good estate agent would get you" and "On a £250,000 home, that's
    £25,000 to £35,000" is the shape for A10, A11 and A12.
12. **The CTA is the cost-of-selling calculator, named, with what it returns and how long it
    takes.** Never "use the form at the foot of the page". Never a closing maxim. Two winners
    independently promise a result in about 60 seconds.
13. **The CGT block is one block, plain words, one pound figure, one link out.** No rate table, no
    annual exempt amount explainer, no PRR mechanics, no 60-day filing walkthrough. Those belong to
    the armed cgt1 batch and restating them here creates the cannibalisation the freeze exists to
    prevent.
14. **Do not chase word count.** The measured winner set runs 962 to 9,801 words. The p1 pages are
    1,005, 1,477 and 3,010 words. The 9,801-word page ranks p10.
